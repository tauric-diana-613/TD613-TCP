## Formal object, revised

Let S be source state; P_A the projection authorized for receiver A; H=h(S) a precisely declared protected target; B_m the observer m's prior permitted view; K_m its allowed auxiliary knowledge; Z_m the complete captured trace for that observer over the declared horizon. H is a target random variable or set of tasks, never an informal subtraction S minus P.

P_A may be hidden from m. Therefore conditioning universally on P_A would give every observer an artificial baseline. Declare a separate B_m. Where P_A is deliberately public, include it in B_m.

For a declared joint distribution and channel, proposed coordinates are:

- baseline exposure: I(H; B_m,K_m);
- incremental disclosure: I(H; Z_m | B_m,K_m);
- total exposure: I(H; B_m,K_m,Z_m);
- task-relative adversarial gain: optimal declared loss/risk with the baseline versus with the trace;
- authorized utility: error of A's declared operation, including transformed-input worst cases.

The chain rule relates the first three; it does not make them interchangeable. Also report remaining uncertainty or reconstruction success. High prior recoverability can make incremental gain small.

Matched-secret experiments hold the authorized operation/projection and declared public context fixed while varying H over supported alternatives. Impossible or nonsensical secret pairs reveal an incoherent privacy contract. They must not be fabricated to improve a score.

## Necessary feasibility check

If the authorized operation must output H exactly to the same observer being protected against, accurate utility and secret non-reconstruction conflict. Architecture cannot repair an inconsistent permission assignment. Narrow the task, change the observer, or explicitly declassify with human authority. Document the conflict before choosing a mitigation.
