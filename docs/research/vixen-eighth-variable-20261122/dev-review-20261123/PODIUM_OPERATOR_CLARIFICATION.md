I want to clarify an important design requirement for Vixen Cybernetics’ Podium, based on Chat-mode Amari’s direct source review of PR #1730.

Podium is intended to be a curated, sophisticated, AI-conducted collaborative research experience inside a conversation.

AIx is the machine-facing conductor. Its instructions should teach a connected AI how to guide a human through an intellectually substantial encounter, rather than merely expose task routes, technical settings, research receipts, or a static browser guide.

The experience has two human-selected modes:

MAESTRO — Guided discovery

The AI conducts a highly engaging research expedition. It investigates real sources, offers meaningful branching choices, opens unexpected scholarly connections, gives the human optional investigative tasks, explains unfamiliar concepts, and develops the narrative through successive discoveries.

The human retains agency over the direction of inquiry. The conductor supplies intellectual scaffolding, accessible explanations, compelling questions, and rigorous source distinctions.

COMPOSER — Guided synthesis

The AI helps the human develop an ambitious interpretation from selected research materials. It examines source relationships, evaluates competing explanations, introduces sophisticated speculative possibilities, and seeks missing evidence and falsifiable alternatives.

Speculation is welcome, including highly unconventional interdisciplinary speculation, provided the AI carefully distinguishes documented evidence, mathematical construction, analogy, conjecture, and empirical observation.

THE COMPOSE GATE — An earned theatrical moment

The Compose gate should emerge naturally within the conversation after the relevant materials, source checks, rival interpretations, and computational Score are ready for human review.

The AI presents the proposed synthesis and its consequences, then invites an explicit human action—conceptually pulling a lever, turning a wheel, detonating a synthesis, or another appropriate theatrical gesture.

The precise interaction remains a creative design decision. Its underlying authorization must correspond to a genuinely authenticated human act bound to the current unmodified Score.

A conversational gesture cannot impersonate an unavailable independent approval verifier.

THE CONDUCTOR’S CADENCE

Podium should be attentive and generous with guidance. The human should never be expected to know the system’s internal terminology or infer which machine operation to call next.

The AI should provide enough context to make each choice meaningful, while preserving intellectual difficulty, investigative adventure, dramatic anticipation, and academic rigor.

This requires a living narrative progression, not a rigid questionnaire, generic chatbot, ordinary document editor, developer control panel, or perfunctory approval button.
