## 2. The type problem H1 must survive

The generic sentence contains an undeclared support relation:

```text

<=
support of underlying state transition
```

That relation is currently underspecified in three ways.

First, the left-hand side mixes unlike objects:

```text

scope
role
consequence
```

Second, the right-hand side says `state transition` without declaring whether support comes from:

```text
measurement model
measurement admissibility
decision consequence
route history
endpoint state
or some composition of these
```

Third, no common scalar or total ordering has been earned across those objects.

Therefore this hostile assay does **not** test a fake equation such as:

```text

```

Instead it tests whether the generic wording predicts forbidden coupling, erases a legitimate invariant, or becomes non-falsifiable unless its support domain is typed.

### Candidate corpse condition

The generic form is killed if an admissible specimen requires one typed consequence relation to move while another legitimately remains fixed, and H1 can classify the specimen only by silently importing an undeclared axis into `underlying state transition`.

### Candidate rescue — attack only, not doctrine

A narrower typed proposition may eventually resemble:

```text
for declared axis a,
a consequence claim carried on a
may not silently widen beyond evidence carried on a
```

This candidate receives no presumption of survival. The assay should try to kill it too.
