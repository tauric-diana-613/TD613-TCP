## A repair certified; a home altered

In the Meridian House counterworld, an independently authorized and competent agency repairs the load-bearing riser. It issues accurate physical certificates and makes no false measurement. In the first corridor, every household can stay. In the second, the building is restored faster by evacuating it, but the return process reassigns housing cost, proof burdens and time to the residents. By the end, the riser is safe, and only 35 percent of households return. The inspector records physical success because that is what the inspection measures.

In the third corridor, the repair promise continues through successive periods. One stipulated synthetic subject remains attached to the promised home because the promise sustains routines and neighborhood ties while postponement prolongs exposure. Another stays for lack of viable alternatives, without the stipulated attachment. Identical observed occupancy conceals different motives. Neither may be diagnosed from a tenancy ledger.

The fourth corridor offers protected relocation with an enforceable right of return. In one variant, support arrives in time and 95 percent return. In the other, nominally identical support arrives after the model's liquidity threshold has been crossed; half fail to return. A promise honored too late does not turn the intervening history backward.

## The observability result

Let `pi_T(c_0,...,c_T)=c_T` observe *only final-period cost*, and `Sigma_w(c)=sum(w_t*c_t)` sum period costs with positive weights. For `T>=1`, `rank(pi_T)=1`, `nullity(pi_T)=T`, and generally there exist histories with identical `c_T` and different cumulative costs. This is a proof about this explicit *cost trajectory projection*, not a theorem about the rank of every terminal seven-field residential ledger. A full terminal record may separately include cumulative history if its schema explicitly preserves it.

The finite two-household demonstration fixes physical compliance at `[0,.5,1]` in both worlds. In one history terminal resident occupancy is zero, in the other it is one. A compliance inspector therefore cannot distinguish these histories; the housing-continuity auditor can. Neither can infer the unobserved reasons for residents' choices.
