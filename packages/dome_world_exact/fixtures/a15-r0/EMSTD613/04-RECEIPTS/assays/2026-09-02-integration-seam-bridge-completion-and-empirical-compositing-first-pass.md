#### Source B — LLM-Agent-Controller

`LLM-Agent-Controller: A Universal Multi-Agent Large Language Model System as a Control Engineer`
arXiv:2505.19567
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19567

```text

```

across five categories of Control Theory problems and three LLMs.

Its described architecture centers on a controller / supervisor and specialized auxiliary agents with RAG, reasoning, self-criticism, memory, simulation, and communication tools.

### Source/row collision

The Em-work table labels the 78.1% row:

```text

```

but 78.1 / 46.1 / 60,000 belong to the distinct Experience Orchestrator paper, while 83 / 87 belong to LLM-Agent-Controller.

Therefore the empirical table does not merely aggregate related evidence. It collapses two distinct evaluated systems into one apparent architecture comparison.

The decisive forensic question is therefore not merely whether every component has a citation.

It is:

## 7. Strong hostile alternatives

This result remains vulnerable to several ordinary explanations:

1. **Generative-report composition artifact** — research synthesis models are rewarded for closing a report into one elegant architecture.
2. **Missing originating prompt/spec** — the absent prompt may have explicitly supplied some bridges.
3. **Draft / playful workspace status** — these are hand-selected workspace exports, not asserted final publications.
4. **Omitted implementation layer** — a real project may contain transducers not present in the exported report.
5. **Citation compression / table-generation error** — the empirical collision may be a report assembly mistake rather than a stable research method.

These alternatives remain live and are preferable to motive attribution absent stronger evidence.
