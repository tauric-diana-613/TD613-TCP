Sorting based on paperwork completeness, compliance, or procedural fit. 6.2 Temporal Triage Sorting based on wait times, urgency, or institutional bandwidth. 6.3 Risk ‑ Based Triage Sorting based on perceived risk, often shaped by surveillance and bias. 6.4 Resource Triage Sorting based on scarcity—who receives limited supports. 6.5 Behavioral Triage Sorting based on perceived “cooperation,” “worthiness,” or “fit.” 7. A Typology of Accumulation 7.1 Case Accumulation Growing backlogs and unresolved files. 7.2 Burden Accumulation Increasing administrative demands placed on individuals. 7.3 Crisis Accumulation Unresolved emergencies that compound over time. 7.4 Structural Accumulation Long ‑ term underfunding, understaffing, and infrastructural decay. 8. Mechanisms of Institutional Response Institutions under strain often respond by:

• tightening eligibility • increasing surveillance • reducing flexibility • shifting responsibility onto individuals • normalizing crisis conditions • prioritizing efficiency over care These responses reinforce exposure, slow harm, and erosion. 9. Additional Real ‑ World Example: Housing Systems Housing systems under strain exhibit the full STAR sequence: • Systems: limited affordable units, long waitlists • Triage: prioritizing families, deprioritizing single adults • Accumulation: growing backlogs, years ‑ long delays • Response: stricter eligibility, increased inspections, reduced flexibility These dynamics expose people to instability, produce slow harm through prolonged uncertainty, and

The STAR Framework recognizes that: • some triage is necessary in resource ‑ limited environments • accumulation may reflect structural underfunding rather than institutional intent • institutional responses are often constrained by law, policy, or budget • not all systems produce harm at the same intensity These conditions shape how STAR dynamics manifest across different infrastructures. 12. Methodological and Practical Implications The STAR Framework invites researchers and practitioners to: • map triage pathways • measure accumulation patterns • analyze institutional responses • identify overload thresholds • design interventions that reduce systemic strain • evaluate policies that buffer exposure, slow harm, and erosion STAR provides a practical diagnostic tool for redesigning systems that currently default to crisis logic. 13. Contribution to the Literature The STAR Framework advances the literature by: • naming triage as a structural mechanism • identifying accumulation as a system ‑ level process • linking institutional behavior to SIT’s harm mechanisms • providing a practical diagnostic tool for system analysis It clarifies how systems distribute harm under strain and how overload becomes normalized.
