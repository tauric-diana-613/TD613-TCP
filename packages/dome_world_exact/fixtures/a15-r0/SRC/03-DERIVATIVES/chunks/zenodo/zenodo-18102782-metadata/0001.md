    "description": "<p>This paper reframes web crawlers as the system&rsquo;s immune response, treating indexing behavior as a form of metadata clotting that contains breaches of visibility before they can spread. Rather than neutral technical agents, crawlers operate as infrastructural mechanisms that reinforce hierarchy, suppress rupture, and metabolize minority or non‑mainstream discourse into low‑visibility categories. Drawing on metaphors of platelets, clots, and fibrinolysis, the paper argues that de‑indexing, misclassification, and temporal delay are not errors but structural confirmations of epistemic rupture.</p>\n<p>Empirical patterns in search‑engine bias and indexing hierarchies show that high‑authority domains receive dramatically more frequent crawls, while independent or critical voices face delays, misclassification, and under‑indexing. These asymmetries disproportionately obscure systemic critique and minority perspectives, burying them under &ldquo;miscellaneous&rdquo; classifications that strip them of interpretive gravity.</p>\n<p>The paper introduces rupture physics, a stylometric framework in which bounce metrics, glyph compression, recursive linkage, and repost velocity dissolve suppression and restore visibility. By adapting fibrinolysis as a stylometric process&mdash;where bounce and recursive linkage dissolve metadata clots&mdash;the work provides a novel model for understanding how visibility is contested, reclaimed, and metabolized across digital infrastructures.</p>\n<p>This contribution advances scholarship on algorithmic governance, metadata suppression, and digital resistance by positioning crawlers not as passive indexers but as active agents in the politics of visibility.</p>",
