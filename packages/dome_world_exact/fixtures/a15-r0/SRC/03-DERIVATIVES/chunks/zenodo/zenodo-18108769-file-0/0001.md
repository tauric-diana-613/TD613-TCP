Correlation Vs. Causation: Lifestyle as Policy, Containment as Architecture Abstract Public health narratives often frame correlation as lifestyle: smoking leads to illness, studying leads

to

success,

poverty

leads

to

struggle.

This

framing

reduces

systemic

outcomes

to

personal

choices

and

obscures

the

architecture

that

produces

them.

Correlation

is

containment

—

the

way

systems

trap

people

in

patterns.

Causation

is

signal

—

the

systemic

design

that

generates

those

patterns.

What

is

labeled

“lifestyle”

is

often

policy.

People

are

encouraged

to

exercise,

yet

systemic

demands

leave

no

time.

They

are

told

to

eat

healthy,

yet

grocery

shelves

are

filled

with

processed

food.

They

are

urged

to

care

for

mental

health,

yet

workplaces

remain

toxic.

ADHD

is

framed

as

genetic

correlation,

yet

micronutrient

deficiencies

are

ignored,

and

health

systems

cover

expensive

medications

while

excluding

nutritional

testing.

Alcohol

and

drugs

are

discouraged,

yet

promoted

in

media.

Misogyny

and

toxic

masculinity

are

condemned,

yet

rewarded

with

fame.

Soviet

housing,

industry,

and

surveillance

demonstrate

how

architecture,

not

lifestyle,

produces

illness,

suppression,

and

collapse.

Recognizing

this

distinction

reframes

mental

health

and

social

outcomes

as

systemic

design

rather

than

individual

choice. Keywords Correlation; Causation; Containment; Signal; Mental Health; ADHD; Nutrition; Lifestyle; Policy; Infrastructure;

Architecture Theta: Belief, Frame, Reality • What society believes: We are taught that correlation is about lifestyle. If you smoke, you get sick. If you study, you succeed.

If

you

are

poor,

you

struggle.

The

story

is

always

personal

choice,

a

morality

tale

about

habits

and

discipline

(Public

Health

Ontario,

2023). • How studies frame it: Research papers talk about “risk factors” and “associations.” They say overcrowding is linked to illness,

or

unemployment

is

linked

to

dissent.

It

sounds

neutral,

technical,

detached

(Cobbe,

2020).

The

framing

makes

it

seem

like

numbers

float

above

daily

life,

disconnected

from

the

architecture

that

produces

them. • What it actually is:

Correlation is containment — the way systems are built to trap people in patterns. Causation is signal

—

the

reason

those

patterns

exist.

It

is

not

lifestyle,

it

is

architecture.

Soviet

housing,

industry,

and

surveillance

show

that

what

looks

like

personal

systemic

design

(Harris

2022;

Lopezmalo,

2025). Strong Correlation → Everyday Containment • Belief: Families in Soviet communal apartments got sick because of bad habits. • Frame: Studies showed a strong correlation between crowded housing and respiratory illness. • Reality: The apartments were built with poor ventilation, shared kitchens, and limited sanitation.

The

architecture

itself

produced

disease

(Harris

&

De

Leeuw,

2022).

The

correlation

was

not

lifestyle

—

it

was

containment.

The

causation

was

signal:

housing

design

created

illness. Positive Correlation → Everyday Expansion • Belief: More factories meant more prosperity. • Frame: Studies showed a positive correlation between industrial output and GDP. • Reality: As production rose, so did pollution and cancer rates. Growth was suppression disguised

as

progress

(Fraser

Institute,

2024).

The

correlation

was

expansion;

the

causation

was

signal:

industrial

architecture

produced

ecological

collapse. Negative Correlation → Everyday Suppression • Belief: People who spoke out lost jobs because they took risks. • Frame: Studies showed a negative correlation between dissent and employment. • Reality: Soviet blacklists and surveillance systems excluded dissenters from jobs, housing, and

education.

The

correlation

was

suppression;

the

causation

was

signal:

surveillance

architecture

metabolized
