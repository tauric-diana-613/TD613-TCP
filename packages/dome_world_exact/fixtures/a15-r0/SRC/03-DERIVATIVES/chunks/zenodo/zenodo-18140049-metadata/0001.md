    "description": "<p>This paper introduces the concept of slow harm: the cumulative, often invisible injuries produced by systems, infrastructures, and institutions over time. Drawing on Social Infrastructure Theory (SIT), it reframes harm as an infrastructural output rather than an individual failure. Building on foundational work in slow violence, structural violence, administrative burden, and social suffering, the essay argues that slow harm is a distinct mode of governance &mdash; one that obscures causality, diffuses responsibility, and normalizes deprivation. Through examples ranging from trauma and bureaucratic delay to the criminalization of survival, the paper demonstrates how design choices and policy architectures produce predictable patterns of injury. It concludes by calling for a shift from event‑based morality to systemic ethics, positioning slow harm as a core analytic for understanding contemporary governance.</p>",
