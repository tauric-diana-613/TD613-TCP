Design Governance: Enforceable Prescriptions To address attention collapse, regulators should adopt design ‑ level guardrails: • Default friction: autoplay opt ‑ in, natural stopping cues, session summaries. • Arousal ‑ aware diversification: cooldown content after high ‑ intensity streaks. • Age ‑ sensitive defaults: long ‑ form prioritization, time budgets, privacy ‑ preserving age assurance. • Independent audits: annual evaluations of ranking systems using attention metrics. • Creator incentive rebalancing: reward depth and session quality. • Research access: standardized, privacy ‑ preserving data for independent study. These measures realign incentives toward attention stewardship. 8. Addressing Common Objections

• Free expression: design governance regulates architecture, not ideas. • Innovation: safety baselines enable healthier competition. • Complexity: phased implementation and standardized audits reduce friction. The alternative—unchecked attention extraction—imposes diffuse social and civic costs. 9. Policy Memo: Operational Steps for Regulators 1. Define attention, harm, and measurable indicators. 2. Set scope thresholds for platforms and feed types. 3. Mandate friction defaults and time budgets for minors. 4. Require privacy ‑ preserving age assurance. 5. Enforce annual independent audits. 6. Provide research access to anonymized datasets. 7. Establish remediation timelines and penalties.
