Relationship to Digital Platform Theories Societal Infrastructure Theory is the macro ‑ layer of the SR canon. Digital platforms operate at the meso ‑ layer. User behavior and cognition operate at the micro ‑ layer. This theory explains: • why symbolic infrastructure matters • why Quiet Governance works • why Metadata Suppression harms • why Attention Collapse is systemic • why Containment Logic emerges • why Infrastructural Exposure is dangerous Digital platforms are not anomalies; they are extensions of societal infrastructures. They inherit, amplify, and sometimes distort the symbolic and epistemic systems that already exist. Implications Societal Infrastructure Theory suggests that: • social change requires infrastructural change • meaning ‑ making is a form of governance • institutions reproduce symbolic power • digital platforms inherit and amplify societal infrastructures • societal harms are infrastructural, not incidental • governance must be understood at the level of meaning, not just policy This theory reframes society as an infrastructural system of meaning, not merely a collection of individuals
