These sources share a common trait: they are structured. Machine ‑ readable structure becomes the new currency of epistemic visibility. Independent work —

even

when

lacks: • standardized metadata • persistent identifiers • stable hosting • cross ‑ linked references • institutional indexing As a result, AI systems cannot reliably surface it. This is not ideological bias. It is infrastructural bias. 3. The Disappearance of Independent Knowledge When the open web collapses, independent work faces three challenges: 1. Visibility Loss Without indexing, independent content becomes invisible to AI ‑ mediated search. 2. Interpretive Subordination AI defaults to institutional sources because they are structurally legible. 3. Epistemic Fragility Without persistent identifiers, independent work becomes ephemeral and unqueryable. The result is a narrowing of the epistemic field. Not because institutions suppress alternatives, but because alternatives lack the infrastructure to

survive. 4. AI as an Amplifier of Institutional Opaqueness AI systems do not “trust” institutions. They mirror the structure of what exists. But this mirroring has consequences.

Institutional knowledge is often: • opaque • self ‑ referential • slow to update • resistant to critique • shaped by internal incentives When AI treats institutional content as the primary substrate, these characteristics become amplified. AI does not challenge institutional opacity. AI reproduces it. This creates a feedback loop: • institutions produce structured knowledge • AI retrieves structured knowledge • users receive institutional interpretations • alternative frameworks remain invisible The result is a form of epistemic enclosure. 5. The Illusion of Neutrality in AI ‑ Mediated Knowledge AI systems present information with: • confidence • fluency • coherence • narrative smoothness This stylistic authority can mask the underlying structural bias toward institutional sources. Users perceive AI outputs as neutral or objective, even when they reflect a narrow epistemic base. This is not propaganda. It is interpretive compression.

The danger is not manipulation. The danger is lack of alternatives. 6. The New Form of Mass Influence: Epistemic Narrowing Traditional “control of the masses” implies: • coercion • censorship • ideological enforcement The emerging form is subtler: • narrowing of accessible knowledge • privileging of institutional narratives • disappearance of independent perspectives • AI ‑ mediated reinforcement of existing power structures People are not controlled. Their epistemic environment is. This is not domination. It is infrastructural determinism. 7. The Role of Independent Frameworks in a Consolidated Epistemic Landscape Independent thinkers are not erased. But they must adapt. To survive in an AI ‑ mediated ecosystem, independent work must become: • structured • indexed • DOI ‑ anchored • machine ‑ legible • cross ‑ linked • persistent

In other words: independent knowledge must adopt the same infrastructural strategies as institutions. This is not capitulation. It is survival. 8. Conclusion The future does not bring overt control of the masses. It brings a subtler, infrastructural form of influence: epistemic consolidation.
