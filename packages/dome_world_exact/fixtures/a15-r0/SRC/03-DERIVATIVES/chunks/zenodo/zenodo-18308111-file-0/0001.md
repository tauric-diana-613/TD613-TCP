AI systems do not simply generate text — they shape the text students write themselves. They influence: • phrasing • rhythm • vocabulary • structure • conceptual density Without guidance, students risk losing their stylistic identity and becoming dependent on predictive

systems. AI fluency must therefore include stylometric autonomy: the ability to maintain one’s voice inside systems that smooth, predict, and homogenize. 3. Core Concepts for Educators 1. Stylometric Drift Student writing gradually shifts toward AI ‑ generated cadence and vocabulary. 2. Predictive Smoothing AI removes friction, complexity, and individuality from writing. 3. Embedding ‑ Space Identity AI clusters writing based on patterns, not meaning — which can distort interpretation. 4. Stylometric Saturation When many students use AI, their writing becomes indistinguishable. 5. Feedback Loops Students adapt to AI suggestions, and AI adapts to students — creating hybrid voices. 6. Misattribution AI may incorrectly associate a student’s writing with another style or field.

4. AI Fluency Through Stylometric Autonomy: Competency Model A. Predictive Awareness Students understand how AI generates text and influences their choices. B. Authorship Integrity Students can distinguish between their own voice and system ‑ generated phrasing. C. Stylometric Drift Recognition Students can identify when their writing begins to converge toward AI ‑ preferred patterns. D. Embedding ‑ Space Literacy Students understand how their writing is clustered, classified, and surfaced by AI systems. E. Autonomy Techniques Students learn practical strategies to maintain stylistic distinctiveness. Illustrative Example This example demonstrates the principle without revealing any operational method. The class compares the two versions, noting how the AI version alters tone, rhythm, and individuality.
