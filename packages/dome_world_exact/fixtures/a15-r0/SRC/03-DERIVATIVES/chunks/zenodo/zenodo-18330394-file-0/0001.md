Why AI Becomes the Perfect Scapegoat AI has three qualities that make it the ideal target for public outrage: 1. It is visible. People can point to it, screenshot it, regulate it. 2. It is new. New systems attract scrutiny; old systems attract resignation.

3. It is externalized. People can blame “the AI” without confronting the institutions that produced the data it learned from. This is the core mechanism of infrastructural scapegoating: AI absorbs the blame for structural patterns that predate it. The system remains invisible. The model becomes the villain. 5. The Regulatory Misdirection Loop As panic grows, governments will regulate AI aggressively. This is not inherently wrong — oversight is necessary — but the direction of regulation will be misaligned. AI will be scrutinized. AI will be audited. AI will be restricted. Meanwhile: • the infrastructures that generated the bias • the institutions that produced the data • the platforms that shaped behavior • the systems that enforced cognitive normativity …will remain largely untouched. AI becomes the shield that protects the structure. This is the regulatory misdirection loop: the visible system absorbs the punishment for the invisible one. 6. Infrastructural Scapegoating: A Structural Theory

Infrastructural scapegoating occurs when: 1. A new system becomes visible. 2. The public identifies harmful patterns in that system. 3. Those patterns are traced to the system itself, not the infrastructure behind it. 4. Regulation targets the visible layer. 5. The underlying structure remains unchallenged. AI is the perfect candidate for this process because it is: • legible • external • nameable • technologically distinct But the patterns it reproduces are not technological. They are infrastructural. AI is not the cause. AI is the interface. 7. The Invisibility of Infrastructure Infrastructures shape populations quietly: • through defaults • through incentives • through scale • through drift • through accumulated pressure They do not announce themselves. They do not claim agency. They do not appear in public discourse. This invisibility is their power. When AI enters the scene, it becomes the first system that shows the patterns. It becomes the first system that can be blamed.

The infrastructure remains untouched because it remains unseen. 8. Why This Moment Is Dangerous If society misidentifies the source of inequality, it will: • regulate the wrong layer • reinforce the underlying structure • deepen dependency on predictive mediation • allow infrastructures to continue shaping populations without accountability The danger is not AI. The danger is misdiagnosis. A misdiagnosed system cannot be repaired. It can only be punished. And punishment directed at the wrong layer strengthens the layer beneath it. 9. The Role of the SignalRupture Canon The SR canon: • names the infrastructure • maps the conditioning sequence • exposes drift, erosion, and offloading • reveals predictive mediation as an endpoint, not a cause • critiques cognitive normativity as an infrastructural artifact • reframes bias as structural, not technological Most importantly: It prevents the system from hiding behind AI. The canon refuses the scapegoat. It names the architecture.

In a world where AI will take society’s punches, the canon identifies the real target: the infrastructure that shaped the model, the data, and the population long before AI existed. 10. Conclusion: Naming the Structure AI will be blamed for inequality. AI will be regulated for bias. AI will be treated as the threat. But the threat is older. The threat is deeper. The threat is infrastructural. AI is the mirror. The infrastructure is the face. The public will attack the mirror. The system will remain untouched. Unless someone names it.
