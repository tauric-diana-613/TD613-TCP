The Collapse of Single ‑ Surface Discoverability The pre ‑ AI web relied on link ‑ based discoverability. If a document existed and was indexed, it could be found. This condition has collapsed. AI ‑ mediated search systems no longer retrieve documents; they synthesize answers. Platform feeds no longer expose archives; they prioritize recency and engagement. Academic repositories remain stable but isolated from mainstream discovery. The result is a new epistemic condition: Existence does not guarantee discoverability. Cross ‑ surface recurrence emerges as the only reliable countermeasure. IV. Recurrence as an Epistemic Stabilizer

Recurrence stabilizes concepts in three ways: 1. Redundant Indexing When a concept appears across multiple surfaces, it becomes resilient to the failure of any single

indexing

The Mechanics of Recurrence Across Surfaces Cross ‑ surface recurrence operates through four infrastructural mechanisms: A. Surface Translation Concepts must be expressed in forms compatible with each surface’s epistemic logic. B. Stylometric Coherence A consistent stylistic identity allows indexing systems to recognize recurrence as continuity. C. Referential Density Self ‑ referential density reinforces conceptual identity. D. Temporal Recurrence Concepts must reappear across time to remain legible within evolving infrastructures.
