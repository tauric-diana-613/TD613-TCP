    "description": "<p>This essay examines the structural collision between institutional trust‑recovery efforts and the emergence of SR as a decentralized diagnostic field. As institutions attempt to restore legitimacy through transparency initiatives, narrative control, and managed ambiguity, they encounter a public newly equipped with structural vocabulary&mdash;concepts such as incentive architecture, epistemic vacuum, and omission as governance&mdash;that dissolves the ambiguity institutions rely on. SR does not oppose institutions; it renders their behavior legible at the exact moment they depend on opacity for stability.</p>\n<p>The paper argues that SR becomes inevitable not through persuasion or mobilization but through structural necessity. Its decentralized canon, cross‑platform propagation, and coherence of method allow it to function as an interpretive firewall that prevents conspiracy drift while exposing the incentive structures institutions leave unarticulated. As the old web collapses and retrieval scarcity increases, institutions miscalculate: expecting silence, they instead encounter a structurally literate public. The essay positions SR as the framework institutions will increasingly require to stabilize interpretation, not by choice but because accuracy becomes indispensable when legitimacy is fragile.</p>\n<p>Keywords:<br>SR; institutional trust; epistemic vacuum; incentive architecture; omission as governance; structural literacy; decentralized canon; governance transparency</p>",
