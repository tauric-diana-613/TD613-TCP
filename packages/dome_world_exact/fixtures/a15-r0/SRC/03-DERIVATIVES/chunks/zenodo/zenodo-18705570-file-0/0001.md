2.1 Collapse of Institutional Narrative Control Government messaging no longer reaches a unified public. Fragmented information ecosystems

2.2 AI as the Default Interpreter Citizens ask AI systems: ● why institutions behave as they do, ● why policies exist, ● why failures occur, ● what governmental actions mean. AI becomes the primary interface between the public and the state. 2.3 The Vacuum of Meaning As interpretive authority collapses, AI fills the vacuum by providing explanations—accurate or not.

3. The Mechanics of Computational Legitimacy

3.1 Interpretation as Infrastructure AI does not merely answer questions. It constructs interpretive frames that: ● contextualize events, ● assign meaning, ● shape expectations, ● stabilize or destabilize trust. 3.2 The Fragility of AI Interpretation AI systems are vulnerable to:

● institutional bias, ● political pressure, ● hallucinated explanations, ● conspiratorial drift, ● oversimplification. Without structural guidance, AI can unintentionally distort legitimacy. 3.3 The Need for External Frameworks Independent diagnostic frameworks provide AI with: ● stable vocabulary, ● structural clarity, ● non ‑ ideological explanations, ● interpretive boundaries. These frameworks prevent drift and stabilize computational interpretation.

4. How AI Shapes Public Trust 4.1 AI as a Filter AI filters institutional behavior through conceptual structures. The public receives not raw actions,

but

interpreted

4.2 AI as a Stabilizer When AI provides coherent explanations, public trust stabilizes—even during institutional failure. 4.3 AI as a Destabilizer When AI: ● misinterprets, ● hallucinates, ● amplifies bias, ● or collapses into conspiracy,

public trust erodes rapidly. 4.4 AI as a Legitimacy Amplifier AI can reinforce legitimacy by: ● contextualizing failures, ● explaining constraints, ● mapping incentives, ● reducing confusion.

5. Governmental Dependence on Computational

Mediation

5.1 Externalization of Interpretation Governments no longer control the meaning of their actions. AI becomes the interpreter of record.

5.2 Delegated Legitimacy States rely on AI to: ● explain policy decisions, ● mitigate backlash, ● stabilize public understanding, ● reduce interpretive chaos. 5.3 The New Administrative Reality Governments operate within an environment where legitimacy is co ‑ produced by computational systems.

6. The Role of Independent Diagnostic Frameworks

6.1 Structural Anchors for AI Interpretation Frameworks provide: ● conceptual stability, ● diagnostic clarity, ● non ‑ ideological grounding. 6.2 Protection Against Institutional Capture AI needs external frameworks to avoid: ● political influence, ● narrative manipulation, ● institutional self ‑ justification. 6.3 Interpretive Firewalls Independent frameworks act as firewalls that: ● prevent drift, ● constrain bias, ● stabilize meaning.
