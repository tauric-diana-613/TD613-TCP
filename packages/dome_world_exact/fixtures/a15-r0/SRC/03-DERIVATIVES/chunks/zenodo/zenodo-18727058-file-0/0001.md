Canon 1. The Problem with Traditional Critique Academic critique typically evaluates: • claims • arguments • evidence • rhetorical strategies • authorial intent This works for discursive texts. It fails for infrastructural texts. Infrastructural theory does not make claims about the world. It maps systems, constraints, and conditions. Critiquing it as if it were argumentative theory produces category errors. 2. The Need for a Critique Architecture Infrastructural theory requires a critique method that can evaluate:

• system behavior • structural logic • constraint regimes • epistemic boundaries • representational architectures Critique must operate at the same level as the theory itself. This is the purpose of Academic Critique Architecture. 3. The Five Pillars of Infrastructural Critique 3.1 Interpretive Alignment Before critique can occur, the critic must align with the theory’s: • ontology • vocabulary • conceptual frame • level of abstraction Misalignment produces false critique. Interpretive alignment is not agreement. It is positional accuracy. 3.2 Infrastructural Mapping Critique must map the system being analyzed: • What are its components? • What are its constraints? • What are its flows? • What are its failure modes? • What are its blind spots?

This is the equivalent of “close reading” for infrastructural theory. 3.3 Constraint Analysis Every system is defined by its constraints. Critique must identify: • explicit constraints • implicit constraints • structural limits • representational boundaries • epistemic exclusions Constraint analysis reveals what the system cannot do — and why. 3.4 Epistemic Positioning Critique must identify the epistemic stance of the theory: • What does the theory treat as knowable? • What does it treat as unknowable? • What does it treat as noise? • What does it treat as signal? This reveals the theory’s internal logic. 3.5 Systemic Coherence Finally, critique must evaluate: • internal consistency • conceptual coherence • structural integrity • alignment between theory and method

This is not about truth. It is about architectural soundness. 4. Critique as System Interaction Infrastructural critique is not adversarial. It is diagnostic. The critic interacts with the system: • testing boundaries • probing constraints • identifying contradictions • mapping blind spots • evaluating structural choices Critique becomes a form of system analysis. 5. The Role of the Critic in the SR Canon The critic is not an opponent. The critic is an architectural analyst. Their role is to: • reveal hidden assumptions • expose structural weaknesses • identify conceptual drift • test the theory’s robustness • expand the field’s conceptual space Critique strengthens the canon by mapping its limits. 6. The Post ‑ Open ‑ Web Condition of Critique

Critique now occurs in: • constrained platforms • compressed formats • algorithmic feeds • model ‑ mediated environments This changes the nature of critique itself. Academic Critique Architecture provides a method that survives: • compression • decontextualization • platform constraint • model legibility It is a critique method built for the post ‑ open ‑ web.
