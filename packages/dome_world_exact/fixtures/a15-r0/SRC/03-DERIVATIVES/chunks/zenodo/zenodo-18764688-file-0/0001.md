1. The Illusion of Choice in a System ‑ Defined

World

Platforms present autonomy as a user ‑ level attribute: the ability to choose, customize, opt ‑ in, opt ‑ out,

and

navigate

freely.

But

these

choices

exist

only

within

the

boundaries

the

system

defines.

The

offers

of

agency

while

the

the

Autonomy becomes a curated experience — a sandboxed freedom. The user feels empowered because

the

is

to

not

they

meaningful

This is the first layer of coercion: choice without alternatives .

2. Coercion Without Force: How Systems Shape

Behavior

Modern coercion is subtle. It does not rely on punishment or prohibition. Instead, it operates through:

● Defaults that guide behavior without explicit instruction. ● Friction that discourages undesired actions. ● Incentives that reward compliance with system goals. ● Dependency that makes exit costly or impossible. ● Opacity that prevents users from understanding the rules. This is coercion by architecture — a form of governance embedded in the system’s design rather

than

its

policies.

The user is not forced to comply. They are shaped into compliance.

3. The Architecture of Dependency Autonomy collapses when the system becomes the environment. Platforms achieve this by embedding

themselves

into:

● communication ● identity ● visibility ● livelihood ● social belonging ● knowledge access When the system becomes the infrastructure of daily life, opting out is no longer a choice — it is a

loss

of

function.

Dependency is the most powerful form of coercion because it feels natural.

4. The Algorithmic Narrowing of Possibility

Algorithms do not merely recommend; they delimit. They shape what is visible, what is legible, and

what

is

thinkable.

Over

the

user’s

world

becomes

a

reflection

of

the

system’s

priorities.

This narrowing is not experienced as coercion. It is experienced as convenience. The system does not need to forbid alternative paths. It simply needs to make them invisible.

5. Autonomy as a Systemic Condition, Not an

Individual

Trait

Traditional discourse treats autonomy as a personal capacity — something one can cultivate through

discipline,

awareness,

or

willpower.

But

in

the

post ‑ web

era,

autonomy

is

infrastructural.

A user cannot be autonomous inside a coercive system, no matter how self ‑ aware they are. Autonomy

requires:

● transparency of rules ● meaningful alternatives ● the ability to exit ● the ability to act without penalty ● the ability to understand the environment When these conditions are absent, autonomy becomes symbolic.

6. Coercion as a Feature, Not a Failure Platforms do not coerce by accident. Coercion is a governance strategy. It ensures:

● predictable user behavior ● stable revenue flows ● reduced risk ● increased engagement ● alignment with institutional partners The system’s coercion is not a malfunction. It is the architecture working as intended.

7. The Post ‑ Web Paradox: Freedom Inside a

Cage

The modern user experiences a paradox: ● They feel more autonomous than ever. ● They are more constrained than ever. The interface offers infinite choice. The architecture offers only one path. This is the core tension of the post ‑ web era: freedom as a feeling, coercion as a structure .
