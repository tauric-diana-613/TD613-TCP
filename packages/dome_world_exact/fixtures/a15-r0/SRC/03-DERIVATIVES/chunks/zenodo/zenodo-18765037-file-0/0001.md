1. Exhaustion Is Not Personal — It Is Structural

People are told they are tired because they lack discipline, boundaries, or self ‑ care. But exhaustion

● long work hours ● low wages ● rising costs of living

● digital overstimulation ● constant surveillance ● chronic uncertainty Fatigue is not a personal failure. It is a predictable outcome of systemic design.

2. Labor as a Source of Engineered Fatigue

Workplaces generate exhaustion through: ● understaffing ● unpredictable schedules ● unrealistic productivity demands ● emotional labor ● surveillance and micromanagement These conditions drain physical, cognitive, and emotional energy. A tired workforce is compliant, risk ‑ averse,

Exhaustion becomes a form of behavioral control.

3. Economic Precarity as Chronic Stress Financial instability produces a constant background hum of stress. When people worry about: ● rent ● groceries ● medical bills ● debt ● childcare …their cognitive bandwidth collapses. Precarity consumes attention, leaving little capacity for planning,

organizing,

or

Stress is not incidental. It is instrumental.
