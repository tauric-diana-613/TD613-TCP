1. The Myth of Autonomy in a Scarcity ‑ Driven

Society

Institutions insist that individuals possess autonomy — the freedom to choose, act, and shape their

own

lives.

But

autonomy

When

Scarcity is not a personal failure. It is a structural design.

A person working two jobs to afford rent is not “choosing poorly.” They are navigating an environment

2. Coercion Through Economic Constraint Economic coercion is the most pervasive form of modern governance. It operates through: ● Low wages that force people into survival mode. ● Unaffordable housing that eliminates mobility and choice. ● High food prices that turn nutrition into a luxury. ● Stagnant incomes that erode long ‑ term planning. ● Debt structures that trap people in perpetual repayment. These conditions do not merely limit autonomy — they replace it. When every decision is shaped by scarcity, the system is making the decisions.

3. The False Narrative of Personal Responsibility

Institutions frame health, financial stability, and life outcomes as matters of personal responsibility.

But

this

narrative

collapses

under

the

weight

of

lived

experience.

A person earning poverty wages is told to “eat healthy,” while: ● 60% of grocery aisles are filled with ultra ‑ processed, low ‑ cost foods. ● Fresh produce is priced as a premium product. ● Junk food is subsidized through industrial agriculture. ● Time to cook is consumed by long work hours. The system creates the conditions, then blames individuals for the outcomes. This is not autonomy. It is structural gaslighting .

4. Health as a System ‑ Engineered Dependency

Poor nutrition, chronic stress, and toxic workplaces produce predictable health outcomes: ● metabolic disorders ● mental health deterioration ● chronic fatigue ● anxiety and depression ● long ‑ term illness These outcomes are not random. They are statistically significant without experimentation —

observable

across

populations,

income

brackets,

and

And once health collapses, the system introduces a new dependency: ● pharmaceuticals ● medical debt ● insurance entanglement ● long ‑ term treatment cycles The system harms, then monetizes the harm.

5. Toxic Workplaces as Coercive Environments

Workplaces often function as micro ‑ systems of coercion: ● unpredictable schedules ● abusive management ● surveillance ● burnout as a norm ● retaliation for speaking up Yet workers are told they are “free to leave,” ignoring the reality that: ● rent is due ● children need food ● healthcare is tied to employment

● alternative jobs pay the same or less Exit is not a choice when survival is at stake.
