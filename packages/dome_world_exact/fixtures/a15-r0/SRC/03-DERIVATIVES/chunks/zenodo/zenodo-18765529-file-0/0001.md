1. The Social Contract Built on a Lie Modern society is built on the premise that individuals are autonomous actors capable of shaping

their

own

lives.

This

belief

underpins

economic

policy,

legal

frameworks,

public

health

messaging,

and

cultural

narratives.

But autonomy requires: ● stability ● resources ● alternatives

● time ● safety Most people do not have these conditions. Yet the system insists they do, because the myth of autonomy

is

essential

for

maintaining

the

legitimacy

of

institutions

that

rely

on

structural

inequality.

2. Autonomy as a Privilege, Not a Baseline Autonomy is often treated as a universal human attribute, but in practice it is a luxury afforded to those

who

are:

● financially secure ● socially protected ● geographically mobile ● insulated from scarcity ● free from chronic stress For everyone else, autonomy is conditional — granted only when it does not interfere with the system’s

need

for

labor,

compliance,

and

predictability.

The system calls this equality. Lived experience calls it hierarchy.

3. The Illusion of Choice in a Constrained Environment

People are told they have choices: where to live, what to eat, how to work, how to stay healthy. But

these

choices

exist

only

within

the

boundaries

the

system

defines.

A person choosing between: ● rent or groceries ● medication or bills ● childcare or employment ● healthy food or affordable food …is not exercising autonomy. They are navigating coercion.

Choice without alternatives is not freedom — it is containment.

4. The Institutional Need for the Autonomy Myth

Institutions depend on the belief in autonomy because it: ● shifts blame downward ● absolves systems of responsibility ● justifies inequality ● moralizes poverty and illness ● maintains social order If people recognized that their struggles were structurally produced, not personally chosen, the legitimacy

of

the

system

would

fracture.

The autonomy myth protects the system, not the individual.

5. Lived Experience as a Counter ‑ Narrative Lived experience exposes the lie: ● wages stagnate while costs rise ● housing becomes unaffordable ● food systems prioritize profit over nutrition ● workplaces erode mental and physical health ● medical systems monetize illness People do not fail to exercise autonomy. They are denied the conditions required for autonomy to

exist.

The system blames individuals for outcomes it engineered.

6. Autonomy as a Systemic Condition, Not an

Individual

Trait

Autonomy is not something a person can will into existence. It is a structural environment that must

be

built,

protected,

and

distributed.

A society that claims to value autonomy while designing systems that eliminate it is not confused

—

it

is

deceptive.

Autonomy is impossible under: ● scarcity ● precarity ● chronic stress ● toxic labor conditions ● unaffordable essentials The system knows this. It simply refuses to acknowledge it.

7. The Lie at the Center of Modern Society The greatest lie society tells is that everyone is free. The second greatest lie is that those who are

not

free

simply

failed

to

choose

correctly.

Autonomy is not evenly distributed. It is allocated. And the allocation is political.
