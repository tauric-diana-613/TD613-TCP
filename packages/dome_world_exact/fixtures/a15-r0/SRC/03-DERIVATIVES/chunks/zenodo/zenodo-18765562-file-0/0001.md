1. The Stack: Coercion as a Layered System

Modern governance operates through a stack — a multi ‑ layered architecture in which each layer constrains

autonomy

and

reinforces

the

others.

No

Together,

The Coercion Stack consists of seven layers:

1. Material Coercion 2. Bodily Coercion 3. Cognitive Coercion 4. Social Coercion 5. Behavioral Coercion 6. Narrative Coercion 7. Structural Coercion Each layer is a mechanism of governance. Together, they form the system.

2. Layer One: Material Coercion Material coercion governs through scarcity. It includes: ● unaffordable housing ● rising food costs ● wage stagnation ● debt dependency ● survival ‑ level employment When survival is precarious, autonomy collapses. People cannot resist the system that controls their

access

to

the

basics

of

life.

Material coercion is the foundation of the stack.

3. Layer Two: Bodily Coercion Bodily coercion governs through exhaustion, illness, and depletion. It emerges from: ● toxic workplaces ● harmful food systems ● chronic stress ● unaffordable healthcare ● time scarcity A depleted body cannot organize, resist, or imagine alternatives. Bodily coercion ensures that people

remain

too

tired

to

challenge

the

system.

4. Layer Three: Cognitive Coercion Cognitive coercion governs through distraction, overload, and attention extraction. It includes: ● algorithmic stimulation ● digital noise ● information saturation ● emotional volatility ● behavioral prediction systems When attention is fragmented, critical thinking collapses. Cognitive coercion keeps people mentally

occupied,

reactive,

and

unable

to

sustain

focus

on

structural

harm.

5. Layer Four: Social Coercion Social coercion governs through isolation and fragmentation. It is produced by: ● long work hours ● housing instability ● digital displacement ● erosion of public spaces ● community collapse A fragmented society cannot build collective power. Social coercion ensures that individuals face

systemic

harm

alone.

6. Layer Five: Behavioral Coercion Behavioral coercion governs through incentives and punishments. It includes: ● workplace surveillance ● algorithmic visibility ● social approval loops ● exclusion threats ● reward structures for obedience People learn to self ‑ regulate in ways that benefit the system. Behavioral coercion turns governance

into

conditioning.

7. Layer Six: Narrative Coercion Narrative coercion governs through stories that justify the system. These include: ● meritocracy ● upward mobility ● personal responsibility ● the myth of equal opportunity ● the framing of structural harm as individual failure Narrative coercion ensures that people blame themselves instead of the system.

8. Layer Seven: Structural Coercion Structural coercion is the top layer — the architecture that binds all others. It includes: ● dependency loops ● manufactured crises ● extraction systems ● institutional self ‑ preservation Structural coercion ensures that the system remains stable even as individuals collapse within it.

9. How the Layers Reinforce Each Other The Coercion Stack is not linear. It is recursive. ● Scarcity produces exhaustion. ● Exhaustion reduces cognitive capacity. ● Cognitive overload accelerates isolation. ● Isolation increases compliance. ● Compliance reinforces narrative control. ● Narrative control obscures structural coercion.

Each layer strengthens the next. Each layer depends on the others. The stack is a closed system.

10. Coercion as Environment, Not Event Coercion in the modern era is not enforced through force. It is enforced through conditions. People

The Coercion Stack is the environment in which modern life unfolds.
