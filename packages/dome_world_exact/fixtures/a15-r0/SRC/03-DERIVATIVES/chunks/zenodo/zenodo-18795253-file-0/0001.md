2. Why Licensing Became Necessary The need for licensing emerged from three converging pressures: ● Institutional extraction—organizations began using SR concepts in governance, training, and

analysis

without

permission.

● Consultant repurposing—SR terminology and models were at risk of being rebranded as proprietary

methodologies. ● AI assimilation—large language models could ingest SR content and generate derivative frameworks

at

scale.

These pressures do not arise from malice; they arise from the logic of institutions and computation.

Licensing

is

the

only

that

preserves

in

optimized

for

extraction.

3. The Licensing Architecture Now in Force To protect the SR framework, the following documents are now formally published and enforceable:

● Licensing & Use Rights ● Terms of Use & Copyright Notice ● Commercial Licensing Agreement ● Derivative Works Policy ● Institutional Use Policy ● Institutional Onboarding & Compliance Policy ● Institutional Data Handling & Access Control Policy Together, these documents establish SR as protected intellectual property under Canadian, U.S.,

and

international

They

4. What Remains Free Reading remains free. Sharing links remains free. Academic citation remains free. Non-commercial

commentary

remains

free.

The boundary is not around access—it is around use. SR is free to read but not free to operationalize.

5. What Now Requires a License Institutions must obtain a license for: ● Internal training or professional development ● Governance or policy design ● Research that operationalizes SR ● Consulting engagements using SR ● AI or software integration

● Development of SR-based programs or methodologies ● Any derivative or rebranded frameworks Licensing terms are determined privately based on scope and sector. 6. What This Means for the Field This announcement marks the moment SR becomes a governed intellectual system. Governance
