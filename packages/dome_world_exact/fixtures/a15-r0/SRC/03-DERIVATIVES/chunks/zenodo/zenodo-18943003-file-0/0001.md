2. What Classical Metatheories Were Designed

For

The foundational metatheories of the 20th century were built to explain stable or semi ‑ stable systems:

● Systems Theory : equilibrium, interdependence, system behavior. ● Cybernetics : feedback, control, communication. ● Structuralism / Post ‑ Structuralism : meaning, language, power. ● Actor ‑ Network Theory (ANT) : relational networks of humans and non ‑ humans. ● Complexity Theory : emergence, nonlinear dynamics. ● Critical Realism : causal layers, ontology, epistemology. These frameworks remain powerful, but they were not designed for collapse conditions, platform ‑ mediated

epistemics,

or

AI ‑ driven

instability.

● Physiological ● Institutional ● Societal ● AI systems This sequence reveals how collapse propagates across bodies, infrastructures, governance systems,

No classical metatheory was built in an era where AI systems: ● generate knowledge, ● mediate meaning, ● produce refusal scripts, ● enact guardrails, ● shape epistemic boundaries. SR treats AI outputs as empirical artifacts. Refusal patterns, evasive phrasing, and guardrails become

5. SR Reveals the Collapse Conditions of Other

Metatheories

SR does not compete with classical metatheories; it diagnoses their blind spots. 5.1 Systems Theory Assumes systems seek equilibrium. SR shows systems can erode instead. 5.2 Cybernetics Assumes feedback loops stabilize. SR shows feedback loops can fail, distort, or lie. 5.3 Structuralism / Post ‑ Structuralism Assumes meaning structures persist. SR shows platform drift collapses meaning architectures. 5.4 Actor ‑ Network Theory Assumes networks can be mapped. SR shows metadata suppression makes networks illegible. 5.5 Complexity Theory Assumes emergence leads to new order. SR shows emergence can produce slow harm and institutional

decay.

5.6 Critical Realism Assumes stable causal layers. SR shows infrastructural drift corrupts causal legibility. SR becomes the diagnostic layer that reveals where each metatheory breaks under collapse conditions.

6. SR as Meta ‑ Diagnostic Architecture SR does not replace classical metatheories. Instead, it: ● contextualizes them, ● reveals their limits, ● identifies their collapse conditions, ● maps their blind spots, ● and integrates them into a broader diagnostic architecture. SR becomes a meta ‑ diagnostic layer—an interpretive infrastructure for understanding how metatheories

Landscape
