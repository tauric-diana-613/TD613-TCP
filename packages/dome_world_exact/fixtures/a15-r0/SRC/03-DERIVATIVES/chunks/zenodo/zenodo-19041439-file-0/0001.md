2. Defining Epistemic Rupture Epistemic Rupture occurs when: ● internal models no longer reflect real-world signals ● synthetic data contaminates training loops ● embeddings drift away from human meaning ● classification systems become self-referential

● platform outputs contradict observable reality The system continues producing outputs, but those outputs no longer map to the world. 3. Causes of Epistemic Rupture 3.1 Synthetic Data Loops Models trained on their own outputs lose grounding in human knowledge. 3.2 Semantic Drift Embedding spaces shift, causing meaning to detach from original referents. 3.3 Infrastructural Drift Operational changes outpace interpretive layers, degrading epistemic accuracy. 3.4 Dominant-Cluster Overfitting Systems over-index on a single conceptual attractor, distorting classification. 3.5 No-Bucket Conditions When systems cannot classify new concepts, they force them into incorrect categories. 4. Symptoms of Epistemic Rupture Users observe: ● outputs that contradict real-world facts ● hallucinations presented as authoritative ● misclassification of novel concepts ● circular reasoning within AI systems ● platform explanations that no longer match behavior These symptoms indicate that the system’s epistemic grounding has failed. 5. Case Study: AI Model Degradation As AI models increasingly train on synthetic data, they: ● lose connection to human meaning

● generate plausible but incorrect outputs ● reinforce their own errors This is Epistemic Rupture triggered by recursive training loops. 6. Case Study: Search Engine Drift Search engines experience rupture when: ● ranking models prioritize engagement over accuracy ● embeddings drift from human semantics ● misinformation becomes structurally favored The system’s representation of the web no longer matches the web itself. 7. Relationship to Other SR Diagnostics 7.1 Legibility Collapse Collapse hides reality; rupture replaces it. 7.2 Dominant-Cluster Formation Clusters distort epistemic boundaries. 7.3 No-Bucket Condition Misclassification accelerates rupture. 7.4 Recursive Collapse Rupture is the epistemic dimension of recursive failure. 8. The SR Diagnostic Model Epistemic Rupture is identified through three indicators: 8.1 Representational Inaccuracy Outputs contradict observable reality. 8.2 Synthetic Reinforcement

The system amplifies its own errors. 8.3 Epistemic Drift Meaning detaches from original referents. These indicators distinguish rupture from simple hallucination. 9. Theoretical Implications Epistemic Rupture reveals: ● the fragility of digital epistemology ● the risks of synthetic data loops ● the instability of semantic models ● the structural consequences of platform drift ● the emergence of self-referential infrastructures It demonstrates that systems do not merely misrepresent reality—they can lose contact with it entirely.
