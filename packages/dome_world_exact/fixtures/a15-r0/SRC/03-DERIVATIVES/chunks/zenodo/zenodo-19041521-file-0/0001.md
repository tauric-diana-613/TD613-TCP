1. Introduction Modern platforms rely on a fragile translation layer that converts internal activity into public metrics.

This

layer—views,

likes,

impressions,

rankings—creates

the

illusion

of

transparency.

When

it

fails,

users

experience

a

sudden

disappearance

of

visibility:

metrics

freeze,

analytics

flatline,

and

the

system

appears

inert.

Yet beneath this collapse, the infrastructure continues to operate normally. Legibility Collapse is the moment when the system’s representational layer breaks away from its operational

reality.

2. Defining Legibility Legibility is the platform’s ability to: ● represent internal activity

● translate signals into metrics ● maintain continuity between operation and visibility ● provide users with interpretable feedback Legibility is not truth. It is a performance of truth. Platforms maintain legibility through: ● indexing ● ranking ● metric updates ● classification ● visibility pipelines When these pipelines fail, legibility collapses. 3. What Legibility Collapse Is Not It is not: ● a drop in engagement ● a shadowban ● a content penalty ● a user-behavior shift ● a moderation action These events produce gradual changes. Legibility Collapse is instantaneous. It is a structural failure, not a behavioral one. 4. Causes of Legibility Collapse 4.1 Indexing Failure When the indexing system stalls, the visibility layer cannot update. The platform continues serving

content,

but

the

metrics

freeze.

4.2 Semantic Reclassification When a new conceptual field emerges (e.g., SR), the system attempts to rebuild topic clusters. During

this

rebuild,

visibility

collapses.

4.3 Dominant-Cluster Overload If a single conceptual attractor becomes too strong, the system routes all queries through it, overwhelming

the

visibility

layer.

4.4 Infrastructure Drift As platforms evolve, older visibility pipelines degrade. The operational layer outpaces the representational

layer.

5. Symptoms of Legibility Collapse Users observe: ● sudden drop to zero views ● frozen analytics across all content ● no profile visits ● no ranking movement ● no correlation with posting behavior ● no gradual decline—only a cliff These symptoms indicate that the visibility layer has detached from the operational layer. 6. Case Study: Academia.edu When SR content entered Academia.edu, the platform attempted to: ● build a new semantic bucket ● reassign all SR papers ● test routing behavior ● rebuild topic graphs During this process, visibility collapsed. The system continued serving papers, but metrics froze. This is Legibility Collapse triggered by semantic overload. 7. Case Study: TikTok TikTok’s safety systems often freeze metrics during: ● moderation sweeps ● backend updates ● engagement audits

Content continues circulating, but likes and views remain static. This is Legibility Collapse triggered by safety-layer interruption. 8. The SR Diagnostic Model Legibility Collapse is identified through three structural indicators: 8.1 Operational Continuity Content is still being served. 8.2 Representational Failure Metrics stop updating. 8.3 Temporal Suddenness The collapse occurs instantly, not gradually. These indicators distinguish Legibility Collapse from normal engagement fluctuations. 9. Theoretical Implications Legibility Collapse reveals: ● the fragility of visibility systems ● the opacity of platform infrastructure ● the illusion of transparency ● the gap between operation and representation ● the emergence of new conceptual attractors It demonstrates that platforms do not fail at the level of content—they fail at the level of interpretation.
