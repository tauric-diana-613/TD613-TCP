2. PBMs: The Shadow Government of Medication

PBMs are the most powerful, least visible entities in the pharmaceutical ecosystem. They decide:

● which drugs are covered ● which drugs are denied ● which drugs are substituted ● which drugs are placed in high ‑ cost tiers ● which drugs become scarce ● which manufacturers survive ● which pharmacies collapse PBMs operate with: ● no transparency ● no public oversight ● no regulatory accountability ● no obligation to disclose pricing logic PBMs are not intermediaries. They are governors .

3. The Rebate System: Kickbacks Disguised

as

Discounts

A rebate is not a discount. It is a kickback. How the rebate system works: 1. Manufacturer sets a fake high list price (e.g., $20 drug → $600 list price). 2. PBM demands a massive rebate (40–70%) to place the drug on the formulary. 3. Manufacturer raises the price to fund the rebate.

4. PBM pockets the rebate. 5. Insurer takes a cut. 6. Patient pays a copay based on the inflated list price. 7. Copay money goes to PBMs/insurers—not the pharmacy. This system incentivizes high prices , scarcity , and dependency . Rebates are the engine of pharmaceutical inflation.

Money disappears into: ● opaque contracts ● secret negotiations ● undisclosed rebate structures ● algorithmic pricing engines The black box is not a flaw. It is the architecture.

5. Engineered Scarcity: How PBMs Create Back

Orders

Drug shortages are not accidents. They are structural outputs of PBM economics.

Mechanisms of engineered scarcity: ● PBMs exclude low ‑ rebate drugs → manufacturers stop producing them. ● PBMs push demand toward high ‑ rebate drugs → supply collapses elsewhere. ● PBMs incentivize consolidation → one factory failure = global shortage. ● AI allocates supply to high ‑ profit regions → rural areas get nothing. Examples: ● ADHD meds (Adderall, Vyvanse) ● Diabetes drugs (Ozempic, Trulicity) ● Antibiotics (amoxicillin) ● Chemotherapy agents ● Pediatric pain medications Scarcity is profitable. Scarcity is engineered. Scarcity is algorithmically managed.
