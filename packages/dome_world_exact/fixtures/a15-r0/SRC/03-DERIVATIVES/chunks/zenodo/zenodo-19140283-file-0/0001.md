Structural truth reframes neutrality as structurally unstable , not because researchers are biased,

● incentives ● infrastructures ● access constraints ● methodological limits ● temporal drift These forces do not eliminate the value of research. They simply mean that neutrality is contingent ,

Why neutrality destabilizes in practice Neutrality becomes unstable when: ● incentives shape which questions get asked ● infrastructures shape which data can exist ● access constraints shape who is included or excluded ● methods must adapt to what is measurable, not what is ideal ● drift changes the system faster than institutions can update their models Under these conditions, neutrality is not “false”—it is fragile .

This section explains why the pattern emerged. 11.1 The Era of Meaningful Findings: When Incentives Aligned With Discovery

Earlier decades produced rich, structural insights because institutional conditions supported them:

● Stable infrastructures : systems changed slowly enough for methods to keep pace. ● Aligned incentives : funding and publication norms rewarded structural critique. ● Lower political risk : exposing systemic issues did not threaten institutional survival. ● Manageable complexity : systems had not yet outpaced institutional capacity. Under these conditions, institutions could produce structural knowledge without destabilizing themselves.

11.2 The Stagnation Gap: When Incentives Shifted Away From Structural Findings

The stagnation period does not mean research stopped. It means structural research became high ‑ risk

and

low ‑ reward.

Three forces drove this shift: 1. Incentive Realignment Institutions increasingly depended on: ● competitive grants ● reputational risk management ● politically safe deliverables ● incremental, measurable outputs Structural findings became institutionally dangerous . 2. Infrastructural Acceleration Platforms, supply chains, and administrative systems began drifting faster than: ● peer ‑ review cycles ● methodological updates ● ethical oversight ● institutional adaptation Methods could no longer track system behavior. 3. Legitimacy Preservation Institutions became less willing to publish findings that: ● exposed systemic failures ● contradicted official narratives ● implied loss of control ● required structural reform The result was stagnation through narrowing : research continued, but structural insight declined.

This stagnation is cross ‑ domain , but not universal. It appears most strongly in fields where findings

could

destabilize

institutional

legitimacy.

11.3 The Pivot to AI: A Narrative Scapegoat for Structural Contradictions

When stagnation became visible, institutions needed an explanation that: ● preserved legitimacy ● externalized blame ● aligned with public anxieties ● attracted funding ● avoided confronting systemic drift AI became the perfect narrative object. AI absorbed contradictions that belonged to the system. Instead of saying: ● “Our methods can’t keep up with infrastructural drift,” institutions could say: “AI is destabilizing

knowledge.”

Instead of saying: ● “We no longer have incentives to study structural harm,” they could say: “AI is creating new

harms.”

Instead of saying: ● “Our research pipeline is misaligned with reality,” they could say: “AI is overwhelming our institutions.”

AI became a narrative stabilizer that masked the underlying stagnation. 11.4 Why This Timeline Matters for the Neutrality Argument The temporal pattern reveals a deeper structural truth: Neutrality destabilizes when incentives destabilize. ● When incentives supported structural discovery → neutrality appeared plausible. ● When incentives shifted toward safety → neutrality became fragile. ● When stagnation became visible → neutrality required a new narrative stabilizer (AI). Neutrality is not a property of research. Neutrality is a function of institutional conditions . The temporal gap is not an anomaly — it is evidence of the structural forces that make neutrality unstable.
