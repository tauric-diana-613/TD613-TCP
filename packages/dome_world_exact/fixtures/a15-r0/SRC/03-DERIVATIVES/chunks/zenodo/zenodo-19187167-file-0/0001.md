Agency Depletion: How Systems Exhaust Human

Capacity

Abstract This essay develops Agency Depletion Theory , a central component of the SignalRupture framework.

Agency

depletion

refers

to

the

systematic

erosion

of

an

individual’s

capacity

to

act,

decide,

and

self-direct

under

conditions

shaped

by

institutional

drift,

economic

precarity,

technological

narrowing,

and

infrastructural

exposure.

Drawing

on

research

in

sociology,

psychology,

labor

studies,

and

media

theory,

this

essay

argues

that

agency

depletion

is

not

a

personal

failure

but

a

structural

outcome

produced

by

environments

that

overload

cognitive

bandwidth,

fragment

attention,

and

constrain

autonomy.

Agency

depletion

manifests

through

burnout,

decision

fatigue,

emotional

exhaustion,

and

the

collapse

of

long-term

planning

capacity.

SignalRupture

positions

agency

depletion

as

a

predictable

consequence

of

modern

systems

that

extract

compliance,

predictability,

and

labor

while

eroding

the

conditions

necessary

for

human

self-determination.

Keywords Agency depletion; burnout; cognitive overload; precarity; structural exhaustion; digital mediation; SignalRupture.

1. Introduction: When Capacity Collapses Agency is the ability to act intentionally, make decisions, and shape one’s environment. In contemporary

systems,

agency

is

increasingly

depleted—not

through

overt

coercion,

but

through

structural

pressures

that

exhaust

cognitive,

emotional,

and

temporal

resources.

Agency depletion is not a psychological weakness. It is a systemic condition . SignalRupture treats agency depletion as a structural outcome of environments that demand constant

adaptation

while

providing

diminishing

support.

2. Defining Agency Depletion Agency depletion occurs when individuals lose the capacity to: ● make decisions

● plan long-term ● resist external pressures ● act autonomously ● maintain cognitive and emotional stability This depletion emerges from chronic overload, fragmented attention, and structural constraints that

reduce

the

space

for

intentional

action.

Agency depletion is the collapse of self-direction under systemic pressure. 3. Cognitive Overload and the Fragmentation of Attention Digital infrastructures fragment attention and overload cognition. Bayer et al. (2020) show that digital

environments

prioritize

novelty,

speed,

and

emotional

salience,

reducing

the

cognitive

bandwidth

available

for

sustained

reasoning.

Cognitive overload produces: ● decision fatigue ● reduced working memory ● diminished problem-solving capacity ● emotional volatility When attention collapses, agency collapses. 4. Precarity and the Exhaustion of Stability Economic precarity forces individuals into survival mode. Research in labor sociology shows that

unstable

work,

unpredictable

income,

and

rising

costs

reduce

the

capacity

for

long-term

planning.

Precarity produces: ● chronic stress ● emotional exhaustion ● reduced autonomy ● dependence on unstable systems The coercive economy drains agency by forcing individuals to prioritize immediate survival over intentional

action.

5. Institutional Drift and the Withdrawal of Support

As institutions drift from their founding purpose, they withdraw support systems that once sustained

agency.

Public

services

weaken,

bureaucratic

barriers

increase,

and

individuals

face

growing

administrative

burdens.

Institutional drift produces: ● increased friction ● reduced access to resources ● heightened uncertainty ● administrative exhaustion The erosion of institutional support accelerates agency depletion. 6. Algorithmic Mediation and Predictive Containment Digital systems increasingly shape decisions, preferences, and behaviors. Noble (2018) demonstrates

how

algorithmic

systems

reinforce

biases

and

narrow

informational

environments.

Algorithmic mediation produces: ● reduced autonomy ● predictable behavior patterns ● diminished exposure to alternatives ● reliance on automated systems When systems predict behavior, they also constrain it. 7. Emotional Exhaustion and the Collapse of Resilience Agency requires emotional capacity. Chronic stress, digital overstimulation, and institutional friction

erode

emotional

resilience.

Emotional exhaustion manifests as: ● burnout ● irritability ● withdrawal ● loss of motivation Emotional depletion is a core mechanism of agency collapse. 8. Agency Depletion as Governance

Agency depletion is not merely a personal condition. It becomes a governance mechanism. Systems

that

exhaust

individuals

reduce

their

capacity

to

resist,

organize,

or

demand

change.

Agency depletion functions as governance by: ● limiting dissent ● reducing collective action ● increasing dependence ● normalizing exhaustion Exhaustion becomes a tool of control. 9. Restoring Agency Through Structural Awareness SignalRupture positions structural awareness as the first step toward restoring agency. Understanding

the

forces

that

deplete

agency

enables

individuals

and

communities

to:

● reclaim cognitive bandwidth ● resist algorithmic containment ● rebuild social infrastructure ● demand institutional accountability Agency is restored through structural clarity, not individual willpower. 10. Conclusion Agency depletion is a structural condition produced by cognitive overload, precarity, institutional drift,

and

algorithmic

mediation.

It

erodes

the

capacity

for

self-direction,

long-term

and

SignalRupture

exhaust

human

to

and

Restoring
