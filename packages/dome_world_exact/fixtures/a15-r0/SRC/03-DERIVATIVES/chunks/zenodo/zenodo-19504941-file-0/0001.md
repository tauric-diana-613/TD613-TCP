Operational Mechanisms of Non ‑ Recognition

These mechanisms include: ● Institutional triggers (e.g., visit frequency, unstable housing, prior non-escalation), ● Credibility thresholds (e.g., visit 3–4 triggering dismissal), ● Clinician signals (appearance, communication, chart cues), ● System signals (overcrowding, fatigue, shift changes).

These mechanisms make the Invisible Patient reproducible and predictable.

Structural Latency The original paper introduced structural latency implicitly. This edition formalizes it as a named mechanism.

Definition Structural Latency is the delay between systemic transformation and institutional perception

of

that

transformation.

This delay is produced by: ● legacy workflows, ● fixed reporting cycles, ● siloed information channels, ● and the assumption that past conditions still hold. Why It Matters Structural latency ensures that institutions are always perceiving: ● the previous phase of the system, ● the previous configuration of risk, ● the previous distribution of harm. Institutions are therefore structurally incapable of: ● detecting phase transitions in real time, ● perceiving emergent harm patterns, ● updating their internal models at the speed of environmental change. Structural latency is the perceptual gap through which the Invisible Patient falls.

This principle explains why the Invisible Patient is not an anomaly but an inevitability.
