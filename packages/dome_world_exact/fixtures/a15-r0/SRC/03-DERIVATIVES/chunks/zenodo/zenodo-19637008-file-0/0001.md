2. From Labour to Cognitive Extraction

Modern economies rely on forms of labour that extend beyond time: ● sustained attention ● high ‑ frequency decision ‑ making ● emotional regulation ● interpersonal coordination ● identity ‑ based communication These inputs are essential yet rarely measured or compensated. Workers are paid for time, while

4. The Family–Work Fracture Modern wage dependence separates income from care, creating a structural contradiction: ● caring reduces income ● maintaining income reduces capacity to care

5. Observable Indicators Although burnout is not formally categorized, converging indicators document its structural footprint.

Across OECD datasets: ● ~42% of workers report stress ● ~33% experience job strain ● ~10% work 50+ hours per week (OECD, 2023) Behavioural indicators: ● Absenteeism affects ~33% of workers ● Presenteeism affects ~35% (Johns, 2010; OECD, 2023) Economic impact: ● Productivity losses reach ~3.5%–5.5% of GDP ● Presenteeism costs are 2–3× higher than absenteeism (OECD, 2023) Individual ‑ level effects: ● Workers under mental strain take ~50% more sick days (OECD, 2023) Administrative indicators: ● Mental and behavioural categories are a growing share of disability claims (Statistics

Canada,

2023) ● Work ‑ related stress accounts for a substantial proportion of ill ‑ health cases (HSE, 2023)

These categories are separate, but they converge on a single structural pattern.

6. The Economic Amplification Loop Labour strain interacts with economic pressure:

● wage stagnation ● rising housing and food prices ● increasing financial precarity This produces a reinforcing loop: 1. Financial strain increases dependence on work 2. Time scarcity reduces recovery 3. Reduced recovery lowers resilience 4. Lower resilience reduces performance stability 5. Instability increases institutional pressure 6. Pressure increases strain indicators 7. Strain reinforces financial dependence This loop is observable across datasets (OECD, 2023; ILO, 2021). It

13. Policy Implications If burnout is structural, policy must shift from treatment to system design. Key directions: ● recognize cognitive load as labour ● limit continuous work and digital availability ● redesign high ‑ strain roles ● integrate care and income systems ● stabilize wages and cost of living ● expand measurement frameworks
