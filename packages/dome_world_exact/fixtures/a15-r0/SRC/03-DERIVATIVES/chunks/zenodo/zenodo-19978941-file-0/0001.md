3. Information Drift: When Institutions Lose

the

Plot

Information drift occurs when institutional narratives fail to update in response to structural change.

It

is

not

deception;

it

is

lag —a

temporal

mismatch

between

the

speed

of

system

degradation

and

the

speed

of

narrative

In the context of food prices, information drift emerges because:

● institutions continue to speak in cyclical terms (“inflation is cooling”), ● while households experience structural price elevation (“groceries cost more every month”), ● and the drivers (climate, energy, supply chains) are not easily reversible. The narrative layer becomes decoupled from the material layer. This produces a phenomenological effect: people trust their receipts more than their institutions.

3.1 Formal Definition of Information Drift To operationalize information drift as a measurable diagnostic, define two time ‑ dependent signals:

● ( R(t) ) — the reality signal : high ‑ frequency material indicators (e.g., food CPI, basket ‑ level

price

indices). ● ( N(t) ) — the narrative signal : institutional communication (e.g., inflation guidance, policy statements,

media

framing).

Level Drift [ D(t) = R(t) - N(t) ] Rate Drift (the true diagnostic) [ D'(t) = \frac{dR}{dt} - \frac{dN}{dt} ] Interpretation: ● ( D'(t) > 0 ) — reality deteriorates faster than narratives update → drift expands. ● ( D'(t) \approx 0 ) — narratives track reality → low drift. ● ( D'(t) < 0 ) — narratives overcorrect → narrative overshoot. This converts information drift from a conceptual metaphor into a rate ‑ based misalignment metric .

4. Predictive Modelling Under Structural Stress

To quantify the implications of structural food inflation, we model three plausible trajectories for the

next

decade.

Let: [ P_t = \text{food price index in year } t ] [ P_{t+1} = P_t (1 + g_t + \epsilon_t) ] Where: ● ( g_t ) = structural inflation component (climate, energy, labour, concentration), ● ( \epsilon_t ) = stochastic shock (drought, flood, war, supply disruption). This acknowledges non ‑ linearity, fat ‑ tailed shocks, and volatility clustering. Normalize: [ P_0 = 100 ]

4.1 Scenario 1 — Baseline Structural Stress (30% probability)

Assumptions: ● Climate stress remains elevated but does not worsen. ● Food inflation declines gradually: 4% → 3% → 2%. Computation: [ P_{10} = 100 \cdot 1.04^3 \cdot 1.03^3 \cdot 1.02^4 \approx 133 ] Outcome: ≈33% higher food prices in 10 years.

4.2 Scenario 2 — Constant High Stress (45% probability) Assumptions: ● Climate stress remains consistently high. ● Food inflation stays at 5% annually. [ P_{10} = 100 \cdot (1.05)^{10} \approx 163 ] Outcome: ≈63% higher food prices.

4.3 Scenario 3 — Intensifying Stress (25% probability) Assumptions: ● Climate volatility worsens each year. ● Food inflation rises from 4% to 8.5%. Approximate average ( g_t = 6.25% ): [ P_{10} = 100 \cdot (1.0625)^{10} \approx 182 ] Outcome: ≈82% higher food prices.

4.4 Probability ‑ Weighted Expectation [ \mathbb{E}[P_{10}] = 0.30(133) + 0.45(163) + 0.25(182) ] [ \mathbb{E}[P_{10}] \approx 159 ] Expected 10 ‑ year outcome: ≈59% higher food prices. This is a probability ‑ weighted structural expectation , not a point forecast.

5. Governance Implications: The Limits of Narrative

Control

Governance systems rely on narrative coherence. When the narrative layer fails to track the reality

layer,

legitimacy

erodes.

Food

inflation

exposes

Institutions face a choice: ● continue offering simplified explanations (“global factors”), widening the drift, ● or update their narrative to reflect structural constraints. The second option is more honest but politically costly. The first option is safer but accelerates drift.

5.1 Mechanism of Institutional Misrecognition:

Measurement

Regime

Mismatch

Institutions rely on: ● lagged CPI, ● smoothed weights, ● substitution effects, ● quarterly reporting, ● aggregated national data. Households rely on: ● daily receipts, ● category ‑ specific prices, ● zero substitution for essentials, ● local store conditions. Thus, ( R(t) ) and ( N(t) ) measure different objects , making drift structural, not merely communicative.

5.2 Formal Epistemic Asymmetry Statement

Households operate on high ‑ frequency micro ‑ data; institutions operate on low ‑ frequency macro ‑ aggregates.

Under

structural

stress,

the

epistemic

advantage

shifts

to

households.

This is the core of the SR epistemic claim.

5.3 Dual ‑ Path Origin of Drift: Capacity Limits

and

Strategic

Simplification

Information drift can arise from: (1) Capacity Limits ● lagged data, ● smoothing that suppresses volatility, ● models built for stability. (2) Strategic Simplification ● simplified narratives, ● stability ‑ preserving messaging, ● public reassurance. SR’s position: Drift emerges whenever the narrative layer cannot or will not track the material

layer.
