9. Interventions: The Only Three Real Levers

9.1 Constraints: Making Extraction Structurally Expensive or

Impossible

Constraints are hard limits — legal, technical, procedural, or infrastructural — that make certain extraction

patterns

illegal,

impossible,

or

too

Types of Constraints ● Legal: wage floors, work ‑ hour caps, data limits, sunset clauses. ● Technical: auto ‑ delete logs, rate limits, mandatory human oversight. ● Infrastructural: kill ‑ switches, redundancy, manual overrides. ● Procedural: multi ‑ party sign ‑ off, enforced waiting periods. What Constraints Do ● raise the cost of extraction ● slow harmful optimization ● force human involvement ● limit scale ● create friction Constraints attack the system’s dependence on speed, opacity, automation, and scale. 9.2 Buffers: Protecting Humans from Raw Exposure to Extraction

Buffers are shields that absorb or deflect harm. Types of Buffers ● Economic: income floors, universal basic services, debt caps. ● Temporal: guaranteed time off, rest periods, limits on after ‑ hours contact.

● Cognitive: limits on targeted ads, anti ‑ manipulation UX rules. ● Social: unions, tenant associations, mutual aid networks. What Buffers Do ● reduce human capacity available for extraction ● increase resilience ● preserve slack ● slow collapse Buffers keep humans alive inside the system. 9.3 Counter ‑ Architecture: Building Structures the System Cannot

Photocopy

Counter ‑ architecture is the deliberate design of structures that do not run on extraction. Characteristics ● no dependence on over ‑ extraction ● ability to remain small ● embedded kill ‑ switches ● capacity prioritized over output ● refusal built ‑ in Examples ● Personal: protected time, non ‑ optimized life design. ● Local: co ‑ ops, community land trusts, mutual aid. ● Institutional: capped growth, democratic governance, sunset clauses. The Non ‑ Photocopy Rules ● No growth without justification. ● Capacity over output. ● Sunset by default. ● Transparency of harm. ● Refusal is protected. Counter ‑ architecture is hard — but it is the only thing that outlives dominant systems.
