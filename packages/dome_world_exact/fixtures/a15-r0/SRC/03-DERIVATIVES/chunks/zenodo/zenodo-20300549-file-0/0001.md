2. Introduction Institutions compress behavioural complexity in order to remain operationally scalable. But compression is not infinite. Governance systems stabilize at predictable abstraction thresholds where: ● additional procedures, ● additional reviews, ● additional oversight, ● additional documentation, ● additional procedural layering, no longer substantially increase representational distortion. This stabilization point is Compression Equilibrium. Compression Equilibrium is the third structural theorem of institutional cognition within the SignalRupture

framework.

The theorem argues that governance systems require sufficient abstraction to maintain: ● throughput, ● audit coherence, ● legal defensibility,

● procedural continuity, ● and interdepartmental interoperability. However, excessive abstraction eventually reduces operational usability by degrading interpretive

stability

Compression Equilibrium emerges where the marginal gains of additional abstraction approach zero

The theorem therefore explains why institutions neither preserve full behavioural fidelity nor collapse
