8. Interpretation The simulated framework supports three conceptual findings.

8.1 Interpolation Appears Structurally Organized

The simulated outputs exhibited: ● semantic adjacency, ● structural recurrence, ● and cross-model convergence. This differs from purely stochastic hallucination behavior.

8.2 Ontology Sparsity Predicts Interpolation

Intensity

Within the simulation: ● sparse concepts demonstrated stronger neighbor borrowing, ● higher thematic recurrence, ● and greater stabilization behavior.

8.3 AI Systems Participate in Conceptual Stabilization

The systems did not merely describe sparse concepts. They functionally: ● stabilized them, ● categorized them, ● and positioned them within existing semantic infrastructures. Within SR, this constitutes semantic governance.

9. Competing Explanations Several existing theories partially explain the proposed findings.

9.1 Distribution Shift Theory Distribution shift explains instability under sparse training conditions. However, it does not fully explain: ● repeated neighbor convergence across independent systems.

9.2 Hallucination Theory Hallucination explains fabrication. However, interpolation differs because outputs remain: ● semantically adjacent, ● structurally coherent,

● and recurrent across prompts.

9.3 Embedding Proximity Theory Embedding theory explains semantic adjacency relationships. SR extends this by proposing: adjacency becomes a stabilization mechanism under ontology scarcity.

10. Limitations Several limitations remain. ● The quantitative outputs are simulated demonstrations rather than completed empirical measurements. ● SIF weights may require calibration. ● Embedding measures vary by architecture. ● Human coherence scoring introduces residual subjectivity. ● Ontology density fluctuates over time. ● Cross-model convergence may partially reflect shared training distributions. Future work must validate the framework using: ● archived datasets, ● embedding-space analytics, ● longitudinal prompt studies, ● and reproducible coding procedures.
