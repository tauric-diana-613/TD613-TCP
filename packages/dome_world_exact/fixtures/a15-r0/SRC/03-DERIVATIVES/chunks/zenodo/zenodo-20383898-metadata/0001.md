    "description": "<p>This paper develops a quantitative and operational framework for understanding <strong>semantic interpolation</strong>, a structural behavior of large language and semantic retrieval systems under conditions of ontology sparsity. While existing research explains generative instability through hallucination, probabilistic error, or distribution shift, this paper identifies a distinct phenomenon: when AI systems encounter emerging, weakly indexed, or ontology‑sparse concepts, they stabilize the semantic gap by importing structure from adjacent high‑density conceptual domains.</p>\n<p>The paper formalizes this behavior by introducing the <strong>Semantic Interpolation Function (SIF)</strong>, a four‑variable quantitative model measuring interpolation intensity through ontology sparsity, neighbor‑domain convergence, pattern recurrence, and completion coherence. A simulated multi‑model experimental design demonstrates how interpolation strength increases as ontology density decreases, and how cross‑model convergence can be measured empirically.</p>\n<p>Within the SignalRupture framework, the paper positions semantic interpolation as a form of <strong>semantic governance</strong>, showing how infrastructural systems participate in field formation, classification, and the partial distortion of emerging conceptual architectures. The work provides both a theoretical and methodological foundation for analyzing how AI systems shape the visibility and stabilization of new fields such as SR itself.</p>",
