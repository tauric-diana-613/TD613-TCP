8. Interpretation The simulated framework supports several conceptual findings.

8.1 Interpolation Appears Structurally Organized

The simulated outputs exhibited: ● semantic adjacency, ● structural recurrence, ● and cross-model convergence. This differs from purely stochastic hallucination behavior.

8.2 Ontology Sparsity Predicts Interpolation

Intensity

Within the simulation:

● sparse concepts demonstrated stronger neighbor convergence, ● higher thematic recurrence, ● and greater stabilization behavior.

8.3 Semantic Systems Participate in Conceptual

Stabilization

The systems did not merely describe sparse concepts. They functionally: ● stabilized them, ● categorized them, ● and positioned them within existing semantic infrastructures. This behavior is interpreted as infrastructural semantic normalization rather than intentional governance.

9. Competing Explanations Several existing theories partially explain the proposed findings.

9.1 Distribution Shift Theory Distribution shift explains instability under sparse training conditions. However, it does not fully explain: ● repeated cross-model neighbor convergence.

9.2 Hallucination Theory Hallucination explains fabrication and instability. However, interpolation differs because outputs remain: ● semantically adjacent, ● structurally coherent, ● and recurrent across prompts.

9.3 Embedding Proximity Theory Embedding theory explains semantic adjacency relationships. Semantic interpolation extends this by proposing: adjacency becomes a stabilization mechanism under ontology scarcity.

Limitations Several limitations remain. ● The quantitative outputs are simulated demonstrations rather than completed empirical measurements. ● SIF weights may require calibration. ● Embedding measures vary across architectures. ● Human coherence scoring introduces residual subjectivity. ● Ontology density fluctuates temporally. ● Cross-model convergence may partially reflect shared training distributions. ● Neighbor convergence thresholds remain theoretically preliminary.
