    "description": "<p>The Semantic Interpolation Theorem formalizes a structural behavior exhibited by large semantic systems when operating under conditions of ontology sparsity. Rather than treating generative instability as stochastic hallucination, the theorem demonstrates that semantic systems engage in a predictable stabilization process: when a concept lacks sufficient indexed grounding, the system imports structure from semantically adjacent high‑density domains to preserve continuity. This behavior&mdash;semantic interpolation&mdash;is measurable, recurrent, and cross‑model consistent. The paper introduces the Semantic Interpolation Function (SIF) as a quantitative framework for operationalizing interpolation intensity across four variables: ontology sparsity, neighbor convergence, pattern recurrence, and completion coherence. Through a simulated multi‑model experimental design, the paper illustrates how interpolation emerges as an infrastructural semantic normalization mechanism, shaping how emerging concepts become legible prior to institutional ontology formation.</p>",
