    "description": "<p>This paper applies the <strong>Substrate Stability Theorem</strong> to Canada as an illustrative case of a late‑industrial governance system experiencing rising substrate strain. It interprets Canada&rsquo;s recent economic weakness not as a cyclical recession but as a <strong>substrate‑level imbalance</strong> between human capacity, technological substitution, and accelerating systemic complexity.</p>\n<p>The paper constructs <strong>conceptual substrate indices</strong>&mdash;normalized to a 2015 baseline&mdash;using publicly reported macro‑structural indicators such as labour productivity, demographic aging, investment patterns, public‑sector expansion, and administrative layering. These indices are used to demonstrate how Canada&rsquo;s long‑term structural trends align with the SR‑SST conditions for <strong>drift</strong> and <strong>compression</strong>.</p>\n<p>Rather than forecasting deterministic outcomes, the paper presents a <strong>scenario analysis</strong> showing how persistent divergence between complexity growth and adaptive capacity could lead to <strong>post‑institutional operation</strong>, a regime in which institutions remain structurally present but lose adaptive functionality. It also introduces the idea of <strong>GDP as a Zombie Metric</strong>, a conventional indicator that remains numerically active while becoming disconnected from underlying substrate stability.</p>",
