Implication: Democracy

cannot

rely

on

equal

cognitive

Canonical Paragraph The Cognitive Inequality Problem identifies a structural contradiction at the heart of modern democratic

systems:

while

democratic

theory

presumes

equal

political

agency,

empirical

evidence

shows

that

institutions

shape

cognition

unevenly

across

populations

through

resource

scarcity,

social

conditioning,

religious

formation ,

and

differential

exposure

to

complexity.

This

divergence

produces

two

broad

cognitive

profiles—adaptive

and

fixed—whose

distribution

is

not

random

but

institutionally

patterned.

The

result

is

a

form

of

cognitive

inequality

that

precedes

political

participation

and

undermines

the

assumption

of

equal

agency.

The

theorem

that

follows

formalizes

this

contradiction

by

demonstrating

that

cognitive

inequality

is

not

an

aberration

but

a

predictable

outcome

of

institutional

design,

scarcity

conditions,

and

drift

dynamics.

Conclusion Democracy presumes equal agency, but institutions produce unequal cognitive conditions. This essay

has

outlined

the

structural

mechanisms—adaptive

and

fixed

cognition,

scarcity,

conditioning,

political

party

dynamics,

religious

institutions ,

the

constitutional

ideal

of

“the

people,”

and

drift—that

generate

cognitive

inequality.

The

accompanying

theorem

provides

the

formal

proof

that

cognitive

inequality

is

not

incidental

but

structurally

embedded.

Together,

the

conceptual and mathematical components form a unified framework for understanding how democratic

systems

can

fail

to

meet

their

own

assumptions

of

equality.

conceptual

First,

This

Second, the mechanism of recursive abstraction within institutional drift is expanded. Drifted institutions
