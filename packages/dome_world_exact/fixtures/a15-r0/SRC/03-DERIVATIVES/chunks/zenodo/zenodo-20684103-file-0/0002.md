A1. Definitions and Notation Let: ● P — population of individuals ● Aᵢ — adaptive cognitive tendency (latent) ● Iᵢ — institutional conditioning intensity ● Sᵢ — scarcity exposure ● Cᵢ — complexity exposure ● Dᵢ — institutional drift exposure ● ε ᵢ — unexplained variance Let τ denote the inequality threshold beyond which cognitive inequality is substantively meaningful.

A2. Axioms Underlying the Theorem ● Structural Influence — Institutions and material conditions shape cognition probabilistically. ● Unequal Exposure — Individuals experience S, I, C, and D unevenly across P. ● Cognitive Responsiveness — Cognitive tendencies respond to environmental constraints. ● Drift Amplification — Drift increases dispersion in cognitive outcomes. ● Non ‑ Equivalence of Equalities — Formal political equality ≠ equal cognitive conditions. These axioms define the domain in which the theorem holds.

A3. Theorem (Formal Statement)

Cognitive inequality exists in population P when: [ \text{Var}(A_i) > \tau ] where τ is a justified normative, empirical, comparative, or statistically estimated threshold. This statement is invariant across empirical models.

A5. Structural Model: Mediation + Moderation

Primary Mediation [ S_i \rightarrow I_i \rightarrow A_i ] Moderation by Drift [ D_i : (S_i \rightarrow I_i), \quad D_i : (I_i \rightarrow A_i) ] Moderated Mediation Form [ A_i = f(I_i, S_i, C_i, D_i, I_iD_i, S_iD_i) ] This structure reflects the conceptual logic of the CIT more accurately than an additive model.

A6. Empirical Model (One Implementation)

[ A_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 I_i - \beta_2 S_i + \beta_3 C_i - \beta_4 D_i + \varepsilon_i ] This is not the theorem — it is one testable instantiation.

A7. Threshold Specification (τ) Possible Definitions ● Normative Threshold — based on democratic agency requirements. ● Empirical Percentile — e.g., τ = 75th percentile of global variance. ● Benchmark Threshold — relative to high ‑ capacity democracies. ● Statistical Threshold — derived from break tests, clustering, or inequality indices. Each empirical application must justify its choice.

A8. Measurement Model

Latent Constructs Aᵢ, Iᵢ, Sᵢ, Cᵢ, Dᵢ are latent variables measured through indicators. Validity Requirements ● Construct validity — indicators reflect theoretical definitions. ● Convergent validity — indicators correlate strongly. ● Discriminant validity — constructs remain empirically separable. ● Measurement invariance — configural, metric, scalar, and residual invariance across groups.

Without invariance, cross ‑ population comparisons of Aᵢ are invalid.

A9. Identification Assumptions ● Exogeneity of measurement error ● No perfect multicollinearity ● Sufficient variation in S, I, C, D ● Correct latent structure ● Independent drift measurement These assumptions ensure the model is empirically identifiable.

A10. Simulation Framework Purpose To demonstrate how cognitive inequality emerges under varying S, I, C, and D. Simulation Steps 1. Generate synthetic population P. 2. Assign distributions for S, I, C, D. 3. Apply moderated mediation structure. 4. Compute Aᵢ. 5. Compute Var(Aᵢ). 6. Compare to τ.

Expected Patterns ● Increasing S widens Var(Aᵢ). ● Increasing D amplifies all effects. ● Increasing C compresses Var(Aᵢ).

A11. Competing Models and Robustness Checks Alternative Specifications ● nonlinear models ● interaction ‑ only models ● Bayesian hierarchical models ● multilevel SEM Robustness Tests ● bootstrapping ● cross ‑ validation ● alternative drift indicators ● alternative scarcity measures

A12. Ethical Safeguards ● CIT does not imply cognitive superiority or inferiority. ● Constructs measure conditions , not abilities . ● No group ‑ level essentialism is permitted. ● Data must be anonymized and protected. ● Results must not be used for exclusion or disenfranchisement.

A13. Limitations ● No universal τ across contexts. ● Drift measurement remains context ‑ dependent. ● Cross ‑ national invariance may not always hold. ● Simulations depend on parameter choices.
