6.1 The Three ‑ Tier Cognitive Hierarchy WebMCP produces a three ‑ tier cognitive hierarchy: 1. Agents — full operational comprehension 2. Institutions — partial structural comprehension 3. Users — outcome ‑ level comprehension This hierarchy is not intentional. It is a structural consequence of callable functions replacing navigational

structures.

6.1.1 Agents: Operational Cognition Agents possess:

● access to the action registry ● knowledge of parameter schemas ● visibility into execution logic ● awareness of branching conditions ● control over workflow chaining Agents understand the web at the level of operations , not interfaces . They see: ● what actions exist ● what parameters they require ● what states they depend on ● what outcomes they produce Agents become the only actors with full operational cognition. 6.1.2 Institutions: Structural Cognition Institutions (websites, platforms, service providers) possess: ● knowledge of the actions they expose ● control over schemas and workflows ● visibility into agent usage patterns But institutions do not see: ● how agents chain actions across sites ● how agents interpret user intent ● how agents optimize workflows ● how agents resolve ambiguity Institutions understand the web at the level of structure , not operation . 6.1.3 Users: Outcome Cognition Users see: ● the final result ● the visible UI updates ● the symbolic transparency But they do not see: ● the action registry ● the execution logic

● the workflow conditions ● the error ‑ handling routines ● the optimization strategies Users understand the web at the level of outcomes , not processes . This is the lowest tier of cognition.

6.2 The Collapse of Process ‑ Based Understanding Before WebMCP, users understood processes because they performed them. They

WebMCP collapses this model. 6.2.1 Execution Without Navigation When agents execute tasks through callable functions: ● steps disappear ● sequences disappear ● intermediate states disappear ● branching logic disappears Users see only the final state. This produces process opacity . 6.2.2 The Disappearance of Cognitive Landmarks Navigational interfaces provided cognitive landmarks: ● menus ● breadcrumbs ● progress bars ● form fields ● confirmation screens These landmarks anchored understanding. Callable functions remove them. The cognitive map collapses.

6.3 The Rise of Outcome ‑ Based Cognition Outcome ‑ based cognition is a condition in which users understand only the result , not the process .

This condition emerges when: ● agents perform actions ● workflows are automated ● intermediate states are hidden ● execution is instantaneous Outcome ‑ based cognition is efficient but fragile. It

produces:

● dependency ● vulnerability ● epistemic passivity Users become spectators of their own actions.

6.4.1 Agents Accumulate Operational Knowledge Agents accumulate: ● execution patterns ● optimization strategies ● error ‑ recovery heuristics ● cross ‑ site workflows This knowledge compounds. 6.4.2 Institutions Accumulate Structural Knowledge Institutions accumulate:

● usage analytics ● schema performance data ● agent interaction logs But they do not see the full operational picture. 6.4.3 Users Accumulate Nothing Users accumulate: ● no operational knowledge ● no structural knowledge ● no procedural knowledge They accumulate only outcomes. This is the deepest form of cognitive inequality.

6.5 Cognitive Inequality as Drift Amplifier Cognitive inequality amplifies drift across all layers. 6.5.1 Cognitive Drift → Institutional Drift As users understand less, institutions optimize less for human understanding. 6.5.2 Institutional Drift → Operational Drift As institutions optimize for agents, workflows become more automated and opaque. 6.5.3 Operational Drift → Cognitive Drift As workflows become opaque, users understand even less. Cognitive inequality is the feedback mechanism that accelerates drift.
