Outcomes increasingly emerge from: ● coordination effects, ● procedural accumulation, ● automated decisions, ● infrastructure interaction. Under these conditions, responsibility becomes layered rather than singular. The analytical focus shifts from: Who governs? toward: What produces?

3.5 Recursive Analysis Recursive analysis examines feedback loops. Human erosion is treated simultaneously as: ● output of conditions, ● input into future outcomes. Examples include: ● administrative exhaustion reducing participation, ● reduced participation limiting access, ● limited access increasing instability, ● instability producing further exhaustion. Within SR, individuals are neither fully autonomous nor fully determined. They operate within recursive

This distinction becomes critical for understanding agency.

5. THE SR–RELIGION TENSION: AGENCY UNDER CONDITIONS

OF

CONSTRAINT

The deepest difference between SR and many religious explanatory systems concerns agency. Many religious traditions assume that moral life requires meaningful human action. SR does not reject agency; it proposes that agency is conditional. Agency becomes constrained by: ● time, ● access, ● resources, ● information, ● institutional friction, ● environmental load. The Agency Myth introduces a distinction between: Formal Agency: theoretical ability to choose. Practical Agency: real capacity to realize choices. This distinction changes interpretation. Deviation from ideals may no longer indicate unwillingness; it may indicate constraint. A person may fail to: ● follow recommendations, ● access treatment, ● maintain routines, ● participate fully, not because of moral deficiency— but because required conditions are absent. This creates explanatory divergence. Religious interpretation often emphasizes choice . SR emphasizes available action space . The claim is not that agency disappears. The claim is that agency becomes environmentally dependent.

5.1 The Agency Realization Ratio (ARR) SR proposes the Agency Realization Ratio (ARR) as a conceptual heuristic. ARR is defined as: Practical outcomes achievable / formally available choices. ARR is not presented as an empirically validated universal metric; it is a diagnostic construct. Its purpose is to measure divergence between theoretical freedom and executable freedom. As ARR declines: ● responsibility becomes harder to isolate, ● intervention shifts upstream, ● moral interpretation loses precision. This introduces the next section: whether responsibility itself changes under infrastructural causation.
