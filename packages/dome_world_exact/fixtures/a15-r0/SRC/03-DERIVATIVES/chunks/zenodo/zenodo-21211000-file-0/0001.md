0.1 Conceptual Modeling as Structural Method SR begins with conceptual modeling, a method commonly used in institutional theory, systems science,

to

Conceptual

The modeling process proceeds in three steps: ● defining structural primitives (pressure, topology, reflexes, collapse geometry); ● specifying relationships among these primitives; ● integrating them into the SR Cycle. This approach is consistent with early-stage theoretical development in institutional economics, organizational

sociology,

and

0.2 Structural Mapping of Institutional Topology To analyze how pressure propagates through institutional architecture, SR employs structural mapping,

Structural mapping includes: ● communication channels, ● decision bottlenecks, ● procedural dependencies,

● authority networks, ● regulatory interfaces, ● organizational fracture lines. These elements are treated as components of institutional topology. Structural mapping does not

0.3 Comparative Institutional Analysis SR evaluates institutional recalibration through comparative analysis, examining how institutions with

Comparative analysis includes: ● cross-case comparison, ● within-case structural tracing, ● reflex pattern identification, ● cycle reconstruction. This method aligns with established comparative institutional research but adds a structural dimension

by

on

pressure

propagation

and

0.4 Structural Inference and Reflex Identification SR introduces a taxonomy of reflex classes. To identify reflexes empirically, SR uses structural inference,

Structural inference involves: ● identifying observable organizational actions, ● mapping these actions to reflex classes, ● analyzing whether reflex activation corresponds to pressure thresholds or topological constraints.

0.5 Modeling Collapse Geometry To evaluate collapse geometry, SR uses failure pathway analysis, a method derived from systems

Failure pathway analysis includes: ● identifying initial instability points, ● tracing propagation through structural dependencies,

● mapping cascade patterns, ● reconstructing fracture-line propagation. 0.6 Cycle Reconstruction The SR Cycle (P → T → Φ → R → P′) is evaluated through cycle reconstruction, a method that identifies

within

Cycle reconstruction includes: ● detecting pressure accumulation, ● mapping topological mediation, ● identifying phase shifts, ● documenting reorganization, ● measuring new equilibrium conditions. 0.7 Methodological Assumptions SR’s methodological approach rests on four assumptions: ● institutions possess structural architecture; ● pressure propagates through this architecture; ● institutional responses follow reflex patterns; ● recalibration occurs through structural cycles. 0.8 Empirical Applicability and Future Operationalization Although SR is currently conceptual, its primitives can be operationalized through: ● network metrics (topology), ● threshold indicators (pressure), ● behavioral coding (reflexes), ● cascade modeling (collapse geometry), ● longitudinal process tracing (cycle reconstruction). 0.9 Summary of Methodological Approach SR’s methodological strategy combines: ● conceptual modeling, ● structural mapping, ● comparative institutional analysis, ● structural inference, ● failure pathway analysis,

● cycle reconstruction. Together, these methods provide a coherent approach for analyzing institutional recalibration as a
