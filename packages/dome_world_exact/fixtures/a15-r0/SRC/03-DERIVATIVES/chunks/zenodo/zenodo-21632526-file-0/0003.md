Chapter I — Field Boundary: Criminology Under Structural Erosion Analytical status. Existing criminology already explains crime through multiple structural,

situational,

developmental,

and

institutional

theories.

This

defines the residual problem Collapse Criminology must explain rather than declaring

those

traditions

Classical and contemporary criminology provide essential foundations for explaining

crime,

victimization,

and

It was constructed during eras in which institutions were stable, narratives were

Crime

was

The

But the contemporary subject — the human who lives inside Quiet Governance,

and

—

is

was

SR proposes that changes in human functioning can precede visible institutional failure;

Collapse

The relevant criminological question is whether structural and psychological strain

existing

risk,

The erosion of agency, identity, imagination, and emotional stability is not a side

The

Quiet Governance governs not through force but through conditions. Not

Traditional criminology does not, through any single framework, fully explain:

●

why crime emerges from administrative overload ●

why algorithmic exclusion produces deviance ●

why emotional containment leads to explosive acts ●

why dependency creates access ‑ crime ●

why identity fragmentation produces tribal crime ●
