    "description": "<p>Collapse Criminology is a discipline‑creating framework that explains crime as a structural and psychological adaptation to modern environments shaped by friction, ambiguity, dependency, algorithmic mediation, and quiet governance. Unlike classical criminology&mdash;which assumes stability, autonomy, and coherent social order&mdash;Collapse Criminology begins from the empirical reality that contemporary systems are increasingly complex, opaque, and infrastructurally mediated. These conditions erode agency, fragment identity, and generate criminogenic pressures long before any criminal act occurs. Collapse Criminology provides a unified architecture for understanding how crime emerges from erosion rather than deviance, and how offenders are shaped by containment rather than choice.</p>\n<p>Although the field is new, three contemporary criminology works directly support its foundations.<br><strong>Vigh and Sausdal (2025)</strong> demonstrate that crime increasingly embeds itself inside systems, infrastructures, and governance flows, showing that offenders adapt to structural conditions rather than simply violating norms. This aligns with Collapse Criminology&rsquo;s central claim that crime is a response to system‑generated friction, access barriers, and procedural labyrinths.<br><strong>Ducate, Bostrom, and Niemeyer (2025)</strong> identify a theory crisis in criminology, arguing that existing frameworks are imprecise and unable to explain modern crime modalities. Their analysis supports the need for a replacement paradigm&mdash;one capable of addressing erosion, drift, and system‑embedded harm.<br>Finally, <strong>&ldquo;Money in the bank: Progressive problem shifts in criminological science&rdquo; (2025)</strong> shows that criminology is already undergoing conceptual shifts as crime becomes more entangled with institutions and infrastructures. This confirms that Collapse Criminology is not speculative but a timely synthesis of trends already visible in the discipline.</p>\n<p>Together, these three works demonstrate that criminology is moving toward the very questions Collapse Criminology formalizes: how systems produce crime, how governance architectures become criminogenic, and how offenders are shaped by erosion rather than deviance. Collapse Criminology contributes a coherent, integrated framework that organizes these emerging insights into a discipline built for the world that actually exists.</p>",
