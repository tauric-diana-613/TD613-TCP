and

Risk SignalRupture (SR) PREFACE Modern societies are entering a period defined not by isolated crises, but by directional worsening

across

climate

systems,

institutional

capacity,

economic

simultaneously,

interact,

and

alter

drift

science

as

a

program

for

directional

structural

pressure.

It

their

and

the

institutional

disorder,

economic

fragility,

ABSTRACT Modern societies exhibit simultaneous pressures across climate stability, institutional capacity, economic

resilience,

epistemic

coherence,

technological

governance,

geopolitical

dependency,

and

cultural

cohesion.

This

manuscript

introduces

the

Drift

Collapse

Field

(DCF)

as

a

formal

analytical

framework

for

representing

those

pressures

as

a

time-varying,

six-dimensional

system

generated

by

twenty-seven

structural

drivers.

DCF

defines

driver

vectors,

dimensional

projections,

interaction

coefficients,

field

magnitude,

comparative

drift

indices,

and

dynamic

change.

It

explicitly

distinguishes

descriptive

indicators

from

SR

transformations

and

distinguishes

correlation

from

causal

interaction. A pilot Canadian operationalization demonstrates the method using current official indicators: national

greenhouse-gas

performance

relative

to

the

2030

target,

trust

in

the

federal

government,

concern

about

online

misinformation,

business

AI

adoption,

export

concentration

toward

the

United

States,

and

generalized

interpersonal

trust.

Under

the

stated

normalization

rules,

these

indicators

form

a

reproducible

illustrative

vector

rather

than

an

official

national

score.

The

manuscript

also

proposes

a

Drift

Interaction

Matrix

and

formalizes

amplification,

dampening,

and

intervention

effects

without

treating

interaction

coefficients

as

established

empirical

constants. The cross-national Canada–United States–United Kingdom comparison is retained as a research hypothesis

rather

than

asserted

as

already

proven.

A

valid

gradient

requires

matched

indicators,

common

normalization,

common

observation

windows,

uncertainty

and

sensitivity

This

change

makes

the

gradient

testable

rather

than

rhetorical. DCF is non-deterministic: vectors change over time, interaction strengths change, interventions alter

trajectories,

and

field

magnitude

is

not

fixed.

The

framework

is

falsifiable

at

the

level

its

hypotheses: proposed driver relationships can fail replication; interaction coefficients can be indistinguishable

from

zero;

predicted

ordering

can

fail

and

field-aware

interventions

can

fail

to

outperform

simpler

models.

The

manuscript

therefore

establishes

Drift

Science

not

as

completed

empirical

consensus,

but

as

a

structured,

falsifiable

research

program

for

studying

directional

societal

pressure.

Many

increasing

The

1.2 Drift: The Missing Concept Drift is defined here as persistent directional change in a structural indicator or latent system state

that

increases

exposure

to

adverse

outcomes

faster

than

adaptive

capacity

offsets

it.

This

definition

adds

an

empirical

requirement

to

the

original

SR

formulation:

worsening

must

be

specified

relative

to

an

explicit

reference

condition,

target,

baseline,

or

resilience

criterion. Drift is not fate, prophecy, or a synonym for all change. It is a measurable relationship among direction,

persistence,

exposure,

and

response

capacity. • a pressure represented through indicators • a direction represented through signed change • a magnitude represented through normalized intensity • a dynamic state represented through time

• a structural condition that remains modifiable Drift can explain why improvement in one subsystem may fail to alter a broader structural trajectory.

Electricity

can

decarbonize

while

oil-and-gas

emissions

remain

elevated.

A

government

can

retain

substantial

public

trust

while

facing

misinformation

exposure

or

external

economic

concentration.

A

society

can

be

stable

in

one

dimension

and

strained

in

another.

DCF

is

designed

to

preserve

those

differences

rather

than

compress

them

into

a

binary

collapse

label.

The

1.3X Relation to Existing Theories: What Drift Science Borrows and Where It Departs Complexity science What DCF borrows: non-linearity, feedback, emergence, cross-scale coupling. Where DCF departs: adds an explicit signed concept of directional structural pressure and requires

operational

benchmarks

for

worsening.

Risk society What DCF borrows: systemic risks generated by modernity and institutional difficulty managing them. Where DCF departs: moves from distribution and perception of risk toward a quantitative cross-domain

state

representation.

Resilience theory What DCF borrows: adaptive cycles, thresholds, reorganization, cross-scale effects. Where DCF departs: focuses on persistent pressure and response-capacity gaps, while remaining compatible

with

recovery

and

reorganization.

Political economy What DCF borrows: lock-in, infrastructural inertia, dependence, power and capture.

Where DCF departs: extends analysis across epistemic, technological, geopolitical and cultural dimensions

rather

than

treating

material

interests

as

the

sole

explanatory

layer.

Climate science What DCF borrows: measured trajectories, physical impacts, thresholds and uncertainty. Where DCF departs: uses climate variables as one component of a wider societal state rather than

redefining

climate

science.

Digital governance and platform studies What DCF borrows: algorithmic amplification, gatekeeping, moderation, visibility and market power. Where DCF departs: treats digital systems as interacting structural drivers whose effects must be estimated

rather

than

assumed.

Sociology and cultural theory What DCF borrows: identity, norms, meaning, trust and social cohesion. Where DCF departs: formalizes selected constructs as indicators while recognizing that many cultural

variables

are

latent

and

measurement-sensitive.

Systems ecology What DCF borrows: stress gradients, thresholds, feedbacks and multi-scale dynamics. Where DCF departs: translates some systems concepts into institutional and informational domains

without

claiming

physical

equivalence.
