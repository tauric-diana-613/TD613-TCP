Chapter 1 — Introduction: The Problem of Modern

Agency

Modern states increasingly operate through infrastructures they do not own, do not fully control, and

cannot

easily

replace.

Telecommunications

networks,

cloud

These

The Infrastructural Political Economy field emerges from this structural reality. It examines how

The central claim of this monograph is that agency is no longer a purely institutional property .

It

A

This

The GAUID/GAI framework addresses this need by formalizing four core components: ● Infrastructure Dependency — the extent to which government relies on external infrastructures ● Substitution Capacity — the ability to replace or bypass those infrastructures ● Regulatory Leverage — the degree of influence government can exert over operators ● Structural Power — the ability of operators to shape or constrain state action These components interact to determine the operational boundaries of modern state agency.
