1. Sectoral Measurement Architecture The GAUID/GAI framework evaluates agency sector by sector , because infrastructural dependency

Each sector receives a full GAUID profile: ● Telecommunications ● Cloud Platforms ● Logistics ● Payment Systems ● Wage ‑ Setting Mechanisms ● Energy ● Transportation Each sector is scored independently before aggregation.

2. Component Scoring (0–1 Normalization) Each of the four GAUID components is scored on a 0–1 scale : ● 0 = no constraint / full agency ● 1 = maximum constraint / no agency Infrastructure Dependency (ID) Measured through: ● reliance on external operators ● operational continuity requirements ● crisis ‑ response mediation ● exclusivity of infrastructural pathways Substitution Capacity (SC)

Measured through: ● technical alternatives ● operational workarounds ● institutional emergency powers ● cost/time feasibility of switching Regulatory Leverage (RL) Measured through: ● statutory authority ● oversight mechanisms ● enforcement capacity ● licensing and compliance regimes Structural Power (SP) Measured through: ● operator indispensability ● infrastructural chokepoints ● monopoly/oligopoly conditions ● operational veto power Each component is scored using sector ‑ specific indicators.

3. Weighting Strategy Not all components contribute equally to agency. The weighting system is: ● ID = 0.35 ● SC = 0.25 ● RL = 0.20 ● SP = 0.20 This reflects empirical reality: ● Dependency is the strongest determinant of constraint. ● Substitution is the strongest determinant of resilience. ● Regulatory leverage and structural power shape the operational boundary.

Weights can be adjusted for country ‑ specific contexts, but the default model uses the above distribution.

4. Sectoral GAUID Score For each sector: GAUID_sector = w_ID·ID + w_SC·SC + w_RL·RL + w_SP·SP Where: ● w_ID=0.35, w_SC=0.25, w_RL=0.20, w_SP=0.20 This produces a sector ‑ level constraint score .

5. National ‑ Level GAI Aggregation The national Government Agency Index (GAI) is computed by aggregating sectoral GAUID scores

using

a

weighted

sectoral

importance

Sector weights reflect: ● national criticality ● crisis relevance ● operational centrality ● interdependency Default sector weights: ● Telecommunications — 0.20 ● Cloud Platforms — 0.20 ● Logistics — 0.15 ● Payment Systems — 0.15 ● Wage ‑ Setting — 0.10 ● Energy — 0.10 ● Transportation — 0.10 National GAI: GAI_national = Σ( w_sector · GAUID_sector)

Normalized to: GAI ∈ [0,1]

6. Interpretation of National GAI ● 0.80–1.00 — strong agency ● 0.50–0.79 — moderate agency ● 0.30–0.49 — constrained agency ● 0.00–0.29 — severe infrastructural dependency This classification is used throughout the empirical chapters.

1. Why Telecommunications Is the Foundational Sector

Telecommunications infrastructures are: ● essential for emergency response ● essential for inter ‑ agency coordination ● essential for public communication ● essential for digital service delivery ● essential for continuity of government They are also: ● privately owned ● privately operated ● consolidated ● chokepoint ‑ prone ● substitution ‑ limited This makes telecommunications the ideal first sector for demonstrating GAUID measurement.

2. Component Scoring for Telecommunications Each component is scored using the 0–1 normalization system. Infrastructure Dependency (ID) Telecommunications dependency is extremely high because: ● government cannot operate without networks ● emergency services rely on private carriers ● public alerts require operator cooperation ● continuity of government depends on uptime ID score: 0.85 Substitution Capacity (SC) Substitution capacity is low because: ● alternative networks are limited ● redundancy is partial, not full ● satellite substitution is expensive and slow ● emergency switching requires operator support SC score: 0.25
