GAUID distinguishes three concepts. Agency is the ability to initiate and execute intentional

state

Capacity

Autonomy

is

It

3.2 Infrastructure Dependency (ID) Infrastructure Dependency measures the extent to which critical governmental functions

rely

on

that

does

or

Dependency

because

concentration

switching

and

3.3 Substitution Capacity (SC) Substitution Capacity measures the availability, speed, feasibility, and scalability of alternatives

when

an

incumbent

infrastructure

provider

becomes

unavailable

or

non-cooperative.

Public

ownership

is

one

of

substitution

but

is

interoperability,

multiple

private

providers,

geographic

redundancy,

stockpiles,

and

interoperable

can

increase

SC.

3.4 Regulatory Leverage (RL) Regulatory Leverage measures the government's demonstrated ability to secure compliance,

impose

sanctions,

enforce

decisions,

and

maintain

cooperation

when

private

actors

have

incentives

to

resist.

RL

is

therefore

broader

than

the

number

of

enforcement

actions.

3.5 Structural Power (SP)

Structural Power captures power generated by the relationship between dependency and

control.

It

is

divided

into

operational

and

structural

components. SP = α SP_operational + β SP_structural, α + β = 1 Operational structural power arises from control over infrastructure the government requires.

Structural

political

power

arises

from

the

ability

to

shape

rules,

standards,

expectations,

lobbying

environments,

and

institutional

arrangements.

3.6 The central mechanism High ID + Low SC + Low RL → High SP → Lower Effective Agency The mechanism is conditional rather than deterministic. High dependency can be offset

by

high

substitution

capacity

or

high

regulatory

leverage.
