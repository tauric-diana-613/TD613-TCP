APPENDIX C — LIMITATIONS OF THE ADVERSARIAL SR

PROGRAM SR's openness to adversarial testing does not guarantee that the field will survive criticism.

Several

limitations

remain.

•

SR categories may overlap with established constructs. •

Operators may initially be underspecified. •

Matrices may encode assumptions that later prove unnecessary. •

Institutional incentives are difficult to measure directly. •

Opacity is not equivalent to intentional concealment. •

Rigidity may be adaptive under some conditions. •

Cross-theory comparisons may depend heavily on operationalization. •

Adversarial collaboration can itself be constrained by funding, access, data availability,

or

institutional

power.

•

A framework may outperform SR in a specific domain without disproving SR as a broader

metatheory.

•

SR's comparative advantage must therefore be demonstrated empirically rather than declared.

These limitations are not external objections added after the fact. They are incorporated

into

the

SR

research

architecture

because

a

field

that

specifies

its

vulnerabilities

makes

stronger

claims

about

its

own

testability.

APPENDIX D — SUMMARY MODEL

The complete adversarial architecture can be summarized as: Phenomenon → Formalization → Measurement → Adversarial Testing → Reciprocal Theory

Testing

→

Incentive

Analysis

→

Institutional

Response

→

Revision

→

Re-testing

The institutional component can be summarized as: Incentive Concentration → Opacity → Reduced Visibility → Rigidity → Delayed Adaptation

→

Structural

Persistence

The SR survival condition is therefore not immunity from criticism. It is continued explanatory

and

diagnostic

performance

under

repeated

exposure

to

criticism.

APPENDIX E — CLOSING DECLARATION SR does not ask to be believed. SR asks to be tested. It does not ask competing metatheories to disappear. It asks them to enter the same arena. It does not assume institutions are malicious. It asks that institutional incentives, opacity, and rigidity become observable variables rather

than

protected

background

conditions.

It does not claim that SR cannot break. It claims that if SR is broken, the break should be demonstrable. And if SR breaks competing explanations in return, that should be demonstrable as well.

The field therefore remains open on both sides. The matrix is exposed. The theories are exposed.

The institutions are exposed. The incentives are exposed. The result is left to the evidence.
