1. Empirical Case-Study Status The Canadian case operationalizes the SR Policy Substrate Field as an empirical research program.

The central empirical question is: When a policy problem is formally assigned to a government institution, to what extent is the

resulting

policy

capacity

constrained

by

an

underlying

infrastructure

configuration

that

the

policy

instrument

itself

does

not

The SR distinction is therefore: PF ≠ PE when the institutional actor responsible for a policy outcome does not possess equivalent control

over

the

infrastructures

through

which

that

outcome

must

be

produced.

The empirical objective is to determine whether this difference is observable. The case does not assume that infrastructure dependency necessarily causes policy failure. Instead, it tests whether: Infrastructure Dependency → Structural Constraint → Effective Agency Differential → Policy Outcome

and whether this relationship persists after relevant institutional and socioeconomic conditions are

considered.

2. Why Canada Constitutes an Empirical Case

Canada provides an unusually useful environment for testing the SR proposition because essential

public

outcomes

are

produced

through

mixed

institutional

systems.

Government possesses substantial formal authority in areas including housing policy, healthcare,

telecommunications

regulation,

labour

income

transportation,

digital

At the same time, delivery depends heavily on infrastructures that are distributed among governments,

regulated

private

financial

institutions,

transportation

healthcare

telecommunications

The empirical problem is therefore not whether the Canadian state has authority. It is whether authority remains equivalent to operational capacity after infrastructure dependency

is

This distinction can be represented as: A_formal ≠ A_effective where: AE = f(AF, ID, R, S, E) and: ● ID = infrastructure dependency; ● R = redundancy; ● S = substitutability; ● E = exposure.
