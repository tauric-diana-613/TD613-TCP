Section XX — Conclusion: The Anthropology of the Present

SR Anthropology proposes that contemporary anthropology needs an integrated

way

to

study

the

human

where

cultural

life

is

increasingly

entangled

with

infrastructures,

platforms,

administrative

systems,

and

algorithmic

classifications.

The

field’s

strongest

form

does

not

claim

that

culture

has

ended,

that

collapse

is

universal,

or

that

existing

scholarship

already

proves

SR.

It

identifies

a

research

problem

and

supplies

constructs

that

can

be

tested.

XX.1 Collapse as a Conditional Structural Regime

Collapse is defined here as sustained multi-domain deterioration or capacity loss

with

human

consequences.

It

is

not

synonymous

with

change,

complexity,

dissatisfaction,

or

technological

mediation.

XX.2 Five Analytical Dimensions •

Erosion •

Drift •

Hollowness •

Infrastructural dependency •

Algorithmic mediation XX.3 Field Sites Households, workplaces, institutions, neighborhoods, cities, platforms, databases,

and

infrastructures

can

all

become

anthropological

field

sites

when

researchers

follow

relations

across

them.

XX.4 Contribution •

a human-facing synthesis within the SR canon •

five differentiable analytical dimensions •

a multidimensional formal model •

provisional operational indicators and CCI architecture •

case-study designs rather than fictionalized empirical cases •

integrated ethnographic and systems methods •

falsifiability and boundary conditions •

ethical constraints against stigmatizing or deterministic use

XX.5 Closing Statement The central proposition of SR Anthropology is not that collapse is the only shape

of

society.

It

is

that

structural

erosion

can

become

a

durable

human

environment

and

that

anthropology

should

be

able

to

identify

when

this

occurs,

how

it

is

mediated,

who

is

protected

from

it,

who

bears

its

costs,

and

when

the

theory

itself

fails.

A

field

becomes

stronger

when

its

language

can

be

tested

against

the

world

rather

than

merely

imposed

upon

it.
