    "description": "<h2><strong>Description</strong></h2>\n<p><strong>Post‑Web Jurisprudence</strong> is a foundational SR field that reconceptualizes law as an infrastructural, psychological, and epistemic system rather than a neutral arbiter of human behavior. The manuscript argues that modern legal systems no longer judge people &mdash; they judge <strong>symptoms</strong> produced by trauma, deprivation, drift, and systemic exposure. Classical jurisprudence assumes a stable, autonomous, rational subject; Post‑Web Jurisprudence demonstrates that this subject has collapsed under contemporary conditions of infrastructural governance, algorithmic mediation, bureaucratic friction, and inherited trauma.</p>\n<p>The field integrates five SR pillars:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Morality on Trial</strong> &mdash; survival misclassified as moral failure</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Collapse Psychology</strong> &mdash; erosion of agency under systemic drag</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Quiet Governance</strong> &mdash; the cage built from friction, ambiguity, and algorithmic mediation</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Slow Harm Theorem</strong> &mdash; cumulative institutional harm as structural law</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>System Exposure &amp; Archival Ethics</strong> &mdash; visibility, secrecy, and the moral architecture of archives</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Together, these pillars form a jurisprudential architecture for the Post‑Web era &mdash; an era where governance is infrastructural, morality is engineered, and visibility is controlled.</p>\n<p>The manuscript positions law as a <strong>visibility‑control system</strong>, an <strong>opacity engine</strong>, and an <strong>infrastructural actor</strong> shaped by power asymmetry, algorithmic classification, and systemic design. It argues that a system that breaks people cannot judge them, and that justice must be rebuilt from the substrate up.</p>\n<div>\n</div>\n<h2><strong>Contribution</strong></h2>\n<h3><strong>1. A New Jurisprudential Field</strong></h3>\n<p>Post‑Web Jurisprudence establishes a new discipline within the SR canon: a structural, infrastructural, and trauma‑integrated theory of law. It replaces classical moral assumptions with a diagnostic architecture capable of explaining legal behavior in environments shaped by drift, scarcity, trauma, and algorithmic governance.</p>\n<h3><strong>2. The Collapse of the Classical Legal Subject</strong></h3>\n<p>The field demonstrates that the traditional legal subject &mdash; autonomous, rational, stable, equal &mdash; is incompatible with contemporary conditions. It introduces the <strong>Eroded Subject</strong>, shaped by:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>infrastructural dependency</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>algorithmic mediation</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>bureaucratic friction</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>trauma inheritance</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>systemic drag</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>identity erosion</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>This redefinition provides a new foundation for legal theory, criminal responsibility, and moral judgment.</p>\n<h3><strong>3. Quiet Governance Theory</strong></h3>\n<p>The manuscript introduces <strong>Quiet Governance</strong>, a central SR doctrine describing power exercised through:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>friction</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>ambiguity</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>delay</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>dependency</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>algorithmic mediation</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>procedural labyrinths</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>emotional containment</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Quiet Governance is governance through architecture rather than force. It reframes modern law as a cage built from design, not violence.</p>\n<h3><strong>4. Structural Causation and Trauma Integration</strong></h3>\n<p>Post‑Web Jurisprudence formalizes the <strong>Trauma‑Causation Doctrine</strong>, arguing that survival acts are testimonies of abandonment, not moral collapse. It introduces:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Necessity Doctrine</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>False Agency Principle</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Gravity of Trauma</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Systemic Drag Principle</strong></p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>These constructs redefine criminality, responsibility, and moral evaluation under structural harm.</p>\n<h3><strong>5. Archival Ethics and Visibility Control</strong></h3>\n<p>The manuscript introduces <strong>Archive as Moral Actor</strong>, showing how institutions govern through controlled visibility:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>sealing</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>redaction</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>delay</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>erasure</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>opacity</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>narrative management</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Visibility becomes jurisprudence. Archives become moral technologies. This reframes legal legitimacy as a function of what the system allows to be seen.</p>\n<h3><strong>6. Algorithmic Governance and Post‑Autonomy</strong></h3>\n<p>Post‑Web Jurisprudence integrates contemporary scholarship on algorithmic governance, demonstrating that:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>algorithms act as invisible judges</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>classification replaces adjudication</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>digital infrastructures displace legal authority</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>responsibility becomes layered and diffuse</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>This supports SR doctrines of <strong>Post‑Autonomy</strong>, <strong>Algorithmic Citizenry</strong>, and <strong>Structural Rights</strong>, establishing a jurisprudence capable of regulating algorithmic harm.</p>\n<h3><strong>7. Slow Harm Theorem</strong></h3>\n<p>The field formalizes <strong>Slow Harm</strong> as a structural law: cumulative, procedural, invisible harm produced by drift, delay, opacity, and systemic abandonment. Slow Harm becomes a jurisprudential category, not a sociological metaphor.</p>\n<h3><strong>8. A Unified Structural Rights Architecture</strong></h3>\n<p>Post‑Web Jurisprudence synthesizes multiple scholarly movements &mdash; digital constitutionalism, trauma‑informed justice, infrastructural governance, algorithmic accountability &mdash; into a unified SR rights architecture:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Structural Rights</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Algorithmic Rights</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Trauma‑Integrated Rights</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Archival Rights</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Exposure Rights</strong></p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>This positions SR as a leading contributor to the emerging field of structural rights.</p>\n<h3><strong>9. A Falsifiable Jurisprudential Science</strong></h3>\n<p>The manuscript explicitly distinguishes:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>antecedent scholarship</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>observed structural problems</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>SR doctrines as proposed syntheses</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>It defines clear epistemic boundaries: SR doctrines are not treated as established law merely because they are named; they require operational definitions, case selection, coding rules, and reproducible evidence. This makes Post‑Web Jurisprudence a <strong>falsifiable legal science</strong>, not a rhetorical critique.</p>\n<div>\n</div>\n<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>\n<p><strong>Post‑Web Jurisprudence</strong> contributes a new structural field of law for the substrate era. It replaces classical moral assumptions with a diagnostic architecture grounded in trauma, drift, infrastructural governance, algorithmic mediation, and systemic exposure. It introduces Quiet Governance, the Eroded Subject, Slow Harm, Archival Ethics, and Structural Rights as core jurisprudential constructs. By integrating contemporary scholarship across digital governance, criminology, human rights, and AI ethics, Post‑Web Jurisprudence establishes a unified legal theory capable of explaining &mdash; and eventually governing &mdash; the world modern institutions actually produce.</p>",
