divided

already

shaped

System Inheritance Studies establishes a scientific field for examining how historical

structure

travels

through

data,

becomes

models,

is

activated

returns

Abstract Artificial intelligence systems are commonly evaluated as bounded technical objects,

yet

their

inputs

and

outputs

are

embedded

in

institutional

histories.

This

manuscript

establishes

System

Inheritance

Studies

(SIS),

a

cross-domain

field

examining

the

transmission

of

historical

structure

through

administrative

data,

algorithmic

models,

institutional

action,

environmental

change,

and

recursive

retraining.

The

field

distinguishes

four

processes:

inherited

representation,

proxy

reconstruction,

deployment-mediated

amplification,

and

recursive

inheritance.

It

formalizes

a

system-inheritance

chain,

proposes

measurable

indices

for

inheritance,

amplification,

persistence,

proxy

recoverability,

exposure

concentration,

and

corrective

capacity,

and

specifies

evidentiary

grades

that

separate

descriptive

disparity

from

causal

discrimination.

Four

empirical

demonstrations—historical

redlining

and

contemporary

tract

conditions,

predictive-policing

exposure,

mortgage

underwriting,

and

school

risk

classification—show

how

the

framework

can

be

operationalized

using

public

data. These demonstrations support the plausibility and measurability of the architecture

but

do

not

establish

that

every

observed

disparity

was

caused

by

a

deployed

AI

system.

The

manuscript

further

integrates

experimental

human–AI

feedback,

performative

prediction,

algorithmic

confounding,

and

recursive

model-collapse

SIS

contributes

a

unified

framework,

a

cross-domain

measurement

an

intervention

and

a

falsifiable

research

program

for

determining

when

systems

preserve,

intensify,

reduce,

or

reverse

inherited

social

structure.

Executive Proposition Historical structure → records → features → model → institutional action → changed

environment

→

The system-inheritance hypothesis states that a technical system can inherit an institution’s

prior

structure

without

explicit

instruction.

The

structure

may

remain

recoverable

through

geography,

cost,

institutional

contact,

accumulated

wealth,

observed

enforcement,

school

attendance,

healthcare

utilization,

language,

or

other

variables

generated

by

the

earlier

system.

Deployment

may

then

convert

this

inherited

representation

into

resource

allocation,

scrutiny,

opportunity,

or

exclusion.

If

those

actions

modify

what

is

subsequently

observed

and

used

for

training,

inheritance

becomes

recursive.

1. The Field Problem 1.1 Why a New Field Is Justified No new discipline is justified merely by renaming a known bias. A field becomes defensible

when

it

identifies

a

stable

object

of

study,

integrates

mechanisms

that

remain analytically fragmented, develops distinct measurements, makes falsifiable

predictions,

and

produces

an

empirical

program

that

existing

specialties

do

not

coordinate.

System

Inheritance

Studies

satisfies

that

test

conditionally:

its

are

but

their

organization

into

a

genealogy-to-retraining

architecture

is

not

standard

within

any

single

established

Fairness research often begins with a dataset and a protected attribute. Historical sociology

often

stops

before

implementation.

Public

administration

examines

routines

but

seldom

how

decisions

become

machine-learning

Urban

research

documents

durable

without

necessarily

through

model

pipelines.

Performative-prediction

research

formalizes

response

but

from

the

institutional

that

produced

SIS

links

1.2 Object of Study The object of System Inheritance Studies is the transmission of system state across

A

“generation”

later

Inheritance

exists

later

or

1.3 Core Questions •

Which historical institutions generated the variables treated as present-day evidence?

•

Can protected or historical status be reconstructed from apparently neutral features?

•

Does model output merely reproduce the source distribution, or does it increase

disparity?

•

Does deployment alter surveillance, allocation, behavior, eligibility, or opportunity?

•

Do deployment-produced outcomes enter subsequent administrative records

or

training

data?

•

Which interventions attenuate inheritance without concealing genuine need or

degrading

valid

prediction?

•

Under what conditions does an institution reverse, rather than automate, its inherited

trajectory?

2.2 What SR Does Not Claim •

SR does not claim to have discovered algorithmic bias, feedback loops, redlining

persistence,

proxy

discrimination,

or

performative

prediction.

•

SR does not infer intent from disparity alone. •

SR does not treat race as a biological cause or assume that group membership

explains

institutional

outcomes.

•

SR does not claim that removing protected attributes makes a model fair, or that

retaining

necessarily

makes

it

unfair.

•

SR does not present theoretical equations as estimated laws unless parameters

have

been

fit

to

data.

3. Core Concepts 3.1 Institutional Genealogy Institutional genealogy identifies the process that produced a variable before that variable

entered

a

model.

It

asks

not

only

what

a

feature

predicts

but

why

the

feature

exists,

who

was

observed,

who

was

absent,

what

rule

determined

the

observation,

and

what

prior

allocation

made

the

feature

informative.

A

police

contact

count

is

partly

a

record

of

conduct

and

partly

a

record

of

police

presence.

Healthcare

cost

is

partly

a

record

of

illness

and

partly

a

record

of

access,

insurance,

pricing,

and

treatment.

School

absence

is

partly

a

record

of

attendance and partly a record of housing, disability accommodation, transportation,

family

care,

discipline,

and

institutional

response.

3.2 Inherited Representation Inherited representation occurs when a later dataset or model contains information

about

an

earlier

institutional

ordering.

This

can

explicit

category

is

Geography

may

encode

assets

may

exclusion

from

prior

credit;

prior

arrests

may

encode

enforcement

concentration;

cost

may

access.

Inheritance

is

an

empirical

property

of

information

not

moral

itself.
