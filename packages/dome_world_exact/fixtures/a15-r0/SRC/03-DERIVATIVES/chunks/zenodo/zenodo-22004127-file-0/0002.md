15. Test Protocol A reproducible SSO test follows seven steps: 1.

Declare the outcome or condition Y. 2.

Map the empirically supported causal/reproductive chain C(Y). 3.

Declare the actual operative boundary B used by the study, metric, policy, or institution. 4.

Compute or identify O = C(Y) \ B. 5.

Assess whether omitted components materially reproduce Y or determine intervention effectiveness.

6.

Attempt falsification by showing that the allegedly omitted substrate is already represented,

16. Falsification Conditions

SSO is falsifiable. The operator fails or must be narrowed when: •

The alleged substrate is not causally important to reproduction of the measured condition.

•

The allegedly omitted component is already inside the operative analytical, measurement,

policy,

or

governance

boundary.

•

Adding the omitted component does not materially change interpretation, intervention effectiveness,

or

•

A downstream intervention demonstrably changes upstream reproductive capacity without

•

The causal chain used to declare omission is unsupported, incorrectly specified, or confounded.

•

An apparent transition omission disappears after lifecycle, trade, substitution, recycling, and

replacement-substrate

accounting.
