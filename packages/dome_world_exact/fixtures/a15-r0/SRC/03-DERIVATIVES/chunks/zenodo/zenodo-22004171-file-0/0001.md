to

preserve

causally

1. Definition and Necessary Conditions EAO = Persistent Causal-Preservation Failure with Material Governance Consequence

Condition Meaning Empirical requirement C1 — Causal Structure Loss Critical nodes or edges are omitted, siloed, or fragmented Demonstrable missing causal relationships C2 — Material Sensitivity Restoring the missing structure changes the conclusion or intervention Boundary-sensitivity evidence C3 — Persistence The same mismatch recurs Repeated over time, decisions, or domains C4 — Institutional Operability The institution remains functional and knowledgeable Reports, experts, programs, budgets, implementation continue

2. Epistemic Preservation Ratio Let E_c be the set of causally critical relationships required to represent the governed problem

and

E_i

the

subset

represented

operationally

by

the

institution. EPR = |E_c ∩ E_i| / |E_c| EPR approaches one when critical causal structure is preserved and approaches zero as institutional

representation

loses

critical

structure.

3. Governance Sensitivity to Restoration GSR = |Y_restored - Y_institutional| For this first empirical paper, GSR is categorical: 1 when restoring omitted causal structure

materially

changes

the

structural

interpretation

or

policy

endpoint,

and

0

when

it

does

not. Low EPR + GSR = 0 → EAO not demonstrated 4. Persistence P Meaning

0 isolated case or no recurrence 0.5 repeated within one domain or over time 1 repeated across domains, layers, or institutional settings

5. Exploratory EAO Score EAO* = (1 - EPR) × GSR × P The score is exploratory and case-bounded. It is not a psychometric scale. Its purpose is to

force

EAO

to

satisfy

all

three

requirements:

lost

causal

structure,

material

consequence,

and

recurrence.
