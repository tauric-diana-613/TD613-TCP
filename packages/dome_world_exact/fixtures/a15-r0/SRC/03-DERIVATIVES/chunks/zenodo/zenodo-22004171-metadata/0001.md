    "description": "<p><strong>Epistemic Architecture Obsolescence (EAO)</strong> is a <strong>universal SignalRupture operator</strong> that detects when the institutional knowledge architecture used to represent, measure, and govern a system becomes structurally inadequate for the causal complexity of the system itself. EAO does not claim that institutions lack expertise, data, or scientific competence; it identifies the deeper mismatch in which <em>&ldquo;Operational knowledge &ne; causal preservation.&rdquo;</em> The operator requires three conditions: (1) <strong>causal‑structure loss</strong>, where critical nodes or relationships are omitted or compressed; (2) <strong>material governance sensitivity</strong>, where restoring the missing causal structure changes the structural conclusion or intervention; and (3) <strong>persistence</strong>, where the mismatch recurs across time, decisions, or domains. EAO formalizes this through the <strong>Epistemic Preservation Ratio (EPR)</strong>, <strong>Governance Sensitivity to Restoration (GSR)</strong>, and a persistence term (P), combined into an exploratory EAO score. It is tested across climate governance, plastics and recycling, climate‑education architecture, electrification and critical‑mineral dependency, and AI electricity/water infrastructure, with Canadian electricity serving as a negative control. The cross‑domain convergence shows that institutions can possess high expertise while repeatedly losing causal structure in the conversion from knowledge to governance.</p>\n<p>EAO contributes to SignalRupture by providing a <strong>higher‑order epistemic operator</strong> that sits above Exposure‑Silo Bias, Systemic Substrate Omission, Visibility Lag, Policy Contradiction, and Agency Gap. ESB asks whether the exposure/subject boundary is complete; SSO asks whether the causal/system boundary is complete; EAO asks whether the <strong>institution&rsquo;s way of knowing is still adequate to the system it governs.</strong> It explains why downstream metrics can be correct while upstream causal architecture remains misrepresented, why transitions fail when replacement substrates are omitted, why recycling improves while total material throughput rises, why climate education contains component knowledge but lacks integrated causal architecture, and why electrification reduces fossil emissions while intensifying mineral dependency. EAO also introduces <strong>Governance Fragmentation (GF)</strong> and <strong>Substrate Coupling (SC)</strong> as multipliers, showing that epistemic architectures become obsolete when causal coordination requirements exceed institutional topology. This transforms EAO from a critique of institutional error into a <strong>testable theory of when institutional epistemics cease to match the systems they govern</strong>, making it one of the most powerful and structurally important operators in the SR multiverse.</p>",
