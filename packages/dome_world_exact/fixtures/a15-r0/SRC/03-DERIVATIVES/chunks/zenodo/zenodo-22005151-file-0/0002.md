The OECD reports that 30% of students were distracted by digital devices in PISA 2022 and that students reporting distraction by other students’ device use in mathematics lessons scored lower. This supports regulation of classroom distraction. It does not establish that a cellphone ban repairs attention outside school, reduces platform exposure, or equalizes home learning environments. The policy acts on the visible endpoint—the device in the classroom—while the attention infrastructure extends through platforms, school software, homework systems, family routines, and labour conditions.

8. What the evidence confirms

The assessment mandate is real: Bill 101 received Royal Assent and authorizes province-wide assessment policies.

The performance problem is real but not uniformly worsening: recent EQAO mathematics results improved, while large below-standard and never-met-standard groups remain.

The capacity trajectory is constrained: real per-student provincial operating funding was at a ten-year low in 2024–2025 and is projected to fall further by 2027–2028.

Preparation capacity is unequal: international and Canadian evidence links socioeconomic status and work arrangements to relevant learning and caregiving conditions.

The contradiction is presently a demonstrated structural risk, not yet a measured post-policy causal effect.

9. What is not yet established

Because the examination regime begins after the baseline period, this paper cannot claim that it has already lowered mathematics scores, widened Ontario achievement gaps, increased tutoring expenditure, or harmed students with disabilities. Nor can aggregate funding alone prove that individual classrooms lost specific supports. These are prospective outcomes. Treating them as completed facts would weaken the argument.

The valid result is conditional: Ontario has increased assessment authority under conditions of persistent mathematics difficulty, unequal preparation capacity, and constrained real provincial funding. If implementation does not supply strong compensatory capacity, the model predicts burden transfer and widening dispersion.

10. Prospective test: 2026–2029

The field test should compare students before and after implementation while estimating differential exposure to compensatory capacity.

Record examination weight, course, grade, accommodations, attendance rules, and implementation date for every board.

Construct school-capacity measures: student–teacher ratios, special-education staffing, tutoring hours, absenteeism, course failure, and access to quiet supervised preparation.

Measure household capacity through parent work schedule, weekly paid hours, income, education, language, number of children, housing crowding, paid tutoring, and available study space.

Estimate outcome dispersion, not only the provincial mean: score variance, failure rates, gap changes, credit accumulation, absenteeism, and course withdrawal.

Use difference-in-differences or event-study models where implementation timing or exam intensity differs, with pre-trend tests and board fixed effects.

Audit disability effects separately: accommodation approval, implementation failures, appeals, non-completion, and score changes by identified need.

Measure burden transfer directly: teacher preparation hours, parent assistance time, tutoring expenditure, and student out-of-school study time.

12. Political contribution to Systems Propagation Research

The Suez case showed physical backlog propagation: a resolved obstruction survived as queued work. This case identifies institutional burden propagation: a policy demand moves through administrative levels until the unabsorbed portion reaches teachers, students, and households. The propagated object is not a vessel queue but an obligation without matched capacity.

The case adds four concepts:

Policy load: the new work created by a rule, including preparation, administration, compliance, and accommodation.

Absorption capacity: the portion of policy load an institution can meet without displacing other necessary work.

Burden transfer: the residual load passed to downstream actors when institutional capacity is insufficient.

Contradiction closure: the process by which unequal downstream outcomes are used to justify more of the same upstream enforcement.

This is the political form of propagation: authority creates a common demand, institutions distribute it, capacity determines where it stops, and inequality determines who absorbs the residual harm.

13. Policy repair

The model does not require abandoning examinations. It requires preventing assessment from substituting for instructional capacity. Before attaching higher consequences to final examinations, Ontario should publish an implementation-capacity statement for each board; fund supervised preparation within the school day; guarantee disability accommodations and appeal pathways; measure parent and student burden; separate mastery measurement from attendance penalties; and report outcome distributions by socioeconomic and disability status rather than provincial averages alone.

A valid recovery policy must increase the capacity to learn before increasing the consequences of failing to demonstrate learning.

14. Conclusion

Ontario’s examination policy creates a testable policy contradiction. The province is standardizing the endpoint of evaluation while the conditions of preparation remain unequal and real per-student provincial operating funding is historically constrained. Mathematics performance has recently improved, but approximately one-third of tracked English-language cohorts never met the provincial standard across successive assessment points. Under these conditions, mandatory examinations may improve consistency of grading, but they may also convert unequal preparation capacity into formally comparable scores and move remediation burdens into households.

The field contribution is not a prediction of inevitable failure. It is a measurable conditional law: when policy enforcement grows faster than institutional absorption capacity, residual policy load propagates downstream. Where downstream capacity is unequal, universal policy produces unequal exposure to consequences. Ontario now provides a live test.
