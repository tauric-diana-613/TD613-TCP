EMPIRICAL CASE STUDY

After the Canal Reopened

Measuring propagation, congestion, and recovery lag from the 2021 Suez blockage

Systems Propagation Research

Case Study 02  |  August 2026

Central finding

The blockage ended when the Ever Given was refloated. The disruption did not. Official evidence indicates that more than 350 vessels were still queued when the canal reopened and that the queue required roughly another week to return to usual levels. The observable disturbance therefore lasted about thirteen days—more than twice the duration of the initiating obstruction—before considering later cargo bunching at ports.

This paper treats that recovery tail as the empirical object. It does not infer that the Suez event caused global inflation. It tests the narrower and defensible proposition that a bounded infrastructure shock can persist, migrate, and change form after its initiating condition disappears.

Abstract

A common event-study mistake is to stop observing when an initiating failure ends. Systems propagation requires the opposite: the observation window must remain open long enough to detect delayed transmission and recovery. The grounding of the Ever Given offers a bounded case. The Suez Canal was obstructed from 23 to 29 March 2021. World Bank and United Nations materials report a queue exceeding 350 vessels at reopening, approximately one additional week before queues returned to usual levels, and later cargo surges at downstream ports. Using only publicly documented quantities, this paper reconstructs a minimum propagation profile. A six-day obstruction generated an estimated seven-day canal recovery tail, yielding a recovery-to-shock ratio of 1.17 and a minimum total disturbance window of thirteen days. The result confirms propagation in operational state: obstruction became queue, then synchronized arrivals and congestion. It does not, by itself, identify effects on aggregate prices or inflation. The case supports three methodological rules: measure state transitions rather than correlations alone; continue observation after apparent recovery; and grade each downstream claim by identifiability.

1. Research question

Can a short, localized infrastructure interruption produce a disturbance that persists after the original interruption is removed? More specifically: did the Suez system return to its prior operating state when the Ever Given was refloated, or did accumulated work continue to propagate through the maritime network?

The testable proposition is:

H1 — If disruption propagates through a capacity-constrained network, measurable congestion will persist after the initiating obstruction ends, and the disturbance will reappear downstream as delayed or bunched arrivals.

The null proposition is that reopening ends the relevant disturbance quickly: queues dissipate without a material recovery tail and downstream operating states do not depart detectably from baseline because of the event.

2. Why this case is analytically useful

The event supplies an unusually clear origin, a physically legible mechanism, and a known reopening date. The canal is a constrained corridor rather than an abstract market. Vessels cannot occupy the same transit capacity simultaneously; when service stops, work accumulates. When service resumes, the stored work must be processed. This makes the case well suited to distinguish an initiating event from the disturbance it produces.

Bounded onset: the obstruction began on 23 March 2021.

Bounded release: the vessel was refloated and the canal reopened on 29 March 2021.

Observable stock: waiting vessels formed a queue on both sides of the canal.

Observable recovery: official sources describe the time required to reduce the queue to usual levels.

Observable downstream mechanism: delayed vessels arrived at ports in clusters, creating cargo surges where spare capacity was limited.

3. Evidence and measurement window

The reconstruction uses institutional reports from the World Bank and UNCTAD. These sources are not a vessel-level causal dataset, but they contain sufficient event quantities to test persistence. The measurement window is divided into three states: pre-event baseline, obstruction, and post-reopening recovery. The analysis deliberately extends beyond 29 March because reopening is a change in input condition, not proof of system recovery.

4. Reconstruction of the disturbance

4.1 Initiation and exposure

UNCTAD dates the blockage from 23 to 29 March 2021 and identifies the Ever Given as a 20,150-TEU container ship. The World Bank estimated that approximately 12 percent of world merchandise trade passed through the canal at the time. Its March 2021 Trade Watch also estimated that each day of interruption added about fifteen container ships to the queue at the canal ends, representing stalled capacity equivalent to roughly 0.5 percent of global shipping capacity per day. These figures establish that the initiating event intersected a globally consequential corridor, but exposure is not itself proof of downstream outcome.

4.2 Queue as stored disruption

The queue is the crucial intermediate state. Work that would ordinarily be processed continuously was not destroyed; it accumulated spatially and temporally. A later World Bank Port Reform Toolkit reports that more than 350 ships were queuing when the waterway reopened. UNCTAD’s Port Said case study reports 367 vessels blocked by the end of the rescue phase. The small difference is immaterial to the proposition being tested: hundreds of vessels remained displaced from schedule at the instant the obstruction ended.

4.3 Recovery tail

The World Bank reports that it took approximately one week after reopening to reduce queues to usual levels. Define the obstruction duration as S = 6 days and the canal recovery tail as R = 7 days. Then:

Recovery-to-shock ratio:  R/S = 7/6 = 1.17

Minimum disturbance window:  D = S + R = 13 days

On this reconstruction, the post-release recovery tail was slightly longer than the obstruction itself, and the minimum operational disturbance lasted about 2.17 times the initiating event. “Minimum” matters: the estimate stops when the canal queue normalized and therefore excludes any later recovery at destination ports, warehouses, factories, or retailers.

4.4 Downstream transformation

The World Bank further reports that delayed ships later arrived at ports in large numbers and at nearly the same time, producing surges of backlogged cargo and congestion, especially where ports lacked spare capacity. This is not merely persistence of the same variable. The observable form changes: a canal obstruction becomes a vessel queue; the queue becomes synchronized arrivals; synchronized arrivals become port congestion. The event therefore meets a stronger propagation criterion than simple temporal association: an upstream state is converted into a downstream state through a specified capacity mechanism.

6. What is confirmed—and what is not

6.1 Confirmed at the operational level

Persistence: disruption remained measurable after the initiating obstruction ended.

Accumulation: the interrupted flow was stored as a large queue rather than disappearing.

Transformation: the disturbance changed observable form across system locations.

Capacity dependence: congestion was more consequential where downstream ports had limited spare capacity.

Non-equivalence of reopening and recovery: restored access did not restore normal operation immediately.

6.2 Supported but not fully identified

The case supports downstream port congestion and delivery delay as mechanisms, because official reports describe bunched arrivals and cargo surges. A stronger estimate—such as the causal increase in dwell time at each affected port—would require vessel-level AIS tracks, scheduled and actual arrival times, terminal throughput, and a counterfactual set of unaffected routes or ports.

6.3 Not identified by this design

The reconstruction does not establish that the blockage caused a specific movement in global inflation, oil prices, food prices, or consumer prices. March 2021 maritime markets were already affected by pandemic-era demand shifts, container imbalances, port restrictions, production interruptions, and elevated freight rates. Price coincidence during this period cannot isolate the Suez contribution. A price paper would require product-route exposure, inventory buffers, contract timing, and an explicit counterfactual design.

7. The propagation model

The case suggests a compact model in which propagation is the transmission of an unresolved state through connected, capacity-constrained nodes. Let B(t) be backlog, A(t) arrivals, and C(t) service capacity. During the obstruction, C(t) approaches zero and backlog rises according to:

ΔB(t) = A(t) − C(t)

After reopening, capacity becomes positive but backlog remains. Recovery occurs only while service exceeds new arrivals:

Recovery condition:  C(t) > A(t), sustained until B(t) returns to baseline

Downstream propagation occurs because clearing the upstream queue compresses previously separated arrivals into a narrower time interval. If downstream capacity does not expand proportionally, the cleared upstream backlog becomes a downstream queue. The failure is therefore not simply transmitted as an identical object; it is translated by the receiving node’s capacity, timing, and buffers.

8. A stronger empirical test

A publishable extension can be executed with vessel tracking and port-performance data. The design should pre-register an event window and keep price outcomes secondary.

Construct exposed voyages: identify vessels scheduled through Suez during 23–29 March 2021 and record actual canal transit and destination arrival times.

Construct comparison voyages: use similar Asia–Europe voyages outside the blockage window, plus routes not dependent on Suez, matched by vessel class, origin, destination, and seasonal week.

Measure transmission delay: for vessel i, TDᵢ = actual arrivalᵢ − scheduled or matched-counterfactual arrivalᵢ.

Measure port congestion: estimate excess anchorage time, berth waiting time, port calls, and container dwell time in destination ports by event time.

Measure recovery: determine the first date each outcome returns within its pre-event confidence band and remains there for a defined stability period.
