9. Falsification and rival explanations

A propagation claim should be falsifiable. The case would be weakened if exposed vessels did not experience larger delays than matched alternatives; if downstream congestion preceded the blockage rather than followed exposed arrivals; if non-Suez routes showed the same event-time pattern; or if estimated effects vanished after controlling for pandemic port restrictions and pre-existing congestion trends.

The principal rival explanation is that global shipping was already congested and the Suez event merely coincided with continuing deterioration. That rival cannot explain the existence of the canal queue or the week-long clearing period, but it may explain part of the downstream congestion. For this reason the current case confirms canal-level persistence and mechanism, while leaving the magnitude of downstream causal effects for vessel-level estimation.

10. Contribution to Systems Propagation Research

This case adds four things beyond the familiar observation that supply chains are interconnected.

A stopping rule: do not terminate measurement when the trigger disappears; terminate when the affected state returns to baseline and remains stable.

A state-transition criterion: propagation is stronger evidence when obstruction, queue, arrival bunching, and port congestion occur in the predicted order.

A recovery metric: the recovery-to-shock ratio and disturbance multiplier make persistence comparable across domains.

An evidentiary boundary: operational propagation can be demonstrated without claiming every distal economic outcome was caused by the event.

The broader implication is precise: restoration of infrastructure is not equivalent to restoration of the system. Systems retain history in queues, schedules, inventories, and displaced assets. That stored history is the material through which a past event continues to act.

11. Conclusion

The 2021 Suez blockage passes a minimum empirical test of propagation. A six-day obstruction left a queue of more than 350 vessels and required about another week to restore usual canal queues. The minimum operational disturbance therefore lasted roughly thirteen days, with a recovery tail slightly longer than the initiating shock. Official reports also describe a subsequent transformation into bunched port arrivals and cargo congestion. The result is not that one ship “caused inflation.” The result is narrower, measurable, and more useful: in a capacity-constrained network, a resolved trigger can survive as accumulated state, migrate through connected nodes, and delay recovery beyond the event itself.
