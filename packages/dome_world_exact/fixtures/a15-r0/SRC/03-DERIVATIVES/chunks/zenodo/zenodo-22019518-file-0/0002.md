Let M_t represent the measured system state at time t and M_0 a justified baseline. A simple drift magnitude is D_t = ||M_t − M_0||. For multidimensional systems, variables must be standardized and weighted before aggregation. Direction matters: a large distance is not automatically harmful drift unless the direction is linked to degraded correspondence or capacity.

6.2 Visibility lag

VL = T_recognition − T_onset. Recognition must be defined behaviorally—for example, policy change, incident declaration, budget allocation, model retirement, or formal diagnosis—not inferred from rhetoric alone.

6.3 Load and capacity

L_t = Demand_t / Capacity_t. Values above a calibrated resilience boundary indicate overload risk. Capacity includes staff, time, substitution options, information quality, and infrastructure—not merely nominal funding.

6.4 Propagation

A general transfer model is φ_ij = ΔS_j(t+k) / ΔS_i(t), conditional on network connection, temporal ordering, and controls for common shocks. In empirical work, φ may be estimated with event studies, distributed-lag models, network diffusion models, sparse vector autoregression, hazard models, or process tracing.

6.5 Stability and collapse probability

A provisional stability function is Stability_t = w_EE_t + w_II_t + w_DD_t − w_φφ_t − w_HH_t, where E represents epistemic correspondence, I infrastructure capacity, D diagnostic visibility, φ propagation pressure, and H uncompensated harm or compensation load. A probabilistic model may use P(Collapse_t=1)=σ(α+β_1D_t+β_2VL_t+β_3L_t+β_4φ_t+β_5H_t). Coefficients are empirical parameters, not canonical numbers.

7. The SR Research Method

An SR study proceeds through a disciplined sequence. The method is designed to prevent a researcher from labeling any undesirable trend as drift or any institutional problem as collapse.

Define the system boundary, observation window, unit of analysis, and outcome.

Identify the pressure and document when it plausibly began.

Specify the institutional reflex: categories, policy, metrics, models, workflows, and infrastructure.

Measure correspondence between conditions and reflex across time.

Separate common shock, direct causation, mediation, selection, and propagation.

Map dependencies, substitution capacity, and visibility points.

Measure human compensation and distributional exposure.

Test thresholds, alternative explanations, negative cases, and temporal ordering.

State the falsification condition before interpreting results.

Report uncertainty, domain limits, and whether the result transfers beyond the case.

8. Causal Separation

SR requires causal restraint. Four relationships must not be conflated:

Relationship

Meaning

Minimum evidence

Association

Variables vary together.

Magnitude, uncertainty, robustness, and plausible confounders.

Direct causation

A change in X produces a change in Y.

Identification strategy, temporal order, counterfactual logic.

Mediation

X changes M, which changes Y.

Measured mediator and decomposed pathway.

Propagation

A disturbance travels through connected nodes and may transform.

Network/path evidence, lags, transfer mechanism, competing routes.

This separation is central to SR. The field is weakened whenever propagation is inferred only from simultaneous trends, drift is inferred only from dissatisfaction, or collapse is inferred only from a poor outcome.

9. Falsifiability

SR is falsifiable at the level of mechanisms, models, and cross-domain claims. A study must specify which proposition is under test.

No measurable divergence: the institutional representation tracks the material condition within the predicted tolerance.

No temporal lag: recognition and response reliably precede or coincide with the alleged drift.

No pathway: the proposed propagation route is absent, temporally reversed, or explained by a common shock.

High substitution capacity: institutions replace failing infrastructure without the predicted agency loss.

No human-buffer mechanism: measured compensation does not rise as system capacity deteriorates.

No cross-domain invariance: the mechanism cannot be operationalized consistently beyond the originating case.

Intervention failure in the opposite direction: restoring visibility, substitution, or capacity does not reduce the predicted outcome.

9.1 What would not falsify all of SR

Failure of one index, threshold, case, or domain application does not falsify the entire metatheory. It falsifies the specified measurement model or claimed transfer. Conversely, one successful case does not validate the entire field. SR advances through cumulative, preregistered, independently replicated tests.

11. Applications

11.1 Artificial intelligence

SR studies AI as an institutional and infrastructural system, not only as a model. It asks what an AI system inherits, which classifications it amplifies, whose behavior it conditions, what capacities are offloaded, and how deployment changes visibility and agency. Bias is therefore examined across data, labels, geography, objectives, interfaces, procurement, and institutional use.

11.2 Healthcare

In healthcare, SR separates nominal service from effective access. It examines visibility lag, workload, patient transfer, digital dependency, workaround labor, and the difference between a functioning facility and a functioning care pathway. Outages and model errors become propagation events when they cross clinical, administrative, and human-compensation layers.

11.3 Education and childhood

In education, SR examines contradictions among learning expectations, technology deployment, attention environments, household time, unequal support, testing policy, and resource cuts. It does not presume that technology alone causes declining performance. It models compound exposure and tests whether instructional policy transfers system costs to children, teachers, and families.

11.4 Government and political economy

SR distinguishes formal sovereignty from effective agency. A state may retain legal authority while depending on private cloud, telecommunications, financial, logistics, data, and platform infrastructures. The relevant question is whether it can observe, regulate, substitute, and operate without the dependency it formally governs.

11.5 Discrimination and administrative harm

Discrimination is measured not only as unequal model error but as inherited exposure. Historical housing patterns, school boundaries, policing intensity, credit data, healthcare access, and neighborhood infrastructure can enter contemporary classification systems. SR tests whether algorithms merely reproduce these patterns or amplify them through scale, speed, opacity, feedback, and institutional reliance.

11.6 Cascades in prices and infrastructure

Electricity, telecommunications, banking, transport, hospitals, and food systems form coupled networks. SR propagation studies trace how failures or price shocks change form across those networks. The goal is to identify routes, lags, buffers, and thresholds rather than describe every co-occurring increase as one causal event.

12. Predictions

SR produces conditional predictions. They are strongest when the pressure, dependency, visibility, and substitution terms are specified.

Where visibility lag rises while dependency deepens, corrective action will occur later and require more disruptive intervention.

Where substitution capacity falls, formal authority will diverge from effective agency.

Where human compensation masks operational decline, measured institutional stability will overestimate true capacity.

Where algorithmic systems inherit historically structured data and are deployed at scale, disparities will persist or amplify unless the deployment changes the generating environment.

Where institutions regulate visible downstream behavior while preserving upstream incentive and infrastructure conditions, the regulated outcome will reappear through another pathway.

Where multiple critical sectors share hyperscale infrastructure, local failures will have increasingly cross-domain consequences.

Where children encounter high-frequency algorithmic attention environments while schools retain fragmented schedules and performance pressure, attention and learning burdens will compound rather than remain separable.

Where scarcity increases and public capacity contracts, unpaid household labor will become a larger hidden component of institutional functioning.

13. Boundaries and Non-Claims

SR does not claim that every negative trend is collapse.

SR does not claim that technology is a singular cause of cognitive, educational, or social change.

SR does not claim that institutions possess unified intent.

SR does not treat correlation as causation or temporal sequence as proof of propagation.

SR does not assume that reorganization is necessarily worse; it may restore correspondence and capacity.

SR does not assign universal numerical thresholds before domain calibration.

SR does not replace domain expertise, qualitative interpretation, or causal identification.

These boundaries are not concessions external to the field. They are part of SR’s scientific identity. A metatheory becomes useful by constraining interpretation, not by absorbing every outcome into itself.
