or

These

to

This

establishes

Structural

Dynamics

(SRD)

as

empirical

field

within

the

SignalRupture

architecture

for

measuring,

predicting,

and

mitigating

that

SRD

defines

a

Structural

Restoration

Requirement

(SRR),

effective

Restoration

Capacity

(RC),

Restoration

Deficit

(RD

=

SRR

−

RC),

Cumulative

Restoration

Deficit

(CRD),

Restoration

Efficiency

(RE),

The

is

North

planning,

Canadian

U.S.

The

Healthcare

electricity

exhibits

restoration

This

The

Its

Field existence ≠ theory validation ≠ external recognition 1. Introduction Modern systems are unusually good at continuing. Hospitals add overtime and purchased staffing.

Electricity

planners

procure

future

capacity

before

forecast

loads

arrive.

Housing

systems

increase

starts

without

necessarily

restoring

affordability.

Governments

maintain

decades-old

software

while

simultaneously

attempting

modernization.

In

each

case,

visible

operation

can

persist

even

when

the

underlying

restoration

problem

remains

unresolved. This creates a measurement problem. A rise in spending can be mistaken for recovery. A rise

in

output

can

be

mistaken

for

recovery.

The

absence

of

catastrophic

failure

can

be

mistaken

for

recovery.

SRD

begins

from

the

proposition

that

these

are

different

variables. Intervention ≠ Output ≠ Continuity ≠ Structural Restoration

Structural restoration is defined here as sustained reduction of a specified structural deficit

together

with

rebuilding

of

durable

capacity

or

slack.

The

term

'specified'

is

essential.

Restoration

cannot

be

measured

without

a

target

state.

Housing

restoration

to

2019

affordability,

for

example,

is

a

different

claim

from

maximizing

housing

starts.

Functional

recovery

of

an

electricity

lifeline

after

an

earthquake

is

different

from

maintaining

adequacy

under

rapidly

rising

demand. The central question of SRD is therefore not whether a system is functioning. It is whether

the

system

is

rebuilding

durable

functional

capacity

faster

than

its

restoration

requirement

is

accumulating. RD_t = SRR_t − RC_t where SRR_t denotes the Structural Restoration Requirement at time t and RC_t denotes effective,

durable

Restoration

Capacity

available

to

meet

that

requirement. ADVERSARIAL CHALLENGE — Is this merely resilience under a new name?

Partly

adjacent,

yes.

Resilience

engineering

and

functional-recovery

research

already

study

continuity,

recovery

times,

system

capacity,

organizational

action,

and

lifeline

restoration.

NIST

explicitly

develops

functional-recovery

objectives

for

buildings

and

lifelines

and

integrates

assets

with

organizational

actions.

SRD

cannot

defensibly

claim

that

restoration

itself

is

novel.

Its

proposed

contribution

is

narrower:

persistent

restoration

deficits

in

continuously

operating

systems

where

no

clean

post-disaster

recovery

phase

exists,

combined

with

explicit

separation

of

intervention,

output,

compensatory

continuity,

durable

capacity,

accumulated

deficit,

and

restoration

timing.

If

that

distinction

does

not

generate

additional

explanatory

or

decision

SRD

should

be

absorbed

into

existing

resilience

research

rather

as

a

separate

field.

2. Relationship to Existing Research Functional-recovery research provides the closest established analogue. NIST describes functional

recovery

as

rapid

return

of

buildings

and

infrastructure

systems

to

function

or

service

after

and

recent

work

defines

when

are

and

whether

system

and

action

can meet those recovery times (Davis et al., 2024; NIST, n.d.). This literature is substantive

and

directly

relevant. SRD differs primarily in temporal framing. Its primary object is not necessarily a discrete hazard

followed

by

restoration.

It

includes

systems

experiencing

persistent

demand

growth,

accumulated

backlog,

aging

infrastructure,

workforce

depletion,

maintenance

drag,

or

technological

while

continuously

operational.

In

such

systems,

there

may

be

no

'after'

in

recovery

begins. The proposed extension can be summarized as follows: Shock-Recovery Model: Shock → Loss → Recovery → Baseline SRD Model: Burden → Compensation → Partial Restoration → New Burden →

Accumulated

Deficit

This distinction is a hypothesis, not a declaration of disciplinary novelty. It must be demonstrated

empirically. ADVERSARIAL CHALLENGE — Could ordinary capacity planning already do

this?

In

yes.

Grid

planners

compare

and

resources;

compare

and

SRD

common

representation

improves

diagnosis,

or

The

3. Core Constructs 3.1 Structural Restoration Requirement SRR is the amount of durable change required to reach an explicitly defined restoration target.

It

is

not

'need'

in

the

abstract.

The

target

must

be

stated

and

contestable. SRR_t = f(Target_t, CurrentState_t, Demand_t, Backlog_t, ReplacementNeed_t,

Constraints_t)

Because targets differ across domains, SRR is not inherently comparable in raw units. Housing

may

use

annual

units

required

to

approach

a

defined

affordability

condition.

Electricity

may

use

dependable

capacity,

transmission,

flexibility

and

reserve

requirements.

Healthcare

may

require

a

vector

of

access,

workforce,

throughput

and

backlog

variables.

3.2 Effective Restoration Capacity RC is the durable capacity that can actually reduce SRR. Gross resources are insufficient because

some

capacity

is

consumed

by

maintenance,

coordination,

emergency

compensation,

or

other

obligations. RC_eff = RC_gross − M_legacy − C_coord − C_extraordinary This equation is conceptual unless each subtraction term is operationally measured. It should

not

be

numerically

populated

by

assumption.

3.3 Restoration Deficit and Cumulative Restoration Deficit RD_t = SRR_t − RC_eff,t CRD(T) = ∫[t0,T] max(RD_t, 0) dt The max operator is used in the cumulative-deficit form to avoid automatically treating restoration

reserve

in

one

period

as

cancelling

historical

deficit

unless

the

reserve

is

actually

deployed

to

reduce

that

deficit.

A

second

may

explicitly

model

deficit

3.4 Restoration Efficiency RE_t = Δ( Deficit Reduction)_t / Δ( Intervention Effort)_t RE is intentionally outcome-based. It asks how much structural deficit is removed per unit

of

intervention.

It

is

not

a

universal

scalar

until

intervention

effort

and

deficit

are

3.5 Restoration Time and Buffer Endurance CW_t = τ_ B,t − τ_ R,t τ_ R is the estimated time required for a selected intervention to mature; τ_ B is the estimated

endurance

of

the

buffer

currently

preventing

functional

discontinuity.

CW

is

therefore a scenario-dependent temporal margin, not a countdown clock. A negative value

indicates

that,

stated

the

intervention

matures

after

the

buffer

is

Overtime

The
