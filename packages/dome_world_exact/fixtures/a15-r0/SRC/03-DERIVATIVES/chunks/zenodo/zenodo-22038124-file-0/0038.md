32.8C Professionalization, Measurement, and the Medical Gaze

Modern medicine did not evolve only by discovering new treatments. It also developed

professions,

classifications,

laboratories,

registries,

licensing

systems, inspection regimes, and standardized measurements. These infrastructures

changed

what

The

Medical

and

Visibility has consequences. Once a pattern is counted, mapped, compared, and

an

office

can

governable.

Before

that

transition,

suffering

may

remain

dispersed

across

and

experienced

by

individuals

but

weakly

represented

institutionally.

This

is

precisely

the

condition

SR

calls

visibility

lag:

the

material

process

precedes

the

system

capable

of

recognizing

it.

Contemporary medicine has far more data than its nineteenth-century predecessors,

but

abundance

does

not

eliminate

blindness.

Electronic

records

can

capture

diagnoses

while

omitting

housing

instability;

occupational

exposures

may

be

inconsistently

food

insecurity

can

relevant

yet

absent

from

routine

may

Infrastructural

Medicine therefore treats data architecture itself as part of medical infrastructure.

32.8D The Hospital as an Absorber of External Pressure The modern hospital is often judged by what occurs within its walls: staffing, beds,

diagnostics,

surgery,

infection

control,

discharge,

mortality,

and

patient

Infrastructural

Medicine

Hospitals

When

When

When

When

The

system

This absorption function can make healthcare appear to be the location of failure

Conversely,

can

generate

processes,

errors,

infections,

or

The

therefore refuses a one-way story. Healthcare is both recipient and producer of

infrastructural

The analytical advantage of this framing is that capacity problems can be decomposed.

Instead

as

delayed

demographic

discharge

administrative

constraints.

Pressure

becomes

traceable

merely

visible.

32.8E Pharmaceuticals and the Expansion of Downstream Repair

The pharmaceutical era dramatically expanded medicine’s ability to modify biological

Antibiotics,

Researchers

32.8F Chronic Disease and the Problem of Distributed Causation

This is where simplistic structural language becomes especially dangerous. If every

The

and

Genetic

This redistribution can be understood as recovery infrastructure. A person recovering

Time

Contemporary medicine now depends on infrastructures that earlier hospitals did

The historical genealogy consequently does not end with the hospital. Medicine

The
