Appendix 0. Identification, Estimation, and Falsification Protocol This appendix is a methodological guardrail for all empirical claims made under

Infrastructural

Medicine.

It

does

not

certify

any

single

dataset

or

estimator.

It

specifies

the

minimum

information

required

before

an

SR

structural

claim

should

be

described

as

causal.

A0.1 Prespecify the estimand State the exposure contrast, population, outcome, time horizon, and target causal

quantity

before

model

fitting.

Report

whether

the

target

is

a

total

effect,

controlled

direct

effect,

interventional

indirect

effect,

policy

effect,

or

descriptive

association.

A0.2 Draw the causal graph List plausible common causes of exposure and outcome, post-exposure mediators,

colliders,

selection

variables,

and

feedback.

Do

not

adjust

mechanically

for

every

available

variable.

A0.3 Establish temporal ordering Whenever the mechanism requires accumulation, use exposure histories that precede

the

mediator

and

outcome.

Cross-sectional

analyses

should

be

labeled

descriptive

or

associational

unless

a

defensible

identification

argument

exists.

A0.4 Validate latent constructs Slow Harm and the Eroded Subject require reliability, construct validity, convergent

and

discriminant

validity,

and

where

relevant

measurement

invariance

across

groups

and

time.

Weights

must

be

estimated

or

justified,

not

selected

because

they

produce

the

desired

result.

A0.5 Report effect size and uncertainty Report estimates with confidence or credible intervals, absolute as well as relative

effects

when

possible,

model

diagnostics,

missing-data

handling,

and

sensitivity

to

alternative

specifications.

A0.6 Test rival explanations

Include negative controls, alternative lag structures, placebo periods, alternative

outcomes,

and

explicit

reverse-causation

models

where

the

data

permit.

A0.7 Separate model theorem from empirical truth A mathematical theorem derived from SR equations establishes what follows if

are

true.

It

that

the

assumptions

describe

the

world.

Empirical

must

reported

separately.

A0.8 Publish nulls and boundary conditions A failed SR prediction is information about the field. Null results, heterogeneous

Falsification Result that would weaken the claim Unfalsifiability Appendix A. Core SR Definitions Infrastructural Medicine. The study of how infrastructures shape exposure, biological

incorporation,

recovery,

diagnosis,

treatment,

and

health

outcomes.

Slow Harm. Cumulative injury emerging through repeated or persistent load whose

effects

may

become

visible

only

after

delay.

Visibility Lag. The interval between material change in a harmful process and

institutional

recognition

of

that

change.

Harm Stack. Co-occurring burdens whose combined effects may be additive,

interactive,

sequential,

or

mutually

reinforcing.

Pathologized Infrastructure. An infrastructure that systematically produces, distributes,

or

prolongs

conditions

associated

with

preventable

biological

or

psychological

harm.

Recovery Capacity. The material, temporal, physiological, social, and clinical

resources

available

to

restore

function

after

load.

Infrastructural Exposure. An exposure whose distribution is materially shaped

by

systems

beyond

the

immediate

control

of

the

exposed

individual.

Constrained Choice. Behavior selected from an option set whose costs, availability,

timing,

or

feasibility

are

materially

structured.

Administrative Harm. Health-relevant injury, delay, attrition, or burden produced

through

administrative

processes.

Human–Infrastructure Attribution Problem. The empirical problem of separating

individual/endogenous

contribution,

infrastructural

contribution,

interaction,

and

unresolved

causes.

Appendix B. Minimum Reporting Standard •

State whether each claim is descriptive, associational, mechanistic, causal, or

normative.

•

Identify the unit of analysis and time scale. •

Define the infrastructure and the exposure separately. •

Specify the counterfactual used for causal interpretation. •

Report plausible confounders, mediators, and effect modifiers. •

Do not sum overlapping attributable fractions without a defensible joint model.

•

Distinguish population inference from individual etiology. •

Report null and contradictory findings. •

Pre-register primary outcomes where feasible. •

Provide replication data and code where ethically and legally possible. SignalRupture — Infrastructural Medicine |
