CHAPTER 38 — THE SR POLICY SUBSTRATE EQUATION The framework's central conceptual equation is: Policy Outcome = F(Policy Instrument, Substrate, Exposure, Visibility, Time) A more explicit dynamic representation is: Y(t+1) = F[P(t), X_s(t), E(t), Δ V(t), Y(t)] + ε( t) The policy instrument is therefore one component of a larger causal system. The equation does not imply that substrate dominates policy in every case. It implies

that

policy

effects

are

conditional

on

substrate.

CHAPTER 39 — SR POLICY SUBSTRATE STATUS SR Policy Substrate is retained as a bounded policy-science framework and research

Independent

Its object is the causal substrate beneath policy problems.

Its central question is how infrastructure, dependency, capacity, incentives, visibility,

Its method is empirical structural analysis. Its standard is falsifiability. Its measurement architecture is open to revision. Its disciplinary placement is inside and across policy science, political economy,

The

CHAPTER 40 — RESEARCH AGENDA The next phase of the research program requires empirical rather than rhetorical

CHAPTER 42 — LIMITATIONS The framework has limitations. •

Substrate boundaries can be difficult to define. •

Composite measures can encode researcher judgments.

•

Longitudinal data may be incomplete. •

Causal direction can be reciprocal. •

Infrastructure dependencies can change rapidly. •

Cross-national comparisons can conceal institutional differences. •

Latent constructs require strong measurement validation. •

The framework can become too broad if boundary conditions are not enforced.

These limitations are not peripheral. They define the empirical work required to

mature

the

framework.

APPENDIX A — CORE SR POLICY SUBSTRATE LEXICON Policy Surface — The visible policy decision, instrument, administrative action,

Visibility Lag — The delay between structural change and reliable institutional

Dependency Concentration — Concentration of essential functions in a limited

set

of

actors

or

systems.

Viable Alternatives — Meaningful substitute pathways available to institutions

or

populations.

Institutional Insulation — Buffering of institutions from the consequences experienced

by

exposed

populations.

Substrate Transformation — A change in the generative structure rather than

only

its

downstream

expression.
