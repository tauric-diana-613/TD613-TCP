    "description": "<p>SR Policy Substrate is an empirical policy-science framework for analyzing the causal structures that exist beneath visible policy problems. Rather than treating a policy instrument, administrative response, or measured outcome as the complete unit of analysis, the framework examines the infrastructures, dependencies, institutional capacities, incentives, information conditions, exposure distributions, and temporal processes that determine whether policy can actually alter the conditions generating a problem. Its central distinction is between the policy surface&mdash;the observable rule, program, decision, or outcome&mdash;and the policy substrate&mdash;the underlying causal system through which that policy operates.<span class=\"Apple-converted-space\">&nbsp;</span></p>\n<p>THE SR POLICY SUBSTRATE FIELD</p>\n<p>The framework introduces tools such as the Causal Depth Test, Policy Surface&ndash;Substrate Gap, dependency mapping, Visibility Lag, Substrate Persistence, and distinctions among surface, buffer, transfer, control, and transformative interventions. Its empirical method compares conventional policy models against substrate-augmented models to determine whether infrastructure, capacity, dependency, visibility, and exposure variables provide additional explanatory or predictive value.<span class=\"Apple-converted-space\">&nbsp;</span></p>\n<p>Following comparison with existing policy-capacity, implementation, institutional, political-economy, infrastructure, and policy-feedback research, SR Policy Substrate is best classified as a specialized SR policy-science framework and research program rather than an autonomous scientific field. Its contribution lies not in claiming that underlying structural conditions were previously unknown, but in organizing them into a reproducible causal-depth architecture that asks a specific question:</p>\n<p>Did the policy change the outcome, or did it change the structure that keeps producing the outcome?</p>\n<p>Its scientific value therefore depends on empirical model comparison, longitudinal testing, causal identification, transparent measurement, falsification, and reporting of null results rather than disciplinary declaration alone. The original architecture itself already requires that substrate variables improve explanation or prediction beyond policy-surface models for its stronger claims to survive.<span class=\"Apple-converted-space\">&nbsp;</span></p>\n<p>&nbsp;</p>",
