    "description": "<h2><strong>Collapse Society: Structural Inversion Scene Model&nbsp;</strong></h2>\n<p>The Structural Inversion Scene is the central analytic object of Collapse Society. It is the configuration in which a formally continuous social surface coexists with a materially different underlying architecture of requirement, durable capacity, dependency, burden transfer, extraordinary compensation, exposure, epistemic visibility, and outcome. A scene begins when a surface classification appears intact&mdash;employment, institutional attachment, service continuity, occupancy, legal process&mdash;yet an independently measured contradiction demonstrates that the conditions required to reproduce stable participation are not being met under the same parameters implied by the surface. In these moments, <strong>it is a field&nbsp;</strong>for forensic reconstruction: the visible description of social functioning and the generating architecture beneath it diverge enough to allow the full SSR protocol to operate. Structural Scene Reconstruction (SSR) proceeds through a fixed sequence&mdash;Surface &rarr; Contradiction &rarr; Requirement &rarr; Durable Capacity &rarr; Dependency &rarr; Viable Alternatives &rarr; Burden Transfer &rarr; Extraordinary Compensation &rarr; Exposure &rarr; Epistemic Visibility &rarr; Outcome &rarr; Recovery&mdash;while classifying each node as measured, derived, inferred, or missing. This evidentiary discipline prevents narrative inflation and ensures that reconstruction remains grounded in independently observed contradictions rather than interpretive overreach.</p>\n<p>This construct contributes to Collapse Society by providing the field&rsquo;s core forensic unit and its methodological backbone. It operationalizes the distance between formal continuity and effective reproduction, enabling researchers to identify when observable social functioning is sustained through hidden compensatory mechanisms rather than durable endogenous capacity. By requiring explicit measurement of requirement&ndash;capacity gaps, dependency structures, burden transfers, compensation pathways, and visibility conditions, the Structural Inversion Scene differentiates Collapse Society from poverty research, deprivation studies, cumulative disadvantage, administrative burden, social quality theory, and structural violence. It also embeds a restoration branch, allowing the field to classify recovery when durable capacity increases or requirements fall. As a unified analytic instrument, the Structural Inversion Scene enables cross‑domain reconstruction&mdash;integrating labour, housing, healthcare, credit, infrastructure, crime, epistemics, and psychological burden&mdash;while preserving the field&rsquo;s commitment to empirical constraint, counterexamples, and predictive testing. It is the mechanism through which Collapse Society identifies, evaluates, and compares structurally inverted social environments.</p>",
