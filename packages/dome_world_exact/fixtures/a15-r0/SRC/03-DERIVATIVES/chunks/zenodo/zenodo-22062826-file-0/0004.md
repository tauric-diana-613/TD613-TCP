Evidence domain O bserved support Field implication Global forced displacement UNHCR Mid-Year Trends 2025 reported 117.3 million forcibly displaced people at the end of June 2025, including an estimated 67.8 million internally displaced people. Establishes displacement as a major structural pathway; does not by itself validate downstream SR propagation. Religious discrimination among refugees Studies of Syrian and Afghan refugees find perceived religious discrimination associated with rejection, social exclusion, and loss of significance. Supports religion as post-displacement exposure marker. Religious identity in humanitarian response Research on Syrian refugees in Jordan shows that neutrality/universality assumptions can downplay religious identity and miss minority vulnerabilities. Supports institutional religious invisibility as a Structural Inversion Scene. Asylum religious credibility Kagan’s religious imposter problem shows risks when secular adjudicators assess sincerity through doctrinal knowledge or belief tests. Supports religion as administrative classification problem. Refugee employment Reviews identify language, credential recognition, family structure, gender, discrimination, and refugee status as barriers. Supports propagation from displacement into economic constraint. Foreign credential recognition Canadian evidence reports high overqualification among immigrants with foreign degrees and recognition barriers in regulated occupations. Supports capacity-recognition gap. Language courses Natural- and quasi-experimental evidence from Germany and Denmark shows that language-course access and program design can affect language and employment/integration outcomes, with a credible null for a rapidly scaled ad hoc program. Supports language opportunity structure and a bounded restoration branch; rejects a universal “any program works” claim. Privileged migration / whiteness Scholarship on privileged migration and whiteness as transnational capital links race, nationality, citizenship, passports, and knowledge to mobility advantage. Supports portability advantage. Residential schools Canadian records show federal-church operation of residential schools and TRC cultural genocide framing. Supports religion as institutional actor, not only coping resource. Gaza / ICJ / infrastructure ICJ provisional measures and UN humanitarian reports document rights-risk and urgent humanitarian infrastructure concerns while merits remain legally pending. Supports careful analysis of war as infrastructural harm without overstating legal determination. Religious coping meta-analyses Meta-analytic evidence separates positive and negative religious coping and links them differently to psychological adjustment. Supports heterogeneous coping branches; not an SR novelty claim.
