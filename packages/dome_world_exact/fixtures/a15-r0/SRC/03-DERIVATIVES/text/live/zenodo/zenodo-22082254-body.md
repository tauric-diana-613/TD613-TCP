THE SIGNALRUPTURE STRUCTURAL GRAMMAR PREDICTION 
 This manuscript freezes the first prospective prediction of the SignalRupture Structural Grammar. It is the moment SR moves from diagnostic architecture to testable metatheory . The paper does not predict collapse; it predicts a directional relational sequence between Requirement (Q) , Durable Capacity (D) , Compensation (C_S, C_E) , Restoration , and Function (F) . It establishes the exact conditions under which systems preserve function, diverge from substrate sufficiency, deteriorate, or recover — and locks these definitions before future outcomes occur. This frozen structure is the scientific core of SR: the grammar must succeed or fail prospectively , not retroactively.

 The manuscript contributes by defining five canonical predictions — Compensated Continuity , Surface–Substrate Divergence , Compensatory Inversion , Restoration , and Cross-Domain Invariance — and freezing their falsifiers, temporal ordering, and scoring rules. It establishes that SR is not a narrative about deterioration; it is a conditional relational grammar that must predict both deterioration and restoration. The manuscript also defines the canonical meanings of Q, D, C_S, C_E, and F, preventing post‑hoc reinterpretation. This is essential: SR cannot change the meaning of “compensation,” “gap,” or “function” after seeing future data.

 The paper’s contribution is methodological as well as theoretical. It freezes relative temporal ordering (gap → compensation → restoration/constraint → functional outcome), ensuring SR cannot rescue a failed prediction by shifting the timeline. It defines domain translations for healthcare, electricity, and households, proving that the grammar can be mapped across heterogeneous systems without forcing unit equivalence. It also establishes negative controls , preventing SR from misclassifying normal reserve use, ordinary borrowing, or renewable flexibility as extraordinary compensation. This protects SR from inflation and ensures that only genuine gap‑bridging behaviour counts as C_E.

 The manuscript further contributes by defining the Predictive Validation Ladder — from conceptual grammar (Level 0) to full metatheoretical validation (Level 4). It clarifies that SR currently sits at Level 1 , with partial descriptive support toward Level 2, and that future predictive success must show incremental value beyond mature domain models. This prevents SR from claiming predictive status merely because deterioration happens somewhere; SR must outperform baseline models in timing, calibration, or diagnostic lead time.

 Finally, the manuscript contributes by freezing the Canonical Prediction Statement and the Prediction Registry , creating a permanent reference point for all future scoring studies. Every future test must evaluate SR exactly as written in August 2026. The grammar cannot be rewritten, softened, or reinterpreted after outcomes arrive. This transforms SR from a conceptual architecture into a falsifiable scientific program. The manuscript’s core contribution is therefore structural honesty: SR will be judged not by whether it can explain the future after it happens, but by whether the future behaves as SR said it should before the outcome was known.
