# COLLAPSE DEMOGRAPHY: The Architecture of Demographic Erosion

## Zenodo description

COLLAPSE DEMOGRAPHY — The Architecture of Demographic Erosion 
 This manuscript establishes Collapse Demography as a constrained, empirically disciplined SR field. It does not replace conventional demography and does not claim novelty for fertility decline, aging, migration, population shrinkage, dependency ratios, cohort replacement, or demographic transition. Those belong to established demographic science. The manuscript instead identifies the narrow, scientifically defensible remainder: demographic reproduction and recovery capacity — the ability of a population to reproduce cohorts, sustain age‑structured social functions, absorb shocks, replace losses, maintain care and household infrastructures, and adapt through migration without simply displacing strain elsewhere.

 The manuscript contributes by defining the core SR object: Demographic Recovery Capacity . It formalizes a demographic state vector that includes classical demographic variables (fertility, mortality, migration, age structure) and interface variables (care capacity, labour composition, household formation, regional retention). This cross‑domain architecture allows Collapse Demography to study when demographic processes become coupled with care systems, labour markets, housing, regional viability, and institutional capacity in ways that reduce recovery capacity over time.

 The manuscript’s strongest contribution is distinguishing demographic change from demographic erosion . Low fertility, aging, or population decline are not collapse by definition. Collapse requires a regime : persistent adverse demographic configuration, coupling across demographic and social systems, impaired recovery capacity, and downstream functional consequences. The manuscript freezes explicit regime criteria — persistence, coupling, recovery impairment, downstream consequence, counterfactual comparison, and falsifiability — preventing SR from labeling ordinary demographic transition as collapse.

 The manuscript contributes by defining Migration Dependence and separating migration compensation from migration absorption capacity . A country may grow numerically through migration while failing institutionally to absorb newcomers through housing, labour matching, care systems, and retention. This distinction prevents Collapse Demography from collapsing into anti‑immigration narratives or simplistic replacement‑migration claims.

 The manuscript also contributes by formalizing Demographic Erosion as a multi‑component pressure index involving natural‑increase decline, age‑structure strain, care gaps, household‑formation gaps, and regional demographic imbalance. It clarifies that erosion is not collapse; collapse requires entering a persistent low‑recovery regime.

 The Canadian case provides the manuscript’s empirical anchor. Canada exhibits ultra‑low fertility (1.25 in 2024), structural aging (19.5% aged 65+ in 2025), weakened natural increase, and extreme migration dependence (97–99% of growth from migration). Yet Canada is not a demographic‑collapse case. It is a case of demographic dependence and demographic restructuring , where collapse becomes testable only at interfaces: migration absorption, care capacity, housing constraints, household formation, regional viability, and institutional adaptation. This prevents SR from overclaiming collapse where demographic transition and migration‑supported growth remain functional.

 The manuscript contributes by defining recursive demographic‑social loops — e.g., regional population loss → service withdrawal → lower retention → further loss; care burden → household time pressure → delayed fertility → smaller cohorts; housing constraint → delayed household formation → delayed fertility. These loops are hypotheses requiring longitudinal testing, not assumptions. Collapse Demography becomes falsifiable because these loops must be empirically demonstrated rather than asserted.

 The manuscript also contributes by freezing a measurement architecture grounded in established demographic indicators while adding cross‑domain interface measures. It prohibits premature composite collapse scores and requires longitudinal, quasi‑experimental, cohort, multistate, or microsimulation designs to test propagation and recovery capacity.

 Finally, the manuscript contributes by defining explicit falsification conditions : if recovery capacity cannot be operationalized; if cross‑domain demographic effects are fully explained by existing demographic and economic models; if recursive pathways do not persist; if migration absorption has no independent importance; if care, household, labour, and regional adaptation do not moderate demographic pressures; or if low‑recovery regimes cannot be distinguished from ordinary demographic transition, Collapse Demography must be rejected or narrowed.

 In unified form, the manuscript establishes Collapse Demography as a specialized SR field focused on demographic reproduction‑and‑recovery dynamics under coupled structural pressures. It is empirically subordinate to conventional demography, conceptually disciplined, cross‑domain in architecture, and falsifiable. It survives only by studying when demographic processes interact with care, labour, housing, and regional systems in ways that reduce recovery capacity , not by relabeling low fertility or aging as collapse.
