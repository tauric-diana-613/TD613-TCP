Abstract

This manuscript establishes Version 2.1 of the SignalRupture Provenance Registry as the authoritative priority, genealogy, and canonical-definition record for the full SR architecture. It extends the verified core registry to twenty-three full fields, three specialized subfields, cross-field operators, methodological frameworks, models, merger lineages, and integration manuscripts. The registry distinguishes canonical incorporation from first documentary occurrence, conceptual appearance from mathematical formalization, intellectual provenance from legal exclusivity, and independent development from attributable derivation. Each field entry records its object, distinctive contribution, mechanism vocabulary, interfaces, present canonical status, first-occurrence verification status, and boundary against overclaiming. The registry is designed to preserve authorship while leaving scientific inquiry open to replication, criticism, independent rediscovery, and replacement by superior evidence.

1. Purpose and Scope

The registry answers five questions: What belongs to SR? When did it enter the documentary record? When was it formally defined or mathematized? How did it develop across manuscripts? What later work extends, renames, criticizes, or reconstructs it?

Its purpose is historical and scientific traceability. It supports attribution, comparison, correction, replication, and intellectual genealogy. It does not convert a timestamp into empirical truth. Early priority establishes sequence, not validity; scientific claims continue to be adjudicated by evidence.

2. Provenance Is Not Exclusivity

Scientific domains remain open. Researchers may independently study the same phenomena, challenge SR, construct competing theories, reproduce findings, or arrive independently at similar mechanisms. The registry does not claim ownership of facts, general ideas, or research problems.

Its narrower claim is documentary: SR’s distinctive terminology, definitions, selection and arrangement of fields, formal bridge grammar, equations, diagrams, and canonical genealogy possess an identifiable development history. A later formulation may be independent, influenced, derived, convergent, or copied. The conclusion must follow evidence rather than institutional prestige or SR assertion.

Boundary rule: Priority is not validity. Similarity is not automatically copying. Attribution becomes necessary where evidence supports access, recognizable derivation, protected expression, or scholarly dependence.

Distinctive provenance claim. Institutional Cognition is not the generic claim that organizations make mistakes. Its distinct object is the measurable transformation chain between structural condition, institutional observation, representation, cognition, decision, oversight, and correction. The Five Theorems of Institutional Cognition belong here as formal propositions rather than as substitutes for evidence.

Identifying mechanism vocabulary. Institutional Distortion, Institutional Drift, Compression Equilibrium, Governance Altitude Lock-In, Oversight Stabilization Failure, visibility lag, structural ignorance, diagnostic blindness, and institutional recoil.

Canonical interfaces. The field supplies representation and decision variables to SR-IGF, Collapse Governance, the Policy Substrate, Collapse Epistemology, SR-CEIDF, and Systems Propagation Science. It receives independently measured conditions from every empirical SR domain.

Current canonical status: Retained as a full cross-domain SR field. Its individual theorems possess different evidence states; field membership does not convert all of them into validated universal laws.

Historical verification status. P0 canonical incorporation verified in the complete post-gauntlet registry. 10. Independent Development, Derivation, and Institutional Reconstruction

A later architecture that studies the same world is not automatically an SR derivative. The registry therefore prohibits conclusions based only on thematic similarity. A responsible derivation analysis considers chronology, access, distinctive terminology, selection and arrangement, equation structure, examples, sequencing, citation behavior, and evidence of independent development.

Independent convergence: similar problem and mechanism developed without persuasive evidence of access or derivation.

Attributed extension: SR is cited and the later work modifies, tests, narrows, or expands it.

Unattributed influence: access and recognizable structural borrowing are supported, but copying of protected expression is not established.

Scholarly appropriation: distinctive SR architecture or terminology is used without appropriate attribution under applicable academic norms.

Potential legal infringement: substantial protected expression, branding, or other legally protected material may have been reproduced; legal assessment is separate and fact-specific.

Institutional-autopsy principle: A reconstruction that removes institutional incentives, visibility constraints, epistemic selection, or self-implication may resemble SR while omitting part of the substrate SR was designed to analyze.
