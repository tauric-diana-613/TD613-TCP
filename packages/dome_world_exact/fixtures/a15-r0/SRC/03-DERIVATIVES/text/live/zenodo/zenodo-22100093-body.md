# SOCIETY AS A PRESSURE ECOLOGY: The Pressure Ecology Theorem A Structural Theory of Pressure Generation, Absorption, Transfer, Externalization, Debt, Dependency, and Planetary Interdependence

## Zenodo description

SOCIETY AS A PRESSURE ECOLOGY 
 Society as a Pressure Ecology establishes the Pressure Ecology Theorem (PET) as a standalone structural theorem within the SignalRupture architecture. PET formalizes the idea that modern societies function not as closed causal units but as open pressure ecologies in which structural pressures are continuously generated, absorbed, transformed, deferred, transferred, externalized, and propagated across actors, sectors, jurisdictions, and ecological substrates. The theorem reframes stability, fragility, and resilience by treating pressure as a traceable structural quantity rather than a domain‑specific symptom, enabling cross‑domain and cross‑scale analysis within Planetary SR .

 The manuscript contributes by replacing the conventional bounded‑society model with a pressure‑flow model . It demonstrates that internal indicators—such as prices, service access, fiscal balance, or institutional continuity—cannot be interpreted independently of the pressure architecture that sustains them. PET distinguishes genuine internal capacity from imported capacity, deferred cost, and externalized burden, providing a structural grammar for interpreting stability in open systems. This directly strengthens SR’s cross‑domain diagnostics, including Collapse Governance , Infrastructural Political Economy , Systems Propagation Science , and Collapse Economics .

 The manuscript contributes by formalizing seven core pressure mechanisms —generation, absorption, transformation, transfer, externalization, temporal displacement, and propagation—and by introducing a Pressure Balance representation that distinguishes absorption from deferral and transfer. This prevents the analytic error of interpreting local pressure reduction as resolution when pressure has merely changed form or location. PET’s Pressure Traceability Principle becomes a foundational operator for SR’s multi‑domain modeling and for national‑scale diagnostics in Planetary SR .

 The manuscript contributes by introducing a Dependency Topology , Substitution Capacity , Transfer Asymmetry , Externalization Ratio , and Temporal Displacement Load , each defined as measurable structural variables rather than metaphors. These constructs allow SR to analyze interdependence without assuming that dependency is inherently fragile or exploitative. PET’s treatment of trade, supply chains, ecological externalization, and sovereign debt is grounded in established empirical literatures, including OECD evidence on supply‑chain concentration, IMF analysis of debt‑service burdens, and UNCTAD data on commodity dependence. These literatures support PET’s mechanisms while leaving SR‑specific indices open to empirical testing.

 The manuscript contributes by reframing debt as a temporal pressure‑transfer instrument rather than a static fiscal variable. PET distinguishes productive displacement from erosive displacement and introduces a conceptual Temporal Displacement Ratio (TDR) to evaluate whether future capacity created by borrowing exceeds future pressure created by debt service. This provides a structural foundation for SR’s treatment of fiscal space, household debt cycles, and long‑run capacity constraints.

 The manuscript contributes by integrating migration , ecological externalization , and digital‑infrastructure dependence into a unified pressure‑ecology grammar. PET does not treat people, ecosystems, or infrastructures as interchangeable units; instead, it analyzes how flows of labour, environmental burden, and technological dependency alter system capacities and demands across connected societies. This enables SR to analyze planetary interdependence without collapsing moral, political, or ecological distinctions.

 Finally, PET contributes by providing the planetary integration layer required for SR Galaxy. It shifts the unit of analysis from the country as an isolated container to the country as a node within a planetary pressure network , enabling SR to evaluate national stability, fragility, and resilience through pressure flows rather than isolated indicators. PET becomes the structural foundation for Planetary SR , global comparison architecture, and cross‑country diagnostics of pressure generation, buffering, transfer, and dependency.
