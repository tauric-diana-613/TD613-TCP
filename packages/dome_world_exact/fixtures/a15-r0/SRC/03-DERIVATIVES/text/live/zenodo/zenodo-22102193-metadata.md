Recognition latency therefore approaches a residual floor rather than zero. This formulation is consistent with cyber‑physical systems research, sequential detection theory, and real‑time monitoring literature, grounding CRI in empirically observed precursor systems.</p>\n<p>The manuscript contributes by redefining <strong>drift observability</strong>. This distinction is essential for SR&rsquo;s governance analysis and for understanding how persistent sensing interacts with institutional power.</p>\n<p>The manuscript contributes by defining <strong>three possible CRI trajectories</strong>: adaptive micro‑correction, continuous maladaptive correction, and ambient coercion. These trajectories show that persistent observability increases the frequency and speed of feedback, but the normative direction of that feedback depends on grounding, interpretation, governance capacity, and power distribution. CRI thus provides a structural grammar for evaluating both stabilizing and destabilizing effects of continuous sensing.</p>\n<p>The manuscript contributes by integrating precursor literatures&mdash;cyber‑physical systems, industrial monitoring, anomaly detection, smart‑city telemetry&mdash;into a unified substrate model.
