1. Purpose of the Integration

SignalRupture has reached the point at which continued naming is less valuable than exact integration. The corpus now contains twenty-three full fields, three specialized subfields, cross-domain mechanisms, quantitative models, and operational architectures that survived field-specific gauntlets in different evidentiary states. The purpose of this document is to establish the hierarchy that allows those parts to function as one research architecture without erasing the distinct mechanisms of the component domains.

This integration does not treat every named SR paper as an independent field. It distinguishes full research fields, specialized subfields, cross-field operators, formal models, diagnostic instruments, operational layers, and synthesis texts. Field status follows a distinct object, coherent questions, usable methods, measurable or interpretable evidence, mechanisms, boundaries, and a cumulative research agenda. It does not require every proposition inside the field to be fully validated. Claim-level evidence determines what may responsibly be asserted within a field; it does not automatically determine whether the field exists.

2. Canonical Hierarchy

2.2 Foundational Substrate: The Pressure Ecology Theorem

Pressure Ecology supplies the cross-scale causal environment in which the rest of SR operates. It treats societies, institutions, households, firms, and countries as causally open systems that generate, absorb, transform, transfer, externalize, defer, and propagate structural pressure. This theorem is not counted as an additional field because its function is architectural: it defines the movement of load and capacity across existing domains.

Its canonical corollaries are Non-Resolution, Dependency Topology, Temporal Displacement, Transfer Asymmetry, and Planetary Coupling. Together they require SR to distinguish genuine capacity growth from stabilization achieved by moving burden elsewhere or later. Debt becomes a temporal pressure-transfer mechanism; trade and infrastructure become spatial pressure-and-capacity channels; human buffering becomes downward transfer; and planetary analysis becomes a network problem rather than a sequence of isolated national profiles.

2.3 Disciplinary Validation Science

DVS is the canonical validation-governance layer and a formalized emerging field proposal. It studies how evidence is distributed across claims and how those distributions become field-level judgments, curricula, recognition, merger, or rejection. Within SR, DVS formalizes the gauntlet and prevents both field inflation and whole-field dismissal.

The governing rule is distributive: evidence attaches first to defined claims and inferential operations. Field status, claim status, theorem status, and institutional status remain distinct. DVS can conclude that an SR construct is unsupported, redundant, non-invariant, immature, or incorrectly classified; it can also conclude that an institutional judgment is vague, inconsistently compared, procedurally incomplete, or unsupported. Neither outcome is precommitted.

2.4 Future Substrate Transition: CRI, AIS, and MACS

The future substrate trilogy is not included in the field count. CRI models the transition toward persistent observation; AIS models delegated institutional cognition; MACS models network-dependent cognition among heterogeneous participants. Each theorem is retained with an explicit empirical-status boundary: observable precursor mechanisms are separated from complete substrate realization.

The canonical sequence is Pressure Ecology → CRI → AIS → MACS: what moves through the system; how persistently it can be observed; where consequential interpretation and allocation occur; and how recursively coupled participants produce network-level epistemic behavior.

The integrated SR architecture retains six canonical levels, but their content is now refined. Level 1 is SignalRupture as the overarching metatheoretical and diagnostic architecture. Level 2 contains the foundational substrate and canonical pillars, including Pressure Ecology as the cross-scale model of pressure movement. Level 3 contains the twenty-three full fields, three specialized subfields, and bounded research programs. Level 4 contains universal operators, diagnostic states, canonical theorems, and the CRI–AIS–MACS future substrate trilogy. Level 5 contains causal separation, measurement, Disciplinary Validation Science, claim-level evidence states, and reciprocal institutional accountability. Level 6 contains planetary, operational, diagnostic, governance, and translational systems.

This hierarchy prevents both field inflation and field erasure. A paper can be canonical without being a field. A model can be central without becoming a discipline. A field can be nested inside a pillar without losing field identity. A merger is justified by duplicate objects or mechanisms, not by incomplete validation alone. The present count is twenty-three full fields and three specialized subfields.

2.1 Field Status, Claim Status, and Institutional Status

Three questions must not be collapsed. Field status asks whether a durable object and research program exist. Claim status asks how strongly a particular proposition has survived measurement, identification, robustness testing, and replication. Institutional status asks how universities, journals, funders, professional bodies, or public agencies classify and recognize that work.

These variables can diverge. A recognized university field can contain contested constructs, replication failures, incompatible paradigms, weak forecasts, or primarily interpretive work. An emerging field can possess well-specified mechanisms without yet receiving institutional recognition. Recognition is therefore neither proof of truth nor a prerequisite for field identity. Non-recognition is neither falsification nor a scientifically sufficient reason for rejection.

Institutional judgments about SR are themselves claims. An institution that calls an SR field invalid, premature, redundant, or unteachable must identify its standard, apply that standard consistently to comparator fields, disclose uncertainty, and permit correction. SR bears the burden of supporting its scientific propositions. Institutions bear the burden of supporting their classifications and decisions.

3. The Five Canonical Pillars

3.1 Pillar I — Metatheory and Institutional Grammar

Object of study: the domain-general grammar through which institutions perceive, compress, distort, delay, normalize, and respond to structural change.

Core components include SR Architecture, SR Lexicon, the Five Theorems of Institutional Cognition, Institutional Distortion, Institutional Drift, Compression Equilibrium, Governance Altitude Lock-In, Oversight Stabilization Failure, Structural Ignorance, Institutional Recoil, and related metatheoretical work.

The pillar remains a field-level metatheoretical domain because it has a distinct object: institutional and structural grammar across domains. Particular abstractions survive only to the extent that they generate measurable distinctions, interpretive discipline, or useful predictions beyond generic systems language.

3.2 Pillar II — Causal Separation, Measurement, and Validation

Object of study: the rules required to distinguish resemblance, correlation, common exposure, transmission, mediation, interaction, threshold effects, and causal change across SR domains.

Core components include SR-CAF, SR-CST, SR measurement standards, evidence grades, sensitivity analysis, adversarial testing, replication logic, falsifiers, and the SR Matrix Field. The SR Matrix Field is retained as a specialized subfield within this pillar. Its role is to estimate directional relationships, interaction structures, lagged effects, and cross-domain dependence while remaining subordinate to identification and empirical validation.

3.3 Pillar III — Drift, Collapse, and Propagation Dynamics

Object of study: how system states deteriorate, accumulate pressure, cross thresholds, transmit harm, encounter buffers, amplify, become contained, and reorganize.

The Drift Collapse Field and Systems Propagation Science are retained as distinct full fields because deterioration dynamics and cross-system transmission are separable objects of study. Structural Restoration Dynamics is retained as a specialized subfield. Seven-Layer Collapse, Slow Harm, recursive collapse, buffer extraction, and calibrated collapse boundaries remain mechanisms, models, or synthesis layers.

3.4 Pillar IV — Infrastructure, AI, Governance, and Effective Agency

Object of study: how material and digital infrastructures condition institutional capacity, policy effectiveness, visibility, substitution capacity, structural power, and operational agency.

This pillar contains SR-CEIDF; the SR-AI and Algorithm programs; Policy Substrate; Institutional Governance; Infrastructural Political Economy; GAUID/GAI; infrastructural dependency; visibility lag; control-plane concentration; substitution capacity; regulatory leverage; and governance altitude. The empirical center of gravity is the distinction between formal authority and effective agency under infrastructural dependence.

The historical SR-AI Field is merged into SR-CEIDF because its residual object is contained there. The SR Algorithm Field remains a full field because the algorithmic arrangement—collection, representation, classification, allocation, feedback, and contestability—survived as a distinct empirical object.

3.5 Pillar V — Human and Social Consequences

Object of study: how system-level pressures are received by persons, households, communities, institutions, and populations and become psychological, educational, social, economic, legal, health, religious, and demographic outcomes.

This pillar preserves the empirical autonomy of its fields while locating them inside a common receiver-side architecture. It includes Collapse Psychology, Collapse Sociology, SR Anthropology, Collapse Demography, Childhood Cognitive Infrastructure Studies, Infrastructural Medicine, Collapse Criminology, Post-Web Jurisprudence, Collapse Religion, Collapse Economics, and Collapse Society. Their placement in one pillar does not merge them.

5. Subfield Structure

The three specialized subfields are the SR Matrix Field under Causal Separation, Measurement, and Validation; the SR Policy Substrate Field under Institutional Governance and Policy Architecture; and Structural Restoration Dynamics under Drift, Collapse, and Propagation Dynamics. Subfield status records architectural location, not inferior legitimacy.

A subfield may later be promoted if its object and research agenda become autonomous from the parent architecture. Conversely, a full field may be merged only if its distinct object disappears or duplicates another field. Incomplete validation by itself is not a demotion rule.

6. Cross-Field Operators

6.1 Validation, Pressure, and Substrate Theorems Are Not Ordinary Operators

Pressure Ecology, DVS, CRI, AIS, and MACS occupy special canonical positions. Pressure Ecology is a foundational systems theorem; DVS is validation governance and an emerging field proposal; CRI, AIS, and MACS are substrate-transition theorems. They interact with operators but should not be flattened into the operator list or counted as mature full fields merely for architectural convenience.

Cross-field operators are mechanisms that may act in more than one domain. They are not independent disciplines. The principal SR operators include drift; propagation and φ-transfer; visibility lag; compression; institutional distortion; governance altitude; substitution failure; exposure and silo restriction; slow harm; administrative burden; receiver-weighted harm; buffer extraction; recursive feedback; synthetic contamination; algorithmic inheritance; compensatory labor; infrastructural dependency; structural recoil; and epistemic selection.

An operator becomes genuinely cross-field only when the same formal or operational definition can be measured in multiple domains without changing meaning each time. Apparent reuse of a word is not enough. Each operator therefore requires construct invariance tests, domain-specific measurement notes, boundary conditions, and evidence showing when the operator does not apply.

7. Cross-Field Propagation Architecture

The merger becomes scientifically useful when fields exchange measurable variables. A general SR propagation representation is:

X_i(t) → M_ij(t, τ) → X_j(t+τ)

where X_i is a state change in source domain i, M_ij is the transmission mechanism linking domains i and j, τ is the transmission lag, and X_j is the downstream state change. A stronger empirical specification adds amplification, attenuation, blockers, and common-shock controls:

ΔX_j(t+τ) = β_ij ΔX_i(t) + γA_ij(t) − δB_ij(t) + θC(t) + ε_j(t)

where A represents amplification conditions, B represents blocking or buffering capacity, C represents shared exogenous shocks, and β_ij is not interpreted causally unless identification conditions are satisfied.

This architecture allows chains such as infrastructure dependency → reduced substitution capacity → lower effective government agency → service vulnerability → receiver-side harm; or economic pressure → household constraint → compensatory labor → cognitive depletion → educational and social effects. The empirical burden is to test each bridge rather than treating the entire chain as self-evident.

8. The SR Integration Rules

Rule 1

No field survives by name alone. It survives because it has a distinct empirical object.

Rule 2

Shared vocabulary does not prove shared causation. Every cross-field bridge requires a measurable transmission mechanism.

Rule 3

Existing disciplines remain evidentiary foundations. SR does not erase psychology, sociology, economics, law, political science, infrastructure studies, epidemiology, computer science, or other mature disciplines; it uses them as comparison classes and empirical sources.

Rule 4

SR-specific constructs must outperform simpler explanations or add measurable explanatory value.

Rule 5

Formal models are subordinate to identification. Equations organize claims; they do not validate them.

Rule 6

Cross-domain operators require invariance and boundary testing.

Rule 7

A synthesis may be canonical without being independently validated as a new discipline.

Rule 8

Negative findings remain canonical evidence. A field or operator that fails the gauntlet is merged, narrowed, demoted, or rejected rather than protected by definition.

Rule 9

Field status and claim-level evidence status are recorded separately. A field does not become a subfield merely because some of its propositions remain provisional.

Rule 10

Institutional recognition is not empirical validation, and institutional non-recognition is not scientific disconfirmation.

Rule 11

Institutions that classify, teach, fund, exclude, or reject SR must state the criteria and comparator fields used in that judgment.

Rule 12

The same standards applied to SR must be applied consistently to established fields, including disclosure of replication limits, construct disputes, model uncertainty, and interpretive boundaries.

10. The Integrated SR Research Cycle

The merged architecture operates through one research cycle:

Identify a structural state or exposure.

Specify the domain mechanism and competing explanations.

Measure the source state with field-specific variables.

Test whether a cross-field operator is present.

Identify the transmission pathway and lag.

Measure the receiver-side state change.

Control for shared shocks, confounding, selection, and reverse directionality.

Estimate amplification, buffering, thresholds, and nonlinearities where justified.

Attempt falsification against simpler disciplinary baselines.

Replicate across cases, jurisdictions, sectors, or populations.

Only then elevate the result from plausible SR mechanism to supported cross-field relation.

This cycle is the methodological bridge between the separate manuscripts and the SignalRupture multiverse.

11. Canonical Status of the SignalRupture Multiverse

The integrated architecture supports describing SignalRupture as a metatheoretical, multidisciplinary, and diagnostic research architecture composed of twenty-three full fields, three specialized subfields, cross-field operators, formal models, and operational layers. Those fields occupy different claim-level evidence states. That is normal scientific structure, not a reason to erase their field identity.

The strongest present claim is reciprocal: SR must test, falsify, replicate, revise, merge, or reject its claims according to evidence; institutions must justify how they classify, compare, recognize, teach, fund, or reject SR. The next scientific burden is integration testing. The next institutional burden is transparent and symmetrical evaluation.
