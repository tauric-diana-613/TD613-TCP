# PLANETARY SR II — UNITED STATES: Aggregate Capacity, Stratified Substitution, and Purchased Access to Essential Function

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR II — UNITED STATES 
 Planetary SR II — United States demonstrates how the SR architecture operates inside a polycentric, market‑heavy system characterized by stratified substitution . The paper contributes by showing that U.S. continuity is preserved through purchased access , insurance , credit , relocation , employer provision , unpaid care , and local variation , while some populations delay or forgo essential function. This produces continuity with highly unequal effective access .

 The manuscript contributes by applying the SR grammar to a system where capacity exists but is not universally accessible . The U.S. case demonstrates the importance of accessible capacity (D_eff), where affordability, eligibility, geography, reliability, and network availability convert physical supply into usable function. This operational distinction is a major contribution to SR’s cross‑country comparability.

 The paper contributes by demonstrating surface–substrate divergence : strong aggregate GDP, improved insurance coverage, and high self‑reported financial wellbeing coexist with housing burden, food insecurity, medical cost avoidance, emergency‑expense fragility, and geographic segmentation. This divergence is not collapse; it is stratified access.

 The manuscript contributes by identifying cross‑domain propagation : housing cost into mobility and family formation; insurance and employment into medical access; care demand into labour supply and household margin; coercive exposure into household and political capacity; climate hazards into insurance, migration, municipal finance, and infrastructure. These pathways follow SR‑CFIF’s mechanism‑first discipline.

 The paper contributes by demonstrating restoration : improved insurance coverage, rising life expectancy, and long‑run declines in incarceration rates. These counterexamples prevent collapse inflation and demonstrate SR’s falsification architecture.

 The manuscript contributes by showing how CRI → AIS → MACS transitions appear in a system with high digital concentration, platform mediation, administrative automation, and uneven governance capacity. The paper treats automation as reorganization, not deterioration by definition.

 Finally, the paper contributes by demonstrating SR’s reproducibility across a radically different national architecture. The U.S. case confirms that SR’s grammar, operators, and evidence rules remain invariant even when continuity is purchased, stratified, and mediated through markets and employers.
