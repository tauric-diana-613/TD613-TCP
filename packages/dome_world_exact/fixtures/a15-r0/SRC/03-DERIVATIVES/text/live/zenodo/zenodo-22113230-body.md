# PLANETARY SR III — MEXICO: Formal Capacity, Informal Continuity, Territorial Inequality, and the Human Buffer

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR III — MEXICO 
 Planetary SR III — Mexico demonstrates how the SR architecture operates inside a dual‑system national configuration characterized by formal capacity + informal, household, community, and transnational continuity . The paper contributes by showing that Mexico’s stability is not the absence of function, but the interaction of formal institutions with informal markets, unpaid care, community governance, migration, and remittances .

 The manuscript contributes by applying the SR grammar to a system where formal and informal provision are mutually constitutive . Mexico demonstrates SR’s ability to diagnose systems where households, communities, and transnational circuits supply essential function alongside formal institutions. This dual‑system continuity is a major contribution to SR’s planetary architecture.

 The paper contributes by demonstrating territorial inequality and effective‑access divergence : national restoration in multidimensional poverty, health‑access improvement, and digital expansion coexist with water insecurity, violence exposure, informal labour, remittance dependence, and uneven municipal capacity. These divergences are not collapse; they are structural heterogeneity.

 The manuscript contributes by identifying human‑buffer precedence : unpaid care, informal work, remittances, migration, household construction, and community coordination absorb pressure before visible institutional failure. This is a distinct SR mechanism not present in Canada or the United States.

 The paper contributes by demonstrating cross‑domain propagation : informality into social protection and health; migration into consumption and local continuity; water stress into health and schooling; violence into mobility, business, journalism, and governance; climate exposure into household burden and municipal finance. These pathways follow SR‑CFIF’s mechanism, lag, and falsifier requirements.

 The manuscript contributes by showing how CRI → AIS → MACS transitions appear in a system with uneven records, informal populations, territorial gaps, mediated access, and community governance. Automation is treated as conditional reorganization, not deterioration by assumption.

 Finally, the paper contributes by demonstrating SR’s reproducibility across a third, radically different national architecture. Mexico confirms that SR’s grammar, operators, and evidence rules remain invariant even when continuity is supplied through informal, relational, and transnational systems.
