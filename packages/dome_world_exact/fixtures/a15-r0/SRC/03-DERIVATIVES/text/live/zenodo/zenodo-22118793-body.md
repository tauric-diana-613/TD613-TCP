# COLLAPSE ECONOMICS: A Foundational Field Manuscript on Zombie Metrics, National Loss, and the Economy of Structural Decline

## Zenodo description

This manuscript integrates two complementary analytical fields within the SR framework. Collapse Psychology empirically examines how structural exposure, uncertainty, and cumulative burden erode human agency, capability, and psychological stability. Collapse Economics empirically examines how measured economic activity can drift away from the underlying human, ecological, infrastructural, and institutional condition of a nation. Together, these fields provide a unified diagnostic architecture for understanding collapse‑pattern dynamics across human and economic systems.

 The psychological domain identifies erosion in individual and collective functioning under conditions of insecurity, volatility, and constrained alternatives. The economic domain measures drift between GDP and national‑loss stocks, recovery mismatches, compensatory production, aggregation blindness, and flow‑stock divergence. The integration of these domains demonstrates that collapse‑pattern phenomena can be observed both in human systems and in economic indicators, and that these patterns can be measured, tested, and falsified using official data.

 This work contributes to the literature in three principal ways.

 1. It establishes a unified cross‑domain collapse architecture. 
By linking psychological erosion with economic drift, the manuscript provides a coherent framework for analyzing how structural pressures manifest simultaneously in human outcomes and national indicators. This integration expands the scope of collapse‑pattern research beyond single‑domain analysis.

 2. It introduces empirically testable constructs in both fields. 
Collapse Psychology contributes measurable constructs such as structural exposure, uncertainty‑arousal pathways, bandwidth erosion, and agency‑change metrics. Collapse Economics contributes constructs including national‑loss stocks, zombie metrics, recovery mismatch, aggregation blindness, flow‑stock divergence, and compensatory‑production decomposition. Each construct is defined with falsification criteria and measurement pathways.

 3. It demonstrates cross‑domain empirical alignment using official data. 
The manuscript shows that psychological erosion and economic drift can be observed in real‑world data, including Canadian wildfire exposure, infrastructure renewal gaps, opioid mortality, hospital overtime, insolvency trends, and COVID‑19 recovery timelines. This cross‑case evidence supports the claim that collapse‑pattern dynamics are measurable across human and economic systems, and that the SR framework provides a structured method for analyzing these dynamics.
