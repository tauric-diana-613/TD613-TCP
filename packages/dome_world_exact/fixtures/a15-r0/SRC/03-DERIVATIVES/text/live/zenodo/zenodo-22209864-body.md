# PLANETARY SR XVI — INDIA: High-Growth Federal Heterogeneity, Digital Public Infrastructure, Informal Compensation, and Capacity Expansion

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR XVI — INDIA 
 Description 
 Planetary SR XVI — India provides a structural diagnostic of India as a coupled institutional–population system, using evidence from 2000–2025 with 2026 verification. The paper identifies India’s continuity architecture as high‑growth federal heterogeneity, digital public infrastructure, informal compensation, and capacity expansion . India’s macro profile— “6.5% real GDP growth in FY2024/25 and 6.6% in FY2025/26” (IMF, 2025), with MoSPI’s rebased series estimating “7.1% growth in FY2024/25” (MoSPI, 2026)—coexists with structural pressures including “general‑government debt near 81% of GDP” and vast state‑level divergence in service conversion. The study distinguishes formal coverage from effective access, showing how informality, female participation, learning quality, rural health access, housing pressure, and state‑level capacity shape continuity beneath rapid infrastructure, energy, and digital‑platform expansion. India’s “484.8 GW installed generation capacity by June 2025” (Ministry of Power, 2025) and nationwide digital identity/payment systems form major conversion layers, while uneven human‑development outcomes remain central substrate constraints.

 Contribution 
 This paper expands the Planetary SR comparative series by introducing a new continuity topology distinct from Canada, the United States, Mexico, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Italy, Spain, Sweden, Norway, Poland, Japan, South Korea, China, and New Zealand. India’s configuration strengthens SR fields such as Planetary Pressure Ecology , Institutional Reflex Science , Planetary Drift Mechanics , and Systems Propagation Science by demonstrating how rapid national capacity expansion interacts with large informal systems, heterogeneous state trajectories, and digital public‑infrastructure scaling across 28 states, 8 union territories, and diverse urban/rural service geographies. The document provides high‑resolution evidence for SR’s frozen hypotheses, including compensated continuity, unequal insulation, surface–substrate divergence, and reorganization. It also contributes to the ATLAS architecture by showing how a common national digital and fiscal substrate can support radically different state‑level development paths without collapsing them into one aggregate score. As the paper states, India’s mechanism is empirically distinguishable through its blend of “rapid capacity expansion and federal heterogeneity, with strong growth, large infrastructure/digital scaling and uneven human‑development/service conversion,” thereby adding a new structural configuration to the planetary series.
