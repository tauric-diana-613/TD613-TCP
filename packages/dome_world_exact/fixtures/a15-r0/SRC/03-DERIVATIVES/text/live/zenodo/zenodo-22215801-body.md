# PLANETARY SR XXI — ARGENTINA: Inflationary Reorganization, Social-System Buffering, Energy-Agricultural Capacity, and Macro-Fiscal Reset

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR XXI — ARGENTINA 
 Description 
 Planetary SR XXI — Argentina analyzes Argentina as a coupled institutional–population system defined by inflationary reorganization, social‑system buffering, energy‑agricultural capacity, and macro‑fiscal reset . The study documents a continuity architecture shaped by recurrent inflation, currency instability, and repeated household adaptation, alongside strong agricultural and energy assets and broad public and social‑insurance systems. IMF’s 2026 country data— “real GDP growth around 3.5% and consumer‑price inflation around 30.4%” —illustrate that disinflation and macro normalization remain incomplete even after major adjustment. Effective access diverges sharply from formal provision: provincial fiscal stress, staffing, specialist supply, transport, and inflation‑driven price volatility create uneven conversion across 23 provinces, Buenos Aires, metropolitan regions, and rural production zones. Argentina’s productive base—oil, gas, hydro, nuclear, renewables, and agriculture—provides substantial physical and foreign‑exchange capacity, but domestic distribution depends on grid investment, pipeline expansion, and inflation‑adjusted affordability. Households, unions, firms, and community networks repeatedly absorb unresolved pressure through savings, dollarization, informal work, and adaptive consumption, forming a central SR test of reorganization versus restoration.

 How it Contributes 
 This study contributes a distinct continuity topology to the Planetary SR comparative series by demonstrating how a middle‑income federal system maintains continuity through inflation‑conditioned reorganization and household adaptation , while unresolved macro pressure propagates into real‑income erosion, provincial divergence, service‑capacity strain, and fiscal‑reset uncertainty . Argentina strengthens SR fields such as Systems Propagation Science , Drift Collapse Field , Infrastructural Political Economy , and Institutional Cognition by showing how inflation → real income/poverty/consumption, FX constraint → imports/investment/production, and fiscal stabilization → service capacity/household transfers operate as named propagation bridges. The paper expands the ATLAS architecture by illustrating how macro‑financial reorganization can alter prices and institutions without yet repairing underlying requirement–capacity gaps, providing a critical test of reorganization vs restoration . Argentina’s mechanism is empirically distinguishable through its blend of “highly recurrent macroeconomic and price reorganization with strong agricultural/energy productive capacity, federal heterogeneity and household adaptation to inflation/currency instability,” adding a new structural configuration to the planetary series and supplying a decisive case for surface–substrate divergence , unequal insulation , and agency‑conditioned response .
