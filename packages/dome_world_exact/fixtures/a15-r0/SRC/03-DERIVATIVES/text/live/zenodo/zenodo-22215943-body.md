# PLANETARY SR XXII — CHILE: Market-Public Hybrid Capacity, Copper Dependence, Demographic Deceleration, and Social Rebalancing

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR XXII — CHILE 
 Description 
 Planetary SR XXII — Chile analyzes Chile as a coupled institutional–population system defined by market‑public hybrid capacity, copper dependence, demographic deceleration, and social rebalancing . The study documents strong macro‑institutional continuity— “pandemic‑era macro imbalances had largely resolved and growth returned near trend” (OECD, 2025)—alongside deep structural transitions driven by “a total fertility rate of 0.99 births per woman, down 59.4% since 1993” (INE Chile, 2026). Chile’s continuity architecture blends privatized and public service mixes, advanced capital markets, large mining and renewable‑energy sectors, and geographically concentrated urbanization. Effective access diverges from formal provision: pensions, housing, debt, mixed public/private health financing, and regional service gaps create heterogeneous household margins across 16 regions, provinces, communes, and mining/urban/rural geographies. Renewable expansion and mining electrification provide major structural capacity, while transmission, storage, water‑energy‑mining coupling, and climate exposure determine conversion. Demographic decline, ageing, and shrinking cohorts increasingly shape labour supply, care demand, pension sustainability, and long‑run productivity.

 How it Contributes 
 This study contributes a distinct continuity topology to the Planetary SR comparative series by demonstrating how a market‑public hybrid system maintains continuity through strong macro institutions and mining/renewable capacity , while unresolved pressure propagates into inequality, housing, pensions, demographic decline, and regional conversion gaps . Chile strengthens SR fields such as Systems Propagation Science , Drift Collapse Field , Infrastructural Political Economy , and Institutional Cognition by showing how fertility decline → labour/care/pension pressure, copper/lithium cycles → fiscal/investment capacity, and water/climate → mining/agriculture/urban supply operate as named propagation bridges. The paper expands the ATLAS architecture by illustrating how macro credibility and export capacity do not automatically produce social restoration, providing a decisive test of surface–substrate divergence and unequal insulation . Chile’s mechanism is empirically distinguishable through its blend of “market‑public hybrid continuity with strong macro institutions and mining capacity, facing low fertility, inequality, housing/pension/service gaps and commodity dependence,” adding a new structural configuration to the planetary series and supplying a critical case for reorganization vs restoration and agency‑conditioned response .
