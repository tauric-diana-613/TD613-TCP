# PLANETARY SR XXIII — COLOMBIA: Urban-Rural Heterogeneity, Social Expansion, Commodity Exposure, and Institutional Recomposition

## Zenodo description

PLANETARY SR XXIII — COLOMBIA 
 Description 
 Planetary SR XXIII — Colombia analyzes Colombia as a coupled institutional–population system defined by urban–rural heterogeneity, expanding social capacity, commodity exposure, and institutional recomposition . The study documents a continuity architecture built on universalizing social programs, broad insurance coverage, and growing public capacity, while highlighting sharp territorial divergence— “departmental poverty ranged from 65.3% in Chocó and 60.2% in La Guajira to 15.1% in Cundinamarca” (DANE, 2026). Colombia’s labour market remains structurally mixed: unemployment and informality coexist with employment recovery and migration‑driven labour expansion. Effective access diverges from formal coverage across 32 departments, Bogotá D.C., municipalities, and conflict‑affected rural geographies, where provider distribution, transport, administrative burden, and infrastructure gaps shape conversion. Hydropower, fossil exports, and emerging renewables provide mixed low‑carbon and external‑revenue capacity, while drought, transition policy, and territorial climate exposure transmit across fiscal, electricity, agriculture, and urban systems. Venezuelan migration materially alters labour, housing, and service requirements while adding working‑age population, forming a central SR test of Pressure–Buffer Duality.

 How it Contributes 
 This study contributes a distinct continuity topology to the Planetary SR comparative series by demonstrating how a unitary but decentralized republic maintains continuity through social‑policy expansion and mixed market buffering , while unresolved pressure propagates into territorial inequality, informality, rural access gaps, and commodity‑linked fiscal dependence . Colombia strengthens SR fields such as Systems Propagation Science , Institutional Cognition , Infrastructural Political Economy , and Collapse Governance by showing how informality → tax/social protection/household buffers, regional poverty → health/education/labour access, migration → labour/housing/service demand, and commodity revenues → fiscal/social capacity operate as named propagation bridges. The paper expands the ATLAS architecture by illustrating how national coverage gains can coexist with local functional gaps, providing a decisive test of surface–substrate divergence and unequal insulation . Colombia’s mechanism is empirically distinguishable through its blend of “social‑policy and market continuity with improving poverty outcomes, large regional inequality, informality and commodity/fiscal dependence,” adding a new structural configuration to the planetary series and supplying a critical case for reorganization vs restoration and agency‑conditioned response
