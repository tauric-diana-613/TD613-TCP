# ATLAS II THE ATLAS MEASUREMENT REGISTRY: Variables, Crosswalks, Evidence Grades, and Reproducibility Rules

## Zenodo description

ATLAS II — THE ATLAS MEASUREMENT REGISTRY
Variables, Crosswalks, Evidence Grades, and Reproducibility Rules

 Description

 ATLAS II establishes the measurement and reproducibility architecture governing the SignalRupture ATLAS Research Programme. It defines the conditions under which a variable, comparison, mechanism claim, or forecast may enter the ATLAS canon. Every construct must disclose its numerator, denominator, unit, temporal and geographic coverage, distributional meaning, provenance, data vintage, revision status, missingness treatment, transformation method, uncertainty, and known biases.

 The registry distinguishes gross economic activity from domestically retained and broadly usable value. It prevents commodity dependence, foreign participation, domestic value added, low income, or institutional weakness from being treated as automatic evidence of developmental lock-in. Instead, a lock-in diagnosis requires a temporally ordered and distributionally demonstrated mechanism connecting value retention, uncompensated burdens, capability formation, substitution capacity, external dependence, executable agency, and renewed externalization. Plausible rival explanations—including geography, conflict, productive structure, policy choice, technology, market size, global shocks, and historical path dependence—must also be tested.

 ATLAS II introduces a versioned claim–evidence registry, explicit crosswalks for non-equivalent datasets, and an E0–E6 evidence ladder ranging from unsupported assertion to prospective out-of-sample validation. It establishes rules for case selection, distributional incidence, migration and mobility measurement, missing data, uncertainty, causal identification, falsification, policy evaluation, and publication. Null results, reversals, measurement failures, contradictory evidence, and superior rival explanations remain part of the canonical record and can narrow or revise ATLAS claims.

 How It Contributes

 ATLAS II supplies the empirical control layer required to transform Planetary SR’s country diagnostics into a reproducible cross-country research programme. Planetary SR I–XXX provides time-bounded national observations and locked diagnostic pathways; the Measurement Registry determines whether those observations can be translated into comparable variables, defensible mechanisms, adversarial tests, and prospective forecasts without erasing national, sectoral, household, or regional differences.

 Its central contribution is to make the Developmental Lock-In Hypothesis falsifiable. Rather than assigning deterministic labels to countries, the registry decomposes the proposed pathway into independently testable hypotheses concerning retention, burden transmission, substitution, dependency, bargaining agency, reproduction, and restoration. Support for one link cannot stand in for the others, and no conclusion may exceed its achieved evidence grade.

 The paper also prevents aggregate indicators from concealing distributional outcomes. National gains are separated among households, workers, domestic firms, foreign firms, creditors, and the state, while ecological liabilities, labour capture, ownership concentration, migration pathways, territorial differences, and public capability are examined independently. Foreign participation is not classified as extraction when it produces compensated, durable, and locally governed capability; commodity production is not classified as lock-in when real substitution, learning, bargaining, and upgrading remain available.

 By requiring versioned data manifests, transformation logs, preregistered tests, holdout cases, correction histories, and machine-readable claim–evidence ledgers, ATLAS II creates the audit trail for the entire ATLAS programme. It establishes Zenodo as the canonical DOI layer and OSF as the publication mirror while clarifying that repository publication does not itself constitute scientific validation.

 The result is a measurement constitution for ATLAS: a system in which provenance, uncertainty, counterevidence, comparability limits, and revision obligations travel with every claim. This protects the programme from circular reasoning, false precision, aggregate concealment, and retrospective adjustment while directing analysis toward restoration—the measurable capacity of societies and communities to retain gains, govern essential systems, absorb shocks fairly, develop capabilities, and choose their developmental path.
