# ATLAS XVI ATLAS OF NORTH AMERICA: Resource Wealth, Continental Production, and Asymmetric State Capacity

## Zenodo description

ATLAS XVI — Atlas of North America 
 Description 
 ATLAS XVI formalizes the continental‑integration layer of the ATLAS universe: a diagnostic of how North America’s high productive capacity coexists with resource peripheries, concentrated corporate infrastructure, and deeply integrated value chains . As the manuscript states, “North America combines high productive capacity with resource peripheries, concentrated corporate infrastructure, and deeply integrated value chains.” 

 The paper rejects three shortcuts: commodity dependence is exposure, not proof; domestic value‑added does not guarantee broad retention; and low income or weak institutions cannot be used as circular explanations. It positions North America as a multi‑layered system where Canada, the United States, Mexico, and connected Caribbean/Central American production share value chains but do not share burdens, retention, or agency equally.

 ATLAS XVI extends the formal pathway (R → B → S → D → A → X) by showing how continental integration can either increase retained value (R) through shared upgrading or reduce bargaining agency (A) when concentration and dependency narrow substitution capacity (S). It introduces a measurement architecture focused on bilateral TiVA, critical‑mineral processing, automotive supplier ownership, digital/logistics concentration, labour and migration pathways, and subnational fiscal/ecological incidence.

 The manuscript establishes that continental integration is not automatically beneficial; it becomes beneficial only when retention, capability formation, and burden distribution are aligned across regions.

 How it Contributes 
 ATLAS XVI provides the continental‑systems foundation of the ATLAS universe. It operationalizes Continental Integration by turning SR’s pressure‑ecology insights into a measurable, falsifiable account of how North American value chains distribute gains, burdens, and agency.

 It deepens Developmental Lock‑In by showing how concentrated corporate infrastructure and asymmetric state capacity can reproduce dependency even in high‑income regions. It strengthens Society as a Pressure Ecology by demonstrating how labour, ecological, fiscal, and essential‑goods burdens travel across borders and subnational regions.

 The migration audit expands ATLAS’s cross‑border incidence analysis by tracing origin, transit, destination, Indigenous, temporary‑worker, refugee, and return‑migration systems. As the manuscript states, “North America cannot be coded only as a receiving region.” 

 ATLAS XVI also provides the empirical substrate for evaluating restoration through continental supplier development, enforceable labour standards, viable domestic processing, infrastructure interoperability, redundancy, and Indigenous/local revenue participation.

 Ultimately, ATLAS XVI contributes the continental‑agency grammar required to evaluate whether North American societies can retain gains, govern essential systems, and escape coercive or concentrated dependency.
