 Description 
 ATLAS XXIII — ATLAS OF THE MIDDLE EAST AND NORTH AFRICA evaluates a bounded and falsifiable proposition: hydrocarbon wealth can generate exceptional fiscal capacity while sustaining import, technology, labour, and ecological dependencies, and import‑constrained economies face different but connected lock‑in pathways. The manuscript does not treat countries as essences, does not equate commodity production or foreign participation with extraction, and does not infer constraint from specialization alone. Instead, it specifies the causal, temporal, and distributional conditions under which external pressure, low domestic retention, uncompensated labour and ecological burdens, weakened substitution, and constrained agency can reproduce asymmetric dependence across diverse political and economic systems.

 The manuscript formalizes the SR developmental pathway using five core variables: domestically retained and broadly usable value (R); unremediated labour, ecological, fiscal, and institutional burdens (B); substitution and recovery capacity (S); dependence on external markets, finance, technology, or infrastructure (D); and executable bargaining and policy agency (A). These variables are linked to renewed externalization (X) through an explicit dynamic representation. ATLAS XXIII establishes exclusion rules against overclaiming, defines rival mechanisms for each hypothesis, and sets evidence grades (E0–E6) that govern permissible inference.

 Measurement architecture is fully specified. The manuscript uses OECD TiVA to distinguish gross trade from value‑added origin; UNIDO industrial statistics to track productive structure; UNCTAD indicators to identify commodity dependence and diversification constraints; and World Bank data to measure structural change. Variables include hydrocarbon and non‑hydrocarbon domestic value added, sovereign investment and domestic capability, migrant labour and wage distribution, food, water, and technology dependence, debt and foreign‑exchange constraints, and private‑sector ownership and supplier depth. Every variable must carry source provenance, definition, unit, coverage, transformation code, uncertainty, and missingness rules. The empirical design compares Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Egypt, Türkiye, and additional resource‑rich and import‑constrained economies without treating income or regime type as deterministic. ATLAS XXIII is time‑bounded, mechanism‑driven, and explicitly revisable. It is designed for public‑interest analysis, resilience evaluation, and restoration‑oriented policy assessment.

 How It Contributes 
 1. Cross‑country causal architecture 
ATLAS XXIII converts SR’s country‑level diagnostics into a comparable, mechanism‑based analytical system for the Middle East and North Africa. It supplies explicit variables, dynamic functions, rival explanations, falsification gates, and evidence grades, enabling SR to move from narrative diagnostics to reproducible comparative science. This establishes the methodological backbone for regional and planetary inference.

 2. Formalization of the Developmental Lock‑In Hypothesis in hydrocarbon and import‑dependent contexts 
The manuscript provides a dynamic, testable representation of developmental lock‑in where hydrocarbon rents, migrant labour systems, external technology, and essential‑resource dependence interact. It specifies required temporal order, mediation, and rival mechanisms, preventing deterministic or moralized interpretations. Lock‑in becomes a conditional, falsifiable sequence rather than a label or structural destiny.

 3. Distributional, ecological, and labour‑system integration 
ATLAS XXIII embeds labour capture, migrant‑labour dependence, ecological depletion, water stress, and subnational inequality directly into causal evaluation. Rising domestic value added or sovereign investment cannot establish restoration unless wages, labour rights, ecological remediation, community outcomes, and local capability improve. This prevents misclassification of hydrocarbon‑led growth as structural transformation and ensures that capability formation, burden remediation, and fair retention remain central.

 4. Measurement and reproducibility protocol 
The manuscript establishes SR’s measurement hygiene: source provenance, transformation code, uncertainty, sensitivity checks, explicit coverage windows, and non‑interchangeability of indicators. It defines reproducibility rules—data manifests, code publication, preregistration, holdout cases, and correction visibility—ensuring that SR claims remain transparent, auditable, and scientifically durable.

 5. Restoration architecture 
ATLAS XXIII defines restoration as capability‑based diversification, water, food, and energy resilience, portable labour rights, and transparent sovereign and public investment. It provides policy evaluation gates that prevent superficial upgrading narratives and ensure interventions are assessed on capability formation, burden remediation, and real refusal capacity. This anchors SR’s ethical purpose: increasing societies’ ability to retain gains, govern essential systems, absorb shocks fairly, and choose their developmental path.
