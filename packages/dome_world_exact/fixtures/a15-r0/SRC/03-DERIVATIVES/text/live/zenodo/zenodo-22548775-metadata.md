The manuscript does not treat continents, geographic labels, or administrative boundaries as explanatory categories. Instead, it specifies the causal, spatial, and institutional conditions under which exposure networks, corridor structures, household absorption, institutional heterogeneity, and shared shocks produce synchronized or divergent outcomes within and across regions.</p>\n<p>RSD operationalizes mechanisms imported from <strong>Planetary Pressure Ecology</strong>, <strong>Propagation Dynamics</strong>, <strong>Executable Agency</strong>, <strong>Household Absorption Science</strong>, <strong>Migration &amp; Labour Buffer Dynamics</strong>, and <strong>Drift Diagnostics</strong>. RSD prohibits inference from continent labels and requires that any &ldquo;regional effect&rdquo; be decomposed into composition, network exposure, institutions, spatial dependence, and common shocks.</p>\n<p>RSD uses OECD regional statistics, TL2/TL3 indicators, disparity measures, and ATLAS regional hypotheses to test whether corridor exposure, institutional heterogeneity, and household absorption predict synchronized pressure or divergent recovery within the same region. A spatially explicit diagnostic architecture</strong><br>RSD establishes regions as empirical comparison spaces rather than causal units. It requires explicit measurement of exposure networks, corridor structures, institutional heterogeneity, household burdens, and shared shocks. This prevents misinterpretation of geographic labels as causal explanations.</p>\n<p><strong>2. Applied operationalization of SR planetary fields</strong><br>The programme translates mechanisms from PPE, propagation, agency, household absorption, migration, and drift into spatial diagnostics. It applies pressure transfer, exposure, absorptive capacity, dependency, substitution, and household burden to regional systems, making SR&rsquo;s theoretical architecture operational for subnational and cross‑border analysis.</p>\n<p><strong>3. Incremental prediction over country‑only and geography‑only models</strong><br>RSD&rsquo;s distinct claim is empirical: exposure networks, corridor matrices, and institutional heterogeneity must outperform continent labels, country averages, and geography‑only models in predicting synchronized pressure, divergent recovery, and distributional outcomes. It tests whether functional regions explain variation beyond administrative boundaries.</p>\n<p><strong>4. A falsifiable distinction between shared exposure and divergent transmission</strong><br>The programme distinguishes shared shocks from divergent outcomes. It predicts that corridor exposure synchronizes pressure after national controls, while institutional heterogeneity moderates recovery within the same shock. This provides a scientifically grounded logic for regional infrastructure planning, cross‑border coordination, and resilience design.</p>\n<p><strong>7.
