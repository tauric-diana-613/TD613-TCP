# WORLD INFRASTRUCTURE II: THE EMPIRICAL RECONSTRUCTION How Planetary SR and ATLAS Made the World-System Observable

## Zenodo description

World Infrastructure Science (WIS) 
 The Empirical Reconstruction of the Planetary Operating Environment 

 Description 
 World Infrastructure Science (WIS) evaluates a bounded and falsifiable proposition: multiple heterogeneous infrastructures—physical, energy, logistical, financial, digital, institutional, semantic, ecological, and human—can jointly constitute a planetary operating environment that conditions feasible action, knowledge, coordination, and executable agency across borders. The manuscript does not claim that “the world is one fragile machine,” nor that institutions, markets, or states have been replaced. Instead, it specifies the causal, temporal, and distributional conditions under which cross‑infrastructure configurations become jointly necessary, alter feasible action, and produce effects that cannot be recovered by stacking component SR fields independently.

 WIS formalizes the irreducible mechanism of infrastructural constitutionalization through cross‑infrastructure closure . A configuration becomes constitutionalizing when defaults, protocols, standards, interfaces, capacity limits, eligibility rules, and recovery conditions jointly determine feasible action before deliberation. Closure occurs when nominal substitutes in one layer are blocked by constraints in another—turning advertised options into non‑options. The field distinguishes dependence from constraint, pressure from failure, continuity from restoration, and exposure from executable agency.

 Planetary SR and ATLAS supplied the empirical architecture that the March 2026 conceptual paper lacked: measurable pressures and capacities, buffer sequences, cross‑system bridges, substitution topologies, value‑retention pathways, infrastructure debt, visibility lags, negative cases, and frozen prospective tests. WIS survives only if joint models add stable explanatory, predictive, or intervention‑selection value beyond Systems Propagation Science , Infrastructural Political Economy , Planetary Pressure Ecology , Essential‑Goods Dependency & Survival Systems , Household Absorption Science , and related branches. Failure requires demotion to an integration framework.

 WIS is declared a provisional full SR field , empirically active but not yet fully validated. Its autonomy depends on replicated incremental prediction, mechanism validity, negative‑case separation, and prospective adjudication through 2031.

 How It Contributes 
 1. A distinct planetary‑scale empirical object 
WIS establishes the cross‑infrastructure configuration as an observable unit: payment–cloud–identity stacks, food–energy–water–logistics corridors, housing–labour–transport–care systems, standards–platform–market‑access regimes, and other topologies where heterogeneous infrastructures become jointly necessary. This object is irreducible to any single SR field.

 2. A formal mechanism for infrastructural constitutionalization 
The field supplies a mechanism explaining how defaults, protocols, interfaces, capacity limits, eligibility rules, and recovery conditions jointly determine feasible action. It distinguishes constitutionalization from ordinary dependence and from political or administrative intent.

 3. Incremental prediction over mature component fields 
WIS’s distinct claim is empirical: joint models must outperform propagation, political‑economy, pressure‑ecology, essential‑goods, household‑absorption, logistics, and restoration baselines in predicting feasible action, burden displacement, closure, visibility lag, and restoration. This establishes a falsifiable autonomy test.

 4. A cross‑infrastructure closure architecture 
The field explains how substitutes that appear available within one layer become blocked by constraints in another—standards, finance, transport, energy, skills, data, insurance, time, care, or administrative pathways. Closure converts nominal options into non‑options and reveals the joint necessity of the configuration.

 5. Integration of burden displacement and visibility lag 
WIS embeds burden displacement and visibility lag into planetary analysis. It shows how surface continuity can be preserved by rising household, worker, municipal, ecological, or deferred‑maintenance burden—and how hidden deterioration precedes visible discontinuity.

 6. A planetary agency criterion derived from ATLAS 
The field incorporates ATLAS’s agency criterion: interdependence is not closure unless refusal, substitution, and recovery are practically executable. High external dependence does not imply low agency when alternatives are credible, governable, and affordable.

 7. Infrastructure debt as a cross‑layer diagnostic 
WIS formalizes infrastructure debt as the accumulated difference between required renewal and actual commitment across physical, ecological, institutional, epistemic, and human layers. It distinguishes continuity from restoration and pressure from depletion.

 8. A rigorous demarcation from neighbouring SR fields 
The field establishes strict non‑duplication boundaries. WIS enters only when the joint configuration adds explanatory or predictive value beyond Propagation , IPE , PPE , EGDSS , HAS , GLSA , or PRG . If component fields fully explain the outcome, WIS must be demoted.

 9. A prospective, falsifiable research programme 
WIS defines eight linked hypotheses—joint necessity, constitutionalization, closure, burden displacement, visibility lag, executable agency, restoration, and irreducibility—and a 2026–2031 prediction family. Validation requires temporal order, rival separation, negative cases, and prospective scoring.
