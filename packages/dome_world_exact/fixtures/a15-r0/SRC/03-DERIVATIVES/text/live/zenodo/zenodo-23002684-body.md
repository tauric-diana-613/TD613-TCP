# The Explanatory Power Gap: Academic Knowledge, Educational Transmission, and Institutional Understanding in a Coupled World

## Zenodo description

The Explanatory Power Gap 
 Academic Knowledge, Educational Transmission, and Institutional Understanding in a Coupled World 
 A Comparative Research Paper and Falsifiable Test Protocol 

 Description 
 The Explanatory Power Gap names the measurable difference between the explanation required by a coupled, nonstationary environment and the explanation an institution can produce, teach, operationalize, and publicly communicate. The paper distinguishes three layers of institutional knowledge—scholarly literature, operating models, and public explanations—and shows how explanatory adequacy can degrade when these layers fail to transmit or integrate mechanisms required for present conditions.

 The gap is not a claim that conventional theories have become false. It is a structural hypothesis: locally valid explanations can become jointly incomplete when institutional selection systems, disciplinary boundaries, and revision cycles do not match the causal architecture of the environment. The paper defines four distinct gaps—literature–world, curriculum–literature, curriculum–world, and recognition—and shows why conflating them produces invalid judgments about institutional failure.

 The paper constructs a counterfactual “conventional ensemble” rival model (M2) from institutionalism, bounded rationality, sensemaking, complex systems, social–ecological governance, sociotechnical transitions, and political economy. This ensemble does not exist as an operational institutional model; it is an adversarial benchmark designed to prevent a straw‑man comparison. SR is tested against this ensemble under penalties for complexity, flexibility, and retrospective fit.

 The paper specifies a preregisterable model competition, falsification conditions, rival explanations, and an empirical research program. It places strict limits on what current indicators—skill gaps, curriculum misalignment, AI‑use contradictions, climate‑education omissions—can establish. The contribution is methodological: SR must earn incremental explanatory value through held‑out prediction, intervention discrimination, and transportability , not through breadth alone.

 How It Contributes 
 1. It formalizes a measurable explanatory gap across institutional layers 
 The paper distinguishes literature, curriculum, operating models, and public explanations as separate selection systems. This resolves the common confusion between “theory failure,” “curriculum failure,” and “institutional failure.” It provides SR with a structured diagnostic for identifying where explanatory degradation actually occurs.

 2. It creates a fair, adversarial benchmark for SR model comparison 
 By constructing the strongest possible conventional ensemble from existing literatures, the paper prevents SR from winning by default. SR must outperform:

 institutionalism,

 bounded rationality,

 sensemaking,

 complex systems,

 social–ecological governance,

 sociotechnical transitions,

 and political economy

 after complexity and flexibility penalties. 
This strengthens SR’s scientific legitimacy and prevents compression‑effect illusions.

 3. It identifies conditions under which conventional theories become jointly incomplete 
 The paper shows how coupling, nonstationarity, recursive causation, infrastructure mediation, and revision‑cycle mismatch create environments where domain‑specific theories remain locally true but systemically insufficient. This provides SR with a principled explanation for why integration architectures become necessary.

 4. It defines four distinct gaps that institutions must measure 
 The literature–world, curriculum–literature, curriculum–world, and recognition gaps give SR a mechanism‑level diagnostic tool for evaluating educational and institutional alignment. These gaps prevent overgeneralized claims and allow targeted empirical testing.

 5. It provides a falsifiable research protocol for evaluating SR’s incremental value 
 The paper specifies:

 mechanism coverage tests,

 causal discrimination tests,

 residual burden analysis,

 held‑out prediction,

 intervention validity,

 transportability,

 complexity penalties,

 and post hoc flexibility penalties.

 This makes SR’s explanatory claims empirically testable and revision‑governed, consistent with the Ultiverse evidence architecture.

 6. It clarifies SR’s role as an integration architecture rather than a replacement for domain theories 
 The paper identifies hybrid outcomes where SR + specialist theory outperform either alone. This positions SR as a structural integration layer that coordinates domain knowledge rather than absorbing it.
