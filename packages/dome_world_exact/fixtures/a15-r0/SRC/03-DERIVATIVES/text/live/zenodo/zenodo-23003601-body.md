# Governing Inside Private Infrastructure: Formal Authority Effective Agency and the Closed Circuit of Pressure Transfer

## Zenodo description

Governing Inside Private Infrastructure 
 Formal Authority, Effective Agency, and the Closed Circuit of Pressure Transfer 
 SignalRupture Research Programme — Canonical Research Manuscript (2026) 

 Description 
 Governing Inside Private Infrastructure is a falsifiable SR framework explaining why governments can retain extensive formal authority while losing effective agency when implementation depends on privately controlled systems. The paper distinguishes legal power from material capacity and shows how policy outcomes are shaped by private infrastructure dependency , pressure transfer , and restoration failure .

 As your manuscript states:

 “Governments can retain extensive legal authority while losing the practical capacity to redirect material outcomes.”

 The paper defines a closed circuit of pressure transfer : when a shock raises a real cost, policy can shift who pays, when payment occurs, and how visible the burden becomes — but cannot eliminate the cost unless the underlying constraint is changed. Stabilization without restoration creates recurrent pressure loops across households, firms, public budgets, creditors, infrastructure, and ecosystems.

 The manuscript provides:

 a formal distinction between authority and agency 

 a definition of private infrastructure dependency 

 a mechanism for pressure transfer 

 a ledger for tracing burden movement

 conditions for restoration 

 hypotheses, rivals, and falsification rules

 a comparative empirical design

 negative evidence criteria

 It is not ideological. As the paper states:

 “It does not assume that private ownership is inherently harmful… The question is narrower.”

 The framework is designed for public‑interest analysis , not for identifying vulnerabilities or treating populations as instruments.

 How It Contributes 
 1. It gives SR a measurable mechanism for institutional power loss 
 The paper formalizes the gap between formal authority and effective agency , providing SR with a testable way to diagnose when governments cannot produce intended material outcomes. This is a major addition to SR’s Infrastructure Science and Institutional Coherence architecture.

 It shows that authority ≠ outcome.

 2. It defines private infrastructure dependency as a causal object 
 The manuscript identifies five dependency channels:

 Provision 

 Investment 

 Information 

 Fiscal 

 Coordination 

 This transforms “dependence” from a political intuition into a codable mechanism with empirical thresholds. It becomes part of SR’s ontology of infrastructural constraints.

 3. It introduces the closed circuit of pressure transfer 
 Your manuscript states:

 “A household is protected… the burden moves… the receiving domain then transmits part of the burden back.”

 This is a new SR mechanism: pressure does not disappear — it relocates .

 The circuit explains:

 why stabilization can succeed while restoration fails

 why burdens reappear in wages, prices, debt, service quality, or ecological damage

 why policy can be neutralized by downstream responses

 This is a foundational SR contribution.

 4. It provides a restoration test that institutions currently lack 
 The paper distinguishes:

 Stabilization → indicator stops worsening

 Restoration → underlying constraint changes

 This is a critical SR insight: institutions routinely mistake stabilization for success.

 The restoration test forces governments to show:

 who absorbed the cost

 whether capacity improved

 whether recurrence is prevented

 This becomes part of SR’s evidence‑status architecture.

 This is a cross‑domain incidence map , aligning directly with SR’s CEIDF and Harm Stack logic.

 6. It provides a falsifiable empirical design
