 Description 
 National Statistical Representation Dynamics (SR‑NSRD) is an autonomous SR field that studies how national statistical systems convert complex, heterogeneous population conditions into simplified institutional representations — and how those representations can diverge from the underlying population state even when the measurements themselves are accurate.

 The field distinguishes measurement accuracy from representational completeness , showing that individually valid indicators (GDP, unemployment, CPI, wages, vacancies) can still produce an incomplete or misleading picture of national conditions when:

 aggregation compresses heterogeneous states

 denominators hide labour‑force detachment

 distributional differences are averaged away

 occupational and sectoral recomposition is ignored

 proxy variables (GDP, CPI, unemployment) are treated as full representations

 siloed domains (labour, housing, inflation, debt) are interpreted independently

 temporal asynchrony hides pressure migration

 measurement architectures produce different but valid estimates

 institutions experience epistemic lock‑in

 Your manuscript states:

 “Measurement accuracy does not imply representational completeness.”

 And:

 “Data availability does not imply institutional integration.”

 SR‑NSRD formalizes Population Representation Divergence (PRD) — the measurable distance between the decomposed population state and the operative institutional representation — and identifies conditions under which divergence becomes persistent even after institutions possess the missing data.

 The field was validated through a randomized cross‑national test (Belgium, Mexico, Finland, Costa Rica, Luxembourg, Spain, Iceland, Norway, UK, Italy) plus Canada and the United States. Spain served as a negative control , proving SR‑NSRD does not predict universal divergence.
