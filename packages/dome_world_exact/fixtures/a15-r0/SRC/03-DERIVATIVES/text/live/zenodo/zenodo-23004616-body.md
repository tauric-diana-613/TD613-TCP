# SOCIETY AS A CAGE: Structural Entrapment, Executable Freedom, and the Difference Between Having a Choice and Being Able to Use It

 Description 
 Society as a Cage is an adversarial Collapse Psychology manuscript that examines how subjective experiences of entrapment relate to the actual executable life paths available to a person once all binding constraints across domains are represented simultaneously. The paper does not treat “society as a cage” as a universal condition or ideological claim. Instead, it operationalizes entrapment through three separable constructs:

 Nominal Choice Set (NCS): options formally available or legally permitted

 Perceived Choice Set (PCS): options the person believes are realistically available

 Executable Choice Set (ECS): options that remain genuinely feasible after financial, temporal, caregiving, transport, housing, administrative, health, and social constraints are applied

 The manuscript introduces two provisional quantitative constructs:

 Executable Freedom Ratio (EFR): the proportion of nominal options that remain executable

 Structural Entrapment Index (SEI = 1 − EFR): the corresponding constraint load

 It then compares perceived entrapment with reconstructed structural entrapment to identify four states:

 Calibrated freedom 

 Psychological over‑entrapment 

 Hidden or normalized entrapment 

 Calibrated structural entrapment 

 The paper performs a strict adversarial review against the strongest conventional literatures — entrapment psychology, reactance, system justification, capability theory, administrative burden, job lock, scarcity research — and shows that none of these fields independently reconstruct executable life paths across multiple coupled constraints. The residual SR contribution is therefore architectural rather than conceptual: a reproducible method for reconstructing feasible action sets and testing whether subjective entrapment is calibrated to structural conditions.

 The manuscript ends with a preregisterable empirical protocol designed to allow the SR residual to disappear if conventional models match or exceed its explanatory and predictive value.

 By comparing perceived entrapment with reconstructed structural entrapment, the paper identifies:

 Calibrated structural entrapment 

 This separation is essential for intervention design, clinical interpretation, and cross‑domain causal reconstruction.

 4. Introduces the Executable Freedom Ratio (EFR) and Structural Entrapment Index (SEI) 
 EFR and SEI provide:

 These constructs allow Collapse Psychology to generate falsifiable predictions about behavior, distress, and decision‑making.

 5. Defines “cage conditions” as extreme, multi‑domain structural states 
 The manuscript clarifies that cage conditions exist only when:

 multiple high‑value options remain nominally available

 This prevents overgeneralization and preserves scientific boundaries.
