 Description 
 When Futures Become Unthinkable examines how a person’s imagined future relates to the actual future states they can realistically reach once all binding constraints across domains are represented simultaneously. The manuscript does not claim novelty over psychology’s existing work on episodic future thinking , hope theory , aspirations research , or neighborhood opportunity . Instead, it identifies a narrow SR object: future‑space calibration .

 The paper separates four layers of future possibility:

 Generated futures — futures a person can mentally produce

 Believed futures — futures the person considers plausible

 Planned futures — futures linked to an intended route

 Executable futures — futures that remain feasible after constraints are applied

 SR reconstructs the reachable future space by testing whether each future passes through its required gates: income, liquidity, time, caregiving, transport, geography, housing, eligibility, documentation, health, administrative friction, and institutional reliability. This reconstruction is then compared with subjective future plausibility to identify calibration states.

 The manuscript provides a falsifiable, preregisterable protocol for comparing psychological future‑thinking measures with explicit reconstructions of reachable opportunity. It distinguishes imagination compression from opportunity compression and specifies longitudinal, natural‑experiment, and demotion rules.
