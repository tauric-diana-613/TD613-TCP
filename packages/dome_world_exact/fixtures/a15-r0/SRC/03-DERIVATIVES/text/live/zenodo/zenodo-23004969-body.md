 Collapse Psychology — Adversarial Test II (2026) 

 Description 
 Control Before Helplessness examines how perceived control relates to the actual set of actions a person can execute once all relevant constraints across domains are represented simultaneously. The manuscript does not claim novelty over psychology’s extensive work on learned helplessness , perceived control , self‑efficacy , locus of control , capability theory , time poverty , or administrative burden . Instead, it identifies a narrow SR object: executable control .

 The paper separates four layers of control:

 Nominal control — options formally available

 Executable control — options that remain feasible after constraints are applied

 Perceived control — what the person believes they can influence

 Realized control — whether action actually changes the outcome

 SR reconstructs the feasible action set by testing whether each action passes through its required gates: liquidity, time, eligibility, household obligations, transport, documentation, information, digital access, geographic access, and acceptable penalty risk. This reconstruction is then compared with perceived control to identify calibration states.

 The manuscript provides a falsifiable, preregisterable protocol for comparing psychological control measures with explicit reconstructions of executable action. It distinguishes perceived‑control distortion from calibrated low control and specifies longitudinal, natural‑experiment, and demotion rules.
