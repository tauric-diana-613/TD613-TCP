# Inflation as Pressure Transfer: An Infrastructural Theory of Price Continuity, Disinflation, and Structural Restoration

## Zenodo description

Inflation as Pressure Transfer: An Infrastructural Theory of Price Continuity, Disinflation, and Structural Restoration 

 Description 
 This manuscript reframes inflation within SR Systems Economics as an infrastructurally mediated allocation of unresolved economic pressure. It preserves the conventional measurable definition of inflation while introducing Inflationary Pressure Transfer as a distinct explanatory construct. The paper models how originating pressure—whether from supply, demand, finance, policy, conflict, climate, demography, or infrastructure—moves through production networks, contracts, buffers, and institutions, and is allocated among price and non‑price channels including margins, wages, employment, debt, quality, investment, and public balance sheets. It distinguishes disinflation from structural restoration, defines buffer dynamics and visibility lag, and establishes a formal pressure‑allocation architecture with testable hypotheses, measurement rules, negative cases, and falsification criteria. The manuscript positions SR Systems Economics relative to New Keynesian, monetarist, structuralist, Post Keynesian, conflict inflation, and production‑network research, and defines the terminal boundary with SR Collapse Economics. 

 How it Contributes 
 This paper provides the first SR‑canonical economic architecture capable of tracing where unresolved pressure goes when inflation rises, stabilizes, or falls. It integrates production networks, market structure, substitution limits, buffer depletion, household margin, and infrastructural determinism into a single pressure‑transfer ledger. The framework distinguishes stabilization from displacement and disinflation from genuine restoration, requiring evidence about the destination of pressure whenever headline inflation changes. It introduces a formal allocation vector, buffer‑dynamics equations, and visibility‑lag mechanisms, enabling prospective tests across price and non‑price channels. The manuscript establishes a falsifiable research programme with hypotheses on centrality, substitution, concentration, buffer delay, policy displacement, distributional incidence, restoration convergence, and collapse boundary conditions. Its contribution is a unified, testable theory of inflation as pressure routing rather than price movement alone, forming the economic foundation of SR Systems Economics and its boundary with SR Collapse Economics.
