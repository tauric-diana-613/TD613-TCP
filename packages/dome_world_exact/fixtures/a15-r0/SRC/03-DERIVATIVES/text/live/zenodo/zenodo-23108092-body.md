Human Capacity Load (IS‑HL): A Structural SR Subfield for Measuring Function‑Bound Human Demand, Accessible Capacity, and Renewable Effort 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Human Capacity Load (IS‑HL) is a canonical SR subfield within Infrastructure Science that measures the human work required to preserve or restore a declared function when the substrate is insufficient, fragmented, degraded, inaccessible, or under repair. Its scientific object is the relation between required work and accessible renewable human capacity , not general workload, staffing ratios, burnout, or occupational stress.

 Human Capacity Load distinguishes:

 routine work — ordinary demand under adequate substrate

 compensatory work — human substitution for missing or degraded substrate

 coordination work — human alignment across fragmented systems

 recovery work — human effort required to restore function after disruption

 accessible capacity — skill, time, access, authority, information, coverage, and replenishment

 renewal — the processes that restore human capacity over time

 load ratio — required work divided by accessible capacity

 compensatory margin — remaining renewable capacity

 capacity transition — how capacity changes across intervals

 The field provides a full SR measurement architecture for:

 work classification

 capacity estimation

 replenishment

 saturation

 compensatory inversion

 burden transfer

 longitudinal capacity trajectories

 Human Capacity Load survives only if its measures add incremental predictive, diagnostic, or intervention value beyond established workload, staffing, queueing, occupational, and human‑factors models.
