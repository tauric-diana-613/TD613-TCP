# Canada Essential Goods Dependency and Household Absorption: Coupled Essential‑Access Systems, Trade Exposure, Housing Rigidity, and Conditional Trajectories to 2031

## Zenodo description

Canada Essential Goods Dependency and Household Absorption: Coupled Essential‑Access Systems, Trade Exposure, Housing Rigidity, and Conditional Trajectories to 2031 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Canada Essential Goods Dependency and Household Absorption is a documentary synthesis and prospective study specification examining how Canadian households experience affordability pressure through a coupled essential‑access system rather than isolated sectors. The paper integrates Essential Goods Dependency, Survival Systems, ATLAS XIV, Infrastructural Hygiene, and Representational Displacement into a single national diagnostic architecture.

 The research object is the household’s coupled essential‑access system : shelter, food, energy, transportation, healthcare, care obligations, and debt service. Households draw from the same recurring income, liquid reserves, time, and care capacity; pressures in one domain propagate into others. As the manuscript states:

 “A household does not experience shelter, food, energy, transportation and healthcare as independent policy files. Each draws from the same recurring income, liquid reserves, time and care capacity.” 

 The paper identifies two trade transmission directions :

 import‑side exposure — imported food, energy, shipping disruption, exchange‑rate movements, tariffs, and intermediate input costs

 export‑side exposure — foreign demand, commodity prices, trade barriers, and revenue channels affecting employment, income, and public capacity

 Housing is treated as a rigidity and amplifier , not the sole origin of strain:

 “A house does not absorb the shock. The household absorbs it while trying to preserve housing.” 

 The manuscript documents observable household pressure through Statistics Canada, CMHC, FCAC, OSFI, and Bank of Canada evidence, including:

 shelter, food, and transportation representing 63.6% of average household consumption in 2023

 rising rent and mortgage difficulty

 borrowing for everyday expenses

 food insecurity

 mobility constraints

 financial stress

 It distinguishes exposure , adaptation , harm , and instability , and specifies the data required to differentiate essential‑goods compression from poverty, preference, or isolated shocks.

 The reference trajectory to 2031 is conditional , not predictive: coupled essential‑cost compression with unequal incidence and partial local relief. Restoration, continued compensation, progressive deterioration, and compound disruption remain competing branches.

 Representational Displacement is added as a falsifiable mechanism: sector indicators may remain technically valid while institutions lose the capacity to reconstruct the household condition those indicators were meant to represent.
