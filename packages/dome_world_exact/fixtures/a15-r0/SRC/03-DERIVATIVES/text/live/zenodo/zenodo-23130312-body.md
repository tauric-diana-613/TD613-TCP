Representational Falsification: A General Scientific Framework for Testing, Correcting, and Retiring Proxy‑Based Decision Systems 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Representational Falsification is an SR scientific‑governance mechanism that defines how institutions should test, challenge, correct, narrow, subordinate, suspend, or retire representations—scores, categories, dashboards, models, files, and algorithmic outputs—when adverse evidence shows that their authority no longer matches the governed condition.

 The field introduces:

 the representation‑use pair as the unit of adjudication

 independent reconstruction of the represented object

 severe testing designed to defeat the representation’s claim

 rival representations and negative cases

 transport tests across populations, horizons, and environments

 authority consequences tied to failure

 verification against independent outcomes

 a graded verdict ladder (retain → monitor → recalibrate → narrow → subordinate → suspend → retire)

 Representational Falsification distinguishes:

 limit disclosure (scientific caveats)

 limit preservation (self‑sealing disclaimers that protect authority without correction)

 It provides a nine‑stage protocol, twelve hypotheses, comparative designs, and rejection conditions for the framework itself.
It is linked to Representational Displacement , Ground Truth Discrepancy , and Infrastructural Epistemic Lock‑In .
