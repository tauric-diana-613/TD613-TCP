Recovery Architecture: SR Theory of Durable Substrate Restoration 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Recovery Architecture is an independently bounded SR subfield within Infrastructure Science . It studies the substrate configuration , repair sequence , and verified state changes required to restore a protected function durably after impairment, drift, or accumulated restoration deficit.

 Its central scientific object is the disturbance‑to‑durable‑restoration trajectory , defined by:

 the protected function

 the initiating condition

 the restoration deficit

 the repair graph and dependencies

 accessible recovery inputs

 authority and coordination

 clocks and buffers

 compensatory mechanisms

 service‑return and stabilization events

 structural repair

 recurrence window

 burden‑transfer pathways

 the durability record

 Recovery Architecture separates service return from durable restoration .
A function can return while:

 buffers remain empty

 debt continues rising

 human compensation remains extraordinary

 burden shifts to another population

 recurrence risk stays high

 Therefore, recovery is not inferred from uptime, project completion, expenditure, backlog movement, or temporary substitution. Durable recovery requires:

 verified function

 falling restoration deficit

 rebuilding slack

 non‑depleting compensation

 no equivalent burden transfer

 recurrence reduction

 The field defines:

 effective recovery capacity 

 recovery sufficiency 

 restoration deficit 

 temporal recovery margin 

 component‑preserving durability record 

 It survives only if its architectures improve prediction, bottleneck localization, recurrence discrimination, and false‑recovery detection beyond established models in resilience , reliability , maintainability , functional recovery , asset management , operations research , and project scheduling .
