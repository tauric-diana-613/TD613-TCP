# Aesthetic SR: SR Theory of Formal Sensory Mediation

## Zenodo description

Aesthetic SR: SR Theory of Formal Sensory Mediation 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Aesthetic SR is a canonical SR subfield within Experience and Value Science . Its irreducible object is formal sensory mediation — the causal effect of perceptual form on access, salience, orientation, approach, avoidance, credibility, participation, and anomaly visibility before symbolic content, functional performance, or moral legitimacy are evaluated.

 The field studies bounded formal sensory encounters , defined by:

 a configured artifact or environment

 a perceiver or population

 a sensory modality

 a task or exposure context

 a group position

 a comparison form

 a receiving outcome

 an observation horizon

 Aesthetic SR measures:

 coherence

 complexity

 hierarchy

 contrast

 salience

 rhythm

 symmetry

 congruence

 novelty

 legibility

 perceptual friction

 atmosphere

 aesthetic authority

 aesthetic concealment

 habituation

 form–function alignment

 Aesthetic SR does not govern:

 beauty as a universal property

 art as an institution

 taste as identity

 lived experience

 cognition

 symbolic meaning

 cultural convention

 narrative sequence

 harm or value

 ethical legitimacy

 It survives only when formal sensory variables remain independently measurable and add explanatory or predictive value after content, function, familiarity, culture, cognition, affect, preference, and usability are controlled.

 HOW IT CONTRIBUTES TO SR 
 1. It gives SR a formal sensory architecture 
 Before Aesthetic SR, SR had:

 Phenomenological SR for lived experience

 Cognitive SR for attention and interpretation

 Symbolic SR for meaning

 Cultural SR for convention

 But SR lacked a field that isolates form itself as a causal mediator.

 Aesthetic SR fills that gap.

 2. It formalizes the bounded aesthetic encounter 
 Aesthetic SR defines the encounter tuple:

 configured object → formal state → perceiver → modality → context → group position → outcome → horizon 

 This prevents:

 free‑floating aesthetic claims

 universal beauty assumptions

 unbounded preference statements

 Linked concept: Bounded Encounter 

 3. It introduces measurable formal variables 
 Aesthetic SR treats form as structured, falsifiable variables , not subjective impressions.

 It defines:

 coherence

 complexity

 hierarchy

 contrast

 rhythm

 symmetry

 congruence

 novelty

 legibility

 friction

 atmosphere

 authority

 concealment

 habituation

 alignment

 These become scientific objects , not aesthetic opinions.

 Linked concept: Formal Sensory Variables 

 4. It defines aesthetic authority and aesthetic concealment 
 Two of the most powerful SR contributions:

 Aesthetic Authority 
 Formal coherence or polish increases perceived credibility without evidence.

 Aesthetic Concealment 
 Styling reduces anomaly or risk detection without changing the anomaly.

 These mechanisms explain:

 why polished dashboards hide uncertainty

 why calm environments suppress urgency

 why styled interfaces mask risk

 why institutional aesthetics create perceived legitimacy

 Linked concept: Aesthetic Concealment 

 5. It introduces form–function alignment 
 Aesthetic SR measures whether the sensory hierarchy matches the functional hierarchy.

 Misalignment explains:

 interfaces that look clear but hide critical information

 documents that appear authoritative but misrepresent evidence

 environments that feel safe but contain hidden risk

 Linked concept: Form–Function Alignment 

 6. It provides salience redistribution as a causal mechanism 
 Aesthetic SR shows:

 Increasing prominence for one element reduces detection of others. 

 This is crucial for:

 anomaly detection

 risk visibility

 accessibility

 institutional communication

 interface design

 public‑facing systems

 Linked concept: Salience Redistribution 

 7. It defines formal sensory regimes 
 Aesthetic SR introduces nine regimes:

 coherent access

 decorative authority

 aesthetic concealment

 perceptual overload

 sterile legibility

 novelty capture

 habituated invisibility

 group‑dependent form

 form–function conflict

 These regimes allow SR to classify encounters without collapsing them into “beauty” or “preference.”

 Linked concept: Formal Sensory Regimes 

 8. It establishes bridges to other SR fields 
 Aesthetic SR connects to:

 Phenomenological SR

 Cognitive SR

 Symbolic SR

 Cultural SR

 Harm and Value Dynamics

 Ethical SR

 Infrastructure Science

 Each bridge preserves:

 transfer object

 pathway

 receiving limit

 falsifier

 temporal alignment

 This prevents aesthetic effects from being mistaken for:

 lived experience

 cognition

 meaning

 harm

 legitimacy

 usability

 Linked concept: Bridge Discipline 

 9. It introduces falsifiable hypotheses for aesthetics 
 Aesthetic SR defines ten hypotheses testing:

 mediation

 salience redistribution

 alignment

 authority

 concealment

 habituation

 group dependence

 multimodal congruence

 bridge discipline

 incremental value

 This makes aesthetics a scientific , not subjective, domain.

 Linked concept: Falsification Discipline 

 10. It becomes SR’s formal sensory foundation 
 Aesthetic SR is the missing field that allows SR to analyze:

 how systems appear

 how systems guide attention

 how systems hide anomalies

 how systems create perceived authority

 how systems shape participation

 how systems influence access

 It completes SR’s experiential architecture.

 Linked concept: Experience and Value Science
