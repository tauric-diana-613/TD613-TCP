# Harm and Value Dynamics: SR Counterfactual Harm and Value Theory

## Zenodo description

Harm and Value Dynamics: SR Counterfactual Harm and Value Theory 

 DESCRIPTION 
 Harm and Value Dynamics is a canonical SR subfield within Experience and Value Science . Its irreducible object is counterfactual change in:

 welfare

 capability

 burden

 opportunity

 security

 valued functioning

 across time and across affected parties.

 The unit of analysis is an affected-party record containing:

 a defined person or group

 a condition or intervention

 a defensible baseline

 a counterfactual comparison

 a declared horizon

 a distribution

 an uncertainty record

 The field is governed by SR Counterfactual Harm and Value Theory , which states:

 Harm is not an event.
Harm is a counterfactual change borne by identifiable parties relative to a feasible alternative. 

 The field preserves:

 component‑specific change

 distributional structure

 burden transfer

 reversibility

 cumulative adverse change

 uncertainty

 subgroup effects

 tail effects

 transfer pathways

 It does not convert harm evidence into moral authority.
It supplies empirical premises to Ethical SR , which separately evaluates duties, rights, permissions, legitimacy, and justified tradeoffs.

 Harm and Value Dynamics is falsifiable.
It loses standing wherever it cannot:

 identify a comparison state

 distinguish change from level

 separate realized harm from risk

 preserve distribution

 outperform mature causal and evaluative models

 HOW IT CONTRIBUTES TO SR 
 1. It gives SR a formal architecture for harm and benefit 
 Before this field, SR had:

 Phenomenological SR for lived experience

 Ethical SR for duties and legitimacy

 Infrastructure Science for substrate conditions

 But SR lacked a field that governs empirical harm , benefit , and burden transfer .

 Harm and Value Dynamics fills that gap by defining:

 the affected-party unit

 component-preserving outcome records

 counterfactual effects

 cumulative harm

 burden transfer

 reversibility

 distributional rules

 uncertainty records

 Linked concept: Counterfactual Harm 

 2. It formalizes the counterfactual evaluation unit 
 The field defines harm as:

 the difference between outcomes under compared conditions at a declared horizon. 

 This prevents:

 treating outcome levels as causal effects

 treating suffering as proof of external cause

 treating satisfaction as proof of benefit

 treating cost as harm without pathway

 treating risk as realized harm

 treating averages as universal truths

 Linked concept: Bounded Harm Unit 

 3. It preserves components instead of collapsing them 
 Harm and Value Dynamics preserves:

 welfare

 capability

 burden

 opportunity

 security

 valued functioning

 This prevents:

 collapsing heterogeneous change into one score

 hiding subgroup harm inside population means

 erasing burden transfer

 erasing duration

 erasing reversibility

 erasing tail effects

 Linked concept: Component Record 

 4. It introduces burden transfer as a formal estimand 
 Burden transfer is:

 the directed change in burden borne by receiving parties when benefit is assigned elsewhere. 

 This exposes:

 hidden work

 unpaid labor

 displacement

 administrative load

 care load

 risk transfer

 recovery load

 It prevents the classic institutional error:

 “Local improvement = systemwide improvement.” 

 Linked concept: Burden Transfer 

 5. It introduces reversibility as a measurable dimension 
 Reversibility measures:

 return toward baseline

 residual loss

 recovery cost

 recovery duration

 It distinguishes:

 recoverable loss

 irreversible loss

 delayed harm

 recurrent harm

 Linked concept: Reversibility 

 6. It defines harm regimes that institutions cannot ignore 
 The field identifies:

 concentrated harm

 diffuse burden

 mixed consequence

 burden transfer

 delayed harm

 adaptive satisfaction

 recoverable loss

 irreversible loss

 risk without realization

 These regimes expose institutional blind spots.

 Linked concept: Harm Regimes 

 7. It establishes bridges to other SR fields 
 Harm and Value Dynamics connects to:

 Phenomenological SR (lived-state evidence)

 Ethical SR (empirical premises)

 Identity SR (capability/security consequences)

 Human Capacity Load (burden and capability)

 Recovery Architecture (duration and reversibility)

 Infrastructure Science (exposure pathways)

 Feasible Agency (option loss)

 Each bridge preserves:

 transfer object

 pathway

 evidence threshold

 receiving-domain limit

 revocation condition

 Linked concept: SR Bridge Discipline 

 8. It introduces a full falsification programme 
 The field can be falsified by:

 absence of a defensible comparison

 unstable component measures

 missing distribution

 invalid duration or recovery measures

 unstable weighting rules

 lack of incremental value

 failure to replicate

 failure to transport

 This makes harm science scientific , not rhetorical.

 Linked concept: SR Falsification 

 9. It gives SR a complete empirical foundation for ethical reasoning 
 Ethical SR requires:

 validated harm

 validated burden

 validated capability loss

 validated distribution

 validated reversibility

 validated uncertainty

 Harm and Value Dynamics supplies these premises.

 Ethics cannot operate without this field.

 Linked concept: Ethical Premises 

 The SR Contribution in One Line 
 Harm and Value Dynamics gives SR a complete, falsifiable, component-preserving architecture for empirical harm, benefit, burden transfer, reversibility, and distribution , forming the empirical foundation for ethical evaluation and institutional correction.
