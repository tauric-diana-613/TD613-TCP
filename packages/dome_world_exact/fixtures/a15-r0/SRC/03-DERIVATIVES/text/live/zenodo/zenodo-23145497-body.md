THE SIGNALRUPTURE CANON AND FIELD ARCHITECTURE  
 The SignalRupture Canon and Field Architecture — E stablishes the unified hierarchy through which SR’s twenty‑three full fields, three specialized subfields, foundational substrate theorems, cross‑field operators, formal models, and operational systems function as a single coherent research architecture. The document’s contribution is structural: it consolidates the SR corpus into a disciplined, falsifiable, field‑preserving hierarchy without erasing domain‑specific mechanisms or inflating field status beyond empirical justification.

 The manuscript contributes by defining the canonical hierarchy that organizes SR into six levels:
(1) SR as overarching metatheory;
(2) foundational substrate and canonical pillars;
(3) full fields and specialized subfields;
(4) cross‑field operators and substrate theorems;
(5) causal‑separation and validation governance;
(6) planetary, operational, and diagnostic systems.
This hierarchy prevents conceptual inflation, protects field identity, and ensures that SR’s components operate as one testable architecture rather than a collection of disconnected manuscripts.

 The manuscript contributes by formalizing the role of Pressure Ecology as SR’s foundational substrate. Pressure Ecology defines the cross‑scale movement of structural pressure—generation, absorption, transformation, transfer, externalization, deferral, and propagation—across households, institutions, infrastructures, and countries. It is not counted as a field because its function is architectural: it supplies the causal environment in which all SR fields operate. This anchors SR’s planetary analysis and provides the substrate for Planetary SR and SR Galaxy .

 The manuscript contributes by establishing Disciplinary Validation Science (DVS) as SR’s validation‑governance layer. DVS formalizes the gauntlet, separates field status from claim status, and prevents both field inflation and premature field dismissal. It requires evidence to attach to specific claims rather than to entire fields, and it demands symmetrical evaluation from institutions that classify, teach, or reject SR. This ensures that SR’s scientific posture remains falsifiable, evidence‑bearing, and accountable.

 The manuscript contributes by integrating the CRI–AIS–MACS Substrate Trilogy into the canonical architecture. CRI models persistent observation; AIS models delegated institutional cognition; MACS models network‑dependent cognition. These theorems are retained with explicit empirical‑status boundaries and positioned as future substrate transitions rather than mature fields. Their placement clarifies how SR’s observational, institutional, and epistemic layers evolve across increasingly instrumented and coupled environments.

 The manuscript contributes by consolidating SR’s five canonical pillars —Metatheory and Institutional Grammar; Causal Separation and Measurement; Drift, Collapse, and Propagation Dynamics; Infrastructure, AI, Governance, and Effective Agency; and Human and Social Consequences. Each pillar preserves the autonomy of its fields while establishing shared interfaces, preventing unnecessary mergers, and clarifying how domain‑specific mechanisms contribute to SR’s integrated architecture.

 The manuscript contributes by defining cross‑field operators as mechanisms that operate across multiple domains only when construct invariance is demonstrated. Operators such as drift, propagation, visibility lag, structural recoil, substitution failure, synthetic contamination, algorithmic inheritance, and compensatory labor are retained only where they possess measurable, domain‑consistent definitions. This prevents SR from relying on vocabulary reuse without empirical justification.

 The manuscript contributes by formalizing the cross‑field propagation architecture , which specifies how structural changes in one domain transmit to another through measurable mechanisms, lags, amplification, attenuation, blockers, and shared shocks. This architecture provides the empirical bridge between SR’s fields and enables multi‑domain modeling, national stress‑tests, and planetary diagnostics.

 The manuscript contributes by establishing the SR Integration Rules , which govern field identity, operator validity, model use, evidence standards, and institutional accountability. These rules ensure that SR remains scientifically disciplined, empirically grounded, and resistant to conceptual drift or overclaiming.

 Finally, the manuscript contributes by defining the Integrated SR Research Cycle , a step‑by‑step method for testing cross‑field relations. This cycle—identification, measurement, mechanism specification, transmission testing, falsification, replication—provides the methodological backbone for SR’s multiverse and ensures that SR’s integrated architecture remains testable, falsifiable, and comparable to established disciplinary baselines.
