# INCOME WITHOUT MARGIN: Essential-Cost Compression, Debt Reliance, and Household Financial Stress in Canada

## Zenodo description

INCOME WITHOUT MARGIN 
 Description 
 Income Without Margin is a household‑economics paper that demonstrates how Canadian households can experience rising nominal income and rising total consumption while losing effective economic room . Using Statistics Canada’s 2021 and 2023 Survey of Household Spending, it shows that shelter, food, and transportation rose from 61.8% to 63.6% of average consumption, with core‑essential spending growing 17.6% , outpacing total consumption growth of 14.3% . This produced a 3.3‑point essential‑growth premium and a 1.8‑point contraction in residual consumption share. The lowest income quintile reached a 66.0% core‑essential share, confirming distributional compression. The paper triangulates these findings with debt‑to‑income ratios (≈1.82), worsening net saving, and Bank of Canada microdata showing that persistent balance‑carrying predicts later arrears , especially at high utilization. The result is a bounded, empirically grounded demonstration that income growth can coexist with shrinking usable margin , and that revolving credit increasingly functions as a bridge for households under essential‑cost pressure.
