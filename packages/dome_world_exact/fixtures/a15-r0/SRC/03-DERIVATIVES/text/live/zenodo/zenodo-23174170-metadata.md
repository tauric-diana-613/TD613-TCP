    "description": "<p>Cognitive SR studies how bounded actors construct decision-relevant representations from incomplete and unevenly accessible information. Its unit is an interpretive episode with a declared actor, analytical level, task, information field, observation boundary, prior state, representational system, memory conditions, confidence report, decision horizon, and evaluation criterion. The field applies to individual, team, institutional, and hybrid human-machine cognition only when the processing arrangement is operationally specified at the claimed level.</p>\n<p>SR Cognitive Adequacy Theory preserves the stages at which interpretation can fail. The theory records availability, attention, encoding, retrieval, updating, and calibration separately; defines a domain-specific cognitive correspondence measure when a defensible reference exists; and measures correction gain after feedback. This decomposition prevents a low-accuracy judgment from being attributed to attention when the information never entered the actor's field, or to memory when the decisive loss occurred during classification. It also prevents a successful outcome from being treated as evidence that every cognitive stage was adequate.</p>\n<p>The field inherits the institutional-cognition corpus while narrowing its claims. Structural Institutional Distortion, Institutional Drift, Compression Equilibrium, Governance Altitude Lock-In, and Oversight Stabilization Failure become testable programmes whose cognitive, symbolic, infrastructural, and execution components remain separable. Higher governance altitude is not presumed inaccurate, frontline proximity is not presumed truthful, and no participant is treated as an infallible ground source. Cognitive SR survives only where its component record improves error localization, held-out prediction, correction selection, or intervention performance beyond bounded rationality, distributed cognition, situation awareness, sensemaking, organizational attention, memory, learning, metacognition, decision theory, and relevant domain models.</p>",
