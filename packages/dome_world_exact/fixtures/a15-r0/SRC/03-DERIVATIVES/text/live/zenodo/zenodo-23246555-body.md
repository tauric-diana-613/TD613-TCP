# What Is Society: The SignalRupture Blueprint of Collective Life

## Zenodo description

Society is a historically formed and continually reproduced order of human relations supported by ecological, material, institutional, relational, and symbolic conditions. The SignalRupture blueprint explains how these conditions connect collective activity to its continuation, human consequences, and capacity for change. Societal Infrastructure Theory accounts for meaning, identity, norms, and legitimacy; Social Infrastructure Theory accounts for the settings and supports through which collective life becomes practicable. Pressure Ecology traces the distribution of demands, while Infrastructural Exposure Theory, Slow Harm, Systemic Erosion, and the Harm Stack explain how those demands enter experience and accumulate across domains. Eight functional layers organize the account: ecological conditions; material provision; reproduction and care; production and allocation; institutional coordination; meaning and norms; knowledge and visibility; and maintenance and transformation. The central distinction is between recorded continuity and the renewal of the conditions sustaining it. A society can maintain visible outputs through investment and cooperation, or through external compensation, depleted reserves, and deferred obligations. The paper connects these trajectories to power, classification, accountability, and repair. It provides a formal model, empirical evidence for specified mechanisms, worked applications, and nine falsifiable propositions. The resulting blueprint identifies how collective life is sustained, who carries its demands, and whether its supporting capacities are being renewed.
