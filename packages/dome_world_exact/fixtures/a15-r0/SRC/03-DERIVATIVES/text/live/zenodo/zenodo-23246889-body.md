# Foundations of SignalRupture: Preconditions, Substrate Formation, and Epistemic Revision

## Zenodo description

Substrate analysis explains how organized conditions enable, constrain, and sustain a function. It also raises a further question: how do those conditions become possible, acquire a particular arrangement, and remain subject to correction? This paper establishes the SR Foundations Programme as a shared research and revision programme within SignalRupture. A structured audit of six SR manuscripts finds that the architecture already specifies functional boundaries, reciprocal feedback, comparator models, claim-specific falsification, and scientific demotion. The unresolved task is therefore selective. It concerns the consistent treatment of physical and biological preconditions, the formation and reproduction of dependencies and requirements, and the translation of scientific revision into changes in institutional implementations. The programme organizes these tasks into three strands: Physical and Biological Preconditions; Substrate Formation and Selection; and Epistemic Foundations and Revision. Relevant literature already explains infrastructural development, increasing returns, adaptive networks, construct validity, causal transportability, and maintenance dependencies. The proposed contribution is a common protocol for connecting those explanations to SR's existing objects and for testing whether the connection improves causal discrimination, prospective prediction, intervention choice, or correction. The paper defines bounded explanatory depth, a conditional formation module, a gap register, six prospective hypotheses, comparative designs, stopping rules, and an institutional migration protocol. The programme preserves the three metatheories. Its establishment does not certify empirical superiority or the exhaustiveness of SR's ontology. Scientific durability requires documented coverage and executable revision.
