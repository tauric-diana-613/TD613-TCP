# ACCOUNTABILITY ASYMMETRY: Distributed Causation, Responsibility Compression, and the Unequal Adjudication of Individuals and Institutions

## Zenodo description

Accountability Asymmetry: Distributed Causation, Responsibility Compression, and the Unequal Adjudication of Individuals and Institutions develops a general framework for analyzing situations in which individual and system-level causal contributions coexist but are subjected to materially different forms of accountability.

 The paper introduces Accountability Asymmetry as the unequal capacity to reconstruct, compel evidence about, adjudicate, sanction, correct, and verify responsibility across different causal levels of the same harmful event. It also introduces Responsibility Compression, describing how a complex causal history may become concentrated onto the most legally or institutionally adjudicable actor while upstream institutional contributions remain fragmented across regulators, professional bodies, inquiries, civil processes, administrative reviews, or no single forum at all.

 The framework distinguishes individual accountability from institutional accountability, criminal closure from causal closure, and verdict closure from system correction. It does not argue that systemic contribution eliminates individual agency or responsibility. Instead, it proposes that responsibility should be differentiated according to demonstrated causal contribution, authority, knowledge, foreseeability, feasible alternatives, and capacity for correction.

 The manuscript situates the framework within established research on organizational accountability, distributed responsibility, systems accidents, normalization of deviance, just culture, regulatory governance, and blame avoidance. Historical system-level investigations, including the Columbia Accident Investigation Board and the Grenfell Tower Inquiry, are used to demonstrate that distributed causation can be reconstructed and that Accountability Asymmetry is not inevitable.

 A bounded discussion of the 2026 Lindsay Clancy mistrial is included as a contemporary illustration of the distinction between adjudicating an individual defendant and reconstructing the broader systems surrounding a catastrophic event. The paper does not determine criminal responsibility or institutional liability in that case.

 The manuscript develops measurable dimensions, seven falsifiable hypotheses, comparative research designs, negative cases, and explicit conditions under which the framework should be narrowed or rejected.

 This work extends earlier SignalRupture research on The Manufactured Burden of Personal Responsibility, Infrastructural Harm & Legal Blindness, and the Institutional Self-Implication Penalty, converting the earlier accountability problem into a more bounded and testable architecture.

 Research status: Theoretical framework and falsifiable research programme; not yet independently validated as a general law.

 Keywords: accountability asymmetry; responsibility compression; distributed causation; institutional accountability; individual responsibility; system accountability; legal responsibility; organizational failure; causal reconstruction; institutional self-implication; systems theory; justice; governance; SignalRupture.
