# THE INSTITUTIONAL VISIBILITY GAP: How Society Becomes Legible to People While Its Architecture Becomes Invisible

## Zenodo description

THE INSTITUTIONAL VISIBILITY GAP

 How Society Becomes Legible to People While Its Architecture Becomes Invisible

 SignalRupture26 | Independent Systems Research | October 2026

 Description

 The Institutional Visibility Gap introduces a SignalRupture diagnostic framework examining why modern societies can produce increasingly sophisticated scientific knowledge about human suffering while remaining unable to adequately reconstruct or correct the institutional conditions contributing to that suffering.

 The paper identifies the Terminal-Subject Inversion: a recurring analytical condition in which the individual becomes the primary object of scientific assessment, diagnosis, and intervention despite being situated downstream from institutional, economic, administrative, and infrastructural processes.

 Drawing on psychology, medicine, economics, sociology, public administration, and systems research, the study distinguishes the visibility of human consequences from the causal legibility of the arrangements producing them.

 The framework integrates the Infrastructural Matrix, Pressure Ecology, Buffer Transfer, Structural Restoration Dynamics, Governance Altitude Lock-In, Infrastructural Epistemic Lock-In, and the Authorization–Executability Gap.

 A Canadian homelessness case study and a hypothetical Terminal-Subject Reset Experiment illustrate how successfully addressing an existing affected population does not necessarily eliminate the conditions generating new cases. The analysis further examines how financial costs, institutional dependencies, fragmented authority, and limited implementation capacity may prevent upstream restoration even when relevant causal evidence is available.

 Eight proposed, falsifiable propositions and a diagnostic methodology distinguish scientific observation, causal understanding, institutional execution, and durable structural restoration.

 Contribution to SignalRupture and Scientific Research

 This paper extends SignalRupture by identifying a critical distinction between the human being as the observable location of institutional consequences and the institutional architecture as a potential location of their causal production.

 Its principal contributions are:

 Terminal-Subject Inversion: Formalizes the risk of confusing the downstream manifestation of a social problem with its complete causal explanation. 
 Institutional Visibility Gap: Establishes a diagnostic framework for investigating incomplete recognition of upstream institutional causes. 
 Knowledge–Restoration Decoupling: Explains why possessing accurate structural knowledge does not automatically produce the authority, resources, coordination, or capacity necessary to implement corrective change. 
 Infrastructural Lock-In: Connects persistent social problems with the costs and dependencies that constrain institutional reconstruction. 
 Empirical Application: Uses Canadian homelessness trends and a stock-and-flow thought experiment to distinguish reducing an existing affected population from preventing the recurrence of the condition. 
 Structural Restoration: Introduces testable criteria for distinguishing temporary relief, downstream management, and durable institutional correction. 
 
 The paper’s broader scientific contribution is a method for evaluating whether interventions change the conditions producing adverse outcomes or primarily manage their observable consequences.

 Rather than rejecting individual-centred sciences, the framework specifies when their explanatory and restorative scope may be insufficient and how they can be integrated with upstream institutional analysis.

 Central proposition: A society may progressively improve its capacity to observe, diagnose, classify, and manage the human consequences of institutional arrangements without achieving a corresponding capacity to reconstruct and restore the arrangements contributing to those consequences.

 The Institutional Visibility Gap provides a proposed diagnostic bridge between scientific knowledge, institutional architecture, effective agency, and structural restoration. Its claims remain subject to empirical testing and comparison with established scientific approaches.
