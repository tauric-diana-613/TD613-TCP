# THE SR EARTH ORBIT SYSTEMS PROGRAMME: A Unified Architecture for Orbital Infrastructure Terrestrial Dependence Authority and Recovery

## Zenodo description

The SR Earth Orbit Systems Programme is a cross‑field research programme within SignalRupture that investigates how orbital conditions, space infrastructure, terrestrial dependence, institutional authority, and recovery capacity interact as a single operational system. Rather than proposing a new scientific field, the programme serves as an integrative architecture that connects established knowledge from space engineering, critical infrastructure analysis, space weather science, governance, law, economics, resilience research, and sustainability studies. Its central object is the Earth‑orbit infrastructural relation: the chain through which orbital environments affect services, services support terrestrial functions, institutions rely upon those functions, populations experience the consequences, and recovery systems determine resilience. The programme introduces the institution–function–service chain as its primary analytical unit and develops candidate constructs including the Orbital Dependency Recovery Gap, Institutional Sovereignty Decoupling, and Compensatory Continuity. Its scientific contribution is conditional and comparative: it succeeds only if integrated SR reconstruction improves explanation, prediction, intervention selection, or cross‑domain tracing beyond a fair combination of the strongest existing disciplinary models.

 HOW IT CONTRIBUTES

 1. It extends Infrastructure Science beyond Earth‑bound systems

 The programme treats orbital infrastructure as part of the same coupled substrate that supports communication, navigation, timing, forecasting, disaster response, finance, logistics, and governance. It enlarges Infrastructure Science from terrestrial assets to Earth–orbit dependency systems.

 2. It introduces the Earth‑orbit infrastructural relation as a formal object

 Rather than studying satellites, governance, resilience, or economics separately, the programme studies the operational chain that links them together. The primary object is not the spacecraft but the dependency relation between orbital systems and terrestrial functions.

 3. It operationalizes Institutional Sovereignty Decoupling

 The programme provides one of the strongest empirical environments for testing situations in which formal responsibility remains public while functional control resides elsewhere. It expands SR’s distinction between authority and effective control into orbital systems and strategic infrastructure.

 4. It introduces the Orbital Dependency Recovery Gap

 The Orbital Dependency Recovery Gap provides a structured way to distinguish dependence from vulnerability by measuring the relationship between exposure, substitution, recovery capacity, operational concentration, and restoration time.

 5. It formalizes compensatory continuity

 The programme demonstrates how services may appear stable while operators increasingly rely on avoidance manoeuvres, redundancy, reserve consumption, replacement assets, or hidden recovery work. This extends SR’s broader theory of visible continuity and hidden depletion into orbital infrastructure.

 6. It expands Propagation Geometry into planetary infrastructure

 Orbital systems create measurable pathways through which disruptions can propagate into communications, navigation, finance, electricity, emergency management, transportation, and population outcomes. The programme therefore serves as an operational test environment for transmission, dependency, and recovery analysis.

 7. It strengthens Infrastructural Determinism

 The Earth Orbit Systems Programme shows that many modern activities are executable only because specific orbital services remain available. It therefore provides a practical demonstration of how infrastructure shapes feasible action before individuals, organizations, or governments make decisions.

 8. It bridges infrastructure, governance, dependency, and recovery

 Most existing research stops within one layer. The programme follows the complete pathway:

 orbital condition → infrastructure → service → function → authority → consequence → recovery

 This makes it one of the strongest examples of SR’s bridge architecture and cross‑field integration logic.

 9. It establishes a comparative test of SR itself

 The programme explicitly commits to comparison against the strongest engineering, governance, resilience, legal, and economic models. It is therefore one of the most self‑falsifying programmes in the SR corpus and serves as a direct test of whether SR integration adds measurable value.

 10. It marks a transition from field creation to architectural integration

 The programme’s deepest contribution is methodological. It does not seek to create another field. Instead, it asks whether SR can reconstruct and govern relations among existing fields more effectively than those fields can reconstruct the whole independently. This represents a shift toward architectural maturity within the SR Multiverse.

 The central contribution is not outer space itself. The central contribution is a falsifiable architecture for tracing how orbital systems become social systems, how dependency becomes consequence, and how recovery becomes possible across coupled Earth–orbit infrastructures.
