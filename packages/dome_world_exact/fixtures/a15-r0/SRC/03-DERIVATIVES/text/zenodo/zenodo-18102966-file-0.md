Lived Experience — What This Looks Like for Real People For millions of people, the system feels like this: • You’re exhausted, but the doctor says you’re “fine.” • You’re anxious, but no one asks what you’re eating or if you can afford groceries. • You’re depressed, but no one asks about housing, stress, or safety. • You’re given medication, but not support. • You’re blamed for “non ‑ compliance” when the system never addressed the cause. • You’re told to “take responsibility” for conditions you didn’t create. This is not personal failure. This is structural design. SR’s morality on trial (2025d) argues that systems moralize survival behaviors to hide their own neglect. This essay extends that argument: people are not failing — the system is structured this way. The System Behind the Symptoms People experience: • long wait times • rushed appointments • misdiagnosis • medication dependence • preventable crises These are not failures. They are outputs of a system built around: • cost containment • pharmaceutical reliance • crisis ‑ driven care

• administrative throughput Starfield et al. (2005) show that strong primary care reduces illness, yet many systems underfund

Marmot (2020) shows that social determinants drive health outcomes. SR (2025d) argues that systems use moral language to mask structural neglect. Government Investment and Tax Return — Why Cuts Still Happen Despite Massive Public Spending Health care is the largest public expenditure in Canada (Canadian Institute for Health Information,

Healthy Populations Increase Revenue Healthy populations: • work more • earn more • pay more income tax • spend more (increasing sales tax revenue) • rely less on disability benefits This creates a positive fiscal return on public ‑ health investment. 2. Preventable Illness Shrinks the Tax Base Chronic illness reduces productivity and increases public spending (Marmot, 2020). This creates a double loss: lower tax revenue and higher costs. 3. Prevention Has the Highest ROI The WHO estimates that prevention yields one of the highest returns in public finance (World Health

2023). Yet prevention is politically invisible because its success looks like “nothing happened.” 4. The Fiscal Inversion The system becomes inverted: • High ‑ return investments (prevention) are cut • Low ‑ return expenditures (crisis care, pharmaceuticals) expand • Private profit increases • Public revenue stagnates

This is why governments invest billions yet still justify cuts: the money flows into the wrong part of the system. Marginalized Communities — The Majority Bearing the Burden Food insecurity, chronic stress, and environmental exposure disproportionately affect marginalized
