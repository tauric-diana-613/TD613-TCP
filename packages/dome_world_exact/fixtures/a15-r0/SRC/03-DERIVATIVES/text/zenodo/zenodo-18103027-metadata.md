    "description": "<p>This essay argues that modern mental‑health systems routinely misclassify biochemical distress as psychiatric disease, treating symptoms in isolation while ignoring the nutritional, metabolic, and infrastructural conditions that produce them. Drawing on emerging evidence in nutritional psychiatry, the paper shows how deficiencies in B‑complex vitamins, magnesium, and other micronutrients can mimic depression, anxiety, ADHD, irritability, cognitive impairment, and even psychosis. These deficiencies often manifest before neurological signs appear, yet clinical systems rarely screen for them.</p>\n<p>Integrating peer‑reviewed research, institutional medical summaries, and mechanistic insights from neuroscience, the essay demonstrates how micronutrients function as essential cofactors for neurotransmitter synthesis, myelin formation, and brain energy metabolism. It situates these biochemical pathways within broader structural determinants&mdash;food insecurity, ultra‑processed dietary patterns, economic precarity, and public‑health neglect&mdash;arguing that misdiagnosis is not random error but a predictable output of systems designed around fragments rather than whole‑person architecture.</p>\n<p>Global data reveal a planetary‑scale nutritional crisis, with billions lacking access to nutrient‑dense food and millions of children living in chronic biochemical instability. These conditions produce symptom clusters that map cleanly onto psychiatric diagnoses, yet the nutritional origins remain invisible within clinical practice. The essay positions this invisibility as infrastructural: a design flaw in public‑health systems that treat the part while ignoring the whole.</p>\n<p>By reframing psychiatric‑like symptoms as potential expressions of micronutrient deficiency and structural deprivation, the essay contributes to scholarship on misdiagnosis, nutritional psychiatry, and the social determinants of mental health. It calls for a systemic shift toward integrative assessment, nutritional screening, and infrastructural reform to prevent biochemical collapse from being absorbed into psychiatric categories.</p>\n<p>This essay is part of the SignalRupture canon, a body of work examining contemporary systems, infrastructures, and social dynamics through a conceptual and diagnostic lens. Each piece contributes to an ongoing analysis of structural stress, digital environments, governance patterns, and the evolving relationship between individuals and large‑scale systems. The work combines theoretical reflection with infrastructural observation, offering frameworks for understanding systemic erosion, cultural shifts, and emerging forms of social complexity.</p>",
