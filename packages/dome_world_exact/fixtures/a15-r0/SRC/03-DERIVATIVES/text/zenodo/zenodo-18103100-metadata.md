    "description": "<p>This paper argues that contemporary public health systems fail not because of temporary strain or insufficient resources, but because their underlying infrastructure is misaligned with the realities of trauma, chronic stress, nutritional depletion, and diagnostic complexity. Drawing on research in diagnostic error, allostatic load, nutritional psychiatry, and health‑system fragmentation, the essay reframes recurring failures&mdash;misdiagnosis, siloed care, preventable crises&mdash;as structural outputs rather than isolated mistakes.</p>\n<p>The analysis synthesizes four intersecting frameworks: misdiagnosis infrastructure, which channels patients into categorical labels without causal inquiry; allostatic load theory, which explains how chronic stress produces cumulative physiological wear; nutritional psychiatry, which highlights the biochemical foundations of mental health; and fragmentation theory, which exposes how disconnected services and misaligned incentives generate predictable harm. Through clinical vignettes and empirical evidence, the paper shows how trauma misclassification, metabolic neglect, and rushed assessment pathways convert complex human experiences into narrow diagnostic categories.</p>\n<p>By positioning public health failure as an infrastructural condition, the essay identifies system‑level levers for reform, including integrated care pathways, trauma‑informed assessment, nutritional evaluation, and updated public‑health metrics that reflect lived complexity rather than administrative convenience. The work contributes to scholarship on diagnostic safety, structural determinants of health, and health‑system design, offering a framework for understanding why preventable suffering persists across populations and decades.</p>\n<p>This essay is part of the SignalRupture canon, a body of work examining contemporary systems, infrastructures, and social dynamics through a conceptual and diagnostic lens. Each piece contributes to an ongoing analysis of structural stress, digital environments, governance patterns, and the evolving relationship between individuals and large‑scale systems. The work combines theoretical reflection with infrastructural observation, offering frameworks for understanding systemic erosion, cultural shifts, and emerging forms of social complexity.</p>",
