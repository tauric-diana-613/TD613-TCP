social ‑ media

Three

2. Commercial food infrastructure — ultra ‑ processed foods engineered to bypass satiety. 3. Biological infrastructure — metabolic pathways disrupted by chronic exposure to obesogenic environments. Together, these systems create a self ‑ reinforcing metabolic and psychological environment in which

obesity

emerges

not

from

personal

failure

but

from

engineered

conditions. Literature Review Algorithmic Targeting and Eating ‑ Disorder Vulnerability Alleva (2024) found that TikTok’s algorithm delivers “strikingly more problematic content” to users

eating ‑ disorder

extreme

dieting

pro ‑ anorexia

Greater

This

privilege

harmful

weight ‑ transformation

disordered

young

indicating

both

scale

and

moderation

social ‑ media

exposure

and

influencer ‑ driven

Lafontaine et al. (2025) concluded that the social ‑ media industry shapes the digital food environment

that

harm

child

and

adolescent

low ‑ satiety,

resistant

appetite ‑ regulation

this

by: • delivering rapid glucose spikes • bypassing normal digestive signaling • overwhelming reward circuits • reducing GLP ‑ 1 responsiveness over time This creates a biological environment of persistent hunger, independent of caloric intake. The rise

of

GLP ‑ 1

agonist

underscores

interfere

with

satiety

biology. Obesogens and Metabolic Disruption Obesogens are environmental chemicals that alter metabolic pathways, increase fat storage, and

disrupt

Chronic

plastics,

pesticides,

and

industrial

chemicals—creates: • increased adipocyte formation • altered insulin sensitivity • disrupted appetite regulation • long ‑ term metabolic programming This biochemical layer reinforces the digital and dietary infrastructures shaping obesity

Policy and Public ‑ Health Implications A systemic problem requires systemic solutions: • Regulate algorithmic targeting of vulnerable users. • Restrict ultra ‑ processed food marketing to children on digital platforms. • Mandate transparency in recommendation systems. • Integrate digital ‑ literacy education into school curricula. • Address obesogenic environments through food ‑ system reform. • Fund research on endocrine disruptors and metabolic programming. Limitations This essay synthesizes existing research but is limited by:

• variability in study methodologies • lack of long ‑ term data on algorithmic exposure • limited research on GLP ‑ 1 disruption from ultra ‑ processed foods • emerging but incomplete evidence on obesogens Future research should integrate digital, biological, and environmental data to map obesity as a unified

system. Conclusion Obesity is not an individual moral failing but a systemic condition produced by algorithmic targeting,

social ‑ media
