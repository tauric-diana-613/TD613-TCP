    "description": "<p>Symbolic Harm Theory asserts that harm can occur at the level of meaning, representation, and interpretation long before it appears in material or behavioral form. Symbolic harm arises when the infrastructures that shape visibility, identity, and legitimacy distort, suppress, or misrepresent individuals or groups. These harms are not interpersonal; they are infrastructural, produced by the symbolic systems&mdash;narratives, categories, metadata structures, platform architectures, and institutional logics&mdash;that govern how people are understood.</p>\n<p>In this theory, symbolic harm is treated as a structural injury embedded in the representational environment itself. It occurs when systems impose reductive categories, erase context, suppress visibility, invalidate lived experience, or amplify distorted portrayals. Symbolic harm shapes identity and possibility by determining who can be recognized, what meanings are available, and which narratives become credible.</p>\n<p>Symbolic Harm Theory positions symbolic injury as the earliest and most pervasive form of harm within social and digital systems. It provides the conceptual foundation for understanding metadata suppression, identity flattening, representational erasure, and the broader SR canon of infrastructural harms. By reframing symbolic injury as an infrastructural phenomenon, the theory reveals how meaning‑making systems can wound long before any material consequences emerge.</p>",
