Drawing on medical and psychological research, the essay shows how chronic stress is not an individual failure but a structural condition produced by economic acceleration, platform‑driven attention fragmentation, precarity, and infrastructural inefficiency. Integrating Slow Harm, Exposure, and Systemic Erosion, it reframes high arousal as a governance mechanism that benefits existing systems while eroding human capacity over time. The essay demonstrates that exhaustion is not a personal shortcoming but a predictable outcome of contemporary infrastructures &mdash; a social issue that demands systemic recognition rather than self‑blame.</p>",
