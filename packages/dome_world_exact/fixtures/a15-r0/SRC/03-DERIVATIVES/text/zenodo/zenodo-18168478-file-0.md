The Four Types of Exit Barriers 2.1 Frictional Barriers Micro ‑ delays and procedural hurdles that exhaust users into staying: • multi ‑ step verification • repeated identity checks • “try again later” loops • intentionally confusing menus 2.2 Structural Barriers Architectural decisions that make leaving technically impossible: • proprietary formats • incompatible exports • missing metadata • broken import tools 2.3 Identity ‑ Bound Barriers Mechanisms that bind the user’s identity to the platform: • login ‑ based ecosystems • platform ‑ specific social graphs • two ‑ factor authentication tied to proprietary apps 2.4 Temporal Barriers Time ‑ based mechanisms that weaponize delay:

• 30 ‑ day waiting periods • slow data exports • “cool ‑ off” periods • timed lockouts Together, these barriers metabolize containment into a systemic condition. 3. Logic Cops: The Invisible Enforcers of Containment Logic cops are not people. They are the rules, defaults, and automated decisions that enforce the platform’s internal logic. A logic cop says: • “You can export your data — but not in a usable format.” • “You can delete your account — but only after 30 days.” • “You can transfer your identity — but only if you stay within our ecosystem.” This is Quiet Governance in its purest form: the system enforces itself. 4. Administrative Trauma in Digital Form Exit barriers create a new category of harm: • loss of access to healthcare portals • inability to retrieve financial records • broken continuity of care • identity fragmentation • emotional exhaustion • procedural despair This is Administrative Trauma applied to digital infrastructure. The harm is not loud. It is procedural, quiet, and cumulative.
