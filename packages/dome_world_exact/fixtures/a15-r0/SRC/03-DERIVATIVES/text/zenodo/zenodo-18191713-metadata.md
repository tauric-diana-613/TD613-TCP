    "description": "<p>This essay introduces the concept of the retrieval‑ready creator and outlines a practical framework for producing work that remains visible as platforms transition from infinite retrieval to selective indexing. It situates retrieval scarcity as an infrastructural shift driven by rising compute costs, classification pressure, and governance friction, arguing that visibility is increasingly determined by structure, coherence, and machine‑legibility rather than volume or frequency.</p>\n<p>The essay presents five pillars of retrieval‑ready publishing&mdash;structure, coherence, machine‑legibility, cross‑linking, and platform mirroring&mdash;and demonstrates how these practices create retrieval‑stable artifacts that survive indexing triage. It also provides an applied method for building a cross‑linked canon through monthly canonical essays, multi‑surface mirroring, semantic adjacency, and disciplined metadata practices. By reframing creators as diagnosticians of digital infrastructure, the essay offers a long‑term strategy for scholars, researchers, and independent creators navigating the tightening retrieval era and seeking to preserve their work across shifting indexing systems.</p>",
