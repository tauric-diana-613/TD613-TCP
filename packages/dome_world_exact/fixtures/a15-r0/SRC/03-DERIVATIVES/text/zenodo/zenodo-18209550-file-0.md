A patient waits six weeks for approval for an MRI. During that time: • symptoms worsen • the likelihood of follow ‑ up decreases • billing cycles shift • the insurer’s liability is deferred The delay is profitable because attrition is profitable. Example: Disability claims A claimant is repeatedly asked for “additional documentation,” each request resetting the review clock. The system counts on exhaustion to reduce payouts. Temporal delay is not inefficiency. It is extraction. 3. Spatial Friction: Geography as Gatekeeper Spatial friction emerges where infrastructure is absent, unstable, or unevenly distributed. Example: Rural digital access A rural resident must drive 45 minutes to a library to upload documents for a government application. The system externalizes its infrastructural gaps onto the individual. Example: Transit deserts A worker in a transit desert must take two buses and a train to reach a government office before closing. One missed connection resets the entire process. Spatial delay is a map of who is allowed to move freely and who must pay for access. 4. Digital Friction: Suppression as Platform Logic

Digital friction is the most visible — and the most denied. Example: Algorithmic choke A creator posts critical analysis of platform governance. Engagement drops by 90% overnight. No violation notice. No explanation. The platform benefits because: • anxiety increases posting frequency • posting increases ad impressions • uncertainty conditions compliance Example: Delayed notifications A job seeker receives interview messages hours late due to “notification batching.” The delay reduces competitiveness while increasing platform engagement time. Digital delay is not a glitch. It is governance. 5. Legal Friction: Ambiguity as Industry Legal friction converts uncertainty into profit. Example: Immigration paperwork A form requires “proof of sufficient ties,” but the definition is intentionally vague. Applicants hire lawyers to interpret the ambiguity. The system generates a secondary market around its own opacity. Example: Workplace accommodations An employer delays responding to an accommodation request, citing “ongoing review.” The delay pushes the worker to resign, avoiding legal liability. Legal delay is not neutral. It is engineered.

6. Emotional Friction: Confusion as Script Emotional friction destabilizes the user’s sense of orientation. Example: Contradictory instructions A government portal instructs users to “upload original documents,” then rejects them for not being

“certified

Example: Customer service loops A user is transferred between five agents, each asking them to “explain the issue again.” The repetition is a form of cognitive extraction. Confusion is not collateral damage. It is a governance tool. 7. Economic Friction: Scarcity as Strategy Economic friction is the monetization of insufficiency. Example: Limited subsidies A housing subsidy opens at 9:00 a.m. and closes at 9:03 a.m. due to “overwhelming demand.” The scarcity drives: • predatory intermediaries • paid “application assistance” • black ‑ market queue ‑ jumping Example: Expedited services A passport office offers a “free” standard process with a 12 ‑ week delay — or a paid expedited option

available

immediately. Delay becomes a pricing model. Economic delay is not natural. It is manufactured.
