• modulate posting frequency • shape emotional investment • create dependency on platform feedback • encourage subscription to premium features • maintain the user’s sense of “almost” reaching an audience The numbers are not neutral. They are behavioral levers. A spike in views encourages more posting. A dip encourages anxiety and re ‑ engagement. A paywall encourages payment to restore the illusion of control. Metrics are not reflections of reality. They are reflections of platform incentives. 3. The Architecture of Omission The most important data is never shown. Platforms hide: • full readership • external referrals • search engine indexing • private shares • institutional access • long ‑ tail propagation • off ‑ platform circulation These flows exist. They shape the real reach of a text. But platforms withhold them because transparency would weaken their power. If users saw the full picture, they would understand: • the platform is not the center of their visibility • their work circulates independently of the platform • the platform’s metrics are a narrow slice of a much larger ecosystem

Opacity protects the infrastructure. 4. Visibility as a Manufactured Commodity Platforms create artificial scarcity by withholding analytics, then sell access back to the user. “Upgrade to see who viewed your paper.” “Upgrade to unlock advanced analytics.” “Upgrade to understand your audience.” The platform manufactures the problem — invisibility — and monetizes the solution — partial visibility. This is infrastructural rent ‑ seeking disguised as insight. Visibility becomes a commodity, not a right. Analytics become a product, not a tool. The user becomes a customer of their own audience. 5. The Psychological Infrastructure of Metrics Curated metrics do not just shape behavior. They shape self ‑ perception. Users begin to equate: • platform visibility with real visibility • platform analytics with real readership • platform feedback with real impact This is cognitive offloading at the level of identity. When metrics disappear, users feel a loss of audience, even though the audience never left. What

6. The Violence of Partial Transparency Partial transparency is more manipulative than total opacity. Total opacity is honest: “You cannot see anything.” Partial transparency is strategic: “You can see only what we want you to see.” This selective visibility: • shapes what users believe about their reach • influences how they interpret their own work • governs their publishing rhythm • creates emotional dependency • distorts their sense of scale This is a form of infrastructural violence — the manipulation of epistemic access to one’s own output. The platform controls not only what is visible, but what becomes thinkable. 7. The Post ‑ Web Shift: From Open Visibility to Curated Legibility The early web allowed raw signals: • referrer logs • backlinks • server analytics • open indexing Users could see how people arrived, where they came from, and how their work circulated. The platformized web replaced this with curated legibility: • dashboards

• simplified metrics • paywalled analytics • algorithmic summaries Users no longer see the world. They see the platform’s interpretation of the world. This is the Post ‑ Web Shift: from open visibility to curated legibility. 8.
