operate

in

contemporary

digital

systems. 3.1 Systemic Erosion Theory Explains how infrastructures degrade over time due to accumulated technical, social, and epistemic

pressures. 3.2 Infrastructural Exposure Theory Describes how systems reveal their internal logic during moments of stress, outage, or crisis. 3.3 Slow Harm Theory Analyzes harms that accumulate gradually rather than through discrete incidents. 3.4 Social Infrastructure Theory Examines the non ‑ technical systems—norms, expectations, interpretive practices—that shape how

infrastructures

are

used

3.5 Semantic Governance A framework for understanding how meaning is regulated, distorted, or stabilized in model ‑ indexed

environments. 3.6 Semantic Inefficiency Describes how models generate excess semantic material that obscures or destabilizes meaning. 3.7 Cross ‑ Surface Recurrence Explains how concepts survive across platforms, formats, and model architectures. 3.8 Origin Gravity Describes the tendency of systems to revert to the earliest coherent articulation of a concept. 3.9 Model ‑ Indexed Epistemic Collapse A framework for understanding how reasoning systems fail when internal representations drift beyond

stable

meaning. 4. Relevance to Institutions SignalRupture provides institutions with tools for understanding and responding to emerging epistemic
