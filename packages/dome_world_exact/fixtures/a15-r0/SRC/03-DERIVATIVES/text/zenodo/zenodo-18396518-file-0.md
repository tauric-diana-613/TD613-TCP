1. The Open Web as Commons For two decades, the web functioned as a shared surface: • links were public • pages were stable • search results were broadly consistent • verification was possible • context was inspectable Even when platforms distorted attention, the underlying web remained accessible. The personalized browse removes that escape hatch. Search results become individualized. Answers are generated. Visibility is filtered. Context is stripped. Provenance is blurred. You no longer browse the web. You browse your web. 2. From Attention Fragmentation to Truth Fragmentation Social media fractured attention. The personalized browse fractures truth. In the feed era, people lived in parallel emotional worlds. But the underlying facts were still findable. The personalized browse collapses that layer. Two people can now ask the same question and receive: • different facts • different interpretations • different historical framings • different causal explanations • different “truths”

This is epistemic atomization. It is the moment the SR canon’s MIEC framework describes: the collapse of shared epistemic infrastructure into individualized cognitive environments. 3. AI as Gatekeeper In the open web, you navigated. In the personalized browse, you are navigated. AI systems now decide: • what is relevant • what is credible • what is visible • what is suppressed • what is too complex • what is too destabilizing This is not a search engine. This is a mediated epistemic environment. The SR canon’s Infrastructural Exposure Theory maps this shift: systems that hide their mechanics while shaping cognition become structurally hostile. 4. Identity Under Personalization Identity becomes unstable when visibility is personalized. Every person sees a different version of you: • different posts • different interpretations • different visibility • different credibility signals

● Your public self fractures into algorithmically generated projections. This is the identity ‑ erosion layer of the post–open web. It is the environment that makes Stylometric Resistance and Forensic Authorship necessary—not

Metadata Collapse In the personalized browse: • pages are scraped • context is removed • structure is dissolved • authorship is blurred • provenance is lost Content becomes extractable material, not a stable artifact. This is the SR canon’s Metadata Suppression Theory in action: the slow erasure of the connective tissue that once held the web together. When metadata collapses, truth collapses with it. 6. Completion of the Manipulation Cycle We have already lived through one cycle of weaponization: • targeted political ads • algorithmic outrage • micro ‑ segmented propaganda • feed ‑ based reality distortion But these were primitive compared to what comes next. The personalized browse allows: • targeted knowledge suppression

• targeted knowledge amplification • targeted historical revision • targeted identity manipulation • targeted epistemic destabilization This is not persuasion. It is substrate control. 7. This is not polarization. It is the dissolution of the demos. The SR canon’s Social Infrastructure Theory explains this collapse: when the shared epistemic substrate dissolves, democratic stability dissolves with it. 8. The personalized browse is not the future. It is the present. Part II — Weaponization and Systemic Instability 9. Weaponization of Personalized Knowledge The personalized browse is a new cognitive battlefield. Manipulation no longer targets emotion. It targets epistemic grounding. Interpretive frames become individualized. Causality becomes malleable. Cognitive vulnerabilities become predictable. This is the ideal terrain for weaponization. A malicious actor no longer needs to persuade a population. It only needs to shape the knowledge substrate each individual receives. Propaganda competes with other narratives. Personalized knowledge eliminates the competition. 10. State and Platform Exploitation States and platforms now possess a precision instrument for shaping reality. A government can: • suppress facts for specific demographics • amplify narratives only to those most susceptible • rewrite historical memory for targeted groups • destabilize opponents by fragmenting their epistemic base • create parallel realities that never intersect Platforms can: • shape political outcomes through invisible ranking

• suppress dissent without accountability • curate reality to maximize engagement • algorithmically reward compliant narratives The personalized browse becomes a governance tool, a behavioral instrument, and a cognitive filter. 11. Systemic Instability A society cannot coordinate when its members inhabit incompatible realities. Systemic instability emerges when: • shared problems cannot be recognized collectively • shared solutions cannot be agreed upon • shared facts cannot be established • shared accountability cannot be enforced The personalized browse accelerates this instability by dissolving the epistemic commons. 12. Identity Collapse Identity becomes fragmented, contextual, unstable, and algorithmically shaped. Authorship dissolves. Credibility becomes contextual. Meaning becomes unstable. Public discourse becomes incoherent. This is the identity environment of the post–open web. 13.
