    "description": "<p>This essay examines how modern institutions create the very conditions in which conspiracy thinking becomes plausible, not through secrecy but through structural omission. Institutions &ldquo;study what aligns with their incentives and neglect what does not,&rdquo; leaving slow, diffuse, or politically inconvenient harms unmeasured and unaddressed. This avoidance produces an epistemic vacuum the public experiences as silence, contradiction, or neglect. In this vacuum, conspiracy theories function as improvised explanations for patterns that institutions refuse to investigate. By showing how phrases like &ldquo;hard to measure&rdquo; operate as institutional anesthesia rather than scientific limitation, the essay reframes conspiracy thinking as a rational response to incentive‑driven neglect. Instead of attributing systemic outcomes to hidden actors, it argues for replacing conspiracy with structure: a clear account of how institutional incentives shape what becomes legible, what remains unstudied, and why mistrust emerges when inquiry is absent.</p>",
