2. The Collapse of the Open Web The open web’s decline is not a metaphorical phenomenon but a measurable infrastructural failure.

Key

indicators

include:

● SEO dominance , which prioritizes popularity over accuracy. ● Link rot , eroding the stability of citations and references. ● Content pollution , driven by automated generation and low ‑ quality replication. ● Institutional withdrawal , as authoritative sources migrate to controlled repositories.

These dynamics transform the open web into a museum: a historical archive rather than a living knowledge

system.

3. The Rise of Agentic AI as the Retrieval Layer Agentic AI systems do not rely on traditional search paradigms. Instead, they operate through: ● Semantic embeddings , enabling conceptual matching rather than keyword alignment. ● Contextual synthesis , producing answers rather than lists of links. ● Task ‑ oriented delegation , where users request outcomes rather than sources. This shift redefines the interface between humans and information. AI becomes the primary interpreter,

while

the

web

becomes

a

secondary

storage

layer.

4. From Searching to Grounding Grounding is the process by which AI systems anchor their outputs in stable, authoritative sources.

Unlike

search,

grounding

prioritizes:

● Durability over recency. ● Structure over popularity. ● Citations over clicks. ● Repository documents over SEO ‑ optimized pages. Grounding transforms retrieval from a competitive ranking system into a stability ‑ driven architecture.

5. Repositories as the New Ground Truth Layer Repositories such as Zenodo, Academia, and OSF provide the characteristics agentic AI requires:

● Stable URLs resistant to link rot. ● Version control , enabling traceable updates. ● Metadata and DOIs , supporting citation integrity. ● Structured PDFs , optimized for embedding and semantic parsing. These repositories become the de facto ground truth layer for AI systems, replacing the open web

as

the

authoritative

substrate.

6. Substack as the Crisis ‑ Era Interpretive Layer

During periods of rapid change or crisis, information does not originate in repositories. It emerges

from:

● Substack ● X ● Mastodon ● Independent newsletters These platforms provide real ‑ time interpretation and conceptual framing. Repositories later absorb,

stabilize,

and

The

Authors

● consistent terminology, ● cross ‑ linked conceptual structures, ● repository ‑ based archives, ● and stable publication rhythms, will be preferentially surfaced by agentic AI systems. This dynamic reshapes how fields emerge, propagate,

and
