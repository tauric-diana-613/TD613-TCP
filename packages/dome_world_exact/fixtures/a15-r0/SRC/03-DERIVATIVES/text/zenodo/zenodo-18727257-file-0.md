Architecture 1. Propagation Is Not Virality Virality assumes: • exponential spread • frictionless sharing • stable attention • open networks None of these conditions exist anymore. Propagation is not virality. Propagation is wave behavior. Ideas move through: • pulses • bursts • decays • rebounds • resonance cycles

Propagation is rhythmic, not exponential. 2. The Architecture of a Propagation Wave Every propagation wave has five components: 2.1 Initiation The moment a concept enters the system. 2.2 Amplitude The strength of the concept’s initial uptake. 2.3 Resonance The degree to which the concept aligns with existing structures. 2.4 Decay The inevitable dissipation of attention. 2.5 Afterglow The residual conceptual presence that remains after decay. Propagation waves are structural, not emotional. 3. Amplitude: The Strength of the Signal Amplitude is shaped by: • conceptual density • platform rhythm • audience readiness • environmental volatility • timing relative to other signals

High amplitude does not guarantee stability. It only guarantees visibility. Amplitude is the height of the wave, not its lifespan. 4. Resonance: The Concept’s Structural Fit Resonance determines whether a concept: • stabilizes • mutates • fragments • collapses Resonance depends on: • alignment with existing nodes • compatibility with platform constraints • interpretive readiness of the audience • conceptual clarity Resonance is the frequency match between concept and environment. 5. Decay: The System’s Default State All waves decay. Decay is accelerated by: • feed volatility • algorithmic resets • cognitive saturation • competing signals • platform churn Decay is not failure. Decay is the system’s baseline behavior.

Propagation is the exception. Decay is the rule. 6. Interference: When Waves Collide Concepts do not propagate in isolation. They collide, producing: 6.1 Constructive Interference Two concepts amplify each other. (e.g., Licensing Architecture + Semantic Governance) 6.2 Destructive Interference Two concepts cancel each other out. (e.g., high ‑ density posts during audience saturation) 6.3 Noise Interference Unrelated signals distort conceptual clarity. Interference is the physics of conceptual interaction. 7. Canon Formation: When Waves Stabilize A concept becomes canonical when: • multiple waves reinforce it • resonance remains high • decay slows • afterglow persists • the concept becomes a reference point Canon formation is not popularity. It is stability across waves.
