    "description": "<p>Propagation Wave Theory: How Concepts Move, Cluster, and Stabilize in Constrained Systems introduces a structural model for understanding how ideas travel through platform‑mediated environments in the post‑open‑web era. Unlike virality, which assumes exponential spread and stable attention, propagation is rhythmic: concepts move through pulses, bursts, decays, rebounds, and resonance cycles shaped by platform constraints, audience metabolism, and algorithmic volatility. This essay maps the architecture of propagation waves &mdash; initiation, amplitude, resonance, decay, and afterglow &mdash; and explains how theoretical content behaves as a dynamic signal within constrained systems.</p>\n<p>The work demonstrates that amplitude determines visibility but not longevity, resonance determines structural fit, and decay is the system&rsquo;s default state. It introduces interference as the physics of conceptual interaction, showing how waves amplify, distort, or cancel each other depending on environmental conditions. Canon formation emerges not from popularity but from repeated reinforcement across multiple waves, where decay slows and conceptual afterglow persists. By reframing conceptual movement as wave behavior rather than viral spread, the essay provides a framework for understanding how ideas stabilize, fragment, or disappear within volatile, compressed, and algorithmically governed environments.</p>",
