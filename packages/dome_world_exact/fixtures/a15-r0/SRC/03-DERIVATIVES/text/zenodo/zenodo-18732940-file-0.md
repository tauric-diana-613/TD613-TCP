1. The SR Method Begins With Structure, Not Content Traditional analysis starts with claims, narratives, or intentions. The SR Method begins with architecture .

It asks: ● What is the system’s structure? ● What constraints define its behavior? ● What flows, limits, and affordances shape outcomes? Meaning emerges from structure , not expression.

2. The Four Pillars of the SR Method The SR Method is built on four analytical pillars. 2.1 Constraint Mapping Identify the structural limits that shape what is possible.

2.2 System Behavior Analysis Observe what the system does , not what it claims to do. 2.3 Epistemic Positioning Determine what the system treats as knowable, ignorable, or noise. 2.4 Representational Grammar Analyze how the system encodes, interprets, and transforms information. These pillars form the backbone of SR analysis.

3. The SR Method Rejects Intentionalism Systems do not act with intent. They act with structure . The SR Method focuses on: ● affordances ● constraints ● flows ● failure modes Not motivations.

4. The SR Method as Canonical Discipline Every SR essay uses the same method: ● map the system ● identify constraints ● analyze epistemics ● reveal structural consequences This creates coherence across the canon and ensures that each node aligns with the field’s infrastructural

orientation.
