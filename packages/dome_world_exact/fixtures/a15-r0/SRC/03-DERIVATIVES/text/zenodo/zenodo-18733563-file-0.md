Post ‑ Open ‑ Web 1. Drift Is the Default State of Systems Systems do not remain stable. They drift. Drift occurs when: • constraints accumulate • noise increases • representational fidelity declines • internal logic becomes misaligned • external pressures reshape internal structure Stability is temporary. Drift is continuous. 2. The Architecture of Drift Systemic drift emerges from four interacting forces:

2.1 Structural Fatigue Components degrade under constant load. 2.2 Epistemic Erosion The system loses the ability to interpret its own inputs. 2.3 Representational Mutation Internal categories shift, distort, or collapse. 2.4 Constraint Pressure External limits force internal reconfiguration. Drift is the cumulative effect of these forces. 3. Structural Fatigue: When Systems Wear Down Every system has: • thresholds • tolerances • load limits Over time, these limits are exceeded. Fatigue manifests as: • slower response • inconsistent behavior • internal contradictions • degraded performance Fatigue is not a bug. It is the cost of operation.

4. Epistemic Erosion: When Systems Forget How to Know Systems rely on: • categories • mappings • interpretive schemas • representational grammars As noise accumulates, these structures erode. The system begins to: • misclassify • misinterpret • misalign • misrepresent Epistemic erosion is the loss of internal legibility. 5. Representational Mutation: When Categories Drift Systems mutate their categories to remain functional under constraint. Mutation occurs when: • old categories no longer fit • new pressures emerge • internal logic shifts • external signals distort meaning Mutation is adaptation under duress. But mutation also produces: • conceptual drift • category collapse • semantic instability

The system becomes less itself. 6. Constraint Pressure: The External Force That Shapes Drift Systems drift because they are forced to adapt to: • platform constraints • resource limits • environmental volatility • user behavior • regulatory pressure Constraint pressure forces systems to reconfigure themselves. Drift is the path of least resistance. 7. Drift as a Precursor to Collapse Drift does not always lead to collapse. But collapse is always preceded by drift. Collapse occurs when: • fatigue exceeds tolerance • erosion exceeds legibility • mutation exceeds coherence • pressure exceeds adaptability Collapse is drift reaching its limit. 8. The Post ‑ Open ‑ Web Condition of Systems In the open web, systems drifted slowly. In the post ‑ open ‑ web, drift accelerates because:

• constraints are tighter • volatility is higher • noise is constant • models mediate everything • platforms reconfigure continuously Systems now drift faster than they can stabilize.
