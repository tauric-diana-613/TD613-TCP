1. Introduction: When Cognition Becomes a Scarce Resource

Human cognition is finite. Modern systems are not. Erosion as a cognitive condition describes what happens when the volume, velocity, and volatility

This essay reframes cognitive erosion as a structural condition, not a personal deficit.

2. The Architecture of Cognitive Extraction

Cognitive erosion emerges when systems: ● accelerate beyond human processing speed ● externalize complexity onto individuals ● collapse boundaries between work and life ● demand constant interpretive vigilance ● produce continuous informational noise These architectures convert cognition into a consumable resource. The result is a subject who is:

● aware but unable to integrate ● informed but unable to respond ● attentive but unable to sustain focus Cognition becomes thin, brittle, and easily overwhelmed.

3. Symptoms of Cognitive Erosion Cognitive erosion manifests as: ● fragmented attention ● difficulty retaining information ● reduced emotional availability ● inconsistent engagement ● inability to initiate or complete tasks ● oscillation between hyperfocus and shutdown ● a sense of being “present but not processing” These symptoms are not psychological disorders. They are environmental effects.

4. Erosion vs. Burnout: A Structural Distinction Burnout is episodic. Erosion is continuous. Burnout resolves with rest. Erosion persists because the environment does not change. Burnout is a reaction. Erosion is a condition. This distinction matters because it shifts responsibility from the individual to the system.

5. Cognitive Erosion as a Precondition for Rupture When cognitive erosion reaches a threshold, rupture occurs. Rupture is not a breakdown; it is the moment when the subject can no longer maintain coherence

within

an

overloaded

environment.

Cognitive erosion is the slow collapse. Rupture is the event.
