    "description": "<p>The Manufactured Burden of Personal Responsibility is a canonical SignalRupture essay analyzing how modern institutions shift structural harm onto individuals through the rhetoric of autonomy and self‑management. In environments defined by scarcity, low wages, unaffordable housing, degraded food systems, and engineered dependency, people are told they possess full responsibility for their health, finances, and life outcomes. This narrative obscures the systemic conditions that constrain choice and produce predictable harm. The essay maps how responsibility is strategically routed downward&mdash;transforming structural failures into personal faults&mdash;and demonstrates how this moral framing functions as a governance mechanism rather than an empowerment strategy. By exposing the asymmetry between institutional design and individual blame, this work expands the SignalRupture framework for understanding coercion, scarcity, and the infrastructural production of struggle in the post‑web era.</p>",
