1. The Illusion of Health Autonomy Society insists that health is a matter of personal responsibility: eat better, exercise more, manage

stress,

make

good

But

requires

access,

A person working long hours for low wages does not choose poor health. They inherit an environment

are

The system calls it autonomy. Lived experience calls it constraint.

2. Food Systems Designed for Harm Grocery stores appear full of choice, yet the structure of the food system reveals a coercive architecture:

● 60% of available food is ultra ‑ processed and nutrient ‑ poor. ● Healthy food is priced as a luxury item. ● Industrial agriculture subsidizes harmful products. ● Fast food is cheaper than fresh produce. ● Time scarcity makes cooking impossible for many workers. These conditions produce predictable outcomes: obesity, diabetes, inflammation, depression, and

chronic

illness.

The system creates the harm, then blames individuals for the consequences.

3. Toxic Workplaces and the Physiology of Exhaustion

Workplaces generate chronic stress through: ● unpredictable schedules ● surveillance ● understaffing ● abusive management ● unrealistic productivity demands Chronic stress is not a psychological inconvenience — it is a physiological assault. It disrupts sleep,

metabolism,

immune

function,

cognition,

and

emotional

regulation.

Yet workers are told to "manage stress" as if the source is internal rather than structural. The system destabilizes the body, then moralizes the symptoms.

4. The Economics of Illness Once health collapses, the system introduces a new layer of dependency: ● pharmaceuticals ● long ‑ term treatment plans ● medical debt ● insurance entanglement ● recurring appointments and diagnostics Illness becomes a revenue stream. The same system that produces harm profits from treating it. This is not a conspiracy — it is an economic

model.

5. Mental Health Under Structural Pressure Mental health is often framed as an individual struggle, but the environment tells a different story:

● financial precarity ● unsafe housing ● food insecurity ● overwork ● social isolation ● lack of community infrastructure These conditions generate anxiety, depression, burnout, and despair. Yet individuals are told to "seek

help"

as

can

for

structural

violence.

Mental health collapses when the environment collapses. The system treats the symptoms, not the

cause.

6. The Health Trap as a Governance Strategy

A population that is sick, exhausted, and financially strained is easier to govern. Illness reduces resistance,

narrows

attention,

and

consumes

time

and

resources.

The health trap functions as: ● a containment system ● a profit engine ● a behavioral control mechanism ● a justification for further intervention The system harms bodies, then governs through the consequences.

7. The System Needs You Unwell A healthy population is autonomous. A sick population is dependent. Dependence is profitable. Dependence is governable. Dependence is predictable. The system does not merely tolerate illness — it relies on it.
