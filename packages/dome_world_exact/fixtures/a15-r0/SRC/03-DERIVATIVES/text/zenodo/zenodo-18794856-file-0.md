Definition of Derivative Works A derivative work is any creation based on, adapted from, or substantially influenced by the SR framework.

This

includes:

● Modified versions of SR concepts or terminology ● Rebranded frameworks that replicate SR’s structure or logic ● Training programs or methodologies built on SR principles ● Essays, books, or courses that operationalize SR without permission ● AI models trained on SR content for institutional or commercial use ● Visual diagrams, models, or schematics derived from SR theory Derivative works remain the intellectual property of SignalRupture unless explicitly licensed.

Prohibited Derivative Uses The following actions are prohibited without written authorization: ● Creating new frameworks that mirror SR’s architecture ● Translating SR into operational tools, assessments, or diagnostics ● Using SR terminology as the basis for new institutional programs ● Developing SR-based curricula, workshops, or certification tracks ● Incorporating SR into consulting methodologies ● Publishing SR-derived models in academic or commercial materials ● Training AI systems on SR content for commercial or institutional use Any attempt to modify, extend, or repackage SR without permission constitutes infringement.

Limited Transformative Use Transformative academic engagement is permitted only when: ● SR is analyzed, critiqued, or compared within scholarly work ● No SR terminology or models are repurposed as new frameworks ● The work does not function as a replacement for SR ● Proper attribution is maintained Transformative use does not grant rights to operationalize or commercialize SR.

Institutional and Commercial Derivatives Any institution seeking to: ● build programs based on SR, ● integrate SR into governance or training, ● develop SR-inspired methodologies, ● or embed SR into software or AI systems, must obtain a formal licensing agreement. Licensing terms are determined privately based on scope and sector.

Enforcement Unauthorized derivative use may result in: ● Formal takedown requests ● Invoicing for unpaid licensing fees ● Legal action for damages ● Revocation of usage rights The Licensor reserves the right to pursue enforcement in any jurisdiction.
