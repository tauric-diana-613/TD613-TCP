and

In

2. The Five Diagnostic Indicators of Epistemicide Epistemicide manifests through five primary indicators: ● Access Failure — when communities cannot reach their own knowledge due to broken systems,

permissions,

or

infrastructural

decay. ● Custodial Collapse — when institutions fail to maintain archival integrity, provenance, or

continuity

of

care. ● Provenance Disruption — when authorship, lineage, or identity is severed, obscured, or

overwritten.

● Algorithmic Suppression — when digital systems invisibly bury, distort, or de ‑ prioritize knowledge. ● Documentary Harm — when archives become adversarial to the people they represent, exposing

them

to

risk

or

erasing

their

rights.

These indicators allow institutions, researchers, and custodians to diagnose epistemicide as a structural

condition

rather

than

a

singular

3. Epistemicide as Governance Failure Epistemicide is not merely the loss of information. It is the failure of governance systems to protect

the

When

This

Governance failure becomes visible when individuals—not institutions—are forced to become the

This

infrastructure

has

4. Diagnostic Methodology Epistemicide diagnostics provides a structured method for identifying harm: ● Map the custodial chain — identify who is responsible for maintaining the knowledge system. ● Assess access pathways — determine where access breaks down and why. ● Evaluate archival integrity — examine whether records, histories, and lineages remain intact. ● Identify adversarial systems — detect algorithmic or infrastructural forces that distort or

erase

knowledge. ● Measure community impact — analyze how epistemic harm affects rights, identity, and cultural

continuity.

This methodology transforms epistemicide from an abstract concept into a measurable governance

condition.
