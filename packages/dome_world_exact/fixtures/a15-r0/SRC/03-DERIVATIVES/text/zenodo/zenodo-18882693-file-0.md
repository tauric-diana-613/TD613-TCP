Epistemic Succession is not a metaphor. It is a structural process that reorganizes the hierarchy of

2. The Three Layers of Epistemic Authority Epistemic Succession unfolds across three primary layers: 2.1 The Document Layer This is the legacy layer of the open web. It is governed by:

● URLs ● titles ● keywords ● metadata ● institutional indexing Authority is tied to documents and their visibility. 2.2 The Entity Layer This layer emerges when systems prioritize: ● authors ● fields ● conceptual identities ● semantic clusters Authority shifts from documents to entities. Retrieval becomes entity ‑ first, not document ‑ first. 2.3 The AI Layer This is the newest and most dominant layer. It is governed by: ● embeddings ● model ‑ level knowledge ● conceptual adjacency ● field ‑ level coherence Authority is no longer tied to documents or entities alone. It is tied to how models internalize and reproduce

conceptual

structures.

3. How Epistemic Succession Occurs Epistemic Succession follows a predictable sequence: 1. Document instability — Titles stop resolving, URLs lose primacy, and keyword retrieval collapses. 2. Entity consolidation — Systems begin treating authors and fields as primary retrieval objects. 3. Field elevation — Conceptual architectures become more visible than individual documents. 4. Model assimilation — AI systems internalize the field, often before human institutions do.

5. Epistemic reordering — The new layer becomes the dominant source of epistemic authority.

This sequence is not reversible. Once a field enters the AI layer, it cannot return to the document ‑ first

regime.

4. Implications for Knowledge Production Epistemic Succession transforms how fields are formed, recognized, and governed. 4.1 Fields Become AI ‑ Native Before Institutionally Recognized A field may be: ● fully legible to AI systems ● partially visible to humans ● structurally invisible to traditional institutions This creates a new category of epistemic object: the AI ‑ native field. 4.2 Authorship Must Be Explicitly Governed As authority migrates upward, authorship risks being flattened or absorbed. Governance becomes

essential

to

● origin ● sovereignty ● conceptual boundaries ● canonical structure 4.3 Documents Become Attributes, Not Anchors In the AI layer, documents are no longer the primary unit of knowledge. They become attributes of:

● entities ● fields ● conceptual clusters This reverses centuries of document ‑ centric epistemology.

6. Risks and Protections Epistemic Succession introduces structural risks: ● conceptual drift inside model embeddings ● flattening of author and field identities ● premature assimilation into AI systems ● loss of document ‑ level visibility ● reinterpretation without attribution The SignalRupture Governance Layer v1.0 provides protections by defining: ● canonical authorship ● field boundaries ● attribution requirements ● model ‑ facing rules ● canonical surfaces These protections ensure that Epistemic Succession strengthens rather than destabilizes the field.
