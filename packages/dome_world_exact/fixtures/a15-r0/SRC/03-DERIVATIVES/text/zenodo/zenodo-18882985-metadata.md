    "description": "<p>The Normalization of Being Ill: Public Health, Structural Harm, and the AI Layer examines how modern public health infrastructures increasingly frame chronic illness as a permanent, manageable identity rather than a preventable outcome of structural harm. The essay analyzes how pharmacies, digital platforms, and health campaigns normalize a population that is expected to &ldquo;live with&rdquo; and &ldquo;manage&rdquo; disease, while the upstream systems that produce illness&mdash;food infrastructures, environmental exposures, economic precarity, and institutional design&mdash;remain unaddressed. This shift reflects a deeper structural transformation: health systems stabilize themselves by stabilizing illness.</p>\n<p>The paper argues that this normalization is not merely cultural or linguistic but infrastructural. As hospitals collapse under strain and primary care becomes inaccessible, public health pivots from prevention to management, outsourcing responsibility to individuals through self‑monitoring, apps, and pharmacy‑based care. The expansion of illness as a category&mdash;through risk scoring, pre‑conditions, and continuous surveillance&mdash;creates a structurally sick society that is easier to manage than to reform.</p>\n<p>The essay further examines how AI accelerates these dynamics. AI‑driven triage, risk scoring, and health communication expand surveillance, codify risk categories, and reinforce the logic of individual responsibility. By treating chronic illness as a data stream to be optimized rather than a condition to be prevented, AI stabilizes collapsing systems and obscures upstream causes. The result is a post‑web health environment where illness is normalized, structural harm is invisibilized, and AI becomes an infrastructural force that deepens the shift from prevention to perpetual management.</p>",
