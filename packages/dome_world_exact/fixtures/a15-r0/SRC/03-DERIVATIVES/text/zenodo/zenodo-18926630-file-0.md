1. Introduction Michel Foucault’s theory of power was built for a world of institutions, norms, and disciplinary structures.

But

contemporary

power

is

● operate automatically ● scale globally ● update continuously ● govern invisibly ● entangle every domain of life This essay maps the shift from institutional power to infrastructural power — a form of governance

that

rather

2. The Limits of Foucault in an Infrastructural Age Foucault assumed that power: ● requires subjects ● circulates through institutions ● operates through surveillance ● produces disciplined bodies ● depends on intentional strategies But modern systems generate power without: ● subjects ● institutions ● surveillance in the classical sense

● disciplinary intent ● strategic actors Power now emerges from architectures, not authorities. 3. The Rise of Infrastructural Power Infrastructural power is produced by systems that: ● define defaults ● shape visibility ● constrain behavior ● structure choices ● embed dependencies Examples include: ● cloud infrastructures ● algorithmic ranking systems ● payment networks ● logistics platforms ● AI ‑ driven classification systems These systems do not discipline individuals. They configure environments. 4. Power Without Actors Post ‑ institutional power does not require: ● a sovereign ● a bureaucracy ● a strategy ● a governing ideology It emerges from: ● automated processes ● economic incentives ● infrastructural lock ‑ in ● feedback loops ● optimization pressures Power becomes a property of the system, not the people within it.

5. The Logic of Entanglement Modern power is entanglement. Systems rule by becoming: ● indispensable ● interoperable ● unavoidable ● infrastructurally central ● too complex to replace A system that cannot be exited does not need to coerce. 6. The End of Discipline Discipline required: ● observation ● correction ● normalization ● training Infrastructural power requires none of these. It governs by: ● shaping defaults ● structuring incentives ● defining what is possible ● embedding constraints ● automating compliance The subject is not disciplined — they are configured. 7. Power as Environment Power no longer acts on individuals. It acts around them. It becomes: ● ambient ● invisible ● infrastructural ● persistent ● environmental The environment becomes the governor.
