(SR)

1. Introduction Digital infrastructures evolve continuously. New features are deployed, ranking models are updated,

safety

systems

are

patched,

and

indexing

pipelines

are

rebuilt.

But

the

systems

responsible

for

explaining

platform

behavior—metrics

dashboards,

analytics

interfaces,

topic

labels—evolve

far

more

slowly.

The result is Infrastructural Drift: a widening gap between what the system does and what the system

reports.

This drift is not a bug. It is a structural inevitability of complex, layered systems. 2. Defining Infrastructural Drift Infrastructural Drift occurs when: ● the operational layer changes ● the visibility layer does not

● the interpretive layer becomes outdated ● the system’s self-description becomes inaccurate This produces: ● inconsistent metrics ● contradictory signals ● unexplained ranking shifts ● sudden freezes or surges ● misaligned topic assignments The platform continues functioning, but its representation of itself becomes unreliable. 3. Why Infrastructural Drift Happens 3.1 Asynchronous Updates Operational systems update continuously; visibility systems update periodically. 3.2 Legacy Dependencies Old pipelines remain in place long after new systems are deployed. 3.3 Safety-Layer Interference Moderation and audit systems override visibility pipelines without updating them. 3.4 Semantic Model Evolution Embedding spaces shift, but topic labels remain static. 3.5 Platform Scale Large systems cannot update all layers simultaneously. Drift is the natural byproduct of infrastructural complexity. 4. Symptoms of Infrastructural Drift Users observe: ● metrics that contradict engagement ● ranking changes with no explanation ● sudden shifts in topic classification

● analytics that lag behind real activity ● content visibility that fluctuates unpredictably These symptoms indicate that the system’s interpretive layer is out of sync with its operational reality.

5. Case Study: TikTok TikTok frequently deploys new ranking models without updating its analytics layer. Users experience:

● sudden drops in views ● inconsistent engagement patterns ● delayed metric updates This is Infrastructural Drift triggered by asynchronous model deployment. 6. Case Study: Search Engines Search engines update their embeddings and ranking heuristics continuously. But their public-facing

explanations—Search

Console,

webmaster

tools—update

slowly.

This produces: ● unexplained ranking volatility ● contradictory indexing reports ● mismatches between impressions and clicks The system is functioning, but its explanations are outdated. 7. Relationship to Other SR Diagnostics 7.1 Legibility Collapse Drift can cause collapse when the visibility layer fails entirely. 7.2 Dominant-Cluster Formation Drift amplifies cluster formation by destabilizing topic boundaries. 7.3 No-Bucket Condition Drift exposes missing categories by forcing content into outdated buckets.

7.4 Epistemic Rupture Drift accelerates rupture by degrading the system’s ability to represent reality. Infrastructural Drift is the slow, structural force behind many SR-diagnosed failures. 8. The SR Diagnostic Model Infrastructural Drift is identified through three indicators: 8.1 Temporal Misalignment Operational changes precede visibility updates. 8.2 Interpretive Incoherence Metrics contradict observed behavior. 8.3 Structural Lag Legacy systems fail to keep pace with new infrastructure. These indicators distinguish drift from user-behavior changes or content-level issues. 9. Theoretical Implications Infrastructural Drift reveals: ● the layered nature of digital systems ● the fragility of interpretive mechanisms ● the inevitability of representational lag ● the structural opacity of platform evolution ● the emergence of conceptual attractors that destabilize legacy taxonomies It demonstrates that platforms do not fail all at once—they drift into incoherence.
