Studies

Neuroscience

2. Methods 2.1 Research Approach This paper uses a meta-research synthesis approach. Rather than conducting new empirical experiments,

it

analyzes

established

research

across

multiple

fields

and

identifies

structural

patterns

that

emerge

across

them.

Meta-research examines how existing knowledge is organized, interpreted, and integrated across

disciplines.

2.2 Inclusion Criteria Studies were included if they: ● reported quantitative results such as effect sizes, correlations, or odds ratios ● examined phenomena relevant to SR’s erosion layers ● represented established empirical literatures The domains examined include: ● scarcity and decision-making research ● stress physiology ● cognitive load theory ● burnout and occupational exhaustion ● institutional trust studies ● platform-mediated information dynamics

2.3 Analytical Strategy Rather than performing a statistical meta-analysis, this paper uses conceptual synthesis . Empirical

findings

are

interpreted

collectively

to

identify

recurring

structural

patterns.

Effect sizes reported in the literature are used to illustrate the magnitude of the documented phenomena,

demonstrating

that

many

of

these

processes

produce

moderate

to

large

impacts

on

cognition,

behavior,

or

institutional

dynamics.

3. Empirical Foundations of Contemporary Erosion

3.1 Scarcity and Cognitive Bandwidth Research in behavioral economics demonstrates that scarcity reduces cognitive performance. A widely

cited

experiment

showed

that

individuals

financial

The study Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function found that scarcity conditions produced performance

13

Raven’s

performance

These findings suggest that scarcity can significantly narrow cognitive bandwidth, affecting reasoning,

Within the SR framework, scarcity is interpreted as a mechanism of cognitive narrowing that can

propagate

across

financial,

temporal,

and

3.2 Chronic Stress and Physiological Load Research on stress physiology demonstrates that chronic stress produces cumulative biological strain.

The

Studies examining stress exposure and health indicators often report correlations around r ≈ .40–.50

These results indicate a strong relationship between sustained stress environments and physiological

depletion.

Within SR, chronic stress represents a physiological erosion layer that interacts with cognitive and

3.6 Institutional Trust Erosion Research on public trust in institutions shows declining confidence in governments, media organizations,

4. Converging Empirical Patterns Although these literatures examine different phenomena, they display several recurring characteristics:

1. measurable cognitive narrowing under resource scarcity 2. physiological strain under chronic stress 3. performance decline under high cognitive load 4. institutional disengagement under burnout 5. epistemic instability in digital information environments 6. declining trust in governance institutions

Across domains, the empirical evidence suggests a recurring pattern of depletion, narrowing, overload,

fragmentation,

and

distrust .

Rather than interpreting these trends as unrelated developments, SR proposes that they represent

interacting

pressures

within

a

shared

socio-technical

environment.

5. Limitations of Classical Theories Existing theoretical frameworks provide important insights but rarely integrate all layers simultaneously.

Systems theory describes complex interactions but does not focus on cognitive or physiological depletion.

Complexity theory models emergent dynamics but does not directly address institutional trust erosion.

Platform studies analyze digital systems but rarely connect them to stress physiology or cognitive

bandwidth.

As a result, much of the empirical landscape remains fragmented across disciplines.

6. SignalRupture as a Meta-Diagnostic Framework

SignalRupture proposes a layered model of contemporary erosion. Four interacting layers form the framework: Physiological layer ● stress exposure ● arousal cycles ● biological depletion Cognitive layer ● bandwidth limitations

● cognitive load ● attentional narrowing Institutional layer ● declining trust ● governance brittleness ● procedural breakdown Digital layer ● algorithmic amplification ● high-arousal information environments ● epistemic instability These layers interact to produce reinforcing feedback loops.

7. Cross-Surface Recurrence A defining feature of the contemporary environment is the recurrence of similar patterns across different

domains.

Across physiological, cognitive, institutional, and digital systems, similar dynamics appear repeatedly:

● narrowing ● overload ● depletion ● fragmentation ● distrust ● informational drift SR refers to this phenomenon as cross-surface recurrence . The same structural pressures appear across domains that were previously studied independently.

8. Infrastructural Erosion

Many empirical fields describe the symptoms of contemporary strain. SR focuses on the infrastructure

that

produces

them.

Examples of potential infrastructural drivers include: ● scarcity-amplifying economic environments ● high-velocity information systems ● algorithmically amplified arousal cycles ● institutional coordination breakdowns These structures shape the environments in which cognitive and social systems operate.

10. Limitations and Future Research Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this paper does not conduct a formal statistical meta-analysis. Instead, it synthesizes findings

across

multiple

literatures.

Second, much of the research cited focuses on Western institutional contexts. Additional work is needed

to

similar

in

non-Western

Future research could expand SR by:

● developing quantitative measures of cross-surface recurrence ● studying long-term changes in institutional resilience ● examining the cognitive impacts of platform-scale information systems ● modeling interactions between digital environments and stress physiology

11. Contributions This paper contributes several elements: 1. an interdisciplinary synthesis of empirical research across multiple fields 2. a conceptual framework linking physiological, cognitive, institutional, and digital pressures 3. the concept of cross-surface recurrence 4. a meta-research architecture for studying contemporary infrastructural strain 5. a foundation for future interdisciplinary research on systemic erosion

12. Policy and Governance Implications If contemporary erosion operates across multiple layers simultaneously, interventions limited to a

Potential governance strategies may include: ● reducing scarcity-driven environments ● designing information systems that minimize cognitive overload ● improving institutional transparency and accountability ● regulating high-arousal digital platform dynamics Addressing these issues may require systemic rather than isolated policy responses.
