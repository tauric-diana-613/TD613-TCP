1. Survival Infrastructure: The Biological Base

Layer

Society

Food and water form the foundational layer of human civilization. Every other social system—including

Unlike natural resources such as sunlight or atmospheric oxygen, food and water infrastructures are

These

● what food is grown ● who has access to clean water ● how resources are distributed ● how scarcity is managed Because survival depends on them, control over food and water confers immense structural power.

In

Water systems show similar consolidation. Private infrastructure firms and bottled ‑ water companies

These actors influence: ● agricultural policy ● land ‑ use decisions ● water extraction rights ● global commodity prices The result is a form of corporate sovereignty , in which private organizations exercise governance ‑ like

3. Engineered Scarcity Scarcity is often framed as a natural consequence of drought, crop failure, or climate change. However,

Modern food systems prioritize efficiency, scalability, and profitability. These priorities encourage:

● monoculture farming ● high pesticide use ● reliance on synthetic fertilizers ● mass production of ultra ‑ processed foods Ultra ‑ processed products dominate many modern diets due to their low cost, long shelf life, and aggressive

7. Supply Chains: Efficiency Versus Resilience

Global food supply chains prioritize speed and cost reduction. This efficiency often reduces redundancy,

Fragile supply chains increase the likelihood of food shortages, price volatility, and humanitarian crises.
