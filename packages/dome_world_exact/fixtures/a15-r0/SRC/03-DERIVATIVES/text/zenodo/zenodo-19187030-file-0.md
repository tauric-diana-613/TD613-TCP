1. Introduction: When the Web Stops Being Public The early web was structured around openness: hyperlinks, public pages, and stable archives. The

Visibility collapse is not a decline in content creation. It is a decline in public legibility . SignalRupture treats visibility collapse as a structural transformation of the digital environment. 2. Defining Visibility Collapse Visibility collapse occurs when:

● content becomes dependent on algorithmic distribution ● public pages lose relevance ● feeds replace search ● platform volatility erases stability ● digital spaces become fragmented and ephemeral Visibility is no longer a function of relevance or quality. It is a function of algorithmic selection. 3. Platform Enclosures and the End of the Open Web Platform enclosures occur when corporations centralize digital activity within closed ecosystems.

Research

● discoverability ● interoperability ● user autonomy ● public access The web becomes a series of private territories rather than a public commons. 4. Algorithmic Mediation and the Disappearance of Structural

Algorithmic mediation produces: ● selective visibility ● distorted information environments ● fragmented realities ● reduced exposure to structural analysis Truth becomes contingent on algorithmic priorities. 5. Feed ‑ Driven Attention and the Collapse of Context Feeds prioritize: ● novelty ● emotional salience ● speed

● engagement This collapses context by: ● severing information from its source ● reducing depth ● privileging reaction over reflection ● eroding long ‑ form visibility Context collapse accelerates visibility collapse. 6. Platform Volatility and the Fragility of Digital Presence Digital presence is no longer stable. Platforms: ● change algorithms without warning ● remove features ● de ‑ prioritize entire content types ● collapse or pivot overnight Volatility produces: ● unstable visibility ● unpredictable reach ● disappearing archives ● loss of institutional memory The digital environment becomes structurally unreliable. 7.
