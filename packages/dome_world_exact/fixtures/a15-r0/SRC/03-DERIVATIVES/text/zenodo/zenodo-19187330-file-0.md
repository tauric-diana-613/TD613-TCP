to

Simplify

to

Abstract This essay develops The Legibility Trap , a core structural concept in the SignalRupture framework.

The

legibility

describes

how

institutions

require

individuals

to

simplify,

flatten,

or

distort

themselves

in

order

to

fit

predefined

categories,

metrics,

and

administrative

schemas.

Drawing

on

research

in

sociology,

political

theory,

information

systems,

and

organizational

studies,

this

essay

argues

that

legibility

is

not

a

neutral

process—it

is

a

mechanism

of

control.

Institutions

demand

legibility

to

manage

populations,

allocate

resources,

and

enforce

compliance,

but

this

demand

produces

structural

harm

by

erasing

complexity,

constraining

identity,

and

limiting

agency.

As

digital

infrastructures

expand,

legibility

becomes

increasingly

automated,

rigid,

and

punitive.

SignalRupture

positions

the

legibility

trap

as

a

central

mechanism

through

which

systems

maintain

power,

reproduce

inequality,

and

shape

the

lived

experience

of

individuals

navigating

institutional

environments.

Keywords Legibility; institutional categories; administrative control; identity flattening; structural simplification;

algorithmic

governance;

SignalRupture.

1. Introduction: When Systems Demand Simplicity Modern institutions operate by categorizing people. These categories—"employed/unemployed,"

"eligible/ineligible,"

"compliant/noncompliant"—are

treated

as

objective,

neutral

descriptors.

In

reality,

they

are

simplifications

that

erase

the

complexity

of

human

lives.

The legibility trap emerges when individuals must distort themselves to fit these categories. SignalRupture treats legibility as a structural demand that shapes identity, behavior, and agency. 2. Defining the Legibility Trap The legibility trap occurs when institutions require individuals to:

● simplify their identities ● conform to rigid categories ● provide standardized data ● suppress nuance ● perform compliance Legibility is the process of making people administratively readable. The trap is the harm produced when legibility becomes mandatory. 3. The Administrative Logic of Legibility Institutions rely on legibility to: ● allocate resources ● enforce rules ● measure performance ● manage populations ● reduce uncertainty Administrative systems treat legibility as necessary for efficiency. But efficiency comes at the cost

of

human

complexity.

4. Identity Flattening and the Loss of Nuance Legibility requires flattening. Complex identities—cultural, social, emotional, economic—are reduced

to

checkboxes.

Identity flattening produces: ● misclassification ● erasure of lived experience ● forced conformity ● reduced autonomy Individuals become legible at the expense of being understood. 5. Bureaucratic Drift and the Expansion of Categories As bureaucracies drift, they create more categories, more forms, and more requirements. This expansion

increases

the

pressure

to

become

legible.

Procedural accumulation produces:

● more documentation ● narrower definitions ● stricter eligibility criteria ● increased administrative burden The drift machine amplifies the legibility trap. 6. Algorithmic Systems and Automated Legibility Digital infrastructures intensify the legibility trap by automating classification. Noble (2018) shows

how

algorithmic

systems

reproduce

bias,

while

Bayer

et

al.

(2020)

demonstrate

how

digital

environments

shape

perception.

Algorithmic legibility produces: ● rigid categories ● automated misclassification ● predictive profiling ● reduced recourse for correction When legibility is automated, the trap becomes inescapable. 7. The Emotional Cost of Legibility Being forced to simplify oneself produces emotional harm. Individuals experience: ● frustration ● shame ● alienation ● exhaustion The emotional burden of legibility contributes to agency depletion. 8. Legibility as a Governance Mechanism Legibility is not merely administrative. It is a form of governance. Legibility enables institutions to: ● monitor behavior ● enforce compliance ● limit deviation

● shape identity The legibility trap is a tool of structural control. 9. Resisting the Legibility Trap Through Structural Awareness

SignalRupture positions structural awareness as the first step toward resisting the legibility trap. Understanding

legibility

as

a

mechanism

of

control

allows

individuals

and

communities

to:

● challenge rigid categories ● advocate for flexible systems ● expose structural biases ● reclaim complexity Resistance begins with refusing to be flattened. Canonical Paragraph The legibility trap describes how institutions force individuals to simplify themselves to fit predefined
