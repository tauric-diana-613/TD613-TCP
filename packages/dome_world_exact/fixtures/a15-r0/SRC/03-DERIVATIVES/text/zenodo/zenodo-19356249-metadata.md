    "description": "<p>The Coercive Economy: When Work Becomes a Containment System examines how contemporary economic structures function as containment infrastructures rather than pathways to mobility. Drawing on research in labor studies, organizational sociology, and political economy, the essay argues that precarity, wage stagnation, algorithmic management, and the erosion of institutional support systems produce structural dependence that restricts agency and limits exit. The coercive economy is presented as a governance mechanism that regulates behavior through economic necessity rather than overt force. By reframing work as a containment system, the essay clarifies how economic infrastructures shape identity, constrain mobility, and reduce the capacity for structural resistance.</p>",
