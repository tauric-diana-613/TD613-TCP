    "description": "<p>Siloed Thinking as Structural Governance examines how disciplinary specialization functions as a governance mechanism that stabilizes modern institutions by producing predictable cognitive roles. Drawing on research from higher education, organizational theory, and media studies, the essay argues that siloed knowledge was intentionally institutionalized to support industrial coordination, bureaucratic hierarchy, and policy coherence. The essay contrasts this with breadth&mdash;the interdisciplinary cognitive capacity that enables structural literacy, pattern recognition, and meta‑theoretical reasoning. As digital environments increasingly narrow attention through algorithmic reinforcement, breadth emerges as a counter‑force that preserves cognitive autonomy and systemic awareness. The essay positions breadth as a governance‑relevant capacity and demonstrates why meta‑theory arises from interdisciplinary formation rather than credentialed specialization.</p>",
