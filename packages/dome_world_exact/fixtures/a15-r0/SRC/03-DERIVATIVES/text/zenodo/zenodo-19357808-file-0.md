The Genealogy of Drift: How Institutions Lose

Their

Purpose

Over

Time

Abstract This essay traces the historical pattern of institutional drift—the gradual divergence between an institution’s

stated

purpose

and

its

operational

reality.

Drawing

on

research

in

higher

education,

organizational

theory,

and

governance

studies,

it

argues

that

drift

is

not

a

failure

of

leadership

or

policy

but

a

structural

inevitability

produced

by

incentives,

bureaucratic

expansion,

and

environmental

change.

Drift

occurs

across

domains:

universities

shift

from

public

mission

to

market

logic;

media

systems

shift

from

information

to

engagement;

technological

platforms

shift

from

tools

to

behavioral

infrastructures.

By

synthesizing

cross-domain

evidence,

this

essay

demonstrates

that

drift

follows

recurring

patterns

across

history

and

accelerates

under

digital

conditions.

SignalRupture

positions

drift

as

a

diagnostic

lens

for

understanding

institutional

behavior,

revealing

how

systems

evolve,

contradict

themselves,

and

expose

their

underlying

logic

over

time.

Keywords Institutional drift; structural change; incentives; bureaucracy; governance; historical patterns; SignalRupture.

1. Introduction: Drift as a Structural Phenomenon Institutions rarely remain aligned with their founding purpose. Over time, they shift, expand, contradict

themselves,

or

become

captured

by

incentives

that

did

not

exist

at

their

inception.

This

phenomenon— institutional

drift —is

not

an

anomaly

but

a

structural

pattern

observable

across

history.

Drift is not caused by individual actors or isolated decisions. It emerges from: ● incentive structures ● bureaucratic inertia ● environmental pressures ● technological change ● narrative-structural contradictions SignalRupture treats drift as a diagnostic tool , revealing how institutions evolve and what their behavior

exposes

about

underlying

structures.

2. Drift in Higher Education: From Public Mission to Market

Logic

Higher education provides one of the clearest historical examples of drift. Universities were founded

as

public

institutions

oriented

toward

knowledge,

citizenship,

and

intellectual

development.

Over

time,

they

shifted

toward:

● credentialism ● market competition ● administrative expansion ● revenue maximization González-Geraldo and García-García (2023) show that siloed curriculum structures were intentionally

institutionalized,

not

emergent.

Becher

and

Trowler

(2001)

demonstrate

how

disciplines

became

“tribes,”

each

with

its

own

incentives,

identity,

and

epistemic

boundaries.

The result is a system that no longer reflects its founding mission but operates according to market

and

bureaucratic

logic.

3. Drift in Governance: Fragmentation and Policy Incoherence

Governance systems also drift. Kharas and Dooley (2023) document how global governance has

become

increasingly

fragmented,

with

institutions

drifting

away

from

coordinated

policy

frameworks

toward

siloed,

reactive,

and

incentive-driven

behavior.

Drift in governance emerges from: ● competing mandates ● bureaucratic layering ● political incentives ● institutional self-preservation This drift produces policy incoherence, where institutions act in ways that contradict their stated goals.

4. Drift in Media: From Information to Engagement Media systems historically served informational and civic functions. Over time, they drifted toward

engagement-driven

models

shaped

by:

● advertising incentives ● attention economies

● algorithmic optimization Bayer, Triệu, and Ellison (2020) show that digital media environments recalibrate attention toward

novelty,

speed,

and

emotional

salience.

Noble

(2018)

demonstrates

how

algorithmic

systems

reinforce

biases

and

distort

information

environments.

The drift from information to engagement is structural, not accidental. 5. Drift in Technology: From Tools to Behavioral Infrastructures

Technological platforms often begin as tools—neutral, functional, and user-directed. Over time, they

drift

into

behavioral

infrastructures

that

shape

cognition,

visibility,

and

agency.

This drift is driven by: ● data extraction incentives ● engagement maximization ● infrastructural entrenchment ● network effects The result is a system where platforms no longer serve user goals but optimize for their own. 6. Why Drift Accelerates Under Digital Conditions Digital environments accelerate drift because they: ● amplify incentives ● increase feedback loops ● reduce friction ● obscure structural contradictions ● compress decision cycles Drift that once took decades can now occur in years. This acceleration makes drift more visible—and more consequential. 7. Drift as a Diagnostic Lens in SignalRupture SignalRupture uses drift as a method for understanding institutions. Drift reveals: ● the gap between narrative and structure ● the incentives driving behavior

● the contradictions within systems ● the direction of institutional evolution ● the exposure of underlying logic Drift is not decay. Drift is data . 8. Conclusion Institutional drift is a recurring historical pattern, not a modern failure. It emerges from structural incentives,

bureaucratic

expansion,

and

environmental

change.
