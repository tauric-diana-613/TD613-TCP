    "description": "<p>Title: The Peer Review Experiment: The Root Failure of the Modern Knowledge System</p>\n<p>Description:<br>This essay reframes peer review not as a timeless safeguard of scientific rigor but as a mid‑20th‑century institutional experiment whose underlying assumptions&mdash;expert neutrality, disciplinary coherence, and methodological stability&mdash;no longer hold under contemporary conditions. Drawing on evidence from mass retractions, compromised review processes, reviewer overload, and the structural rigidity of disciplinary boundaries, the paper demonstrates that peer review produces institutional legitimacy rather than epistemic reliability.</p>\n<p>By analyzing epistemic drift, category lock‑in, institutional narrowing, and the economic dependencies embedded in academic publishing, the essay shows how the weaknesses of peer review cascade upward into the entire knowledge system: publishing, indexing, citation economies, evidence‑based policy, and institutional authority.
