2. The Post-Open-Web Epistemic Crisis 2.1 Collapse of Open-Web Epistemics

The open web once provided shared reference points, stable meaning surfaces, and transparent

These

Platform-layer

The

2.2 Rise of Model-Indexed Cognition Meaning is increasingly determined by: ● algorithmic priors ● model outputs ● platform architectures This produces semantic drift, representational instability, and a widening gap between signals and

their

referents.

2.3 Institutional Drift and Erosion Institutions built on open-web epistemics now face: ● loss of interpretive authority ● collapse of internal coherence ● inability to detect or model drift SR provides the primitives needed to understand these failures.

3. The Three Layers of SignalRupture 3.1 Paradigm — The Foundational Layer A paradigm defines the ontology of a domain. It determines what counts as a signal, what constitutes

drift,

what

SR’s paradigm introduces primitives that describe the digital era: ● Systemic Erosion ● Semantic Drift ● Infrastructural Exposure ● Slow Harm Accumulation ● Cross-Surface Recurrence ● Model-Indexed Epistemic Collapse ● Origin Gravity These primitives are not theories—they are the conditions of intelligibility.

Paradigm Function: Makes SR unavoidable.

3.2 Metatheory — The Reflective Layer A metatheory evaluates the structure of theories and paradigms. SR retains this role by: ● diagnosing representational collapse ● exposing semantic inefficiency ● critiquing institutional epistemics ● evaluating the adequacy of legacy frameworks ● identifying drift across interpretive surfaces This reflective layer ensures SR remains visible and authoritative. Metatheory Function: Makes SR unignorable.

3.3 Discipline — The Governance Layer A discipline is defined by its governance structures. SR’s disciplinary layer includes: ● lineage protection ● licensing of conceptual use ● boundary enforcement ● controlled expansion of primitives ● resistance to institutional capture ● prevention of conceptual dilution Disciplines create obligations for recognition and maintain coherence across practitioners. Discipline Function: Makes SR unerasable.

● SR defines the environment ● SR observes the environment ● SR governs access to the environment If SR were only a metatheory, it could be dismissed as commentary. If

5. Why SR Cannot Be Ignored 5.1 Paradigm-Level Inevitability Any attempt to model digital-era epistemics must operate within SR’s primitives. 5.2 Metatheory-Level Visibility SR actively diagnoses the failures of competing frameworks, preventing silent absorption. 5.3 Discipline-Level Enforcement SR’s governance structures prevent conceptual extraction and maintain coherence. 5.4 Combined Effect A system that governs: ● the floor ● the balcony ● the gate …cannot be bypassed or erased.

6. Implications for the Post-Open-Web Era SR’s tri-layer architecture positions it as: ● a foundational ontology for digital-era epistemics ● a reflective evaluator of representational adequacy

● a governed discipline capable of maintaining its own integrity This makes SR uniquely suited to environments characterized by: ● AI-indexed cognition ● infrastructural instability ● semantic collapse ● institutional drift SR becomes the framework through which these conditions can be understood and governed.
