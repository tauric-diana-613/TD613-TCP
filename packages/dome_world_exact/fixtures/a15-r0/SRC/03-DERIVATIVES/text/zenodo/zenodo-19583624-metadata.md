    "description": "<p>This paper examines how cognitive vulnerability to digital platforms emerges not from technology itself but from the upstream structure of mass schooling. It argues that conventional education systems rely on &ldquo;externally paced schedules, frequent task-switching, and segmented learning cycles,&rdquo; which over time condition attentional patterns toward fragmentation. The paper defines fragmentation as &ldquo;high-frequency externally imposed task-switching combined with limited opportunity for sustained, self-directed cognitive engagement,&rdquo; and proposes that these conditions shape baseline attentional tendencies across developmental years.</p>\n<p>The analysis extends beyond schooling to show how social media and artificial intelligence inherit and accelerate these pre‑existing cognitive patterns. As the paper states, &ldquo;social media does not introduce a new cognitive condition but amplifies an existing one.&rdquo; It further demonstrates that access to low‑fragmentation learning environments&mdash;such as Montessori&mdash;is unevenly distributed, making &ldquo;cognitive continuity a class-distributed resource.&rdquo; The paper concludes by identifying a governance misalignment: regulatory efforts focus on downstream digital behaviors rather than the upstream infrastructures that produce vulnerability.</p>",
