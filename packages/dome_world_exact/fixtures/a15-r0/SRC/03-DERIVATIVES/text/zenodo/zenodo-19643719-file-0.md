Analytical Orientation The analysis is structural rather than behavioral: it identifies systemic mechanisms that produce human

1. Introduction: Systems Without Subjects Zombie Infrastructure emerges when systems remain operational while the human subject is no longer

a

functional

participant

in

their

logic.

AI ‑ mediated systems now operate at speeds that exceed human cognition by orders of magnitude:

● Human reaction time: ~200–250 milliseconds ● High ‑ frequency trading execution: sub ‑ millisecond (≤1 ms) This produces a latency asymmetry of approximately 10²–10³, effectively removing humans from real ‑ time

system

participation.

This dynamic is visible in the Flash Crash, where roughly $1 trillion in market value disappeared and

rebounded

within

minutes—faster

than

meaningful

human

intervention.

As systems accelerate: ● interpretation becomes delegation ● oversight becomes proxy monitoring ● agency becomes compliance The result is a structural inversion: systems no longer serve human cognition; humans adapt to system

constraints.

The Ghost Economy names this condition—economic activity that persists and expands while becoming

increasingly

detached

from

human

comprehension

and

material

benefit.

2. The Eroded Subject: Cognitive Limits as Structural Boundary

2.1 Cognitive Load and Fragmentation Empirical indicators show: ● task switching every ~40–50 seconds ● ~23 minutes required to refocus after interruption ● ~60% of knowledge work spent on coordination tasks These conditions produce persistent attentional fragmentation and degraded decision quality (Mark,

2015).

2.2 Burnout Architecture Burnout is a systemic output rather than an individual failure. ● ~70–80% of workers in high ‑ intensity sectors report burnout symptoms ● WHO recognizes burnout as an occupational phenomenon Under continuous acceleration (Rosa, 2013), systems demand cognitive output beyond recovery

capacity

(Maslach

&

Leiter,

2016).

Burnout Architecture describes infrastructures that require sustained cognitive depletion to function.

2.3 The Human as Substrate

Automation conceals rather than removes human labor. From Ghost Work : ● millions of workers perform invisible microtasks ● human input sustains AI systems at scale Examples include content moderation pipelines requiring exposure to thousands of harmful items

daily.

The human subject becomes: ● infrastructural ● necessary ● non ‑ sovereign Zombie Infrastructure emerges when humans maintain systems they cannot meaningfully influence.

3. The Ghost Economy: Machine ‑ Dominated Activity 3.1 Machine ‑ to ‑ Machine Systems Empirical indicators: ● ~50–75% of equity trading is algorithmic ● execution occurs in microseconds During the Flash Crash, feedback loops among algorithms amplified volatility beyond human interpretability.

3.2 Self ‑ Referential Events The GameStop short squeeze illustrates: ● interaction between retail platforms and algorithms ● rapid divergence from underlying fundamentals ● feedback loops driven by system interaction 3.3 Operational Definition The Ghost Economy can be approximated as:

G = Machine ‑ Executed Activity / Total Activity Where: ● G > 0.5 indicates machine ‑ dominant systems ● G → 1 approaches full machine ecology This converts the concept from metaphor to measurable condition.

4. Model Drift: Epistemic Instability as Infrastructure 4.1 Drift as Baseline Machine learning systems degrade over time due to shifting data distributions (Lu et al., 2022). Hidden technical debt accumulates, increasing systemic fragility (Sculley et al., 2015). 4.2 Hallucination and Error Rates Empirical findings: ● hallucination rates range from ~3% to >25% depending on task ● higher in open ‑ ended or knowledge ‑ intensive contexts 4.3 Infrastructure ‑ Level Failure In documented legal cases: ● AI ‑ generated citations were fabricated ● false outputs entered formal legal processes This represents epistemic instability embedded within institutional systems.

5. Infrastructural Overshoot: When Systems Exceed Governance

5.1 Complexity Beyond Control As system complexity increases:

● interpretability declines ● causal tracing becomes impossible ● intervention windows shrink (Perrow, 1999) 5.2 Automation and Displacement ● ~47% of jobs at risk of automation (Frey & Osborne, 2017) ● partial task automation already widespread 5.3 Externalized Costs Optimization shifts costs onto individuals and institutions: ● cognitive → burnout ● economic → precarity ● institutional → drift Efficiency is achieved through distributed degradation.

6. Surplus Populations in Automated Systems 6.1 Cognitive Surplus Individuals unable to interface with high ‑ speed systems become structurally excluded. 6.2 Economic Surplus Indicators include: ● gig economy expansion ● wage stagnation ● underemployment 6.3 Institutional Surplus Institutions persist but lose functional control, becoming symbolic stabilizers.

7. Governance Lag: Systems Beyond Control

7.1 Temporal Mismatch ● technological change: months ● governance response: years 7.2 Reactive Regulation Governance typically follows crisis, as seen after the 2008 financial crisis. 7.3 Illusion of Stability Dashboards and metrics obscure underlying instability, producing false signals of control. Zombie Infrastructure is thus: operational continuity without epistemic access.

8. Boundary Conditions and Reversal Constraints Zombie Infrastructure emerges under specific conditions: Required Conditions ● high system speed relative to human cognition ● high complexity and interdependence ● low interpretability ● weak or delayed governance Partial Reversal Conditions ● enforced human ‑ in ‑ the ‑ loop systems ● slowed or rate ‑ limited infrastructures ● high interpretability requirements However, once systems reach high machine ‑ dependence and institutional embedding, reversal becomes

structurally

constrained.

9. Temporal Dynamics: The Drift Sequence Zombie Infrastructure develops through identifiable stages:

1. Optimization acceleration 2. Cognitive strain emergence 3. Delegation threshold crossing 4. Interpretability collapse 5. Machine ‑ to ‑ machine closure 6. Governance lag 7. Persistent drift (zombie state)
