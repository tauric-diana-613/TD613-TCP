    "description": "<p><strong>Rebuilding the Meaning of GDP</strong> examines why Gross Domestic Product has become a post‑functional metric within modern economies. Although GDP was originally designed to measure industrial output in the 1930s, it is now routinely used as a proxy for well‑being, ecological stability, and institutional health &mdash; domains it was never built to represent. As the paper notes, <em>&ldquo;GDP is not incorrect&mdash;it is blind to system quality.&rdquo;</em></p>\n<p>The essay reframes GDP as a zombie metric operating within necromantic infrastructure: a system that continues to move and update while misreading the underlying conditions of society. It explains how GDP rises through depletion, illness, disaster, and debt‑driven consumption, and why the mathematical abstraction behind GDP cannot distinguish between regenerative and extractive activity.</p>\n<p>Using case studies from healthcare, housing, telecommunications, the 2008 financial crisis, and climate‑driven disasters, the paper demonstrates how GDP systematically obscures fragility, inequality, and ecological loss. It introduces a four‑layer interpretive framework &mdash; GDP, GDP‑D (Distortions), GDP‑C (Corrections), and GDP‑S (Stability) &mdash; to restore meaning to economic measurement and reconnect metrics with real social and ecological conditions.</p>",
