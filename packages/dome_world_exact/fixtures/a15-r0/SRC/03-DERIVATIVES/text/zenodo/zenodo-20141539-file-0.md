Core Principle SR must be able to measure divergence between: ● its own theoretical predictions ● observed institutional outcomes ● and cross ‑ framework comparative benchmarks This produces a structured multi ‑ reference validation space rather than a closed loop. 4. MDC Architecture

4.1 Symbolic–Operational Separation Test MDC evaluates whether SR’s conceptual claims exceed what its operational analyses can support.

● Symbolic layer: theoretical claims, constructs, and abstractions ● Operational layer: empirical cases, system observations, measurable patterns MDC flags persistent gaps between abstraction and demonstrable structural recurrence. 4.2 Constraint ‑ of ‑ Framework Analysis (CFA) Every theoretical system has bounded applicability. MDC identifies SR’s constraints across: ● domain scope (where SR applies) ● temporal scale (short vs long horizon validity) ● system type (bureaucratic, algorithmic, hybrid) ● informational density conditions This prevents universalist overreach. 4.3 Boundary Condition Testing MDC identifies environments where SR’s predictive strength weakens or transforms: ● hyper ‑ centralized systems ● fully automated governance architectures ● high ‑ speed decision systems with minimal human mediation These are not failures of SR, but structural boundary zones. 4.4 Cross ‑ System Drift Comparator (CSDC) To avoid epistemic closure, MDC is paired with external comparison: SR outputs are compared against: ● alternative institutional theories ● observed administrative performance data ● historical governance behavior patterns This introduces external variance pressure into SR evaluation. 4.5 Meta ‑ ODD (Self ‑ Drift Metric) SR introduces a self ‑ consistency measure:

[ ODD_{SR} = |P - E| ] Where: ● P = SR predicted structural behavior ● E = observed institutional or comparative behavior This metric is not standalone truth validation, but a drift indicator across expectation layers. 5. Application of MDC to SR 5.1 Low Drift Domains SR aligns strongly with: ● welfare system processing delays ● housing supply constraints ● administrative burden accumulation ● institutional fragmentation under demand pressure These show strong structural consistency. 5.2 Moderate Drift Domains ● algorithmic governance systems ● automated eligibility systems ● hybrid human–AI administrative models Here SR remains valid but requires refinement of automation’s secondary effects. 5.3 High Drift Domains ● micro ‑ level behavioral prediction ● short ‑ term political volatility ● symbolic or discursive shocks These lie partially outside SR’s primary causal domain (infrastructure ‑ first dynamics). 6. Why MDC Strengthens SR The MDC strengthens SR by: ● preventing self ‑ sealing theoretical behavior ● maintaining external falsifiability pressure
