    "description": "<p>This essay develops and applies the Outcome Drift Differential (ODD) framework to measure the divergence between institutional policy targets and observed outcomes across four major systems: ODSP, Ontario Works, Canadian housing policy, and U.S. housing policy. Drawing on official policy directives, administrative datasets, and national housing indicators, the paper demonstrates that persistent negative drift is not primarily a function of political will or funding levels but of infrastructural constraints embedded within administrative architectures, eligibility rules, supply systems, and legacy institutional designs.</p>\n<p>The essay advances infrastructure determinism, the principle that institutional systems behave according to their operational architectures rather than their stated intentions. As the paper states, &ldquo;intentions are symbolic while infrastructures are causal&rdquo; (Preface). Through cross‑system ODD calculations, the analysis shows that administrative burden, supply shortages, fragmentation, and path dependence systematically mediate implementation performance. The CERB case is used as a low‑drift comparison, illustrating how simplified infrastructures reduce divergence between intent and outcome.</p>\n<p>The paper concludes that infrastructural design&mdash;not policy aspiration&mdash;sets the feasible range of institutional performance, and that persistent drift across domains reflects structural determinants rather than program‑level variance.</p>",
