    "description": "<p>The Disciplinary Silo Experiment: How Fragmentation Became the Architecture of Institutional Knowledge reframes disciplinary boundaries not as natural divisions of expertise but as a historical experiment in complexity management that has structurally failed. Drawing on evidence from peer‑review failures, publishing practices, retraction data, and post‑publication review platforms, the essay demonstrates that silos generate fragmentation, epistemic drift, and conceptual incompatibility across fields. Rather than enabling coherence, silos enforce narrow vocabularies, discipline‑specific norms, and methodological isolation, producing a system incapable of integrating cross‑domain knowledge. The paper shows how publishing, indexing, and citation economies reinforce these boundaries, creating a feedback loop in which legitimacy is produced through compliance rather than coherence. By exposing silos as an institutional experiment rather than a natural epistemic structure, the essay reveals the fragility of the entire knowledge architecture and the inability of institutions to respond to complex, cross‑domain phenomena in the post‑web environment.</p>",
