2. Introduction Institutions do not encounter people directly. They encounter administratively mediated representations

of

and

Human behaviour is inherently high-dimensional. Behavioural reality contains: ● contextual variation, ● temporal instability, ● relational dynamics, ● environmental pressures, ● adaptive responses, ● emotional states, ● and situational ambiguity. Institutions cannot fully metabolize this complexity at scale. Governance systems require: ● throughput, ● standardization, ● auditability, ● procedural consistency, ● liability containment, ● and categorical legibility. As a result, institutions transform behavioural complexity into administratively manageable representations.

The SignalRupture (SR) framework conceptualizes this process as a multi-altitude transformation

across

four

Governance

Governance Altitude Structural Function GA1 Behavioural reality GA2 Operational mediation GA3 Administrative compression GA4 Oversight stabilization The present study evaluates whether the transformation from GA1 to GA3 follows a stable structural

pattern

across

unrelated

institutional

systems.

The central SR proposition is: Institutions fit people into their theories, not theories to people. This proposition is evaluated empirically through cross-domain metric convergence.

4. Methods 4.1 Study Design

A comparative cross-domain design was used to evaluate institutional transformation across ten public

4.2 Operational Definitions Signal Compression Ratio (SCR) SCR measures the degree to which behavioural information is compressed during institutional translation.

SCR = \frac{F}{I} Where: ● F = behavioural signal units, ● I = retained institutional units. Higher SCR values indicate greater compression.

Administrative Abstraction Index (AAI) AAI measures the dominance of procedural abstraction relative to contextual behavioural detail. Administrative abstraction categories included: ● procedural terminology, ● diagnostic coding, ● eligibility logic, ● compliance framing, ● risk categorization, ● audit terminology. Scores: ● 1.0–1.9 = context-dominant, ● 2.0–2.9 = abstraction-dominant, ● 3.0+ = highly proceduralized.

Ground-Truth Discrepancy Index (GTDI) GTDI measures divergence between behavioural complexity and institutional representation. GTDI = 1 - \frac{I_c + I_t + I_r}{F_c + F_t + F_r} Where:

● c = contextual units, ● t = temporal units, ● r = relational units. Higher GTDI values indicate greater representational divergence.

4.3 Coding Procedure Behavioural Units Included ● contextual references, ● temporal continuity, ● emotional states, ● adaptive behaviour, ● environmental pressures, ● relational dynamics, ● survival strategies, ● behavioural transitions. Administrative Units Included ● compliance labels, ● procedural outcomes, ● eligibility determinations, ● diagnostic abstraction, ● behavioural categorization, ● institutional status coding, ● audit-compatible representations. Each case underwent: 1. behavioural decomposition, 2. institutional abstraction decomposition, 3. divergence coding, 4. metric computation. The objective was structural comparison rather than moral adjudication.

GA1 — Behavioural Reality High-dimensional lived complexity: ● unstable, ● contextual, ● adaptive, ● relational, ● temporally dynamic. This altitude contains maximum signal density.

GA2 — Operational Mediation Frontline translation processes: ● incident reporting, ● intake systems, ● triage coding, ● case management, ● behavioural documentation. This layer partially compresses behavioural signal into operationally manageable form.

GA3 — Administrative Compression Behavioural complexity becomes: ● categories, ● procedural summaries, ● diagnostic abstractions, ● eligibility determinations, ● risk classifications, ● audit-compatible representation. This altitude produces maximum signal reduction and abstraction density.

GA4 — Oversight Stabilization Oversight systems: ● partially restore context, ● identify procedural inconsistency, ● recommend reforms, ● stabilize legitimacy, ● preserve governance continuity. Oversight rarely dismantles compression architecture itself.

9. Discussion 9.1 Institutions as Theory-First Architectures Institutions do not begin from behavioural reality itself. They begin from: ● eligibility theory, ● diagnostic theory, ● procedural theory, ● compliance theory, ● risk-management theory. These schemas determine: ● what becomes legible, ● what becomes actionable, ● what becomes administratively real. Behavioural input is then reshaped to fit institutional cognition structures. 9.2 Necessary Versus Pathological Compression The SR framework does not claim that all compression is avoidable. Large-scale systems require abstraction in order to: ● coordinate action, ● maintain throughput, ● preserve procedural consistency, ● sustain operational continuity. Emergency medicine, corrections systems, and welfare administration cannot metabolize unlimited

SR therefore distinguishes between: ● necessary operational compression,

and: ● pathological compression exceeding adaptive necessity. Pathological compression occurs when: ● contextual fidelity collapses, ● behavioural nuance becomes procedurally invisible, ● institutional representation diverges excessively from lived conditions, ● and administrative categories replace behavioural understanding entirely.

9.3 Why Distortion Is Structural Institutions operate under: ● finite administrative capacity, ● throughput pressure, ● legal liability, ● political visibility, ● resource scarcity, ● audit requirements. Under these conditions, governance systems cannot sustain high-dimensional behavioural representation

at

scale.

Compression becomes operationally necessary. Thus: Institutional distortion is not a malfunction.

9.4 Oversight as Stabilization Oversight systems frequently: ● restore partial context, ● identify procedural breakdowns, ● recommend documentation reform.

However, oversight rarely dismantles the underlying compression architecture itself. Oversight therefore stabilizes governance legitimacy more often than it restores behavioural fidelity.

10. Boundary Conditions of SR SR distortion effects may weaken under conditions of: ● low throughput demand, ● high staff-to-client ratios, ● decentralized governance, ● relational service models, ● long-duration case continuity, ● reduced audit dependency, ● elevated contextual discretion. The framework therefore does not claim uniform distortion intensity across all governance systems.

Instead, SR predicts that distortion intensity varies according to administrative processing constraints.
