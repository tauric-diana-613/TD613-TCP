    "description": "<p><strong>Structural Perception Theory (SPT)</strong> introduces a diagnostic framework for analyzing institutional motion by treating perception as an independent analytical capability rather than a secondary outcome of institutional behavior. The paper argues that institutions frequently become dependent on internally generated narratives that preserve legitimacy while reducing sensitivity to emerging structural conditions. As stated in the paper, institutions often experience <em>&ldquo;a progressive loss of correspondence between institutional self‑understanding and institutional structure.&rdquo;</em></p>\n<p>SPT builds upon the SignalRupture (SR) framework by formalizing the perceptual principles required to observe institutional motion across diverse organizational environments. It defines five structural operators&mdash;<strong>Pressure, Reflex, Drift, Collapse, and Reorganization</strong>&mdash;as recurrent modes of institutional adaptation. These operators allow analysts to perceive latent structural dynamics before they become visible through conventional performance indicators.</p>\n<p>The paper integrates insights from organizational sociology, systems theory, governance research, and cognitive analysis, synthesizing contributions from Berger &amp; Luckmann, Meyer &amp; Rowan, Goffman, Bourdieu, Foucault, Luhmann, and March &amp; Olsen. It positions structural perception as the methodological foundation of the SR metatheory, complementing the perceptual augmentation introduced in <em>Institutional Sharingan</em>.</p>\n<p>SPT concludes with falsifiable propositions and diagnostic applications, establishing the framework as both an analytical methodology and a foundation for future empirical research into institutional motion.</p>",
