Risk SignalRupture (SR) PREFACE Modern societies are entering a period defined not by isolated crises, but by directional worsening

resilience,

epistemic

This

Drift

Collapse

Field

(DCF)

for

representing

those

as

a

time-varying,

six-dimensional

system

generated

DCF

driver

dimensional

interaction

coefficients,

field

It

performance

2030

concern

online

business

United

Under

reproducible

vector

rather

an

official

The

also

proposes

a

Drift

Interaction

Matrix

and

formalizes

amplification,

dampening,

and

intervention

effects

without

treating

interaction

coefficients

as

established

empirical

constants. The cross-national Canada–United States–United Kingdom comparison is retained as a research hypothesis

A

common

normalization,

common

This

testable

and

The

The

Drift

Science

not

for

1.2 Drift: The Missing Concept Drift is defined here as persistent directional change in a structural indicator or latent system state

that

increases

exposure

to

adverse

outcomes

faster

capacity

offsets

This

original

SR

worsening

specified

relative

to

an

explicit

reference

condition,

target,

baseline,

or

resilience

criterion. Drift is not fate, prophecy, or a synonym for all change. It is a measurable relationship among direction,

persistence,

exposure,

and

response

capacity. • a pressure represented through indicators • a direction represented through signed change • a magnitude represented through normalized intensity • a dynamic state represented through time

• a structural condition that remains modifiable Drift can explain why improvement in one subsystem may fail to alter a broader structural trajectory.

Electricity

can

A

A

DCF

1.3X Relation to Existing Theories: What Drift Science Borrows and Where It Departs Complexity science What DCF borrows: non-linearity, feedback, emergence, cross-scale coupling. Where DCF departs: adds an explicit signed concept of directional structural pressure and requires

operational

benchmarks

for

worsening.

Risk society What DCF borrows: systemic risks generated by modernity and institutional difficulty managing them. Where DCF departs: moves from distribution and perception of risk toward a quantitative cross-domain

state

representation.

Resilience theory What DCF borrows: adaptive cycles, thresholds, reorganization, cross-scale effects. Where DCF departs: focuses on persistent pressure and response-capacity gaps, while remaining compatible

with

recovery

and

reorganization.

Political economy What DCF borrows: lock-in, infrastructural inertia, dependence, power and capture.

Where DCF departs: extends analysis across epistemic, technological, geopolitical and cultural dimensions

rather

than

treating

material

interests

as

the

sole

explanatory

Climate science What DCF borrows: measured trajectories, physical impacts, thresholds and uncertainty. Where DCF departs: uses climate variables as one component of a wider societal state rather than

redefining

climate

science.

Digital governance and platform studies What DCF borrows: algorithmic amplification, gatekeeping, moderation, visibility and market power. Where DCF departs: treats digital systems as interacting structural drivers whose effects must be estimated

rather

than

assumed.

Sociology and cultural theory What DCF borrows: identity, norms, meaning, trust and social cohesion. Where DCF departs: formalizes selected constructs as indicators while recognizing that many cultural

variables

are

latent

and

measurement-sensitive.

Systems ecology What DCF borrows: stress gradients, thresholds, feedbacks and multi-scale dynamics. Where DCF departs: translates some systems concepts into institutional and informational domains

without

claiming

physical

equivalence.

What Drift Science Adds as a Research Program • explicit directionality relative to defined baselines • multi-domain integration under common normalization rules • separate vector magnitude and scalar interaction pressure • an interaction matrix with signed coefficients • cross-national comparability requirements • a falsification and out-of-sample validation pathway • an intervention model that can be evaluated against simpler alternatives The intellectual independence of DCF therefore depends on empirical performance.
