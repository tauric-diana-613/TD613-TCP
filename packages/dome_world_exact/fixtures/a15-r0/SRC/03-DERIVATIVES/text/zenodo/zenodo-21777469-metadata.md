    "description": "<p><strong>Infrastructural Political Economy: Government Agency Under Infrastructure Dependency</strong> introduces a new analytical field for understanding how modern states exercise agency within privatized infrastructural environments. The monograph formalizes the GAUID/GAI framework &mdash; <em>Government Agency Under Infrastructure Dependency</em> &mdash; a structural, mathematical, and empirical model designed to measure how dependency, substitution capacity, regulatory leverage, and operator structural power shape the operational boundaries of contemporary state action.</p>\n<p>Modern governments increasingly rely on infrastructures they do not own, do not fully control, and cannot easily replace. As the manuscript states, <em>&ldquo;agency is no longer a purely institutional property. It is infrastructural.&rdquo;</em> Telecommunications networks, cloud platforms, logistics chains, payment systems, and wage‑setting mechanisms have become privately governed socio‑technical systems. Classical theories of autonomy, capacity, and bureaucratic authority cannot account for this condition.</p>\n<p>The GAUID/GAI framework addresses this gap by integrating conceptual theory, non‑linear mathematical formulation, and cross‑sector empirical calibration. The model provides normalized scoring for Infrastructure Dependency, Substitution Capacity, Regulatory Leverage, and Structural Power, along with interaction terms and threshold lock‑in dynamics. A full measurement architecture is included, enabling sector‑level and national‑level Government Agency Index (GAI) computation.</p>\n<p>The monograph includes conceptual foundations, structural logic, mathematical equations, sensitivity analysis, and a complete empirical case study. The Appendix contains the full technical specification required for replication and further research.</p>\n<p>This work establishes <strong>Infrastructural Political Economy</strong> as a foundational field and provides the first replicable, cross‑national method for evaluating how infrastructural conditions constrain or enable modern state agency.</p>\n<div>\n</div>\n<h1><strong>Funding/Contribution</strong></h1>\n<p>This monograph contributes three major advances to contemporary political economy, governance studies, and socio‑technical analysis:</p>\n<h3><strong>1. Establishes a new field: Infrastructural Political Economy</strong></h3>\n<p>It defines the conceptual and analytical space for studying how infrastructural ownership, governance, and operation shape political outcomes and state behavior. It fills a gap left by classical theories that assume states act through systems they control.</p>\n<h3><strong>2. Introduces the GAUID/GAI framework &mdash; the first quantitative model of infrastructural agency</strong></h3>\n<p>The work provides a complete mathematical structure for measuring government agency under infrastructural dependency. It includes normalized variables, interaction terms, threshold dynamics, and a clamping‑based normalization function ensuring interpretive consistency.</p>\n<h3><strong>3. Provides a replicable empirical architecture</strong></h3>\n<p>The monograph offers a full measurement strategy, sectoral scoring system, weighting model, and national‑level aggregation method. The Appendix includes the complete technical specification, enabling researchers, regulators, and institutions to replicate the model across countries and sectors.</p>\n<p>Together, these contributions make the monograph the <strong>foundational text</strong> of the Infrastructural Political Economy field and the first operational framework for evaluating how privatized infrastructures shape the boundaries of modern state agency.</p>\n<div>\n</div>",
