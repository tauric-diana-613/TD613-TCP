CHAPTER 2 — WHAT IS A POLICY SUBSTRATE? The term substrate is used deliberately. A substrate is the layer through which higher-order

processes

operate.

In

SR,

is

It

administrative

others

and

others

Infrastructure — physical, financial, digital, logistical, informational, and organizational systems

•

Dependency — concentration of essential functions in actors or systems outside the direct

of

•

Capacity — the material and administrative ability to execute policy. •

Incentive — the reward and penalty structure influencing actor behavior. •

Visibility — the degree to which relevant causal conditions are observable to decision-makers

and

affected

populations.

•

Exposure — the distribution of consequences across populations, institutions, and time. These dimensions interact. Infrastructure creates dependency. Dependency alters incentives.

Incentives

affect

Institutional

changes

exposure.

Exposure

generates

outcomes.

Outcomes

visible

through

measurement

that

may

be

incomplete. The substrate is therefore dynamic rather than static. 2.1 The Substrate as a Causal Layer The policy substrate can be represented as a vector X_s(t) = [I, Dep, C, Inc, V, E], where each

component

may

itself

The

policy

layer

P(t)

sits

emerge

weaken.

3.4 Collapse Collapse occurs when accumulated drift and pressure exceed the system's ability to absorb

3.6 The SR Policy State The policy system at time t is represented as:

S(t) = {D(t), Δ V(t), H(t), Φ( t)} D(t) = structural drift. Δ V(t) = visibility lag, the temporal distance between structural change and reliable recognition

of

Authority — legal or formal power to act. •

Capacity — ability to execute an authorized action. •

Autonomy — ability to choose among meaningful alternatives.

Interests

Fear

Defensive behavior creates isolation. Isolation fragments systems into competing micro-environments.

Fragmentation

problem

rather

its

elimination. This is a major distinction between policy failure and structural reproduction. 6.1 Policy Incentive Lock-In Policy incentive lock-in occurs when actors receive stronger rewards for maintaining an existing

the

substrate

that

produces

the

problem. Let B_m be the benefit of maintaining the existing arrangement and B_t the benefit of transforming

Transformation

if

the

of

implementing

to

the

behavior.

24. Empirical Falsification The Canadian case can falsify or weaken SR if: 1. infrastructure dependency has no relationship with effective policy agency; 2. concentration has no measurable relationship with constraint after controls; 3. institutional authority consistently translates into effective outcomes regardless of dependency; 4. infrastructure redundancy does not moderate the predicted relationship; 5. cross-domain propagation is absent; 6. visibility lag cannot be measured reliably; 7. alternative causal models explain the observed outcomes substantially better; 8. SR measurement models fail tests of reliability, validity, or measurement invariance. The field therefore remains empirically vulnerable. This is essential to its scientific status.
