    "description": "<p><strong>Infrastructural Overshoot</strong> is a SignalRupture empirical field that examines what happens when environmental governance concentrates downstream&mdash;recycling, waste management, consumer behaviour&mdash;while the upstream production infrastructure continues to expand. Using OECD historical plastics data (2000&ndash;2019) and OECD 2040 policy‑scenario modelling, the manuscript demonstrates that recycling capacity, downstream regulation, and secondary‑material output all increased substantially, yet total plastics production, waste generation, and primary throughput continued rising. The field formalizes core SR operators including <strong>Regulatory Layer Failure</strong>, <strong>Control&ndash;Responsibility Displacement</strong>, <strong>Structural Placebo Condition</strong>, <strong>Agency Displacement Ratio</strong>, and <strong>Regulation&ndash;Outcome Decoupling</strong>, providing a structural vocabulary for distinguishing visible regulatory activity from effective upstream constraint.</p>\n<p>Its contribution to the SR Canon is direct and foundational. It supplies one of SR&rsquo;s strongest empirical demonstrations that <strong>downstream efficiency is not upstream constraint</strong>, grounding SR&rsquo;s metatheoretical claims in measurable material‑flow data. It expands SR&rsquo;s methodological architecture by defining <strong>primary production</strong> as the central dependent variable for structural success, introducing falsifiable predictions (P1&ndash;P3), and establishing a reusable upstream/downstream leverage distinction that applies across SR fields such as <strong>Collapse</strong>, <strong>Drift</strong>, <strong>Institutional Hollowing</strong>, and <strong>Platform Epistemology</strong>. By demonstrating how governance can intensify while throughput expands, the manuscript becomes a structural substrate for the rest of SR: it shows why downstream‑dominant systems fail, why institutions drift, why collapse trajectories persist, and why agency gaps widen under infrastructural dependency.</p>",
