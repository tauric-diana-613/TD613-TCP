2. Core Definitions Construct Operational definition Cognitive infrastructure The institutional, material, relational, temporal, and technological conditions through which cognitive capacity is formed, exercised, expressed, delegated, or suppressed. Exposure-Silo Bias Bias or incompleteness produced when structurally co-occurring exposures are modeled separately and their joint developmental load is excluded. Compound Exposure Measurement Window A declared period in which relevant exposures, supports, transitions, and outcomes are aligned for systematic inference. Output–Capacity Gap The difference between performance achieved with assistance and capacity available without that assistance under comparable conditions. Developmental non-formation Failure to construct a skill because essential practice was delegated before the underlying representation became stable; distinct from loss of a previously acquired skill. Developmental Automation Contradiction The institutional demand that children use automation for learning or productivity while being judged as though cognition developed independently of that automation. Time–Money Care Constraint The limit on adult developmental support created by paid work, irregular schedules, commuting, chores, health, care obligations, and insufficient income. Digital care substitution Use of digital media to supply supervision, relief, entertainment, instruction, or emotional regulation when adult time or affordable alternatives are constrained. Temporal Fragmentation Architecture An environment that repeatedly redirects cognition before immersion, closure, or consolidation can occur. Cognitive Re-entry Tax The time, effort, error risk, and stress required to reconstruct an interrupted task.

14. Identification and Statistical Discipline •

Declare the causal estimand before selecting controls. •

Do not control away mediators when estimating total effects. •

Separate between-child differences from within-child change. •

Model schools, households, and neighbourhoods as nested or cross-classified contexts.

•

Test exposure interactions only with adequate power and preregistered direction.

•

Use negative controls and sensitivity analyses for unmeasured confounding. •

Report missingness and selection into device telemetry or optional surveys. •

Distinguish screen duration, content, platform mechanics, social context, and displaced

activity.

•

Measure prior mastery before interpreting offloading as non-formation. •

Report heterogeneity and protective effects rather than forcing one population-average

conclusion.

15.2 Developmentally Staged Automation •

Specify which cognitive operation students must construct independently. •

Delay full automation until baseline mastery is demonstrated, except where accessibility

requires

assistance.

•

Use calculators and AI for checking, comparison, explanation, and extension after

an

authentic

attempt.

•

Require students to identify errors, justify strategies, and transfer solutions to new

problems.

•

Assess both functional tool-supported performance and independent foundational

capacity.

•

Protect students from surveillance-intensive educational technology and preserve

human

appeal.

15.3 Support-Compatible Assessment •

Every new assessment demand should include funded, schedule-compatible remediation.

•

Offer support during school hours, evenings, weekends, and online without assuming

parental

availability.

•

Publish distributional impact by income, disability, race, language, geography, and

work-schedule

context.

•

Prevent test results from becoming punitive labels that recursively reduce opportunity.

•

Use assessment to trigger resources, not merely document deficits. 15.4 Attention Stewardship •

Create longer protected work periods where task demands require them. •

Teach checkpointing and leave visible resumption cues before transitions. •

Coordinate assignment calendars to reduce artificial simultaneity. •

Build natural stopping points into educational platforms.

•

Measure completion, delayed learning, and wellbeing—not engagement time alone.

•

Provide recreation and social space so algorithmic entertainment is not the only accessible

recovery

environment.
