5. The Algorithmic Structural Loop The foundational process of the field is the Algorithmic Structural Loop (ASL). 1.

Collection: behavioural, administrative, sensor, textual, financial, social, or institutional traces

are

captured.

2.

Representation: traces are converted into features, embeddings, categories, scores, or proxies.

3.

Classification: a rule or model assigns relevance, risk, preference, identity, priority, or predicted

value.

4.

Exposure or allocation: classifications determine visibility, sequence, eligibility, price, scrutiny,

service,

or

resource

distribution.

5.

Adaptation: people and institutions change behaviour in response to the arranged environment.

6.

Recursive ingestion: those adaptations generate new data that are treated as evidence for future

classification.

X_t → R_t → C_t → A_t → B_(t+1) → X_(t+1) Here X is captured data, R is computational representation, C is classification, A is exposure

or

allocation,

B

is

behavioural

adaptation,

and

the

final

term

is

the

next

data

state.

The

loop

becomes

structurally

consequential

when

A

possesses

institutional

or

platform

authority

and

when

B

is

subsequently

as

independent

confirmation

of

C.
