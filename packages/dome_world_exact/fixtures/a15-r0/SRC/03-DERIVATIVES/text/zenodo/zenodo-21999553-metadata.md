    "description": "<h1><strong>SR Algorithm Field </strong></h1>\n<h2><strong>Description</strong></h2>\n<p>The <strong>SR Algorithm Field</strong> is a foundational SignalRupture domain that studies algorithms as <em>recursive structural operators</em>&mdash;systems that observe behaviour, classify traces, allocate visibility or resources, induce behavioural adaptation, and then absorb those altered behaviours as new evidence. As the manuscript states, algorithms increasingly determine &ldquo;<em>what becomes visible, relevant, eligible, credible, profitable, dangerous, employable, insurable, treatable, searchable, or forgettable</em>&rdquo; (from the Preface).</p>\n<p>The field formalizes the <strong>Algorithmic Structural Loop</strong>, a six‑stage causal chain&mdash;collection, representation, classification, allocation, adaptation, and recursive ingestion&mdash;through which computational systems transform the environments they later measure. The manuscript defines measurable constructs including the <strong>Algorithmic Structural Power Index</strong>, <strong>Personalized Browse Divergence</strong>, <strong>Preference&ndash;Exposure Discrepancy</strong>, <strong>Algorithmic Exposure Concentration</strong>, <strong>Delayed Reinforcement Architecture</strong>, <strong>Proxy Translation Error</strong>, and <strong>Contestability Deficit</strong>.</p>\n<p>Unlike conceptual algorithmic theory, the SR Algorithm Field is <strong>empirical</strong>. The manuscript analyzes <strong>17,207,376 interactions</strong> from the KuaiRec dataset, rejecting the simple narrowing hypothesis and demonstrating dose‑responsive <strong>delayed reinforcement</strong>, where strong engagement increases same‑category recurrence &ldquo;<em>five to twenty recommendations later</em>&rdquo; (from Section 8.8). This establishes algorithms as systems that can preserve local diversity while shaping long‑run exposure trajectories.</p>\n<div>\n</div>\n<h2><strong>How It Contributes to the SR Canon</strong></h2>\n<p>The SR Algorithm Field contributes to SignalRupture in four major ways:</p>\n<h3><strong>1. It converts SR&rsquo;s prior algorithmic metaphors into a testable scientific field.</strong></h3>\n<p>The manuscript explicitly consolidates earlier SR work&mdash;crawlers, conditioning, cognitive offloading, semantic governance, metadata suppression&mdash;into a unified architecture, noting that these papers &ldquo;<em>approached one mechanism from different surfaces</em>&rdquo; (Section 3).<br>EAO, CCIS, SPS, Collapse Epistemology, and System Inheritance all rely on algorithmic selection; this field provides the <strong>mechanism</strong>.</p>\n<h3><strong>2. It establishes algorithms as structural operators, not technical tools.</strong></h3>\n<p>Algorithms become part of the causal substrate of modern institutions. As the manuscript states:</p>\n<blockquote>\n<p>&ldquo;<em>A classification changes the distribution of visibility, attention, opportunity, risk, time, or material resources.</em>&rdquo;<br>This reframes algorithms as <strong>governance infrastructure</strong>, aligning with SR&rsquo;s substrate‑level ontology.</p>\n</blockquote>\n<h3><strong>3. It introduces empirical operators that can be used across SR fields.</strong></h3>\n<p>The field&rsquo;s constructs&mdash;ASPI, PED, PBD, AEC, DRA, PTE, CD&mdash;become reusable operators for:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p>CCIS (childhood cognitive infrastructure)</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>SPS (systems propagation science)</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>EAO (epistemic architecture obsolescence)</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>Overshoot</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>Collapse Epistemology</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p>Platform Epistemology</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>These operators allow SR to measure algorithmic influence on cognition, governance, visibility, and resource allocation.</p>\n<h3><strong>4. It provides SR&rsquo;s first large‑scale empirical test of recursive algorithmic influence.</strong></h3>\n<p>The KuaiRec analysis demonstrates:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p><strong>local diversity &uarr;</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>delayed directional reinforcement &uarr;</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>dose‑response patterns</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>rejection of immediate repetition</strong></p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>This supports the manuscript&rsquo;s central claim that &ldquo;<em>local variety and directional reinforcement can coexist</em>&rdquo; (Section 8.9).<br>It is the first SR field with millions of observations and formal statistical comparisons.</p>",
