1.3 Core Questions •

Which historical institutions generated the variables treated as present-day evidence?

•

Can protected or historical status be reconstructed from apparently neutral features?

•

Does model output merely reproduce the source distribution, or does it increase

disparity?

•

Does deployment alter surveillance, allocation, behavior, eligibility, or opportunity?

•

Do deployment-produced outcomes enter subsequent administrative records

or

training

data?

•

Which interventions attenuate inheritance without concealing genuine need or

degrading

valid

prediction?

•

Under what conditions does an institution reverse, rather than automate, its inherited

trajectory?

A

Healthcare

3.3 Proxy Reconstruction Proxy reconstruction occurs when a protected or historical state can be predicted from

A

3.4 Amplification Amplification occurs when a model or model-mediated process increases a measured

3.5 Recursive Inheritance

Recursive inheritance exists when outcomes affected by a system become evidence

The

Recursion

3.6 Attenuation and Reversal Inheritance is not fate. A model may attenuate an inherited disparity by improving measurement,

Accurate

13. Discrimination and Racism: Measurement Without Conceptual Collapse

13.1 Four Distinct Questions 1.

Is there a disparity in outcomes, errors, exposure, or allocation? 2.

Does protected status, or a valid proxy for structurally racialized position, predict

the

difference

after

declared

adjustments?

3.

Did a policy, model, or deployment cause some portion of the difference? 4.

Does the process satisfy a legal, normative, or institutional definition of discrimination,

including

consideration

of

justification

and

less

harmful

alternatives? A “yes” at one level does not mechanically answer the next. Racism can be studied

as

institutional

structure,

differential

disparate

or

The

Intent

for

but

be

solely

13.2 Recommended Metrics •

Selection and denial-rate ratios with absolute percentage-point differences. •

False-positive, false-negative, calibration, and ranking differences with uncertainty.

•

Exposure-adjusted discovery or productivity rates. •

Counterfactual outcome differences under feasible alternative policies. •

Within-institution and within-location comparisons to reduce compositional confounding.

•

Intersectional estimates, with shrinkage or partial pooling where sample sizes

are

small.

•

Spatial spillovers and neighborhood-level allocation effects.

Inheritance requires a durable information channel; rapidly changing or genuinely

channels.

Oversight should not ask only whether the current model is accurate. It should ask

whether

the

institution

is

converting

its

own

past

conduct

into

evidence

for

future

conduct.

This

is

the

governance

question

unique

to

system

inheritance.

21. Discussion The empirical and experimental record supports the plausibility of system inheritance.

Historical

spatial

classifications

remain

associated

with

present

conditions.

Neutral

features

can

reconstruct

racialized

and

historical

status.

Enforcement,

mortgage,

and

school

records

exhibit

patterned

persistence.

Controlled

research

shows

that

algorithms

can

amplify

training

associations,

predictions

can

change

their

targets,

biased

AI

can

alter

human

judgment,

and

recursively

generated

data

can

distort

successor

models.

The evidence does not yet support a universal law of AI amplification. Public datasets

rarely

contain

the

complete

model-action-retraining

chain.

Proprietary

systems

obscure

version

histories,

scores,

thresholds,

and

overrides.

Administrative

records

often

fail

to

distinguish

independently

occurring

events

from

events

discovered

because

the

system

looked

for

them.

These

limitations

are

not

reasons

to

abandon

the

field;

they

identify

its

measurement

agenda.

System Inheritance Studies is strongest when it remains disciplined. Its contribution

is

not

the

declaration

that

all

systems

are

racist

or

that

algorithms

mechanically

preserve

history.

Its

contribution

is

a

method

for

locating

the

transmission

channel,

measuring

its

strength,

determining

whether

deployment

enlarges

it,

and

testing

whether

intervention

can

break

it.

22. Conclusion Artificial intelligence inherits environments before it transforms them. The environment

arrives

in

the

model

as

data,

but

the

data

arrive

from

institutions.

Housing

policy

becomes

geography.

Geography

becomes

price,

health,

school

composition, credit opportunity, and administrative classification. Enforcement becomes

recorded

risk.

Unequal

access

becomes

cost.

Discipline

becomes

a

student

label.

Once

encoded,

these

records

can

be

optimized

at

scale

and

returned

to

the

institution

with

the

authority

of

prediction.

SR does not claim discovery of this inheritance. It establishes the field required to study

it

as

one

continuous

process.

System

Inheritance

Studies

joins

institutional

genealogy

to

statistical

auditing,

deployment

analysis,

environmental

response,

and

recursive

retraining.

It

supplies

explicit

measures,

evidence

grades,

falsification

conditions,

and

interventions.

Its

foundational

proposition

is

therefore

precise:

historical

structure

can

persist

through

apparently

modern

systems

when

institutional

outcomes

become

data,

data

become

models,

models

become

action,

and

action

becomes

the

next

generation

of

evidence.

The system does not merely remember its history. Under identifiable conditions, it can

operationalize

that

history

as

its

future.
