    "description": "<p><strong>Substrate Coupling (SC)</strong> and <strong>Governance Fragmentation (GF)</strong> are universal SignalRupture operators that measure two independent but interacting structural conditions inside modern systems. SC captures <strong>dependency topology</strong>: the degree to which multiple critical functions, sectors, or institutions rely on the same infrastructural substrate for capacity, reliability, continuity, or performance. GF captures <strong>authority topology</strong>: the degree to which the decisions required to transform, expand, stabilize, or govern a system are distributed across independent nodes, jurisdictions, mandates, or veto points without equivalent coordinating authority.</p>\n<p>SC formalizes dependency through four measurable components&mdash;dependent‑system breadth, dependency intensity, criticality, and substitutability&mdash;and is expressed through the Substrate Coupling Index (SCI‑SR). GF formalizes authority dispersion through decision‑node count, jurisdictional dispersion, veto/approval density, mandate heterogeneity, and coordinating‑authority concentration, expressed through the Governance Fragmentation Index (GFI‑SR). The operators are empirically separable: a system may exhibit high coupling but low fragmentation (Ontario electricity), high fragmentation but moderate coupling (housing), or both simultaneously (healthcare, plastics, climate/oil governance).</p>\n<p>The manuscript hardens both operators through empirical tests. The Rogers 2022 outage demonstrates SC propagation across telecommunications, emergency calling, public alerts, payments, government connectivity, and business infrastructure. Ontario electricity demonstrates high coupling under strong coordinating authority and anticipatory planning. OECD housing governance and Japan&rsquo;s standardized zoning provide external comparative evidence for GF. Canada&rsquo;s Housing Accelerator Fund demonstrates that coordination mechanisms can reduce effective fragmentation even when structural fragmentation remains.</p>\n<p>The operators are extended through <strong>Substrate Coupling Exposure (SCE)</strong>, which incorporates capacity pressure and substitution, and <strong>GF_effective</strong>, which incorporates realized coordinating capacity. Their interaction produces <strong>Systemic Governance Pressure (SGP)</strong>, identifying systems where dependency concentration and authority dispersion jointly elevate coordination burden and systemic consequence.</p>\n<p>SC and GF therefore provide SR with a universal empirical architecture for diagnosing when systems become simultaneously dependent on common substrates and governed through fragmented authority, clarifying why some domains transform successfully while others stall despite knowledge, policy activity, or institutional intent.</p>",
