    "description": "<p><strong>Recursive Discrimination Amplification (RDA)</strong> is a dynamic SignalRupture operator inside System Inheritance Studies that tests whether institutional AI systems merely <em>inherit</em> historical disparity or whether they <em>amplify</em> it through closed-loop interaction with the environment. The paper distinguishes four frequently conflated concepts &mdash; inheritance, feedback closure, distributional drift, and amplification &mdash; and requires evidence for each stage separately. RDA formalizes the dynamic mechanism through disparity trajectories (&Delta;ₜ), an amplification ratio (ARₜ), a feedback-closure index (FCI), and two recursion channels: <strong>model recursion</strong> (AI decisions alter future labels used for retraining) and <strong>institutional recursion</strong> (AI decisions alter human or organizational behavior, producing new records that influence later decisions even without model updates). Amplification is defined precisely: ARₜ &gt; 1 with demonstrated feedback contribution. The paper tests this architecture across policing, banking, healthcare, and criminal‑legal risk assessment, grading evidence from conceptual possibility to longitudinal causal amplification.</p>\n<p>The contribution is major: RDA rejects the universal claim that &ldquo;AI amplifies discrimination&rdquo; and replaces it with a <strong>conditional, falsifiable dynamic theory</strong>. Predictive policing provides strong mathematical and simulation evidence of runaway feedback; banking reveals selective-label feedback but also strong counterevidence where FinTech reduces discrimination; healthcare shows powerful inheritance through biased proxies and simulation evidence of feedback-induced model degradation; criminal‑legal systems show theoretical repeat-use amplification but lack longitudinal causal proof. The cross-domain result is precise: <strong>AI can amplify inherited disparities only when historical disparity, consequential interaction, endogenous observation, repeated reuse, and insufficient correction align.</strong> RDA also clarifies that drift is not inherently discriminatory &mdash; drift becomes harmful only when it interacts with asymmetric feedback. The paper freezes the theoretical architecture into a five-stage sequence (Inheritance &rarr; Interaction &rarr; Feedback Closure &rarr; Drift &rarr; Dynamic Outcome) and establishes strict falsification boundaries to prevent overclaiming. Its final contribution is conceptual clarity: <strong>AI does not simply inherit history; it can become part of the mechanism that produces the future evidence that justifies its next decision.</strong> This transforms RDA from a fairness critique into a dynamic SR operator capable of explaining attenuation, persistence, or amplification across institutional loops.</p>",
