bandwidth,

identity

coherence,

and

well-being. The core test is whether changes in structural exposure precede within-person changes

in

psychological

state: Δ X_it → Δ Y_i,t+1 A multilevel or fixed-effects design can separate stable between-person differences

from

within-person

change.

Natural

experiments

—

policy

eligibility changes, administrative simplification, benefit redesign, housing stabilization,

sudden

service-access

changes,

or

platform

rule

changes

—

should

be

prioritized

because

they

provide

stronger

causal

leverage

than

cross-sectional

correlations.

XXXV.1 Provisional Collapse Psychology Evidence Ladder Level 0 — Conceptual: mechanism proposed without measurement. Level 1 — Cross-sectional association: exposure and outcome covary. Level 2 — Longitudinal prediction: exposure precedes outcome change. Level 3 — Quasi-experimental evidence: exogenous exposure change predicts

outcome

change. Level 4 — Experimental/intervention evidence: changing the exposure or mediator

changes

the

outcome. Level 5 — Cross-domain replication: mechanism reproduces across institutions,

populations,

and

countries. No SR proposition should be labeled an empirical law before it reaches replicated

Level

4

or

Level

5

evidence

appropriate

to

the

claim.

XXXVI. Discussion Psychology, psychiatry, public health, behavioral economics, and public administration

contain

substantial

evidence

that

insecurity,

uncertainty,

uncontrollability,

scarcity,

administrative

burden,

and

social

conditions

affect

psychological

functioning.

The

field-level

opening

lies

in

the

integration

of

mechanisms

that

are

usually

studied

in

separate

literatures,

with

separate

outcomes and timescales. Collapse Psychology studies their accumulation and

interaction

as

a

structural

psychological

environment. The most defensible field claim is therefore not “the subject collapses before the

institution.”

That

proposition

is

too

universal.

The

empirical

version

is:

psychological

adaptation

can

begin

before

visible

institutional

failure,

and

repeated

structural

exposure

may

alter

cognition,

affect,

perceived

control,

agency,

and

identity

while

institutions

remain

formally

operational.

This

proposition

is

measurable

and

can

be

defeated

by

evidence. Quiet Governance also requires narrowing. Friction, delay, ambiguity, and algorithmic

mediation

can

shape

behavior

without

overt

coercion,

but

intent

cannot

be

inferred

from

effect.

The

empirical

field

therefore

distinguishes

structural

effect

from

governance

intent: Observed psychological effect ≠ evidence of deliberate psychological control This distinction protects the field from converting every institutional burden into

proof

of

its

own

theory.

Collapse

Psychology

can

measure

burden,

exposure,

response,

and

adaptation

first;

claims

about

deliberate

design

require

independent

evidence

of

policy

intent,

choice

architecture,

or

differential

treatment. Collapse Psychology is an integrative empirical program. Its strongest contribution

will

be

demonstrated

if

combinations

of

structural

exposures

predict

psychological

transitions

that

are

not

adequately

captured

by

single-domain

stress

models,

and

if

interventions

on

structural

conditions

restore agency or reduce distress. Until those tests are run, the field remains empirically

grounded

but

provisionally

integrated.

into

SR constraint ≠ prevalidated SR measurement scale Measured nodes + unresolved relations = SR research object Accordingly, SR constraints are not presented here as measurements simply

They

generated

XXXVI.7 Sociology, Psychology, and the Unit-of-Analysis Problem

The Canadian narrow coercion result is especially consequential because the broader

THEORETICAL AND SR FIELD ARCHITECTURE II. The Eroded Subject in the New Cage Collapse does not begin with riots, shortages, economic crises, or institutional breakdown.

Collapse

begins

with

the

erosion

of

the

subject

—

the

slow,

cumulative

breakdown of a person’s capacity to act, imagine, resist, or even recognize the

boundaries

that

shape

their

life.

Human beings do not merely inhabit political or economic systems. They inhabit

psychological

environments .

Every

institution,

policy,

infrastructure,

and

narrative

produces

psychological

consequences

that

shape

how

individuals

understand

themselves,

their

possibilities,

and

their

futures.

Quiet Governance — the cage one does not immediately recognize — operates

not

through

overt

violence

but

through

structural

design.

It

governs

behaviour

through:

●

friction ●

ambiguity ●

procedural complexity ●

algorithmic mediation ●

emotional containment ●

infrastructural dependency ●

administrative delay ●

narrative management The cage is rarely loud. It does not require visible bars to produce psychological

consequences.

The

subject

adapts.

Yet contemporary societies increasingly rely on governance models that distribute

abundance

and

restriction

asymmetrically.

Universal

access

to

certain resources may coexist with highly mediated access to opportunity, mobility,

and

autonomy.

Under

such

conditions,

material

abundance

becomes

less

psychologically

significant

than

access ,

and

freedom

becomes

increasingly

mediated

by

systems

rather

than

rights.

The captured subject remembers freedom. The

captive ‑ born

subject

learns

to

interpret

containment

as

normality.

Traditional psychological disciplines are not designed to explain: ●

why abundance can produce dependency ●

why optional work may produce existential instability ●

why psychological captivity can exist without material deprivation ●

why identity becomes organized around containment ●

why individuals stop imagining alternative futures despite formal freedom

●

why institutional erosion produces psychological fragmentation long before

visible

collapse

Collapse Psychology exists to address these questions.

II.1 Collapse Begins Before Systems Fail Traditional collapse theories assume institutions fail first and individuals react

second.

Collapse

Psychology

reverses

this

relationship.

The subject collapses before the institution does.

Psychological erosion begins when: ●

rules become unpredictable ●

processes become labyrinthine ●

algorithms become opaque ●

mobility becomes restricted ●

autonomy becomes conditional ●

abundance becomes administratively mediated The subject is not crushed by force. The

subject

is

softened

by

design .

This represents the psychological pre ‑ collapse — the invisible phase in which individuals

lose

agency

long

before

society

loses

structure.

II.2 Quiet Governance as Psychological Atmosphere Quiet Governance creates a psychological atmosphere in which: ●

resistance feels futile ●
