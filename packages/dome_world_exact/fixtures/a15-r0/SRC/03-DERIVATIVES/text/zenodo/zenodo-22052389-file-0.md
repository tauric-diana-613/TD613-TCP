1.3 What the field does not claim •

It does not claim that all contemporary social life is collapsing. •

It does not define every hardship as structural captivity. •

It does not treat employment, debt, unpaid care, overtime, algorithms, or bureaucracy

as

inherently

pathological.

•

It does not infer institutional intent from observed burden. •

It does not claim that every domain shares one causal mechanism. •

It does not treat the “Upside Down” metaphor as a scientific variable. •

It does not replace sociology, criminology, economics, psychology, governance studies,

or

4. Structural Scene Reconstruction (SSR) SSR is the proposed field method. It is a structured reconstruction protocol, not a claim

SSR Stage Forensic question Surface (S) What formal condition appears intact? Contradiction (K) What independently measured outcome conflicts with the surface description? Requirement (Q) What must actually be supplied for stable participation or function? Durable capacity (D) What endogenous resources are available to meet that requirement? Dependency (Dep) How dependent is the actor or system on the relevant infrastructure or institution? Viable alternatives (Alt) What credible exit, substitution, or alternative pathways exist? Burden transfer (BT) Where does the unresolved requirement move? Compensation (C_E) Who or what absorbs the difference, and through what mechanism? Exposure (X) What risk, cost, delay, deprivation, or strain is generated? Epistemic visibility (V_E) Is the burden visible in the metrics, classifications, and institutional representations used to govern the scene? Outcome (O) What human, social, institutional, economic, criminological, or epistemic consequence is observed? Recovery (R) What changes would close the gap, reduce compensation, or restore effective access? 4.1 Competing reconstructions R_0 = individual-only explanation R_1 = single-domain structural explanation R_2 = cross-domain Structural Inversion reconstruction Collapse Society earns independent value only when the cross-domain reconstruction

20. Falsification and Demotion Conditions Falsification target Condition that weakens or defeats the claim Surface sufficiency If standard surface indicators repeatedly predict relevant outcomes as well as deeper reconstructions, SI adds no value. No compensation signal If requirement-capacity gaps do not precede or covary with extraordinary compensation, the compensated-continuity mechanism weakens. No recovery response If independently measured restoration of durable capacity does not reduce extraordinary compensation or improve effective access, the restoration branch fails. No incremental validity If M_CS does not improve held-out explanation/prediction over strong domain baselines, Collapse Society should be demoted to a framework. Construct non-discrimination If SI, BT, C_E, Dep, or V_E cannot be distinguished from established constructs, SR terminology should be narrowed or merged. Reverse causality dominates If outcomes consistently produce the supposed exposure architecture rather than the reverse, causal claims must be revised. Voluntary/healthy compensation If supposed burden transfer is predominantly chosen, sustainable, and not linked to gaps, it should not be classified as erosion. Cross-domain non-portability If SSR only works in one domain and fails elsewhere, field-level generality is weakened. Epistemic counterexamples If mediation, ranking, automation, or metrics improve provenance, correction, and decision quality, they must be classified as restoration or neutral mediation rather than collapse. National overreach If local or subgroup inversions do not aggregate to national conditions, the field must not infer a national collapse state.
