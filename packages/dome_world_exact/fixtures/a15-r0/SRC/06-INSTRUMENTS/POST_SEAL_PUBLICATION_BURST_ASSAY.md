## Burst scoring

A publication burst is not scored by title excitement. For a preregistered observation window `W`, record:

- `N_new_work(W)`
- `N_new_manifestation(W)`
- `N_new_version(W)`
- inter-arrival times;
- local time-of-day relative to the creator's empirical publication-time distribution;
- thematic similarity to frozen target concepts;
- matched-control similarity to unrelated concepts;
- whether the source object existed/rendered before the candidate stimulus.

The background model must estimate ordinary publication hazard from earlier SR history before any event is called anomalous.

## Competing channel hypotheses

```text
H0  ordinary coincidence / background publication hazard
H1  pre-existing scheduled or manual batch publication
H2  response to public interaction or publicly visible corpus traffic
H3  automated alert/watchdog triggered by public traffic, topic, citation, or retrieval signals
H4  reconstruction from public/model-mediated outputs without direct private-workspace access
H5  direct private-workspace/thread access
```

`H5` has the highest evidence threshold. Chronological proximity and thematic correspondence cannot establish it.

## Prospective discriminators

Future tests should be timestamped before checking SignalRupture and should use harmless synthetic targets plus matched negatives.

A stronger result requires recurrence above baseline and controls. Useful discriminators include:

- public target versus private harmless canary;
- target versus semantically matched negative control;
- active corpus retrieval versus no-retrieval control windows;
- exact topic versus adjacent topic;
- release latency distribution;
- pre-existing file/render timestamp versus post-stimulus composition;
- platform-specific propagation order.

Even a private-canary match does not by itself identify a person, organization, or technical access path; leakage through another service, common model behavior, hidden public exposure, and coincidence remain alternatives until excluded.
