## I. Governing Scientific Proposition

Experiment B should permit executing agents to discover that two or more existing research rules imply a previously unencoded relationship, and to propose a new rule, alteration, exception or experimental suspension when that relationship produces a testable structural or relational-geometric consequence.

This extends the research space beyond fixed-rule execution toward **rule-generative inquiry**.

The governing constraint is Ash Keep's **Anti-Predetermined-Discovery Condition**:

> An agent must be free to produce new rules, preserve existing rules, expose contradictions, discover failed compositions, or return no innovation without any outcome being predetermined as scientific success.

The desired scientific object is an independently testable transformation of the operative research-rule system.
The construction of additional rules, by itself, establishes neither novelty nor emergence.

## II. Permission to Challenge Research Rules

During authorized Experiment B execution, research agents may:
- Identify, challenge or propose the replacement of methodological rules.
- Recombine two or more rules to generate a candidate higher-order constraint.
- Suspend a mutable research rule within an isolated, reversible experimental branch.

This permission applies to research heuristics, provisional ontologies, classification conventions, theoretical assumptions, mathematical models and experimental methods.

## III. Wolfram-Style Operational Model

Use the Wolfram Physics Project's rewriting, multiway-system and causal-graph methodology as an explicitly identified **formal inspiration**, adapted to TD613's research environment.
Do not import its physical interpretation as an established fact about TD613.

### A. Research State
Represent an experimental research state as:
\[
S_t=(H_t,\mathcal R_t,\mathcal A_t,\mathcal P_t)
\]
Where:
- \(H_t\): typed, attributed relational hypergraph of research objects, Works, claims, sources, operators and dependencies.
- \(\mathcal R_t\): active mutable research-rule set.
- \(\mathcal A_t\): admissible observations and evidence available at time \(t\).
- \(\mathcal P_t\): provenance, temporal and acquisition identities.

Maintain the frozen experimental constitution separately as \(K_0\). No experimental rewrite may change \(K_0\).

### B. Rewriting Operation
A research rule has the form:
\[
r_i:L_i\xrightarrow{g_i}R_i
\]
Where \(L_i\) specifies a matchable input pattern, \(g_i\) specifies its guard or admissibility conditions, and \(R_i\) specifies the resulting transformation.
A lawful experimental transition is:
\[
S_t\xrightarrow{r_i,e_i}S_{t+1}
\]
Each transition must produce an attributable event \(e_i\), retaining its input identities, applied rule, observed effects, timestamp and custody coordinates.

### C. Multiway Evolution
Rather than selecting a single preferred rewrite sequence, permit bounded exploration of multiple admissible paths:
\[
\mathcal M(S_0,\mathcal R_0) = \{S_0\xrightarrow{r_i}S_1\xrightarrow{r_j}\cdots\}
\]
Construct a directed multiway graph whose vertices represent states and whose edges represent lawful rule applications. Record branching, reconvergence, dead ends, invalid transitions and distinct terminal outcomes. Exploration must have preregistered limits on depth, branching, runtime, memory and computational expenditure.

### D. Higher-Order Rule Generation
Permit a candidate rule-generating operation:
\[
r^\star=\Phi(r_i,r_j,\ldots)
\]
for at least two independently identified source rules.
The operator \(\Phi\) must be accompanied by a concrete construction or explanation. The candidate \(r^\star\) must declare its exact inputs, transformation, new constraints, failure conditions and observable consequences. Its initial classification is: `PROPOSED_RULE_UNVERIFIED`.

## IV. Relational Geometry and Causal Consequences

Use explicit computable structures rather than visually persuasive drawings:
- **Causal Event Graph:** Construct dependency graph \(C\) where nodes are rewrite events and edges identify event dependencies. Distinguish event causality from ordinary claim reachability.
- **Branchial Relations:** Where rewrite paths diverge, construct branchial relationships using recorded common ancestry and a fixed observation slice.
- **Causal Invariance & Path Dependence:** Test whether alternative rewrite orders reconverge to equivalent states. Bounded tests support bounded causal-invariance observations; they cannot establish universal causal invariance.
- **Operational Geometric Observables:** Measure defined combinatorial quantities, such as cycle rank:
\[
\beta_1 = |E| - |V| + c
\]
where \(c\) is the number of connected components. This is a defined combinatorial observable, not physical space.
