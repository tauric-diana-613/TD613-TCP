### 1. Research Question and Null Hypothesis

**Research Question:**  
*Under matched evidence, computational capabilities, retrieval affordances, time and resource limits, does reciprocal operation between the SRC Atelier and an Encyclopedia receiver produce any additional warranted scientific information beyond a competent combination of their constituent methods?*

### 3.1 Structural Baseline Separation (SRC-R02)

To prevent strawmanning the single-receiver arms ($S_0$ and $S_1$), the measurement suite strictly distinguishes between:
1. `NOT_APPLICABLE_BY_DESIGN`: The challenge tests a capability outside the architectural jurisdiction of that arm (e.g. S0 cannot execute SQLite queries; S1 cannot execute projective lexical routing). S0 has 9 N/A challenges; S1 has 14 N/A challenges.
2. `EXECUTED_FAILED`: The challenge falls within the declared capability of the arm, but execution produced an error, invalid warrant, or divergence.
3. `EXECUTED_PASSED`: The arm correctly executed the capability.

This ensures baseline single-receiver scores are reported as rates over applicable challenges rather than artificially depressed totals.

### 4. Integration Cost vs Emergent Research Operations

A central tenet of TD613 scientific discipline is distinguishing between:
1. **Mechanical Integration Cost:** The standard software engineering effort of connecting an ingestion front-end to a relational database backend (e.g., parsing strings into SQL rows).
2. **Genuinely Emergent Research Operation:** An operational capability that cannot be produced by executing the constituent methods in parallel or sequence, but arises strictly from their reciprocal interaction.

$$\text{Emergence Surplus } \Delta = \text{Info}(S_3) - \text{Info}(S_2)$$

If $\Delta = 0$, the capability is fully explained by the competent combination of existing methods, confirming $H_0$.

### 5. First-Class Treatment of Research Histories

S-01 explicitly formalizes the principle:  
**Equal terminal relation graphs do NOT imply equal research histories.**

Two systems may converge to the exact same terminal set of admitted relations $\Pi^*$, yet differ drastically in:
- The number of rejected attempts logged ($|\text{Attempts}_{\text{rejected}}|$).
- The intermediate trajectory of hypothesis revision.
- The retention of unobserved missing hops.
- The preservation of chronological non-retroactivity during incremental discovery.

Therefore, S-01 records both terminal graph equality ($\Delta_{\text{relations}}$) and trajectory history divergence ($\Delta_{\text{trajectory}}$) as first-class scientific outcomes.
